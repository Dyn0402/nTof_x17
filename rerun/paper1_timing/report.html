Paper I §7 — chamber timing from the waveforms (pass 1)

Verdict. On a cosmic muon the chamber gives its own event time to σ ≈ 40–46 ns (det2, det3, det7; 1.4–1.7 mm of drift), from the start of the charge column, at any track angle. Of that, ≈ 22–28 ns per view is not shared by the two views (noise, sampling) and falls with the signal size (roughly as amplitude−0.7); ≈ 35–40 ns is common to X and Y — the arrival of the first electrons next to the mesh — and sets the floor. The trigger adds nothing measurable on those runs (cov(start, end) ≈ 0), but ≈ 40–60 ns on the 6-23 / 6-24 runs (det4). At SPS (det4, CF₄, 14 µm/ns) the same estimator gives 160–200 ns per view (ZS): the low-amplitude end of the bench curve, not a measurement of the intrinsic floor.

Angle

The column start does not move with the track angle (median shift ≤ 8 ns from head-on to |tan θ| = 0.5 on det2/det3), and its width is the same head-on as inclined: the estimator is a time, also at normal incidence. The wft fit's t0 is not (it is pinned to the trigger in the pass, σ = 0.05 ns).

Amplitude walk or signal-to-noise? (walk_snr.py)

Signal-to-noise, not walk. At one HV (Runs 1+2, 490 V), binned in the per-track early amplitude, (X−Y)/√2 falls from 30.2 to 18.7 ns and sits on the HV-scan curve at the same amplitude (ratio 0.89–1.01): the HV improvement is the signal size. The edge estimator fits a scaled template step, so a pure amplitude change does not move it: the median event time moves by 18 ns over the whole amplitude range (most of it in the top bin) and tX−tY by 3.7 ns. Subtracting that walk curve changes σ_evt 40.7 → 40.5 ns and (X−Y)/√2 22.3 → 22.3 ns. What falls with amplitude is the width (noise over the slope of the rising edge), which no amplitude correction removes. The event time is flat in amplitude at fixed HV (40, 39, 40, 42 ns by amplitude quartile): its common part is not set by the signal size.

What this does not rule out

  • The common 35–40 ns could hold a trigger part below the method's reach: cov(start, end) carries an estimator bias of about −60 ns² (det3), so a trigger jitter ≲ 10 ns is not excluded.
  • The physical origin of the common part (first-electron arrival: primary-cluster positions, gain of the first electrons, the threshold set relative to the column maximum) is inferred, not simulated.
  • det6's uncorrelated part is 2× the others at the same HV; not chased (det6 depths already carry the v limitation).
  • The 6-23 / 6-24 trigger term is measured on the column; its cost on det4's positions is not established (the signed regression matches, the angle-specific widening is not seen).
  • SPS: only an upper bound; the intrinsic floor needs a higher-gain chamber or RAW data at the bench S/N.
