Verdict. There are two kinds of "band". The ones that cost det3 its 1.4 points are the fixed-patch bands, seen on det3 (corner x < 50 mm, y > 250 mm) and det7 (x 275–375 mm, y 25–250 mm). They come from the chamber's amplification stage. The baseline under a patch of strips wanders for about 1 ms, with no trigger, at a rate that rises steeply with the mesh field.

  • Field: the rate is ×1.9 per 10 V of resist HV on det3 (0.3 % at 465 V to 18.6 % at 525 V; none at or below 455 V). On det7 it is ×1.8 per 10 V (0.1–0.3 % at 400–430 V to 22 % at 485 V). Both go as gain1.4–1.6. The rate is flat-to-slightly-falling in the drift field (4.6 → 3.3–3.7 % from 100 to 1100 V), while the spark rate changes by ×3 over the same range.
  • Lifetime: in a trigger that comes < 1 ms after a banded one, the same chip is banded again in 44 % of cases on det3 (three runs pooled, 28 of 64; golden row 67 %; base 3 %), and beyond 3 ms at the base rate. The sign survives in only 43 % of those pairs. det7's bands are shorter-lived (10 % against a 5 % base at < 1 ms).
  • Untriggered: band chips are no closer in time to a triggered discharge than quiet chips are. Only 14 % of discharges are followed by a band in a trigger within 3 ms.
  • Same chips at n_TOF: at n_TOF they almost vanish, because the gain there is lower. Chamber A at 540 V in 90/10 gas is equivalent to 437–459 V on the bench, and it shows 0 bands in 61 k cosmic triggers. Chamber D (det7) shows 0.1–0.2 %, on the chips where it banded on the bench.

Excluded, and by what:

  • Data corruption / packet loss: 3.9 M raw words of band and quiet events matched decoded_root with 0 mismatches. The block layout is normal, and the FEU's own trailer word follows the shifted baseline.
  • Pickup on the bench: two chambers read in the same triggers band independently. det2+det3 banded together in 136 triggers, against 134 expected by chance. The patch also stays on the chamber when it is moved to other FEUs.
  • Tails of triggered discharges: the time since the last discharge on the same chip is distributed the same for band chips and quiet chips (medians 19.7 s and 22.0 s). The band amplitude does not fall with that time.
  • Ionisation from the triggering track: the rate is flat in the drift field.

Not identified: what happens at the weak spot. Candidates are micro-discharges, Malter-type emission from charged insulator (det7's board log notes bubbles with uncapped pillars), or field emission. Whatever it is, it disturbs the strips under it for about a millisecond (a slow drain of charge on the resistive strips is the obvious candidate, but the sign changes within that time, so it is not one clean exponential).

The whole-plane bands on det2, det4 and det6 are a different thing. They are scattered single strips with mixed signs. Their rate is flat in HV (det6: 3 %, ×1.06 per 10 V, up to the spark onset), and on det2/det4 they ride on spark triggers (35–38 % of spark triggers carry one). Their mechanism is not characterised here.
