The noise bands on the MX17 chambers: what they are and what they are not

rerun/bands (generated by make_report.py, 2026). Follow-up 1 of the det3 loss-categories study (rerun/satstudy). Existing data only: the June bench runs and the n_TOF runs.

Verdict. There are two kinds of "band". The ones that cost det3 its 1.4 points are the fixed-patch bands, seen on det3 (corner x < 50 mm, y > 250 mm) and det7 (x 275–375 mm, y 25–250 mm). They come from the chamber's amplification stage. The baseline under a patch of strips wanders for about 1 ms, with no trigger, at a rate that rises steeply with the mesh field.

  • Field: the rate is ×1.9 per 10 V of resist HV on det3 (0.3 % at 465 V to 18.6 % at 525 V; none at or below 455 V). On det7 it is ×1.8 per 10 V (0.1–0.3 % at 400–430 V to 22 % at 485 V). Both go as gain1.4–1.6. The rate is flat-to-slightly-falling in the drift field (4.6 → 3.3–3.7 % from 100 to 1100 V), while the spark rate changes by ×3 over the same range.
  • Lifetime: in a trigger that comes < 1 ms after a banded one, the same chip is banded again in 44 % of cases on det3 (three runs pooled, 28 of 64; golden row 67 %; base 3 %), and beyond 3 ms at the base rate. The sign survives in only 43 % of those pairs. det7's bands are shorter-lived (10 % against a 5 % base at < 1 ms).
  • Untriggered: band chips are no closer in time to a triggered discharge than quiet chips are. Only 14 % of discharges are followed by a band in a trigger within 3 ms.
  • Same chips at n_TOF: at n_TOF they almost vanish, because the gain there is lower. Chamber A at 540 V in 90/10 gas is equivalent to 437–459 V on the bench, and it shows 0 bands in 61 k cosmic triggers. Chamber D (det7) shows 0.1–0.2 %, on the chips where it banded on the bench.

Excluded, and by what:

  • Data corruption / packet loss: 3.9 M raw words of band and quiet events matched decoded_root with 0 mismatches. The block layout is normal, and the FEU's own trailer word follows the shifted baseline.
  • Pickup on the bench: two chambers read in the same triggers band independently. det2+det3 banded together in 136 triggers, against 134 expected by chance. The patch also stays on the chamber when it is moved to other FEUs.
  • Tails of triggered discharges: the time since the last discharge on the same chip is distributed the same for band chips and quiet chips (medians 19.7 s and 22.0 s). The band amplitude does not fall with that time.
  • Ionisation from the triggering track: the rate is flat in the drift field.

Not identified: what happens at the weak spot. Candidates are micro-discharges, Malter-type emission from charged insulator (det7's board log notes bubbles with uncapped pillars), or field emission. Whatever it is, it disturbs the strips under it for about a millisecond (a slow drain of charge on the resistive strips is the obvious candidate, but the sign changes within that time, so it is not one clean exponential).

The whole-plane bands on det2, det4 and det6 are a different thing. They are scattered single strips with mixed signs. Their rate is flat in HV (det6: 3 %, ×1.06 per 10 V, up to the spark onset), and on det2/det4 they ride on spark triggers (35–38 % of spark triggers carry one). Their mechanism is not characterised here.

2. Not data corruption (a)

The raw .fdf of golden-row file 003 (FEU 7 = x, FEU 8 = y) was walked word by word with an independent parser (fdfcheck.py, word classes of rerun/decoder_audit) for 60 band events and as many quiet ones per view: 3,932,160 amplitude words compared, 0 mismatches. Every chip block of a band event has the normal layout (4 header words, 64 data, 6 trailer words; band {'(4, 6)': 1920} / {'(4, 6)': 4000}). The header words (trigger id, SCA cell) of a banded chip are distributed as in quiet events of the same chip (largest KS distance 0.12). The one trailer word that differs tracks the chip's own mean amplitude (r = 0.87 x, 0.78 y): the FEU itself digitised the shifted baseline. The whole-file decoder audit (rerun/decoder_audit, 2026-10-23) already had every word of every June file identical between the two decoders with no incomplete events in this file. So the offsets are in the signal that reached the ADC.

3. Where the bands sit (b)

Per strip, the fraction of all triggers in which that strip is part of a band. Grey lines: 64-channel connector (= DREAM chip, = M1 connector) boundaries. det3 is the same corner (x < 50 mm, y > 250 mm) in three runs on two FEU pairs; det7 is x 275–375 mm, y 25–250 mm; det2, det4, det6 are spread over the whole plane.

Patch edges fall on a connector boundary in 2.9 % (x) / 11.3 % (y) of edges on the golden row, against 3.1 % for a random edge (plane ends excluded); the patches are not cut by the readout connectors. The drawings: the readout board gerbers (MX17_Geant/design/gerbers/readout_pcb) give the connector fields (pogo pads at ±100 mm along two edges) and unlabelled top-copper areas at those two edges; the resistive strips run along y and are fed / grounded only by copper buses at both y-ends (detector spec, Dylan 2026-08-07). No drawing in the repo or MX17_Documentation marks where the HV is fed onto the bus or where the mesh is connected, so the patches cannot be tied to either. det7's patch is in the middle of the plane. The only defect information in hand is the board log in the n_TOF run_config: det7 (board B) "had one or two bubbles, but the pillars underneath were still there, so just no caps"; det6 "a column of waves in the mesh and maybe a spot with no pillar caps"; det3 nothing noted. The bubble positions are not recorded.

4. Lifetime: about a millisecond

Triggers are random (Poisson, ~1.4–5.6 Hz), so some arrive within a millisecond of the previous one. On the golden row a band chip is banded again in 67% of the next triggers that come < 1 ms later (30 chips), in 7% at 1–3 ms (44), and at the base rate (3.0%) beyond. The other det3 runs give 0.27 (6-22, 22 chips) and 0.17 (6-28, 12 chips; 0.18 at 1–3 ms): pooled 28 of 64 = 0.44 against a base of 0.03. det2/det4/det6 show 0.4–0.5 at < 1 ms. So a band lasts of order 1 ms: flat across the 1.9 µs window (as measured in bands.py, τ ≫ 100 µs) but gone 3 ms later. In the persisting pairs the sign is kept in only 43% (det3; det4 91%) and the amplitude ratio spreads from ×0.5 to ×2.1 (quartiles): on det3 the baseline wanders on a ~ms scale rather than decaying from one step. det7 does not persist (0.10 vs base 0.05).

With a ~1 ms lifetime, a 4 % occupancy means ~40 band onsets per second on det3's corner alone, untriggered: far more than the discharges seen in triggered windows anywhere on det3 (4.3 % of 5.6 Hz).

5. Bands and discharges (c, d)

Same trigger: on det3, P(band | spark trigger) = P(band | no spark) within errors (table 1). On det2 and det4 a spark trigger carries a band in 35–38 % of cases (whole-plane family; these look like the aftermath of the discharge in the same window). On det6/det7 the comparison is biased — a spark makes the channels non-flat, so the band flag cannot fire in the same trigger.

Sparkiest chamber: det7 has both the most sparks (22.8 % of triggers, ≥ 8 strips of a chip > 3000 ADC in 29.8 %) and the most bands (21.6 %); det6 is nearly as sparky (20.3 %) with a band rate like det2's (6.1 %), and det3 has sparks 3–6 % with bands 4 %. Across five chambers the ranking is not monotonic: spark rate alone does not set the band rate.

Only within 3 ms is there a link: a chip that discharged in a trigger is banded in the next trigger if it comes < 3 ms later in 14% of 51 cases on det3 (det7 9% of 206), against 0.8% beyond 30 ms. A discharge can start a band, but triggered discharges are far too rare to make most of them.

Reading. The n_TOF gain is lower than the bench's at the nominal voltages: by the HV ledger (ntof_july_analysis/hv_tradeoff: gas +72.6 V, site −4.7 V, electronics +12.8/+34.8 V) chamber A at 540 V ≡ bench 437–459 V and D at 520 V ≡ bench 417–439 V. On the bench det3 has no band at ≤ 455 V and det7 0.1–0.9 % at 400–440 V (section 7), so the n_TOF beam-off rates (A 0 of 77 k, D 0.14–0.23 %) are what a gain-driven chamber effect predicts; the higher 5σ threshold alone would keep ~80 % of the bench rate (table above). det7's n_TOF bands sit on the same connectors as on the bench (x connectors 7–8, y connectors 2–6). Under beam (run_145) A and D carry 0.6 % and 1.0 %, spread over many chips: a beam-related baseline effect (flash recovery, ntof-mm-gamma-flash-response) on top, not the bench patch. run_89's B (det2) 1.3 % sits on x connector 5 and the two y-plane ends — the whole-plane family's chamber, not characterised further.

9. What this does not rule out (stated limitations)

  • The source of the ~ms disturbances is not seen directly: the DREAM records 1.9 µs per trigger, so an untriggered discharge, a burst of ion feedback, or a micro-discharge at a defect between triggers is invisible. The HV monitor samples too slowly to resolve ms current pulses. With no detector access, this cannot be closed by measurement.
  • The drawings in hand do not show the HV feed point on the resistive bus, the mesh connection or the protection network, so the patch positions cannot be compared with them.
  • The persistence numbers rest on small samples at Δt < 1 ms (12–96 band chips per run).
  • The n_TOF comparison changes several things at once (gas Ar/iso 90/10 vs 95/5, HV 520–540 V vs 490 V, noise ×2, 20 vs 32 samples, cabling and grounding), so it says the bands are a bench-condition phenomenon of these chambers, not which condition.
  • Only the golden row was checked word by word against the raw file (2 FEUs, 240 events); the other runs rest on the whole-file decoder audit.
