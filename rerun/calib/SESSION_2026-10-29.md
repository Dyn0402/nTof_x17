## 1. Templates and model shape (`templates/TEMPLATE_STUDY.md` T15, T16)
- **X and Y templates.** The X template drives both views; Y = X template ⊗ RC kernel. Last night's "Y own template is
  worse" test counted the RC twice. Redone fairly (Y own template, D_rc,y scanned 0…plan, five chambers, 1320 events):
  worse on every chamber (+0.01…+0.35°). The measured Y pulse holds the RC loss but cannot hand the charge to the
  neighbours. Shared X template stays.
- **One extra parameter per chamber/view (T15, 76 condor nodes).** Template time stretch, Dp scale, a new narrow-core
  footprint (`core_frac_<view>`, two-Gaussian, exact under the Y RC growth), Y own template. None wins on det3
  (best 1.7σ). Lateral parameters lower χ² up to 11 % but never help angles. Stretch helps det4 Y only (−0.12 ± 0.04°
  at s 0.84, split-half confirmed, not its D_rc,y): recorded, not adopted (rule).
- **Why a better shape does not help the angle (T16).** The mean centre/±1 error is 9–17 % of the peak χ² (the rest is
  plane-to-plane scatter) and is left-right symmetric about the track, so the slice centroids, hence the angle, do not
  move. A perfect-model toy gets 0.30–0.35°, det3 1.0°: the remaining error is elsewhere (bow, bright planes, δ rays).

## 3. Saturation and the error model (`saturation/SATURATION_STUDY.md`, `saturation/ERROR_MODEL.md`)
- A separate session (worktree, merged `288f6c9d`) studied the clip treatment critically: censoring variants for clipped
  samples are null; the 3550 rule misses 5–46 % of truly clipped samples (CM moves the rail; `SAT_RAIL`, det7 X −0.08);
  the post-clip floor is a clipped undershoot (`SAT_FLOOR=1`). Treating every sample ≥ ~1200 ADC as a lower bound
  (`SAT_CAP`) helped the angle.
- **Clean-track check (Dylan's objection).** Classes from raw waveforms + M3 only. The cap gain holds on 1312 clean
  single tracks (not a δ artefact) down to a 300 ADC clip; the charge degrades with the clip (x-y log-charge σ
  0.054 → 0.173, pairing power 0.90 → 0.79). The amplitude is informative.
- **Toy with an exact model** (`--sat`): clipping at 300 ADC costs nothing (0.33–0.35°) and removes far outliers; the
  correct Poisson variance gains 7–10 %. So the angle information is in where/when charge arrives; bright amplitudes add
  mostly fluctuation. In data the Poisson variance hurts (+0.10…+0.33°) and the cap helps: the mean model is wrong at
  bright samples.
- **Recipe found** (opt-in, not adopted yet): `SAT_CAP=1200` + `STRIP_TAU=0.05` (per-strip amplitude nuisance) +
  `QSOLVE_PLAIN` (charge re-solved from every sample at the fitted geometry). Improves or is neutral on all five
  chambers (det2 −0.04/−0.07, det3 −0.07/−0.02 and −0.05/−0.05 on a second 4818-event sample, det4 −0.07/−0.03 with
  positions −0.03 mm, det6 +0.03/−0.06, det7 −0.07/−0.06), charge exactly preserved. Not adopted:
  `ERR_K` (Poisson), `ERR_F` > 0.03, `QSOLVE_F` (charge low on det6/det7).
- **Gated 10-30 by a separate session: NOT adopted.** Full reco loses det6/det7 efficiency under the cap (clipped
  near-vertical columns refit as steep lines), and the strip nuisance flips sign with drift field on det3. The cap
  alone wins on all 14 held-out datasets. Per-sample rails now in `wft.io` (opt-in). `ERROR_MODEL.md` Decision.
- **Scoring trap found**: scoring a small class with an angle scale re-fitted on that class faked a +0.15° loss; scorers
  now use the all-events scale.

## Evening: the simple model is locked (Dylan)

creates on heavily clipped columns (fixable in principle: interval censor, < 3-clipped planes only, well-restricted
polish); the centre-strip pattern, the cap gain and the F4 compression read as one mechanism (width–tilt trade fed by
over-trusted bright samples; a reading, not a measurement); a static shape term cannot buy angle, a per-event width
re-opens the degeneracy. Dylan: capping strategies and a width per event are not physically convincing; cost of
leaving both out ≈ 0.05° on det3 and nothing else → **lock the simple model, document the simplifications.**
