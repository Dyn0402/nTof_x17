n_TOF tracking: re-audit before the test campaign

2026-11-24. Baseline: the rebuilt analysis view stage3_r2_final (pass 1 with the audit fixes of 22 and 23 November). Existing data only. Tests, scripts and tables: tests/ next to this file.

Verdict. The work of the last days is consistent and the rebuilt track sample is healthy. However, the test campaign was not ready to run as prepared, and the largest gain in angle resolution that we found does not need a new reconstruction at all.

  • The studies agree with each other and with the products. The code of the test campaign with every switch off reproduces pass 1 track by track. The sample of chambers A and C is uniform over the campaign.
  • The test campaign had a blocking error. Its stage-3 chain would have failed on every sub-run. This is fixed and checked. The comparison script that the campaign needs did not exist, and it now does.
  • The campaign needs changes before it is submitted. It costs about twice the estimate, chamber D needs a longer job flavour, no beam-off cosmic sub-run is in it, several decided items are in no configuration, and one switch (the seed ranking) should be corrected first.
  • A new angle map is the largest measured gain. A smooth spline map fitted to the same cosmic lines is 16 to 26 % better than the current map on held-out lines, in chambers A, C and D. It is a table-level step.
  • The drift geometry found on the bench is present at n_TOF. The tilt of the drift lines is the gradient of one potential, as an electrostatic origin requires. The correction has to be measured at n_TOF, because the cathode changes shape with every mounting. The cosmic lines measure it down to the noise of a single track.
  • A second reconstruction pass would gain efficiency and purity, not resolution. We found no reconstruction-level change that improves the angle of a track.

Configuration ref is the code of the test campaign (code_e86028b5) with every switch off. 1.4 The sample over the campaign

We made one row per sub-run and chamber from stage3_r2_final and looked for sub-runs and eras that deviate. The known conditions all appear: the access of 27 July, the dead connector of chamber A in run_79 and run_81, the latency epochs of D. The fraction of tracks with no time since the flash is 0.68 %, against 14.7 % before the fix of the two-slim error.

  • Chambers A and C are uniform. The v(t) correction holds their angle scale to 1 to 2 % while the drift velocity changes by 14 % on C. No step of tens of ns exists in t0 anywhere. The horizontal source crossing is constant from run_84 on (A −10.81 mm, C −11.14 mm, with a spread of 0.3 mm between runs).
  • Chamber D has an efficiency hole at x_local 0 to 60 mm for the whole campaign. Late gated tracks reach 9 to 13 % of the level of the neighbouring regions. From a linear interpolation, about 26 % of D's late sample is missing there. This region is where D sees the source. The occupancy change at run_133 is not dead strips coming back: the four masked strips stay dead, and five live channels of the same connector became hotter after a new pedestal was loaded on 4 August. The fix is a fiducial exclusion or an efficiency that changes at run_133. No new mask is needed.
  • The angle scale of D does not follow v(t). The first reading of the stability check (0.35) came from a change of which tracks are in the sample. Against the pointing truth the dependence is 0.04 ± 0.08.
  • Chamber B has a noisy channel group (two x channels and y channels 13 to 63) until about run_117. It holds 17.8 % of B's late tracks through run_106, and these are fakes. It touches about 1 % of the B–D lines. The fix is a table-level exclusion of that box through run_132.
  • Unexplained and small: a t0 step of 2 to 6 ns and a velocity jump at run_150, a common dip of 5 to 10 % in the track rate in runs 139 to 147, and a loss of D tracks at x_local 60 to 120 mm in runs 135 to 150 that the beam-off runs do not show.

1.5 Is the beam angle scale off by 1 to 5 %?

Two tests measured the angle scale of the beam tracks as the slope of the measured horizontal tangent against the tangent expected from the track position and the source. They find 0.990 (A), 0.965 (C) and 0.946 ± 0.009 (D). The cosmic lines give D a scale of 0.997 ± 0.004. We therefore applied the same estimator to the Geant4 capsule electrons, where the truth is known.

  • On Geant4 truth, with no detector, the estimator gives 0.989, 0.986, 0.972 (A, C, D) with the nose library and 0.931, 0.924, 0.987 with the EAR2-beam library. The statistical errors are 0.011 to 0.022.
  • In Geant4 the deficit comes mostly from scattering and from the cut |tan| < 0.5. The size of the source contributes less than 1 %.
  • So the deficit seen in the data is consistent with an effect of the estimator. It is not evidence of a detector scale error, and we do not propose a scale correction for D. The cosmic lines remain the reference of the angle scale.
  • Geant4 cannot give the bias to 1 %. The two libraries differ by about 5 %, the order of the chambers does not match the data, and the Geant4 pointing core is 30 to 60 % wider than the data.

Two side findings on the Geant4 digitisation chain, not followed up: the pass-1 reconstruction of the digitised events reads tangents 3.4 ± 1.0 % (nose) and 9 ± 2 % (EAR2) steeper than the truth line, and the y view of chamber C in the EAR2 product is not correlated with the truth (0.02).

2. The drift geometry at n_TOF

On the cosmic bench, the drift lines of chamber A are tilted and bent by two surfaces, the mesh and the cathode. The bench audit of the same night confirmed this picture and found that the cathode is a foil whose shape changes with every mounting, while the dish of the mesh stays (rerun/paper1_gap/audit/AUDIT_2026-11-24.md). For n_TOF this has one direct consequence: a map made on the bench is not a property of the chamber, so the correction must be measured in the n_TOF mounting. We tested three ways to measure it with the data we have.

2.1 The distortion at n_TOF is electrostatic

  • The tilt field is close to curl-free. The x view and the y view each measure one component of the tilt of the drift lines. If the tilt comes from the shape of the electrodes, the two components are the gradient of one potential. The curl of the measured field is 1.03 × 10⁻⁴ per mm on A and 1.06 × 10⁻⁴ on C. The noise gives 0.88 and 0.97 × 10⁻⁴, and a shuffled map gives 3.0 × 10⁻⁴. A small curl above the noise remains (χ² 114 and 155 for 49 points).
  • Requiring one potential costs nothing. A spline map whose tilt is the gradient of one potential is never worse on held-out lines than a map with two free tilt fields (differences −0.30 to +0.06 × 10⁻³).
  • The scale is common to the two views on C, but not on A. On C the per-cell scales of the two views correlate at 0.86 with slope 0.96 ± 0.03. On A the correlation is 0.28. One gap map therefore does not describe the scale of A.
  • The bench bow block over-corrects chamber A at n_TOF and is right for C (bench audit). This is expected if the dish of A is mostly the sag of a horizontal board, since the chambers stand vertically at n_TOF. The in-situ map is fitted on top of the bow block, so it absorbs this.

The smoothing is chosen by cross-validation over runs. 2.3 Capsule pointing: an independent confirmation, and an input for D

A late track from the capsule has an expected horizontal tangent from its position alone. With about one million late tracks per chamber, the pointing measures the distortion per 25 mm cell with an error of 0.001 to 0.0015. At one position the expected tangent is nearly fixed, so the pointing measures one combination of tilt and scale and cannot separate them.

  • The pointing confirms the map. The spline map fitted to the cosmic lines alone predicts the pointing cells with a correlation of 0.93 (A), 0.70 (C) and 0.94 (D).
  • It does not improve the map of A or C. Adding the pointing cells to the spline fit changes the held-out cosmic width by less than 0.3 × 10⁻³.
  • It helps D. On D the cell-to-cell rms on unseen beam tracks falls from 0.0082 to 0.0065 when the pointing joins the B–D lines. The candidate uses both for D.
  • It covers a band only. The pointing works where the expected |tan| is between about 0.10 and 0.44. The edges in x are outside the gate.
  • Electrons and muons differ. The pointing residual depends on the track charge (0.008 to 0.010 rms between charge tertiles).

2.4 Drift-time maps: measurable everywhere, but not a substitute for an angle truth

Each track carries its own drift-time column, so a map of the column duration needs no reference. We used the 90 % point of the charge profile of late tracks.

  • The maps are stable. The first and second halves of the campaign correlate at 0.97 (A), 0.99 (C), 0.99 (D) and 0.90 (B). The x and y views agree, and beam-off tracks give the same maps.
  • On A the map behaves like a gap map. The duration varies by about 5 % rms, and its relation to the cosmic scale has the slope that a gap change predicts (−0.37 ± 0.08 and −0.31 ± 0.05 for −0.34). The correlation is only −0.3, so the duration explains a small part of the scale.
  • On C and D the map is too large to be a gap. C tilts by 10 % per 100 mm along both axes and has a rectangular block that is more than 15 % long. D tilts by 13 % per 100 mm along x. Read as a gap, these give 27 to 33 mm and 28 to 34 mm.
  • The scale gradient of C along y is not a drift-velocity effect. The duration falls by 7.8 % per 100 mm along y, so a velocity change would make the scale rise. The scale falls.
  • As a correction it is weaker than the map. The bench audit predicted the tilt from the drift-time map at n_TOF. This recovers 7.5 % on A x, about 2 % on A y and C, and 12 to 13 % on D. The spline map gives 24 to 35 % against no map.

One possible reading of C and D is that the 90 % point of the charge profile also responds to electron attachment, since B, C and D carried water and A was dry. This is an inference and we did not test it.

2.5 What a track's own timing tells

The two views of a chamber see the same electrons, but the fit treats them separately and leaves the column duration free. We tested on the cosmic lines whether per-track timing predicts the angle error.

  • A view whose column is longer than the local median reads a shallower slope (Spearman −0.08 on A, −0.16 on C). The shift is 2 to 3 % of the slope for steep tracks.
  • A correction built from it gains 0.4 to 3.3 % on held-out lines, and nothing measurable on the beam.
  • The relative errors of the x and y views of one track are not correlated (−0.002 ± 0.008 on A, −0.018 ± 0.008 on C). A joint x–y column in the fit would therefore gain less than 0.5 %.

For this reason we propose no timing correction and no joint x–y fit.

Step 3. The decision on a second pass

From what we measured, a second pass would bring the following.

  • About 4 to 8 % more late single tracks on A and C, near normal incidence (head-on rescue).
  • A cleaner sample before 12 ms (ringing correction). The clean tracks before 12 ms are about 9 % of all clean tracks of A, so this is a gain of statistics of that order at most.
  • Fewer split children at the t0 limit (0.36 to 0.44 → 0.25 to 0.36).
  • More second tracks of separated in-time pairs (injection: 76 → 87 % on A, 71 → 84 % on C).
  • The stored depth profiles and the waveform flag, which no table-level step can supply.

It would not improve the angle of a track. The angle map is at the noise of a single track, per-track timing gains at most 3 %, and a joint x–y fit gains nothing. The drift geometry needs nothing from a new reconstruction: the tangent is recomputed from the fitted speed in stage 3, so the whole correction is a table step.

4. What this audit does not rule out

  • The single switches were not run. Every attribution in section 1.3 is an inference from one configuration with all switches on, on 12 acquisitions of four sub-runs.
  • The gain of the spline map is measured on cosmic muons. On beam electrons the pointing core is set by scattering, so the gain shows only as a smaller bias from cell to cell. We have no direct measurement of the effect on an opening angle.
  • The spline fields have structure that we did not explain: a diagonal ridge on A, a scale band at the bottom of C, and scales down to −0.28 (A) and −0.36 (C) near the edges. Outside the region with training lines the map is clamped. On D this region covers 66 % of the chamber, and 4.4 % of D's beam tracks lie outside it.
  • The noise of a track could not be split into its sources. The residuals of A and C against the same line are anticorrelated (−0.08), so the simple decomposition fails.
  • The drift-time maps of C and D are not understood. They are stable and real, but they are not a gap.
  • The scale of the two views of A differs, which one gap map cannot give.
  • The beam angle scale is known only through the cosmic lines. The pointing estimator is biased by 0 to 8 % in Geant4, depending on the source model.
  • The cause of D's hot channels after 4 August is an inference. The per-channel thresholds were not read.
  • Chamber B has no angle, so nothing here applies to it beyond positions.
