n_TOF pass 1: where the tracking can still improve

Study code rerun/ntof/improve/; inputs: the pass-1 analysis view (v(t) angles, gated = true |tan| < 0.5). Cosmic truth: 46,171 A–C lines from 72 beam-off runs (one gated track per chamber, lines within 60 mm). Beam check: the late (≥ 20 ms) capsule tracks of the production runs.

Verdict. The largest fixable error in the A and C angles is a position-dependent angle distortion of 0.008–0.016 rms in tan (up to 0.04–0.05 at the edges), stable in time. An in-situ correction map fitted on the cosmic lines improves the held-out single-view resolution by 11–18 %. On the beam capsule tracks, which are independent of the map, the map also raises the capsule-pointing excess, narrows the core and removes the A−C focus asymmetry (A − C: +0.050 → +0.000). The bench bow carried into pass 1 helps C and is neutral on A, so it stays. The 13 % angle tail on the cosmic line is mostly impure truth (showers, two-particle events), not failed fits.

1. The angle tails are mostly in the truth sample

13–14 % of views miss the joined A–C line by more than 0.15 in tan. The misses are shared: A and C are both wrong 3× more often than chance (x: 5.5 % against 1.9 %). When the other chamber and this chamber's other view agree with the line within 0.05, a view's own tail is 1.0–2.1 %. The flagged views carry about half the charge, a fifth of the Δχ², and 5–9 competing-cluster strips on A: extra particles in the chamber. So this is not a fit failure to chase.

3. A position-dependent angle distortion remains

The pattern is real. Split by run parity, the two halves agree cell by cell with correlation 0.94–0.97. Split early/late in the campaign, the tilt terms agree at 0.70–0.97 and C's scale at 0.84–0.92. The cell statistical error is ~0.003.

The A−C antisymmetric focus residual of pass 1 (4.6 % in focus_split) had been read as geometry (a distance or an axis offset). The cosmic map removes it without knowing where the source is, so most of it is the angle distortion above. D's source z crossing (−5.55 mm) is therefore not confirmed by the A/C focus. It may carry D's own distortion, which this study cannot measure: the B–D cosmic lines do not close (D's line misses B's point by a median +117 mm in both coordinates), so B cannot yet serve as D's truth.

What this does not rule out

  • The cosmic truth uses A and C positions in the global frame. A position error of 1 mm moves j by 0.002, well below the map, but an error in a chamber's global placement that varies over the plate would enter the map as an angle term.
  • The map is fitted on cosmic angles (|j| < 0.45, mostly steep in y). Beam electrons at the edges of the angle range rely on the per-cell linear term.
  • Edge cells with few lines fall back to the chamber-wide fit; the map clamps at the outer cell centres (±175 mm).
  • D and B have no map; D's angles keep whatever distortion they have.
  • Nothing here changes the same-chamber data/Geant4 factor ~10, which is the source model, not the angles.

Next steps (existing data only)

  1. Decide whether the map becomes a table-level correction in the analysis view (like the v(t) κ step), with <col>_map twins, and rerun the angle-sensitive downstream (capsule focus and pose, same-chamber and two-arm opening angles, vertex imaging).
  2. Fold the map into a bundle-level angle correction for the bench-bow terms on A (whose bench bow is neutral).
  3. Find why the B–D cosmic lines do not close; with them, D gets its own map and its source z can be re-measured.
