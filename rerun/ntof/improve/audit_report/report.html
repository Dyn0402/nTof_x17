n_TOF pass 1: audit of the tracking and of the capsule imaging

Written 2026-11-21. Code: rerun/ntof/improve/tracking_audit.py and make_audit_report.py. Input: the pass-1 analysis view (v(t) angles, in-situ map, censored-window cut, gated = true |tan| < 0.5), production runs after the 27 July access, and Geant4 through the pass-1 reconstruction. Existing data only.

Verdict. The width of the capsule image is not a tracking problem. In Geant4, the true lines of capsule electrons point at the capsule no better than the data do, so the 45 to 60 % "in focus" fraction is multiple scattering of MeV electrons. We also could not explain the 10 mm x offset of the source with a tracking effect. However, the audit found three things that do bias the present analysis.
  1. 14.7% of the production tracks have no time since the flash and no scintillator flags. Sub-runs that span two n_TOF run numbers have two slim files, and the code reads only the first one. Every study that cuts on the time since the flash loses these tracks, and some of them were taken for beam-off cosmic events.
  2. Before about 12 ms most gated tracks in chambers A and C are not particles. They are fits to the disturbed baseline after the flash. They have a quarter of the charge of a real track. They make 63% (A) and 62% (C) of the gated tracks before 12 ms, and 44% (A) of the same-chamber pairs at 12 to 15 ms. A minimum charge of 600 ADC in each view removes them and keeps 99.7% (A) and 98.7% (C) of the real tracks.
  3. Chamber D is still heavily contaminated after the censored-window cut. 31% of its tracks after 20 ms do not point at the capsule (A: 6%, C: 8%), and they sit at a few strip positions. With a clean selection the fraction is 4%, and the z position of the source read through D is the same in every time window (-4.0 and -4.2 mm). This explains why two estimators gave −5.5 and −1.7 mm.
Two code errors were also found: the scintillator-stack study extrapolates the tracks along the bars with the wrong sign of the y slope, and the reconstruction maps the slope of some paired tracks with the other track's position.

1. The capsule image width is electron scattering

We measure how well a track points with the distance between the track line and the source, in the plane through the capsule that is parallel to the chamber. The source position is the band crossing of the late tracks of that chamber. We compare three samples in the central 200 mm of chambers A and C: the data after 20 ms, the true lines of Geant4 capsule electrons in the drift gap, and the same Geant4 events after the digitiser and the pass-1 reconstruction.

Half of the data tracks pass within 10.8 mm (A) and 11.9 mm (C) of the source. For the Geant4 truth the value is 14.3 mm, and the reconstruction changes it to 15.3 mm. The Geant4 width depends strongly on the electron energy: 25 mm at 1 to 2 MeV and 10 mm above 4 MeV. This means that the tails of the image are scattered low-energy electrons and not badly measured tracks. The data are narrower than Geant4, so the electrons that give tracks in the data appear to be more energetic than in the Geant4 library. That is a question for the Geant4 source model and the trigger emulation, like the same-chamber pair rate.

We also looked for a track property that separates pointing from non-pointing tracks after 20 ms. There is none that matters: the half-width stays between 10.3 and 10.7 mm (A) across χ², strip count, seed size, t0, the x to y charge ratio, the column duration and the candidate rank. The tracks that miss the capsule by more than 60 mm are 6% (A) and 8% (C) of the late sample. They are spread uniformly over the chamber, the scintillator wall fires for 84 % (A) and 93 % (C) of them, and their charge is normal. They are real particles that do not come from the capsule.

2. Tracks without a time since the flash

The time since the flash and the scintillator flags of a trigger come from the slim n_TOF file of its sub-run. A sub-run whose DREAM acquisitions span two n_TOF run numbers has two slim files. Four readers take only the first file: trigger_time.slim_path, scintillators.read_slim (and so the slim export), candidate_filter (stage 1) and the slim inventory of freeze_sample. On EOS, 77 of the 293 production sub-runs have more than one slim file, and 14.3% of the slim bytes are in a file that is not the first. For example, run_116 stat090_0027 has the files 224639 (events 1 to 7231) and 224640 (events 8027 to 110526), and only the first is used.

Over the production runs 14.7% of the tracks have no time. These tracks are dropped by every study that selects on the time since the flash, so the capsule imaging, the pair studies and the scintillator maps use about 85 % of the data they could use. In addition, improve/line_residuals.py selects beam-off events as "time is null". For this reason 14,742 of the 46,171 A-C "cosmic" lines that the angle map was fitted on come from production runs. A refit on the true beam-off runs changes the map by about 0.001 in tan, so the map stands, but finding 1 of the improve study (13 to 14 % of views off the line) is 6 to 7 % on real cosmic events.

3. The gated tracks before 12 ms are mostly not particles

A track from the capsule after 20 ms leaves a median charge of about 1,600 ADC in each view, and fewer than 1.5 % of these tracks have less than 600 ADC. Before 8 ms most gated tracks have 100 to 400 ADC. These low-charge tracks are 22 to 30 strips wide, their χ²/dof is 6 to 19, their fitted t0 stays at its starting value, the wall fires for a quarter of them, and the fraction that points within 15 mm of the capsule is the chance value. The raw waveforms of such events show about 80 strips above +5σ and 80 strips below −5σ, all peaking in the same sample. So these are fits to the ringing baseline after the flash. The gate lets them through because it only asks for a column duration, |tan| < 0.5 and an x to y time coincidence. It has no requirement on charge, χ² or significance.

The low-charge tracks often come in groups, so they make pairs. At 12 to 15 ms the pair fraction of chamber A falls from 1.12 % to 0.68 % with the charge minimum. The improve study (finding 9) moved the start of the rate fit from 12 to 25 ms because 12 to 20 ms "carries a different population" with a worst-leg χ²/dof of 11.9. That population is these tracks. 4. Chamber D

In chamber A the tracks that do not point are spread uniformly. In chamber D they pile up at a few positions (x_local near −184, −136, −112 and +124 mm, and y_local near +2 to +30 and −140 mm). These are windows on hot or partly censored strips that the present rule (an all-censored x window, or a y window at least half censored) does not catch. Before 20 ms nearly all gated D tracks are of this kind: the clean selection keeps 1.1% of them at 15 to 20 ms.

The z position of the source seen through D depends on this contamination. With the present selection the band crossing is -1.8 mm at 20 to 30 ms and -3.8 mm at 30 to 75 ms. With the clean selection it is -3.98 ± 0.05 and -4.16 ± 0.03 mm. The clean selection keeps only 74% of D's reference tracks, mostly because of the χ² part, so it is a demonstration and not a final cut for D.

In all three chambers the source is a flat line in height, from -72 mm to about 14 mm, with a sharp lower edge and a softer upper edge. Its length is 86 to 89 mm, which is close to the length of the capsule. Geant4 with the centred, untilted capsule gives a compact peak near −30 mm, and the reconstruction does not stretch it. So the flat line is not a tracking effect. The band crossing (−30 mm) is the centre of this line. It agrees with the Geant4 nose position, but the shapes do not agree: in the data the emitting length reaches about 40 mm below the drawn nose. This is a constraint for the capsule pose question and it is not used in the pose fits.

6. The x offset of the source is not a tracking effect

The late band crossing is -10.85 mm in A and -10.85 mm in C, with or without the clean selection, and the same from 15 ms on. A and C look at the capsule from opposite sides, so their local x axes point in opposite global directions. A bias of the reconstruction that is the same in the local frame of both chambers (an offset of the slope, of the strip coordinate, of t0, or of the reference depth) therefore moves the two images in opposite global directions. It cannot produce a common offset. The A-C cosmic lines cannot see a common shift of the chamber pair relative to the capsule either. Note that the in-situ map is fitted on the A-C lines. For this reason the A and C images agree almost by construction, and half of any relative x misalignment of A and C goes into the common position. The difference A minus C is therefore no longer an independent measurement that a pose fit can use.

Before 12 ms the crossings of A and C move apart in global x (A to -18 mm, C to +24 mm at 4 to 8 ms). This is the same direction in the local frame of each chamber, so it is contamination of the estimator and not a displacement of the source.

7. The imaging estimators

We also reviewed the estimators themselves, with toy sources and by injecting early tracks into late samples. None has a sign or frame error, and the x offset of the source is not produced by an estimator: four estimators give −10.4 to −10.7 mm (A) and −10.5 to −11.4 mm (C) on the analysis view. However, several quoted numbers are inflated or are not the same quantity.

  • D's z from capsule_tof tilt (−1.7 mm) is not a measurement. The step uses all D tracks after 12 ms, and 59 % of that sample are the non-pointing tracks of section 4. The robust line in crossing clips at 3 times the MAD of the residuals. On D the MAD is 52 mm, so nothing is clipped and the fitted slope is −0.15 where a pointing sample gives −1. The value changes from −1.7 to +7.5 to −2.7 mm between the three views. With late, wall-confirmed tracks four estimators give −3.8 to −4.8 mm, and the clean selection above gives −4.1 mm. We would quote z = −4.5 ± 1 mm.
  • crossing fails abruptly when the background grows. Adding 20 % of early tracks to the late samples moves A by −0.9 mm and C by −1.1 mm, and adding 40 % moves A by −7 mm. D's late sample moves by −6.6 mm with only 10 %. A fit restricted to 100 mm around the crossing is much more robust (A moves by 0.2 mm at 40 %).
  • campaign_imaging does not read the analysis view. It uses the raw fit tangent of the reco tree with no gated, no map, no censored-window cut and no cut on the time since the flash, so its output is identical in the map and no-map chains. Its fit window (30 to 130 mm about the surveyed foot) is truncated on one side by the plastic acceptance, which moves A by +0.5 and C by −0.4 mm. About half of the "A-C half difference 1.16 mm" is this truncation. So the −9.1 mm of the imaging step and the −10.9 mm of the pose step are not the same quantity.
  • The focus scale split by the sign of tan is the nominal axis. focus_split and capsule_focus measure the miss about x = 0, and the source is at −10.9 mm. About the measured source the split disappears (A 0.86 / 1.14 becomes 1.00 / 1.00, C 1.14 / 0.85 becomes 0.98 / 1.01). The pooled scale is also about 0.015 too low for the same reason, so the common scale is 0.994 and not 0.977.
  • The pose toy has a fixed A minus C offset. capsule_tof.FOOT_SIM['C'] is 16.4 mm and the Geant4 geometry has 17.3 mm (SimConfig.hh, run_digi.FOOT). This makes the toy's x in C 0.9 mm too negative. The rest of the +2.3 to 2.7 mm that every pose shows is probably the size of the library (about 2,500 capsule tracks per chamber, reused in every pose).
  • Smaller points. The null of track_quality shuffles inside |tan| bins and keeps part of the pointing, so its excess is about one third low. Its cache (tracks_AC.parquet) predates the map and is returned whatever the view. capsule_pose.Z_BD is from the retired chain.

8. Code errors

  • Scintillator stack, y slope. The analysis view stores tan_raw_y with the sign of tany. ntof_scint_stack/extract.py multiplies it by TAN_SIGN_Y again, so tan_v_raw = −tany for every track (checked on run_145, chambers A, C and D). The along-bar prediction is therefore extrapolated in the wrong direction, and the fitted along-bar scale is 0. The x direction (λ = 0.85) is not affected.
  • Bow mapping of paired tracks. wft/reco.py (_worker_fit) maps the slopes of each pair with the pair's own coordinates and then maps the two plane winners together. When the winners belong to different pairs, each takes the coordinate of the other pair. In run_145 this affects 19% to 25% of the tracks in events with two or more tracks in one chamber. The error is small: the median |Δtan| is below 0.0012 and the 90th percentile is 0.020 (chamber C).

The stage-1 merge duplicates two rows (run_139). On chamber D in the early runs the reconstruction's own slope cut ends at 0.48 to 0.50 for a quarter of the tracks, below the 0.5 acceptance. map_view.py has no guard against being applied to an already mapped view. Two-track children ignore the t0 pin (31 % are more than 180 ns from it). The head-on rescue is not wired into the beam driver, and chamber C has half the tracks of chamber A at |tan x| < 0.08.

What this does not rule out

  • We did not test the efficiency of the reconstruction for real tracks before 12 ms. The clean selection removes the ringing fits, but a real track on a ringing baseline may also fail it. Geant4 overlays on early triggers would measure this.
  • The Geant4 comparison of the image width uses the capsule libraries as they are. The data are narrower than Geant4. We did not find out whether this is the electron spectrum, the trigger (wall and plastic) or the source extent.
  • The x offset of 10 mm and the height profile are geometry or physics. Neither the tracks nor the cosmic lines can tell whether the chambers or the capsule are displaced.
  • The clean selection was chosen by hand on the whole campaign. Its thresholds were not optimised, and for chamber D it is too strict.
  • Chamber B was not audited (it has positions only).
