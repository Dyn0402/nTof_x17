n_TOF channel layer: dead/hot masks per chamber and era

Verdict. Two mask eras are enough for the pass-1 scope. A-x connector 8 (FEU 3 ch 448-511) is dead as a whole in E1 (run_79-81) and live from run_83 on; no other connector drops out in any sampled in-scope acquisition. The 9 August A-y connector-8 dropout is confined to run_161 (an HV scan outside the scope); run_160 and run_162 are live. Chamber D carries most of the mask (its period-5 X shorts and connector-edge hot strips); A, B and C are light.

Headline. 1062 acquisitions censused (first and last of every kept sub-run of the pass-1 registry); two mask eras; censored channels summed over the eight views: E1_pre_access 442, E2_post_access 342 (of 4096). Cabling: the n_TOF channel to strip map is identical to the bench map for every chamber, both views, all 512 channels, in all 77 n_TOF run_configs, so bench lists carry by channel number (no remap).

What was compared

Each mask is the F12 rule (rerun/model_audit/ws_dead_charge/REPORT.md): chamber-side faults by position plus this dataset's own faults from its own hits, plus the SPS hot rules (rerun/sps_junk/README.md). Hits (combined_hits_root) are used for QA only. Rules and constants: rules.py; persistence for hot: hot in at least 25% of the era's sampled acquisitions.

What this does not rule out

  • A channel that is faulty only in sub-runs that were not sampled (two acquisitions per sub-run were). The per-row SPS probe in the driver would catch it per acquisition.
  • Hot channels that are hot in fewer than 25% of acquisitions are not masked (listed with their frequency).
  • Flank tests need two bright neighbours: strips at the plate edges and inside long dead runs are judged by the silent rule only.
  • Coupled channels are an event-by-event effect at SPS; a static list can only approximate it.
  • Shorts are voted per acquisition: the first sampled acquisition of every run in E2 (73), every sampled acquisition in E1 (46); a short must replicate in at least half of them.
