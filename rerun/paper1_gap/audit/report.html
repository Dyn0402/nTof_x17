Audit of the chamber A gap and surface analysis (2026-11-24)

This note audits rerun/paper1_gap/ (Paper I §5.1 and appendix app:gapmap). The audit reran the committed chain, then ran twelve independent tests on the existing data. Each test has its own report in results/<tag>_RESULT.md and its scripts in <tag>_*.py. This file is the source of report.html (make_audit_report.py).

Verdict

  • The physical picture is right. The drift lines of chamber A are tilted and bent by the shape of two surfaces, the mesh and the cathode. Four new tests that could have failed confirm this. None of them was used by the audited analysis.
  • Several statements of the appendix do not hold as written. The quoted errors on the surface curvatures are 2 to 3 times too small. "The cathode is flat along y and follows the board along x" is not a stable result. The fitted β = 1.0 is an artifact of averaging the two views. A quadratic surface is the wrong description of the cathode.
  • The cathode is a foil that changes shape every time the chamber is mounted. The mesh dish is the same in every mounting. The gap map is different in each of the three mountings we have (6-22, 6-25, 6-27/28), and it is stable in time, with the mesh voltage and with the drift voltage (700 to 1100 V) inside one mounting. In the golden mounting the gap has a diagonal ridge about 1.5 mm high.
  • The tracking gain is real and can be made larger. An unbinned spline map gives an angle resolution (s68) of 0.69° on X and 0.60° on Y, against 0.92° and 0.81° for pass 1. This is 26 % better than pass 1, and it is at the statistical limit of the map. However, the map belongs to one mounting. It does not transfer to another mounting of the same chamber.
  • Most of the correction can be made without reference tracks. The drift-time map of a mounting gives its gap map, and the tilt follows as ∇h + a ∇g with a ≈ 0.4. In the 6-25 mounting, where the golden map gives nothing, this recovers 82 to 91 % of the gain of that mounting's own reference map.
  • At n_TOF the bench bow block over-corrects chamber A, the bench fine map of chamber A still helps, and the drift-time map gives chamber D its first angle correction (12 to 13 % in one view).

The recommended changes to the analysis and to the paper are in the section "Recommendations". Nothing was shipped and no paper text was changed.

1. Four new tests of the two-surface picture

We call h(x, y) the height of the mesh and c(x, y) the height of the cathode, with z up. The gap is g = c − h. In the long-wavelength limit a drift line at fractional height s (0 at the mesh, 1 at the cathode) has the slope ∇h + s (∇c − ∇h). The audited analysis used only the mean of this slope over the column.

1.1 The tilt field is a gradient field

An electrostatic distortion of the drift field displaces the drift lines by the gradient of a scalar. The tilt measured by the X strips and the tilt measured by the Y strips must therefore obey ∂τ_x/∂y = ∂τ_y/∂x. An effect of one view (electronics, charge sharing, the resistive layer) has no reason to obey this relation. The audited fit imposed the relation and never tested it.

  • At the level of the fitted planes, the two cross gradients differ by (0.6 ± 0.4) and (0.1 ± 0.2) × 10⁻³ per 100 mm in run 1 and run 2. The own-axis gradient is 10.5 × 10⁻³ per 100 mm in both views.
  • At fine scale we compare ∂τ_x/∂y of one run with ∂τ_y/∂x of the other run, so that the noise is independent. The correlation is 0.92 to 0.95, which equals the noise ceiling. The curl-free fraction is 0.96 ± 0.02 (42 mm cells) and 1.00 ± 0.02 (34 mm cells).
  • The solenoidal part is below 1.5 % of the variance (2σ), which is below 0.05° rms.
  • The fine structure itself reproduces between the runs with r = 0.91 to 0.99. Its rms is 0.23 to 0.46° on X and 0.34 to 0.44° on Y. It saturates at cells of 34 to 42 mm.

1.2 The drift lines have the predicted shape

We measured the shape of the drift lines directly from the waveforms. For each time sample we take the charge centroid of the track image and compare it with the reference track. The regression on tan θ_ref gives, for each time bin, the height z of the charge and its sideways displacement δ. This calibrates time to depth with the telescope geometry itself, so the shaping of the signal does not enter.

Charge liberated at height z lands displaced by δ(z) = h′ z + (c′ − h′) z² / (2 g). The slope at the mesh end is therefore the mesh slope, and the curvature is the gradient of the gap.

  • Along y the drift lines bend with the predicted sign and size. A cathode parallel to the mesh is excluded at 8.8σ.
  • Along x four of the five slices show the same constant curvature, which is the tilt of the gap along x. The slice nearest the −x edge bends the other way. For this reason the curvature of the cathode along x is undecided.

1.3 The fine gap structure is on the cathode

A relief δc on the cathode changes the gap by +δc and tilts the drift lines by +∇(δg)/2. A relief δh on the mesh changes the gap by −δh and tilts the lines by −∇(δg)/2. The regression of the fine tilt on the gradient of the fine gap map (cross-run) gives k = +0.67 ± 0.12 on X and +0.46 ± 0.16 on Y. The mesh alone is excluded at 6 to 9σ. The smooth dish along y behaves like the mesh (k = −0.75).

The same test gives β for the fine structure: d ln v / d ln T = −0.41 ± 0.08, so β = 0.69 (0.51 to 0.95). Magboltz gives 0.68. The fitted β = 1.0 ± 0.2 of the appendix came from averaging the two views. The smooth velocity map of the X view has a slope of −0.74 ± 0.02, which is neither a gap change (−0.41) nor a gas change (−1). The Y view gives −0.45 ± 0.03. We have not explained the X value.

1.4 The tilt is predicted from the drift time

If the picture is right, the tilt map follows from the mesh shape and the gap map: τ = ∇h + a ∇g, with a = 1/2 for an equal weighting of the column. The gap map needs no reference track. It comes from the drift time per track (the charge-time quantile q_u90 of the fit, which follows the column duration with r = 0.98).

  • In the golden mounting the fit τ = a_h ∇h + b ∇g gives a_h = 0.91 to 1.21 and b = 0.35 to 0.54. The expected values are 1 and the weight of section 1.2 (0.3 to 0.4).
  • In the 6-25 mounting the Y tilt gradient is twice the golden one (0.0201 ± 0.0003 per 100 mm). The prediction from the golden mesh and the 6-25 drift-time map is 0.0209. Nothing was fitted to the 6-25 angles.
  • Used as a correction, the prediction recovers 33 to 65 % of the direct-map gain in the golden mounting and 82 to 91 % in the 6-25 mounting (table in section 3).
  • About 70 % of the reproducible fine tilt variance is not explained by the gap gradient. This remainder is still a gradient field and reproduces between the runs (r = 0.77 and 0.91). It amounts to about 0.2°.

2. The geometry of chamber A, and how well we measure it

2.1 The mesh is static

The mesh height from the position route agrees between the golden mounting and the 6-25 mounting (r = 0.94 ± 0.06 on X and 0.86 ± 0.07 on Y, slopes 1.17 ± 0.17 and 1.10 ± 0.18). With honest errors (resampling over cells, model order, weighting) the depth of the mesh at 150 mm is 0.87 ± 0.28 mm along x and 1.48 ± 0.34 mm along y.

2.2 The cathode changes with the mounting

  • Golden mounting (6-27 and 6-28). The gap is longest at the center along y (−1.2 to −1.5 mm per (100 mm)²), it rises along x, and it has a diagonal ridge of about 1.5 mm. The two runs agree with r = 0.98.
  • 6-25 mounting. The gap is shortest at the center along y (+0.6 mm per (100 mm)²) and has a twist. The diagonal ridge is absent.
  • 6-22 mounting. The map is nearly flat.
  • Inside one mounting the map is stable. It does not change over the 19 sub-runs of the mesh-voltage scan, between 6-27 and 6-28, in time blocks inside a run, or between 700 and 1100 V of drift voltage. The 500 V sub-run of 6-27 does not show the 6-25 shape (5 to 6σ in each curvature term), so the difference between the mountings is not the drift voltage.
  • Below 700 V the golden map flattens, and it returns at 1000 V. We could not decide whether this is the foil or the response of the time quantile, since the column end is close to the end of the readout window at low field.

The cathode is a 50 µm kapton foil. Its weight per area is about 0.7 to 1.5 Pa, so a pressure difference of 0.01 mbar across the foil is as large as its weight. A foil that takes a new shape each time the chamber is handled is therefore plausible. We have no direct measurement of what moves it.

3.3 Is the gain real without the telescope?

A position-dependent error of the telescope slope would also be a gradient field, so section 1.1 cannot exclude it. Three results do not depend on the telescope angle.

  • The drift-time map is a chamber-only quantity, and it predicts the tilt (section 1.4), including the change between mountings.
  • Chambers C and D were in the telescope together. The difference of their measured slopes does not contain the telescope error. The part of the two corrections that differs between the chambers is confirmed (slope −0.98 ± 0.10 on one axis and −0.47 ± 0.19 on the other, where −1 means a real distortion). However, 60 to 80 % of the two corrections is common to both chambers, and this test cannot see a common part.
  • The bench map of chamber A lowers the residual of the A–C cosmic lines at n_TOF, where there is no telescope (section 4).

In addition, an error of 7 mrad on a telescope with a lever arm of 1.3 m would need position errors of several millimeters, which the telescope residuals exclude. We therefore consider the gain real. The common part of the correction has no direct telescope-free test on the bench.

Recommendations

Analysis

  1. Do not ship the golden cell map as a chamber property. It is a property of the 6-27/28 mounting.
  2. For a bench re-finalization, ship one spline map per mounting (potential Φ and scale maps, K = 16), made by cross-validation inside the mounting. This is a table-level step. The fit does not use the drift velocity, so no waveform re-pass is needed. The prototype for chamber A is results/a2_final_map_det3.json with its evaluator in a2_final.py.
  3. Keep the bow block for the mesh term and add the gap term from the drift time where no reference exists: τ = ∇h + a ∇g. The amplitude a is about 0.4 on three mountings and about 0.2 on two others, so it should be calibrated wherever some reference exists. Chamber E's X view does not gain and can be left out.
  4. n_TOF, three candidates for the Phase 4 roadmap, none applied:
    • a drift-time correction for chamber D (first correction for D);
    • for chamber A, the bench fine map with no dish, compared with the in-situ map on the capsule pointing;
    • the gap map per chamber as an input to the v(t) angle scale, since the gap varies by 0.5 to 1.7 mm rms.

Paper I

  1. Rewrite app:gapmap around the results that do not depend on a quadratic model: the gradient-field test, the shape of the drift lines (the fan figure), the gap maps of the three mountings, the relation τ = ∇h + a ∇g, and the tracking table.
  2. Remove or correct: β = 1.0 ± 0.2; "the cathode is flat along y and follows the board along x"; the errors of tab:surfaces; "the board is not a pure paraboloid" as the explanation of r_f = 1.2 (the −x edge and the cathode contribute).
  3. §5.1 stays. The chamber-wide gap is unchanged. "The gap runs from 27 to 29 mm" should be stated for the golden mounting. The statement that the variation is the gap and not the gas becomes stronger (β = 0.69 from the fine structure).
  4. app:bow: the transfer statement should say that the mesh dish is static and the cathode is not, and that the block over-corrects chamber A at n_TOF.
  5. The Overleaf push of the last table fix is still pending (add/add conflict, see HANDOFF). The appendix on Overleaf carries the statements of item 2.

Outstanding questions and inconsistencies to run down

Each item says what was seen, where it is documented, and the check that would settle it with the existing data. None of them blocks the per-mounting maps, which are empirical and cross-validated.

A. The model

  1. The fine tilt is not fully explained. About 70 % of the reproducible fine tilt variance (about 0.2°) is not described by the gradient of the gap. The remainder is a gradient field and reproduces between runs (r = 0.77 and 0.91) (results/r1_RESULT.md, Part 4). *Check:* solve for both surfaces without the position route. The tilt potential gives (1 − a) h + a c and the drift time gives c − h, so h and c follow at fine scale. Then compare the fine h with the mesh-end slope of the drift lines on a 4×4 grid (e_03_main.py).
  2. The amplitude of the gap term is not one number. It is 0.35 to 0.54 in the golden mounting, 0.37 on B, 0.47 on D, but 0.22 on A's 6-25 mounting and 0.18 on C (results/r1_RESULT.md, results/r3_RESULT.md). The drift lines give a weight of 0.28 ± 0.05 (X) and 0.41 ± 0.02 (Y) (results/e_RESULT.md), the joint fit 0.38 ± 0.13, and a diffusion-weighted line fit predicts 0.43 to 0.47. The low values on 6-25 and C coincide with an uncertain β (the conversion of drift time to gap) and with strong saturation (87 % of the 6-25 events, 92 % of C's first row). *Check:* refit the amplitude with unsaturated events only, and with β measured in that mounting.
  3. β is not consistent between routes. The fine structure gives 0.69 (0.51 to 0.95). The smooth X-view velocity gives a slope of −0.74 (β would be 2.8), the smooth Y view −0.45. Per mounting, the velocity map gives β of 0.8 to 1.6 (results/r3_RESULT.md). On 6-25 the velocity map does not scale with the Magboltz β ratio of 2.2 (measured 0.35 to 1.09, results/c_RESULT.md). *Check:* find what the X-view ladder slope responds to besides the gap (the X strips are one board layer deeper; the X charge sharing is asymmetric on chamber A).
  4. The geometric gap and the drift time disagree in amplitude along y by a factor of about 1.4 (the 11-23 analysis; not resolved here). *Check:* the gap map from q_u90 on the geometric method's own cells, and the response of the half-height edge estimator to the cathode-end diffusion.
  5. The curvature of the cathode along x is undecided. Methods that cover all four columns range from −0.7 to +0.7 mm per (100 mm)² (results/f_RESULT.md, item 6). The drift-line slice at the −x edge bends the other way (results/e_RESULT.md). *Check:* drift-line slices on a finer grid in x, and the gap map near x < 80 mm.
  6. Tilt-plane gradients differ between estimators by up to 25 %. Event-level planes give 10.5 × 10⁻³ per 100 mm in both views. The 4×4 joint fit gives 9.2 (x) and 7.7 (y). The drift-line mean slope gives 11.6 and 9.0 against 10.7 and 9.6 from the table (results/a1_RESULT.md, results/e_RESULT.md). The shape is not a parabola, so a "gradient" depends on the region and the weighting. Quote maps, not single gradients.
  7. The map flattens below 700 V. In the golden mounting the gap map loses most of its ridge at 300 and 500 V and returns at 1000 V (results/r4_RESULT.md). The column end is close to the end of the readout window there. *Check:* the same map from q_u50, and from tracks whose column ends well inside the window.

B. The data

  1. The 6-22 run of chamber A has twice the drift time (mean q_u90 1201 ns against 615 ns at the same nominal 1000 V, results/r4_RESULT.md). Either the gas or the effective field was very different. Its nearly flat gap map should not be read as a third mechanical shape before this is understood. *Check:* the run configuration and the angle-scale velocity of the 6-22 det3 rows (no gate table exists for them).
  2. The 6-25 gas was drier. The velocity ratio to the golden runs is 0.59, where the field ratio predicts 0.44 (0.68 % water instead of 0.97 %, results/c_RESULT.md). β = 1.28 on 6-25 rests on this estimate.
  3. Chamber C changed during its night. The velocity fell from 25.0 to 16.8 µm/ns over three rows and the saturated fraction from 92 % to 0 % (results/r3_RESULT.md). Its maps are per row.
  4. The gas changed between Run 1 and Run 2. Chamber-wide, v rose by 1.8 % and T fell by 1.65 %, a ratio of −1.1 ± 0.26, which is the gas value (results/b_RESULT.md, addendum 7). The spatial structure is gap-like.
  5. The fine drift-time map correlates with the gain map five times more strongly than the event-level amplitude slope predicts (+0.13 ± 0.05 against +0.03, results/b_RESULT.md, addendum 6). The smooth surfaces move by 0.1 mm or less. *Check:* a per-cell amplitude correction before the fine gap map is used quantitatively.
  6. The cell errors of Run 2 are 1.2 to 1.9 times too small (χ²/dof of the gradient-only fit up to 3.5, results/a1_RESULT.md). The cross-run estimators do not depend on them.
  7. The charge centroid is not linear in tan θ. The residual is about −0.3 mm at +tan and +0.2 mm at −tan for |tan| 0.3 to 0.45 (results/e_RESULT.md). Balancing the sign of tan cancels it in the drift-line test.

C. Scope and generality

  1. The telescope. The part of the correction that is common to two chambers (60 to 80 %) has no direct telescope-free test on the bench (results/d_RESULT.md). Fine maps of different chambers in the same slot correlate for A (6-25) with C (+0.45) and for A with E (+0.65), and are near zero for the other pairs. A common plate shape and a slot component cannot be separated with these data.
  2. Only chamber A has been compared between mountings. Chamber E has two mountings with reference tracks (6-23 and 6-24). *Check:* compare E's two gap maps and mesh maps (r3_gap.py, r3_lib.py); it is cheap.
  3. The mean gap is measured in the golden mounting only (28.2 mm), and its 1.8 mm shortfall from the design is not explained. *Check:* the product of the ladder velocity and the drift time in the 6-25 mounting.
  4. What moves the foil is not identified. The weight of the foil is about 0.7 to 1.5 Pa. The electrostatic pull toward the window is 12 to 25 % of that at 1000 V and is excluded as the main cause (results/r4_RESULT.md). A pressure difference across the foil from the gas flow, or the handling itself, are candidates. We have no record of the flow.
  5. The curl test is strong on A and B only. C shows a cross-gradient difference of 2.9σ (8 % of the own-axis gradient) and D of 3.3σ on seven cells (results/d_RESULT.md).
  6. n_TOF. At n_TOF the drift time rises with |tan| (0.11 to 0.22 per unit), which the bench does not show. The gap-gradient fields of chamber A from muons and from late beam tracks disagree in y (results/r2_RESULT.md), while the maps themselves agree (re-audit). On C and D the drift-time map is too large to be a gap alone (re-audit). These belong to rerun/ntof/improve/reaudit/.

How the audit was run

  • One session planned the tests and wrote the briefs; each test ran as a separate agent on the staged data and wrote its own report. The brief every test received is results/COMMON_brief.md. The reports were read against each other, and four headline numbers were recomputed independently (the tilt gradients of both views in the three datasets, the cross gradients, and the sign change of the drift-time curvature between mountings).
  • Order: reproduction; then a1, a2, f, e, b, c, d, g in parallel; then r1 (prediction from the drift time), r2 (n_TOF), r3 (all chambers) and r4 (drift voltage against mounting), which were designed from the first results.
  • Three agents were interrupted by a login timeout after their computations had finished; they were resumed and wrote their reports from the files on disk.
  • Nothing in the reconstruction, the bundles or the pass products was changed.
