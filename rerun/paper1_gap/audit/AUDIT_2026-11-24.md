# Audit of the chamber A gap and surface analysis (2026-11-24)

This note audits `rerun/paper1_gap/` (Paper I §5.1 and appendix `app:gapmap`). The audit reran the committed chain,
then ran twelve independent tests on the existing data. Each test has its own report in `results/<tag>_RESULT.md` and
its scripts in `<tag>_*.py`. This file is the source of `report.html` (`make_audit_report.py`).

## Verdict

- **The physical picture is right.** The drift lines of chamber A are tilted and bent by the shape of two surfaces,
  the mesh and the cathode. Four new tests that could have failed confirm this. None of them was used by the audited
  analysis.
- **Several statements of the appendix do not hold as written.** The quoted errors on the surface curvatures are 2 to 3
  times too small. "The cathode is flat along y and follows the board along x" is not a stable result. The fitted
  β = 1.0 is an artifact of averaging the two views. A quadratic surface is the wrong description of the cathode.
- **The cathode is a foil that changes shape every time the chamber is mounted.** The mesh dish is the same in every
  mounting. The gap map is different in each of the three mountings we have (6-22, 6-25, 6-27/28), and it is stable in
  time, with the mesh voltage and with the drift voltage (700 to 1100 V) inside one mounting. In the golden mounting
  the gap has a diagonal ridge about 1.5 mm high.
- **The tracking gain is real and can be made larger.** An unbinned spline map gives an angle resolution (s68) of
  0.69° on X and 0.60° on Y, against 0.92° and 0.81° for pass 1. This is 26 % better than pass 1, and it is at the
  statistical limit of the map. However, the map belongs to one mounting. It does not transfer to another mounting of
  the same chamber.
- **Most of the correction can be made without reference tracks.** The drift-time map of a mounting gives its gap map,
  and the tilt follows as ∇h + a ∇g with a ≈ 0.4. In the 6-25 mounting, where the golden map gives nothing, this
  recovers 82 to 91 % of the gain of that mounting's own reference map.
- **At n_TOF** the bench bow block over-corrects chamber A, the bench fine map of chamber A still helps, and the
  drift-time map gives chamber D its first angle correction (12 to 13 % in one view).

The recommended changes to the analysis and to the paper are in the section "Recommendations". Nothing was shipped
and no paper text was changed.

## 1. Four new tests of the two-surface picture

We call h(x, y) the height of the mesh and c(x, y) the height of the cathode, with z up. The gap is g = c − h. In the
long-wavelength limit a drift line at fractional height s (0 at the mesh, 1 at the cathode) has the slope
∇h + s (∇c − ∇h). The audited analysis used only the mean of this slope over the column.

### 1.1 The tilt field is a gradient field

An electrostatic distortion of the drift field displaces the drift lines by the gradient of a scalar. The tilt
measured by the X strips and the tilt measured by the Y strips must therefore obey ∂τ_x/∂y = ∂τ_y/∂x. An effect of
one view (electronics, charge sharing, the resistive layer) has no reason to obey this relation. The audited fit
imposed the relation and never tested it.

- At the level of the fitted planes, the two cross gradients differ by (0.6 ± 0.4) and (0.1 ± 0.2) × 10⁻³ per 100 mm
  in run 1 and run 2. The own-axis gradient is 10.5 × 10⁻³ per 100 mm in both views.
- At fine scale we compare ∂τ_x/∂y of one run with ∂τ_y/∂x of the other run, so that the noise is independent. The
  correlation is 0.92 to 0.95, which equals the noise ceiling. The curl-free fraction is 0.96 ± 0.02 (42 mm cells) and
  1.00 ± 0.02 (34 mm cells).
- The solenoidal part is below 1.5 % of the variance (2σ), which is below 0.05° rms.
- The fine structure itself reproduces between the runs with r = 0.91 to 0.99. Its rms is 0.23 to 0.46° on X and 0.34
  to 0.44° on Y. It saturates at cells of 34 to 42 mm.

### 1.2 The drift lines have the predicted shape

We measured the shape of the drift lines directly from the waveforms. For each time sample we take the charge centroid
of the track image and compare it with the reference track. The regression on tan θ_ref gives, for each time bin, the
height z of the charge and its sideways displacement δ. This calibrates time to depth with the telescope geometry
itself, so the shaping of the signal does not enter.

Charge liberated at height z lands displaced by δ(z) = h′ z + (c′ − h′) z² / (2 g). The slope at the mesh end is
therefore the mesh slope, and the curvature is the gradient of the gap.

| quantity (10⁻⁴ per 100 mm) | measured | audited fit | flat cathode | cathode parallel to mesh |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mesh-end slope gradient, X view along x | +86 ± 11 | +78 | | |
| mesh-end slope gradient, Y view along y | +151 ± 14 | +131 | | |
| curvature gradient, Y view along y | −133 ± 15 | −109 | −131 | 0 |
| curvature gradient, X view along x | +73 ± 51 | +28 | −78 | 0 |

- The mesh-end slope follows the mesh dish that the position route measures (0.7σ and 1.2σ).
- Along y the drift lines bend with the predicted sign and size. A cathode parallel to the mesh is excluded at 8.8σ.
- Along x four of the five slices show the same constant curvature, which is the tilt of the gap along x. The slice
  nearest the −x edge bends the other way. For this reason the curvature of the cathode along x is undecided.
- The straight-line fit of the reconstruction weights the mesh end of the column more than the cathode end. The weight
  of the cathode slope is a = 0.3 to 0.4, not 0.5.

Bottom: the same with the linear term removed, which leaves the curvature. Left: X view, slices along x. Right: Y view, slices along y. The lines fan out because the mesh is dished, and in the Y view they bend because the gap changes along y.](figures/e_fig_delta_profiles.png)

### 1.3 The fine gap structure is on the cathode

A relief δc on the cathode changes the gap by +δc and tilts the drift lines by +∇(δg)/2. A relief δh on the mesh
changes the gap by −δh and tilts the lines by −∇(δg)/2. The regression of the fine tilt on the gradient of the fine
gap map (cross-run) gives k = +0.67 ± 0.12 on X and +0.46 ± 0.16 on Y. The mesh alone is excluded at 6 to 9σ. The
smooth dish along y behaves like the mesh (k = −0.75).

The same test gives β for the fine structure: d ln v / d ln T = −0.41 ± 0.08, so β = 0.69 (0.51 to 0.95). Magboltz
gives 0.68. The fitted β = 1.0 ± 0.2 of the appendix came from averaging the two views. The smooth velocity map of
the X view has a slope of −0.74 ± 0.02, which is neither a gap change (−0.41) nor a gas change (−1). The Y view gives
−0.45 ± 0.03. We have not explained the X value.

### 1.4 The tilt is predicted from the drift time

If the picture is right, the tilt map follows from the mesh shape and the gap map: τ = ∇h + a ∇g, with a = 1/2 for an
equal weighting of the column. The gap map needs no reference track. It comes from the drift time per track
(the charge-time quantile `q_u90` of the fit, which follows the column duration with r = 0.98).

- In the golden mounting the fit τ = a_h ∇h + b ∇g gives a_h = 0.91 to 1.21 and b = 0.35 to 0.54. The expected values
  are 1 and the weight of section 1.2 (0.3 to 0.4).
- In the 6-25 mounting the Y tilt gradient is twice the golden one (0.0201 ± 0.0003 per 100 mm). The prediction from
  the golden mesh and the 6-25 drift-time map is 0.0209. Nothing was fitted to the 6-25 angles.
- Used as a correction, the prediction recovers 33 to 65 % of the direct-map gain in the golden mounting and 82 to
  91 % in the 6-25 mounting (table in section 3).
- About 70 % of the reproducible fine tilt variance is not explained by the gap gradient. This remainder is still a
  gradient field and reproduces between the runs (r = 0.77 and 0.91). It amounts to about 0.2°.

## 2. The geometry of chamber A, and how well we measure it

### 2.1 The mesh is static

The mesh height from the position route agrees between the golden mounting and the 6-25 mounting (r = 0.94 ± 0.06 on
X and 0.86 ± 0.07 on Y, slopes 1.17 ± 0.17 and 1.10 ± 0.18). With honest errors (resampling over cells, model order,
weighting) the depth of the mesh at 150 mm is 0.87 ± 0.28 mm along x and 1.48 ± 0.34 mm along y.

### 2.2 The cathode changes with the mounting

![Relative drift-time map of chamber A (ln q_u90, percent; 1 % is about 0.17 mm of gap at β = 0.68). From left: golden run 1, golden run 2, the 6-25 mounting, the 6-22 mounting, and the 700 to 1100 V points of the 6-27 drift scan.](figures/r4_fig2b_maps_spline.png)

- **Golden mounting (6-27 and 6-28).** The gap is longest at the center along y (−1.2 to −1.5 mm per (100 mm)²), it
  rises along x, and it has a diagonal ridge of about 1.5 mm. The two runs agree with r = 0.98.
- **6-25 mounting.** The gap is shortest at the center along y (+0.6 mm per (100 mm)²) and has a twist. The diagonal
  ridge is absent.
- **6-22 mounting.** The map is nearly flat. *(Follow-up q: an artefact. 70 % of its tracks are censored by the depth
  grid of the fit, so 6-22 gives no gap map.)*
- **Inside one mounting the map is stable.** It does not change over the 19 sub-runs of the mesh-voltage scan, between
  6-27 and 6-28, in time blocks inside a run, or between 700 and 1100 V of drift voltage. The electrostatic pressure
  step from 700 to 1100 V equals the one from 500 to 1000 V, and it changes nothing, so the difference between the
  mountings is not a linear response to the drift voltage. *(Follow-up q, fourth round: the 500 V sub-run of 6-27 cannot
  be compared with 6-25, because its columns are cut by the end of the record; see the next point.)*
- **Below 700 V** the golden map flattens, and it returns at 1000 V. *(Follow-up q, fourth round: this is the end of
  the recorded waveform. At 500 V the column is longer than the record after the track start, and at 300 V it is twice
  as long. A fixed gap shape cut at the record end reproduces the flat map. The foil does not move.)*

The cathode is a 50 µm kapton foil. Its weight per area is about 0.7 to 1.5 Pa, so a pressure difference of 0.01 mbar
across the foil is as large as its weight. A foil that takes a new shape each time the chamber is handled is therefore
plausible. We have no direct measurement of what moves it.

- The geometric gap with the X strips covers only two columns of cells in x. It cannot measure a curvature along x.
  The disagreement between the "no timing" subset and the full fit is therefore a coverage effect.

  cathode is not an adequate model, which the maps of section 2.2 show directly.
- The sign convention is confirmed. The timing-free tilt and w0/v agree with slope +1 within errors, and the ladder
  slope is positive in every cell.

### 3.3 Is the gain real without the telescope?

A position-dependent error of the telescope slope would also be a gradient field, so section 1.1 cannot exclude it.
Three results do not depend on the telescope angle.

- The drift-time map is a chamber-only quantity, and it predicts the tilt (section 1.4), including the change between
  mountings.
- Chambers C and D were in the telescope together. The difference of their measured slopes does not contain the
  telescope error. The part of the two corrections that differs between the chambers is confirmed (slope −0.98 ± 0.10
  on one axis and −0.47 ± 0.19 on the other, where −1 means a real distortion). However, 60 to 80 % of the two
  corrections is common to both chambers, and this test cannot see a common part.
- The bench map of chamber A lowers the residual of the A–C cosmic lines at n_TOF, where there is no telescope
  (section 4).

In addition, an error of 7 mrad on a telescope with a lever arm of 1.3 m would need position errors of several
millimeters, which the telescope residuals exclude. We therefore consider the gain real. The common part of the
correction has no direct telescope-free test on the bench.

## 4. n_TOF

- **The quadratic bow block.** At n_TOF the block over-corrects chamber A. About three quarters of its Y gradient is
  undone by the in-situ map, and the X gradient changes sign. For chamber C the block is right in y and slightly too
  small in x. This is what we expect if the dish of chamber A is mostly the sag of a horizontal board.
- **The bench fine map of chamber A** lowers the held-out residual of the A–C cosmic lines by 10.8 % on x and 7.3 %
  on y as it is (in-situ map: 14.6 % and 12.0 %). The agreement is driven by the tilts near the chamber edges, which
  are 2 to 3 times larger at n_TOF. The bench map of chamber C gives nothing.
- **The drift-time maps at n_TOF** are stable between run parities (r = 0.92 to 0.998), between the early and the
  late campaign, and between the X and Y quantiles. Their noise is 0.04 to 0.10 mm. The rms of the gap is 0.5 to
  0.7 mm on A, 0.8 to 0.9 mm on B, 1.3 to 1.4 mm on C and 1.6 to 1.7 mm on D.

- For chamber D the fitted amplitude is b = 0.44 to 0.50, as expected, and the B–D line residual falls from

- β at n_TOF is not known (no Magboltz grid for the n_TOF gas in the repo), so the amplitudes are empirical.

## Recommendations

### Analysis

1. **Do not ship the golden cell map as a chamber property.** It is a property of the 6-27/28 mounting.
2. **For a bench re-finalization, ship one spline map per mounting** (potential Φ and scale maps, K = 16), made by
   cross-validation inside the mounting. This is a table-level step. The fit does not use the drift velocity, so no
   waveform re-pass is needed. The prototype for chamber A is `results/a2_final_map_det3.json` with its evaluator in
   `a2_final.py`.
3. **Keep the bow block for the mesh term** and add the gap term from the drift time where no reference exists:
   τ = ∇h + a ∇g. The amplitude a is about 0.4 on three mountings and about 0.2 on two others, so it should be
   calibrated wherever some reference exists. Chamber E's X view does not gain and can be left out.
4. **n_TOF, three candidates for the Phase 4 roadmap**, none applied:
   - a drift-time correction for chamber D (first correction for D);
   - for chamber A, the bench fine map with no dish, compared with the in-situ map on the capsule pointing;
   - the gap map per chamber as an input to the v(t) angle scale, since the gap varies by 0.5 to 1.7 mm rms.

### Paper I

1. **Rewrite `app:gapmap` around the results that do not depend on a quadratic model**: the gradient-field test, the
   shape of the drift lines (the fan figure), the gap maps of the three mountings, the relation τ = ∇h + a ∇g, and the
   tracking table.
2. **Remove or correct**: β = 1.0 ± 0.2; "the cathode is flat along y and follows the board along x"; the errors of
   `tab:surfaces`; "the board is not a pure paraboloid" as the explanation of r_f = 1.2 (the −x edge and the cathode
   contribute).
3. **§5.1 stays.** The chamber-wide gap is unchanged. "The gap runs from 27 to 29 mm" should be stated for the golden
   mounting. The statement that the variation is the gap and not the gas becomes stronger (β = 0.69 from the fine
   structure).
4. **`app:bow`**: the transfer statement should say that the mesh dish is static and the cathode is not, and that the
   block over-corrects chamber A at n_TOF.
5. The Overleaf push of the last table fix is still pending (add/add conflict, see HANDOFF). The appendix on Overleaf
   carries the statements of item 2.

   - The n_TOF tilt field is curl-free too. Its beam and beam-off drift-time maps of A agree (r = 0.96), so the
     disagreement found here in y is a statement about the gradients, where the noise is amplified.

## Outstanding questions and inconsistencies to run down

Each item says what was seen, where it is documented, and the check that would settle it with the existing data. None

E (0.17, 0.09, no saturation): **answered (q, fifth round)**: about 40 % of its drift-time gradient along y is a gas (velocity) gradient (d ln v / d ln T = −0.68 ± 0.09 against −0.46 for a gap), and its small track count lowers b by about a quarter.    therefore an artefact of the censoring, not a third cathode shape.

## How the audit was run

- One session planned the tests and wrote the briefs; each test ran as a separate agent on the staged data and
  wrote its own report. The brief every test received is `results/COMMON_brief.md`. The reports were read against
  each other, and four headline numbers were recomputed independently (the tilt gradients of both views in the three
  datasets, the cross gradients, and the sign change of the drift-time curvature between mountings).
- Order: reproduction; then a1, a2, f, e, b, c, d, g in parallel; then r1 (prediction from the drift time), r2
  (n_TOF), r3 (all chambers) and r4 (drift voltage against mounting), which were designed from the first results.
- Three agents were interrupted by a login timeout after their computations had finished; they were resumed and
  wrote their reports from the files on disk.
- Nothing in the reconstruction, the bundles or the pass products was changed.
