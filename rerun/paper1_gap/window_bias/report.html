Drift gap: does the end of the record bias the drift time?

Verdict. A record that ends after the drift column does not bias the fitted drift time T. The fit recovers T within 5 ns in toys, and cutting the real stacks of chambers A and B (and D's long run) at 120 ns after the column moves T by 1-7 ns. Chamber E's 6-24 long run, the quoted row with the shortest record (116 ns after the column), is therefore fine: its gap moves by less than 0.2 mm, and a fit with attachment free finds none (tau 22 us, T +2 ns). Only a record that ends before the column end biases T; that is the 177 V/cm point of chamber A, already a lower limit.

What does bias T is attachment, and a short record makes it worse. The uniform-column fit returns T too short when the column loses charge with drift time. Chamber C's 26 June rows prefer an attachment time of about 2 us (1.5-2.4 sigma per row): with the loss fitted, C's gap is 30.1 instead of 29.4 mm (+0.7 mm). D's 26 June HV-scan rows lean the same way (+0.25 mm). This is stated as a limit in Paper I; the choice is Dylan's.

Found on the way: chamber D's long run had no fit. Its interior stack was empty (masked X channels below 270 left the +-2 strip offset under the 60 % rule), and x_width.fit_x returned its starting point (T = 820 ns, rms NaN) as a result. D's water (0.48 %) and its long-run gap came from that number. Refitted with the S4 det7 recipe (X peak on ch 256-511, all events): T = 798.6 +- 17.2 ns (the S4 re-check had 797 +- 17). D's water is now 0.44 %, its gap 29.4 mm (chi2 over its rows 12.6 -> 4.4 / 6), and D's drying 0.67 -> 0.33 %. fit_x now raises on an empty stack.

Clock

The fit's dt is on predict_x's clock (index 0 = -300 ns) with a template whose grid starts at -360 ns, so the last charge of the column reaches the strips at stack time dt + 60 + T. The response then lasts ~500 ns (99 % of its area); the falling edge of the stack is the column end convolved with it. Paper I's Fig. two_velocities (a) draws its triangles at dt and dt + T, 60 ns early (cosmetic; T is unaffected).

Record end against column end, every row

Record end = the last time where more than 60 % of the stacked events still have a sample (rc_diffusion.stack's rule). Chamber E's records are short because its leading edges sit late in the readout window for many events (10 % of events end 720-920 ns after their leading edge).

Toy: the fit's own model, cut and refitted

Truth = x_width.predict_x at each template row's fitted parameters (sigma_0, Dd, dt, T), with an exp(-u/tau) loss of charge in the attachment panels; white noise of the row's fit rms; six noisy replicas per point. Without attachment, T is unbiased for every margin >= 0 and unusable below. With attachment, the uniform fit is short already with the full record (-7 to -9 ns at 4 us, -26 to -48 ns at 1.5 us) and shorter still when the record is cut.

What this does not rule out

  • The attachment model is one shape (exponential loss along the column). Anything else that makes the stack fall with drift time (a gain or a coupling that changes with depth) looks the same to the fit; the toys show the size of the effect, not its cause.
  • Chamber C's attachment is at 1.5-2.4 sigma per row; with more rows the trend is clearer (tau grows through the 26 June HV scan), but there is no independent attachment measurement for C.
  • The toys use white noise; real residuals are correlated. The cut test on real stacks covers that for the chambers that have margin to spare, not for E itself.
