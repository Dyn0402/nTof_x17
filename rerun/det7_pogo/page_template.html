det7 X: shorted pads on the M1 pogo field

June cosmic bench, run g_det7_long (06-26), FEU 6. Source: rerun/det7_pogo/ (commit b399f42) and C20 channel_census. Generated 2026-10-25.

* Asterisk on this run (2026-10-25). det7's M1 cards were not seated well on the 06-26 night (g_det7_long, g_det7_longer), so results near the bridged pads are not representative. Quote det7 X from card 2 only (connectors 5–8, ch 256–511).
Correction: a later, general check (rerun/pogo_check/) confirms the vertical chains with tracks. The links in the dense corner (cols 7–9, rows 0–14, ch 3–58) are not confirmed. Those channels fired in 5 of ~35 k clean single-track events, against ~1600 for their neighbours. They move together only in saturated events of ~430 hits, so read them as not seeing tracks (perhaps lifted pins) rather than as bridged.
What you are looking at. Each M1 card connects 256 DREAM channels to the readout board through a 10 × 30 field of pads on a 1 mm grid, pressed together by a JZ pogo-pin carrier. On det7's first X card (connectors 1–4), 85 channels fall into 19 groups that read identical amplitudes. Drawn on the pad field, every group is a chain of touching neighbour pads, mostly along the board edge (= ±5 channels). The second card is clean.
  • The strip map is right (C20, checked against M3). This is a contact fault, not a wiring permutation.
  • The pads are joined at strip level: a shorted channel fires when M3 crosses its partner strip 5 strips away.
  • A rigid shift or rotation of the pin array does not reproduce it: the best fit gets 78 of the 108 links right but invents 137 (orange in the overlay). The bridges are pin by pin.

Rows (along the board edge, the direction the card slides when tightened) run left to right here, so the README's “vertical” chains appear as horizontal links. Columns run radially. Both pitches are 1 mm. Pads are 0.6 × 0.8 mm rounded rectangles at 45°; the gap to a vertical or horizontal neighbour is 0.23 mm, to a diagonal one 0.61 mm. A pad's vertical neighbour is ±5 DREAM channels, its horizontal neighbour ±1. Card 2 is drawn with the same layout (channel = card-1 channel + 256); its strongest neighbour correlation is .

2 · Which strips each short joins

The 256 strips of card 1 in their nominal order (strip named by the FEU channel that should read it, ~0.78 mm pitch, so 5 strips ≈ 3.9 mm). Each arc joins two strips that are read as one channel. A net of n channels reads (1/n) × the sum of its strips: the charge of a track crossing any of them appears on all of them, which is what wrecks the angle fit on connectors 1–4 while positions survive. What this does not tell us

  • Which non-rigid fault it is. Leaning pins, conductive debris across the 0.23 mm gaps, or a card bowed at one corner all join pads pin by pin. The data cannot separate them. The corner block (cols 7–9, rows 0–14) does not respond to tracks at all (see the correction above).
  • The state after 06-30. The logbook has the X1 JZ connector reseated and glued on 06-30 ("recovered most strips, ~10 still expected out"). This run is from 06-26 and says nothing about the chamber afterwards.
  • The noisy channels. They are ~31 % of both shorted and unshorted channels, with an rms level set per connector. That looks like M1/cable pickup, not pad contact, and is not explained here.
  • The readout-side strip order. The fan-out on the readout board was not traced; each pad's strip is taken from the M3-verified linear map.
