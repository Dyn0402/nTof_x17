Chamber A, Runs 1+2: every measurement against time

Verdict. Over the ten hours of Runs 1+2 (6-27 22:49 → 6-28 08:47) the performance numbers are constant: efficiency, position core, angle s68 and time resolution show no trend beyond 1.7 σ, and every series is consistent with a constant except the efficiency (χ²/dof 29/19, no slope). The drift velocity rises (anchored reference ladder: flat at 35.80 µm/ns through Run 1, 36.64 at the end of Run 2, +2.4 %; water 0.99 → 0.94 %; the reference-free column duration agrees inside Run 2 but not on the step between the runs), and the gain is 4 % higher in Run 2 (a step between the runs, flat inside each).

Drift velocity: what the two runs' v really differ by

The paper (§3.3) says Run 1 and Run 2 have v = 35.8 and 37.2 µm/ns. Those are two different estimators: 35.8 is Run 1's timing velocity (head-on stack), 37.19 is Run 2's reference-ladder velocity (vrefit --fold-kw: 35.8 × Run 2's mean kw, from a reconstruction of up to 8 000 matched events). On one estimator the runs differ by much less: the ladder gives 36.77 and 37.42 µm/ns over the whole rows (+1.8 %), i.e. 35.80 → 36.43 anchored to the timing scale (k = 0.9735, the same timing/ladder ratio as chambers C and D: 0.970 / 0.984); the column duration gives +0.5 %. Recommendation for the text: quote the change on one estimator (≈ +1.8 % ladder, +0.5 % column), not 35.8 against 37.2.

Shape: Run 1 is flat in both estimators (ladder +0.12, column +0.30 µm/ns over 2.3 h); inside Run 2 both rise (ladder +0.41 ± 0.10, column +0.45 ± 0.16 µm/ns over 7.5 h). The ladder also steps up by ≈ 0.3 µm/ns (0.9 %) across the 23 min between the runs, where the column does not step. The gain steps up by 3.5 % at the same place. Whether that step is the gas (a change at the restart) or a difference between the two rows' reconstructions (Run 2 is reconstructed with Run 1's bundle, v folded to 37.19, and its own w0/kw; the alignments are identical) cannot be decided from these products. The ladder is the estimator gas_drying uses for C and D; the column is gain- and attachment-sensitive (rerun/vt_all CROSSCHECK).

Bins and their statistics

  • v (ladder and column), efficiency, position, angle: 30 min (5 bins in Run 1, 15 in Run 2; ~700 tracks per view in the ladder window, ~1 300 interior rays, ~1 250 position fits, ~1 200 angles per view). The ladder needs ~300 tracks per view in its window for 1 % on kw (vt_within_rows); 30 min gives 0.35 %, against a 1–2 % effect.
  • time resolution: 20 min (~450 M3-matched clean triggers, ±1.1 ns on 22 ns).
  • charge and saturation: 10 min (~420 tracks; ±2.7 % on the median, ±2.4 pt on the fraction).

Finer bins are possible (the per-trigger tables are in results/bins.csv inputs), but at 15 min the efficiency error (±0.8 pt) and the ladder error (±0.5 %) approach the size of what they would look for.

Draft paragraph for §3.3 / §6 (not inserted in the .tex)

Runs 1 and 2 ran back to back for ten hours at the same HV and in the same slot. Binned in 20--30 minute slices of wall-clock time (20 bins, ${\sim}1\,300$ reference tracks each), the detection efficiency, the position and angle resolutions and the time resolution are constant within their statistical errors: the interior single-track efficiency stays at 95.7\,\% ($\pm0.6$\,pt per bin; slope -0.02$\pm$0.05\,pt/h), the position core at 0.55/0.53\,mm (X/Y), the angle $s_{68}$ at 0.99/0.90\textdegree{} and the uncorrelated time $(t_X-t_Y)/\sqrt2$ at 22.3\,ns, none with a trend beyond 1.7\,$\sigma$. Two things do move. The drift velocity rises as the gas dries: by the reference ladder it is flat through Run~1 at 35.80\,\textmu m/ns and reaches 36.64\,\textmu m/ns at the end of Run~2 (+2.4\,\%; through Magboltz at 355\,V/cm, 0.99 to 0.94\,\% water). Inside Run~2 the ladder and the reference-free column duration agree (+1.1 and +1.3\,\% over 7.5\,h); between the runs only the ladder steps (+0.9\,\%). The gain is 4\,\% higher in Run~2 (median charge 22.2 against 23.1\,k ADC, saturated tracks 36 against 39\,\%), a step between the runs rather than a drift within them. Neither reaches the performance. Each run is reconstructed with its own velocity, and the 1\,\% left inside Run~2 moves a 20\textdegree{} track by ${\sim}0.2$\textdegree{}, a fifth of the resolution; the gain step is what ${\sim}1$\,V on the resist gives (one doubling per 17\,V, \cref{fig:hvmetrics}), and the HV scan shows nothing but the time resolution moving over 60\,V.

What this does not rule out / what could not be computed

  • The cause of the gain step (+3.5 % between the runs): no pressure, temperature or HV-current log of the bench is in the products; drying (less water) raises the gain, but a step at the restart is not a drying shape.
  • The 0.9 % ladder step at the row boundary (above): gas or row calibration, not separable.
  • The timing-stack v (head-on stack fit, the paper's "timing v") is not available per time bin: the head-on stack of a row has ~50–100 tracks (rerun/paper1_gas), too few to split. The column duration of the timing tables stands in for it (same physics, all angles, edge25 start to the 30 % trailing crossing).
  • The event time includes the trigger; the uncorrelated part does not. Neither drifts; the median event time (latency) is constant to 2.4 ns rms.
  • Efficiency scatter: χ²/dof 29/19 for a constant (p ≈ 0.07), no slope; the bins are not independent of the M3 rate per bin, which is constant.
  • Calibrations (kernel, template, bow, alignment) are per row and cannot be re-derived per bin; the time series measures the stability of the result under the row's fixed calibration.
  • Rays with no chamber event take their time from neighbouring trigger numbers (≤ seconds of error).
