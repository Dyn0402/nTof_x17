# Why does the Y view read a steeper w0 gradient than the X view? (2026-11-03)

Question: on every chamber the own-axis w0 gradient is 1.2-2.1x steeper on the Y strips than on the X strips
(final tables, `results_final/xcheck_*.json`, `cu` of X / `cw` of Y), while 08b_bow.tex calls this "a feature of the fit's
Y view rather than of the board". Tests below use only existing tables (bow_final parquets, `results_final/`);
script `ytilt_tests.py` (2 s runtime) plus the one-liners reproduced in the numbers. Nothing needed condor.

## Result in one paragraph
The "Y-view fit artefact" statement is **not supported** and should be dropped. (1) The position route, which does not use
the fitted w0 or any Y-view response, also sees the dish deeper along y than along x (curvature ratio y/x 1.0-1.6, geometric
mean 1.4 over the five chambers and 19 rows), the same sign and about the size of the w0 ratio (1.2-2.1, geometric mean
1.65). (2) The residual Y excess after dividing by that curvature ratio is 1.0-1.5 (mean 1.2), and is not stable: it depends on
which part of the chamber is fitted. (3) Nothing tested in the fit (kernel, kw, tan window) produces it. Best reading: a
real, moderate y>x asymmetry of the tilt field (the two layers of strips are not symmetric, the PCB is not x-y symmetric as a
laminate), plus an X-view gradient that is suppressed by the outer 75 mm. The Y/X ratio is **not a clean number**.

## Consequence for 08b_bow.tex
- Delete "a feature of the fit's Y view rather than of the board, so the r_f of the X views are the safer ones".
- The 0.15-0.7 mm deeper-along-y statement, "we do not claim as an anisotropy", is consistent with this: the w0 route is an
  independent, M3-free confirmation at the 1.4-1.65 level. It would be fair to say the dish is deeper along y by a factor
  ~1.4 (about 0.3-0.7 mm at 150 mm), with chamber-to-chamber scatter 1.0-1.6, and that the central shape differs from the
  outer one.
- Suggested replacement for the sentence: "The Y strips read a steeper ramp than the X strips by a factor 1.2--2.1. About
  three quarters of the excess (in the logarithm) follows from the dish being deeper along $y$ than along $x$ by a mean
  factor 1.4, as the position route also finds from both views; the rest depends on the fitted window (restricting the fit to
  the central 200 mm lowers the ratio to 0.8--0.9 on A and B, because it is the X gradient that falls short in the outer
  100 mm). We tested a kw or ladder-scale difference (below 4\,\%), the tan(theta) distribution and an extra tan term (below
  5\,\%), and the cluster-width gradient, and none of them produces it; we therefore do not use the X--Y ratio as a measurement
  of anything and quote $r_f$ for the two views together."

## Should the 14-25 % Y-view gain still be quoted?
Yes, but as an empirical result, not as the gain of "the bow". `gate_bow.json`, held-out half, s68 of angle vs the constant
mapping, `bow2d` (position-dependent w0 surface fitted per view, rigid tilt): (order det2, det3, det4, det6, det7) X 8, 9, 6, 29, 2 %; Y 20, 14, 25, 25, 18 %. The correction removes whatever position-dependent w0 each view
has, whether it is board geometry or not; the Y gains are larger because its ramp is larger, which is exactly what the
discussion above leaves unexplained at the 1.2x level. The shape predicted from positions alone (`phys`) gets 13-22 % on Y
(median 20) versus 1-24 % on X (phys: 12, 6, 1, 24, 3), so the geometric reading carries most of it. Wording: "improves the angular resolution by
14-25 % on the Y views and 2-29 % on the X views, by a surface of the same form that the positions predict; the Y gain is
larger because its tilt ramp is steeper, which the position data explain only in part." Do not claim that it demonstrates
the bow on Y. The 2-9 % X gains on A, B, D, E mean the correction is not needed there.

## What this does not rule out
A kernel/readout difference that varies across the board and leaves n_strips unchanged; a real x-y asymmetry from the
lamination (strip layers at different depths) or the support (consistent with the 1.4 ratio of the position route, but not
independently tested); a telescope y contribution to the position-route ratio; the shape (parabola vs flat middle plus hook)
which makes the gradient window-dependent. No new data are proposed.
