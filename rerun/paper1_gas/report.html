Bench gas against time and field (Paper I §6, pass 1)

Verdict. The bench drift velocity is the water.
  • Field: one water describes det3's whole 6-27 drift scan, 0.97 % [0.94, 0.99], χ² 2.5/3.
  • Time: the v(t) drifts read as water drying out overnight. On 6-26 det6 goes 1.47 → 0.77 % and det7 0.68 → 0.38 %. The two follow one time shape (r = 0.98), and det6 carries 2.2× det7's excess.
  • Chambers differ: each chamber has its own water (0.4–1.0 % on the golden rows), so it is per-chamber ingress, not one gas.
  • det6's ladder-vs-column disagreement is attachment. For its first ~4 h on 6-26 the drift column is truncated (visible fraction 0.44 → 0.81) by an electronegative contaminant of order 379–601 ppm O₂-equivalent (×2 model uncertainty). The M3 ladder does not see it; the timing stack and the column duration do.

Other nights

The same pattern on every night: water falls overnight and steps back up between runs or reconfigurations. det4 goes 0.7 → 0.2 % over 6-23, then is back at 0.42 % on 6-24.

What this does not rule out

  • No gas record. No flow, fill or line record exists for the bench (no new data), so the cause of the water (outgassing after opening, a permeable line) is not identified. Neither is the order of the chambers on the line.
  • The attachment number is model-dependent. τ ≈ T / ln 2 assumes the box fit ends at the half-height point of an exponentially decaying column. The stack model has no attachment term. The species is not identified: "O₂-equivalent" means the rate, not the gas.
  • The gap is the leading systematic on each water (the band column). The bench drift scan constrains it only to 28–30 mm.
  • g_det4's −9 % in-row v drift (ladder and column, rerun/vt_all/CROSSCHECK) is not resolved by the stack thirds (33.1 / 32.2 / 33.2 ± 1.2–2.9 µm/ns).
  • det2 sits near the velocity maximum at 321 V/cm, where v hardly depends on water. Its water is the weakest of the five, and one of its thirds has two Magboltz branches.
