What we gave up by running at 540 V

The flash recovery time says how high the amplification voltage may go. This is the other half: what the voltage buys, from the June cosmic bench at Ar/iso 95/5, carried onto the n_TOF voltage axis with the Garfield equivalence map, and checked against n_TOF's own resist scan in the production gas.

Which noise era you ask about decides whether 540 V was on the plateau. The 27 June bench scan puts the chamber's efficiency plateau at 81 % over 455–500 V (95/5). Carried onto the n_TOF axis by the July ledger, 540 V is worth 81 % — on the plateau, indistinguishable from 560 V's 81 %. Carried by the production ledger, after the 23 July noise step, the same setpoint is worth 69 % against 81 % at 560 V — on the shoulder of the turn-on, not the plateau. That difference, ~12 points, is the honest cost of the 540 V decision, and it is a cost the noise step imposed rather than the decision itself. The product of visible X17 rate and reconstructed-track yield still peaks at 550 V, with 540 V within 95 % of it.

The usable window is about 20 V wide and it has a ceiling on both sides. Below ~535 V the chamber falls off its own detection turn-on in the production noise, and stops reconstructing tracks well before that; above ~555 V the recovery passes the thermal peak and the rate goes to nothing; and the bench's own discharge onset maps to 565 V, a few volts above where the dead time already ended the argument.

The 23 July noise step moved the operating point, not just the noise. In the run_55 configuration 540 V is worth bench 459 V; in the production configuration it is worth bench 437 V — 22 V lower, for the same setpoint, because the threshold rides on a doubled noise floor.

1. The gas costs 73 volts

The bench ran Ar/iso 95/5 and n_TOF ran 90/10, so the bench's efficiency curve cannot be read on the n_TOF voltage axis without a gain map. The repository already has one — garfield_sim/results/hv_equivalence.json, per-mixture ln G = a + bV + c₂V² fits at two site pressures — and it is the authority used here, inverted so that the match is made on gain rather than by a constant offset.

What this does not rule out

Read these before quoting a number

  • The n_TOF ladder is not an efficiency. Its denominator is a doubles-trigger whose geometric ceiling per arm is ~50 %, and its numerator needs a 3–20 strip, ≤25 mm MIP-like cluster in both views. A cluster loses strips over threshold as the gain falls, so it turns on much later than detection does. Only its shape is used here.
  • Which is why it can disagree with the mapped bench curve, and does. The mapped curve says the chamber was on its detection plateau across the whole n_TOF scan; the ladder rises ×9 over the same span. Both can be true: what the voltage bought at n_TOF was reconstructability, not detection. If you want a single sentence, it is that one.
  • Withdrawn 2026-08-25: "the bench never measured below 450 V". It did. The 27 June saturday det3 scan runs 425–525 V with the same efficiency definition and shows the turn-on directly — 49 % at 425 V, 66 % at 435, 77 % at 445, plateau by 455. This page previously said the low edge was an extrapolation because only the 22 June scan had been looked at; that scan starts at 450 V, already flat, which is why it looked like there was no turn-on. The two scans agree on the plateau's flatness and on where discharges end it; the 27 June absolute level is ~10 points lower because det3 sat in the top slot (z 702, FEU 7/8) rather than the bottom one, twice the M3 lever arm into the same fixed 5 mm match box. Only the 520 V frame of the deck build is still extrapolated (it maps to bench 417 V).
  • The gas mixture is an operator label. run_config.json says "Ar/Iso 95/5" as free text; the mixer that sets and logs isobutane was commissioned on 7 July, ten days after the bench scan. No certificate exists for June.
  • The drift fields differ. Bench 1000 V, n_TOF 600 V on chamber A in both run_55 and run_57, production 700 V. The bench's own drift control puts that at roughly −15 % in peak amplitude, ~4 V — inside the map's uncertainty, but not zero.
  • The optimum is soft on the low side and a cliff on the high side. The recovery scatters ×1.33 rms about its fit, which moves the cliff by several volts; the low side is flat, so nothing here says 540 V was a mistake.
