What we gave up by running at 540 V

The flash recovery time says how high the amplification voltage may go. This is the other half: what the voltage buys, from the June cosmic bench at Ar/iso 95/5, carried onto the n_TOF voltage axis with the Garfield equivalence map, and checked against n_TOF's own resist scan in the production gas.

540 V cost essentially no efficiency, in either noise era. The 27 June bench scan puts the chamber's plateau at 93.5 % over 455–500 V (95/5) — the same chamber's published headline. Carried onto the n_TOF axis by the July ledger, 540 V is worth 95 % against 560 V's 94 %; carried by the production ledger, after the 23 July noise step, 93 % against 94 %. Both placements put the setpoint on the plateau, and the 540→560 V difference is ~1 point in each — inside the scan's own point-to-point scatter. So the honest cost of the 540 V decision, in detection efficiency, is not measurable here: what the extra volts bought was dead time, and what they cost was nothing the bench can see. Superseded 2026-08-28: this paragraph used to read that the noise step pushed 540 V "onto the shoulder of the turn-on, not the plateau", worth 69 % against 81 %, and called that ~12-point gap the cost of the decision. That came from the 29 June CSVs; there is no turn-on and there was no 12-point gap. The product of visible X17 rate and reconstructed-track yield — which uses the n_TOF MIP ladder, not this curve, and is unaffected — still peaks at 550 V, with 540 V within 95 % of it.

The usable window is about 20 V wide and it has a ceiling on both sides. Below ~535 V the chamber falls off its own detection turn-on in the production noise, and stops reconstructing tracks well before that; above ~555 V the recovery passes the thermal peak and the rate goes to nothing; and the bench's own discharge onset maps to 580 V, a few volts above where the dead time already ended the argument.

The 23 July noise step moved the operating point, not just the noise. In the run_55 configuration 540 V is worth bench 459 V; in the production configuration it is worth bench 437 V — 22 V lower, for the same setpoint, because the threshold rides on a doubled noise floor.

1. The gas costs 73 volts

The bench ran Ar/iso 95/5 and n_TOF ran 90/10, so the bench's efficiency curve cannot be read on the n_TOF voltage axis without a gain map. The repository already has one — garfield_sim/results/hv_equivalence.json, per-mixture ln G = a + bV + c₂V² fits at two site pressures — and it is the authority used here, inverted so that the match is made on gain rather than by a constant offset.

4. How much gain did we actually have?

Efficiency answers were you detecting? and the answer is a flat yes. It does not answer how much gain did you have, and that is the quantity the recovery time is being paid for. Scale it against the gain the readout could actually take: 100 % is the voltage at which the peak strip of the median track just fills the 12-bit sample.

peak_amp is the tallest sample of the tallest strip of the event — the max strip, which is the thing that clips first. It starts clipping in a few per cent of tracks well before the median does:

Reading ~500 V off the gain plot is also right, and it is a different statement. At the 50 % point half the sample is still below the rail, so the median amplitude only reaches the nominal 3871.5 ADC near 500 V — which is where the p50 marker visibly lies on the rail line in gain_vs_hv.png (67 % of events clipped there, median at 97 % of the rail). Anchoring at 500 V instead of 497 would lower every percentage on this scale by ~13 %.

The 50 % point barely cares where the clipping line is drawn: 496.4 / 497.0 / 497.1 V for a cut at 0.88 / 0.92 / 0.95 of the rail. A cut at 0.98 gives 508.6 V, but that is no longer a clipping test — per-channel pedestal subtraction spreads the railed population over ~3700–3900 ADC, so it asks whether a channel's rail landed high rather than whether the event clipped. The spark veto is not what sets it either: 496.8 V on the full M3-golden fiducial set against 497.0 spark-free.

That point is measured, not modelled — the saturated fraction goes 0.39 at 495 V to 0.66 at 500 V in both views, so the 0.5 crossing is bracketed by two points 5 V apart at bench 497 V, which is n_TOF 565 V. Plotted is the total collected charge, not the peak sample: the deconvolved forward fit censors railed samples and so keeps measuring where the peak sample cannot. The model-free window sum agrees to 3 %.

Full scale of what? It is worth a factor 3

Bench 497 V fills the 200 fC DREAM the scan was taken with. n_TOF ran 600 fC — 3× less ADC per electron — so filling that needs three times the avalanche, at bench ~518 V, n_TOF 586 V. Both are honest; they answer different questions, and every percentage here moves by 3× between them (the table carries both).

This page leads with the 200 fC one, for three reasons: it is the scan's own measured saturation point; it leaves nothing on the curve extrapolated (bench 425–505 V covers n_TOF 493–573 V, and 565 V is inside it, where 586 V is 13 V past the last trustworthy bench point); and the 600 fC setting was forced by the gamma flash — 668 pC on a single strip, 1113× the DREAM range — rather than chosen for tracking, so referring a tracking gain to it asks for 3× more avalanche than a MIP measurement needs. Say which one a number came from; never mix them.

How a bench voltage becomes an n_TOF one here. Only the gas and the site pressure move the voltage: n_TOF W is read off the bench ladder at W − 67.9 V, so n_TOF 560 V is bench 492 V — the plain gas equivalence. The readout change then divides the ADC by three (200 → 600 fC full scale); it is a factor, not a voltage. Written as one shift that is +94.1 V, and that form is fine for saying which bench voltage makes the same ADC — but it must not be used to evaluate the ladder, because it is exact only for a straight ladder and this one is curved (0.33 per 10 V near 440 V, 0.52 near 495). What this does not rule out

Read these before quoting a number

  • The n_TOF ladder is not an efficiency. Its denominator is a doubles-trigger whose geometric ceiling per arm is ~50 %, and its numerator needs a 3–20 strip, ≤25 mm MIP-like cluster in both views. A cluster loses strips over threshold as the gain falls, so it turns on much later than detection does. Only its shape is used here.
  • Which is why it can disagree with the mapped bench curve, and does. The mapped curve says the chamber was on its detection plateau across the whole n_TOF scan; the ladder rises ×9 over the same span. Both can be true: what the voltage bought at n_TOF was reconstructability, not detection. If you want a single sentence, it is that one.
  • Withdrawn 2026-08-25: "the bench never measured below 450 V". It did — the 27 June saturday det3 scan runs 425–525 V with the same efficiency definition. Only the 520 V frame of the deck build is extrapolated (it maps to bench 417 V).
  • Withdrawn 2026-08-28: "the 27 June scan shows the turn-on directly". It does not, and neither scan does. Both were re-derived on the current chain (mx_june_cosmic_qa/10b_hv_scan_efficiency.py, closure-checked against this chamber's published breakdown to the third decimal). What this page used to read — 49 % at 425 V rising to a plateau near 81 % — came from CSVs written on 29 June, before the golden M3 recipe, the significance floor and the matched-filter reprocessing. Corrected, the plateau is 93–95 % and 425 V reads 89.6 %: a ~4-point sag, not a turn-on. The chamber's own gain ladder, from these same sub-runs, says why — at 425 V the peak strip carries 69 ADC in the weakest 2 % of events, ~10 σ over the 6.85 ADC bench pedestal, so nothing is near threshold. The scan never reaches the chamber's low edge.
  • Also withdrawn 2026-08-28: the two scans' ~10-point gap and its explanation. The gap was an artefact of the stale CSVs; on the current chain the 22 June and 27 June scans agree. The M3 lever arm that was invoked to explain it is real but shows up in the core residual instead (0.34–0.41 mm bottom slot against 0.44–0.59 mm top) and never cost efficiency at a 5 mm match.
  • “100 % of optimal gain” is a READOUT limit, not a physics optimum. It says the ADC would be full, nothing more. The same 27 June scan measures the chamber's angular resolution against M3 over every fitted plane, and that is best at bench 445–460 V (1.02–1.06°) — already 1.11–1.15° by bench 497 V, where the median track saturates, and 1.31–1.38° by 515 V. A resolution is a threshold quantity, so the ledger that carries it across is the signal-to-noise one (+102.7 V), which puts that optimum at n_TOF 548–563 V — at or a little above where we ran, and well inside the map's own ±20 V. So the honest reading of the vertical scale is how much of the readout's range we were using, not how far from best we were.
  • The vertical scale is a choice, and it is worth 3×. 100 % here is the gain that fills the 200 fC range the bench scan ran. Referred to the 600 fC range n_TOF actually ran, every number divides by three and 100 % moves to n_TOF 586 V. Neither is more correct; they answer different questions. The table carries both.
  • Corrected 2026-08-28: how the CSA range enters. The first version folded the factor of three into the voltage axis as ln 3 / slope = +26.3 V, which is exact only for a straight ladder — and this one is curved, so it read the wrong part of it (−13 % at n_TOF 520 V, +6 % at 560 V) and hid a 26 V extrapolation inside what looked like a measured curve. The ladder is now evaluated at the gas-equivalent voltage, full stop, and the range enters as a factor.
  • The percentages are gain RATIOS. There is no ADC→electron calibration for the June bench range, so nothing here is an absolute gas gain, and the vertical scale means nothing outside its own normalisation. The ratios themselves survive the map: the gas term's ±20 V slides the whole curve sideways without changing them.
  • The flash charge climbs faster than the cosmic gain does. Over 520–560 V the HV-current flash charge goes 35 → 277 nC, a factor 7.8, where the bench gain ladder gives 4.7 over the same span (0.51 against 0.42 per 10 V). A 23 % slope difference across two very different measurements — a MIP's avalanche against a gamma flash read off a supply current, in different gases, mapped between them — is inside the known scatter, but it is not zero, and the imon integration systematic is still open.
  • The gas mixture is an operator label. run_config.json says "Ar/Iso 95/5" as free text; the mixer that sets and logs isobutane was commissioned on 7 July, ten days after the bench scan. No certificate exists for June.
  • The drift fields differ. Bench 1000 V, n_TOF 600 V on chamber A in both run_55 and run_57, production 700 V. The bench's own drift control puts that at roughly −15 % in peak amplitude, ~4 V — inside the map's uncertainty, but not zero.
  • The optimum is soft on the low side and a cliff on the high side. The recovery scatters ×1.33 rms about its fit, which moves the cliff by several volts; the low side is flat, so nothing here says 540 V was a mistake.
