# Waveform pull — 8-13 first campaign, and the plan for the post-re-slim run

**Status: the tool is proven on both raw sources and 99 segments are on EOS.
The remaining 71 buildable segments, and everything the re-slim recovers, wait
on one operational fact — a CTA recall does not stay staged, so recall and read
must be coupled.** The pipeline has been reworked around that and is ready to
run unattended when the re-slim campaign closes.

## 2. The finding that shapes everything else: recall is perishable

On 8-12 a recall of 27 runs was verified complete — **3187 of 3187 files
online**. Under a day later, with no job having touched most of them:

| run | `on_tape` | `online` |
|---|---|---|
| 224570 *(being read at the time)* | true | **true** |
| 224614, 224615, 224616, 224664 | true | **false** |

`requested: false` on all of them — no pin was held, and there is no user-facing
way to ask CTA for a longer one. Two jobs that reached the head of the queue
after the staging lapsed died on `[3005] Unable to open — no disk replica
exists` at their first `xrdcp`.

So **"recall everything, then work through it" is not a strategy that exists.**
Recalled data is a perishable good with a shelf life under a day. The only thing
that keeps a file online is a job reading it.

This also settles the wait-versus-go question that prompted the review: delaying
the pull does not cost one extra recall, it costs a *fresh* recall every time,
and 35 TB of tape drive is not free to the facility.

## 3. Why the pull runs downstream of the slim, and what that guarantees

The puller has no independent time estimate. It opens the slim product and reads
that segment's fitted `events` tree, then centres every window on

```
tof_window_centre = t_pred_ns[event] + tflash[det, detn, bunch]
```

`t_pred_ns` is the clock fit's own output — the same number the slim used to
associate its hits. `tflash` is only the flash-relative → tof-space bridge, and
is taken **unrepaired**, because that is what the slim matched on. So a waveform
in this product is, by construction, the same time neighbourhood the slim's hits
came from, which is what the 100.000000 % closure on 6.18 M hits demonstrates.

The consequence is deliberate: **no slim, no waveforms.** `find_slims` raises
before a byte of raw is read. There is no defensible place to point a window at
a segment whose match was never established.

### The fitted set is self-certifying

A concern was raised that `pulse_match`'s offset scan goes degenerate under the
PS supercycle and that 14 *accepted* segments sit at count-margin ≤ 2 — "healthy
by coin flip" (`join_mislock/README.md`). If one had flipped, its waveforms
would come from unrelated bunches and `verify.py` would still report 100 %,
because the hits it checks against would be wrong in the same way.

That risk does not survive contact with the products. Efficiency over all 170
fitted segments:

| p0 | p5 | p50 | p95 | p100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.9358 | 0.9403 | 0.9493 | 0.9631 | 0.9737 |

No tail, nothing below 0.85. A mis-locked segment sits at the accidental rate,
measured at **0.065 %** on a correctly-recovered segment. The mis-lock failure
mode is *not* a plausible-looking clock: `SLIM_CAMPAIGN_2026-08-12.md` records
that a wrong integer shift "fails loudly rather than producing a plausible
clock" (one peak at S/N 1273–1708, ≤ 2.5 at every other schedule multiple), and
a mis-locked segment "simply writes no file".

**So the margin ≤ 2 concern is about which lock `pulse_match` *picked*, and the
clock fit downstream is an independent test that a wrong pick cannot pass.** The
14 are a provenance-bookkeeping item before a bulk re-slim, not a gate on the
waveform pull. Waveforms built on a fitted segment are safe to build now.

The honest limit: the closure check is circular with respect to the match. It
proves the waveforms agree with the slim, not that the slim is right. It is the
*efficiency* of the fit, not `verify.py`, that rules out a mis-lock.

## 4. Scope — what is buildable, and what a later sweep costs

From `slim_inventory_2026-08-12.csv`: 291 segments over 83 n_TOF runs, of which
**170 OK**, 107 FAILED, 14 SKIPPED_LOW_JOIN.

| | segments | n_TOF runs |
|---|---|---|
| Fitted — buildable today | **170** | 65 |
| Failed, inside runs that have other fitted segments | 77 | 49 |
| Failed, in runs with no fitted segment at all | 44 | 18 |

Pulling a run today does **not** cover that run's failed segments — windows are
built per segment, and a failed segment contributes none. 49 of the 65 pulled
runs contain failed segments, so a post-re-slim sweep re-reads most of the raw
regardless.

There is no cheap insurance for this. The mis-lock error is an integer bunch
shift of +20…+41 — *seconds* — so a provisional window from
`shift_predictions.txt` at ±5 µs catches nothing related. Recovery genuinely
requires re-reading raw.

**Projected sizes** at the measured 2.74 GB/segment: 170 segments ≈ **466 GB**
(99 already on EOS); all 291 ≈ **797 GB**. Input side, the full raw is 34.9 TB.

### The disk copies are draining at ~4 runs/day

`campaign.sh plan` re-measures disk-versus-tape on every invocation, and two
consecutive days of it give the rate directly:

| | 8-12 | 8-13 |
|---|---|---|
| runs with a slim, raw **on disk** | 41 | **37** |
| runs with a slim, raw **on tape only** | 24 | **28** |

Data taking ended 8-10 and the disk copy expires ~2 weeks after each run, so
this is a pure countdown on a fixed set: **at ~4 runs/day the whole campaign is
tape-only within about nine days.** Plan the post-re-slim run as an
all-from-tape job — which is what the batching in §6 assumes — rather than
hoping some fraction is still resident.

## 5. Failure modes seen, and what was changed

| # | What happened | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 224614, 224616: tape path, `[3005] no disk replica exists`, staging had lapsed | `wrapper.sh` now queries `prepare 0` for every file **before** reading and exits 3 if any is offline — no wasted slot, and no chance of a partial read that looks like a quiet detector |
| 2 | 224667: `_require_complete` refused a 17-of-41 partial set, failing the whole run | The guard is right and stays. `--tflash-fallback-on-incomplete` now costs the run its **cross-check** instead of its waveforms — tflash falls back to the slim's own back-solve, recorded as `tflash_crosscheck_skipped` in the provenance |
| 3 | 224614/224616 published **7 empty 1,778-byte ROOT files** — a job that died mid-scan, indistinguishable from a product until opened | Publish is now gated on the `_provenance.json`, which `SegmentWriter.close` writes **last**; its presence is exactly the statement "this product is finished". Half-written products are named and withheld. The 7 have been deleted |
| 4 | Verify ran under `|| true`, so its verdict lived only in job log prose | `verify --write-json` drops `<product>_verify.json` beside each product and it is published with it. New `fleet_report.py` builds the campaign status from those files — never from log text |

Verify still does not gate the publish, deliberately: a product that fails
closure is still evidence, and the raw may be gone by the time anyone looks. The
verdict now travels *with* it instead of dying in a sandbox.

## 6. How the next campaign runs

`lxplus/campaign.sh` — a pipeline of recall-then-read batches, resumable, with
all state on disk rather than inferred from a log tail.

```bash
./campaign.sh plan runs.txt          # classify: disk / tape / no-slim
nohup ./campaign.sh run > campaign.log 2>&1 &
./campaign.sh status                 # from any session, any time
```

`plan` refuses to schedule a run with no slim. `run` walks the batches: request
the recall, poll until every file of every run is online, submit, **and
immediately pre-request the next batch so the tape system works while the farm
works**. Nothing sits staged waiting for a free slot.

Sizing, from the measurements above: `X17_WF_BATCH=8` runs ≈ 3 TB ≈ ~4 h to
recall at the observed 0.8 TB/h, against ~2.3 h to read 8 runs in parallel. The
pipelined cost is the larger of the two, so **~4 h per batch, ~11 batches for
83 runs — about two days of wall clock** for a full re-pull, most of it tape.

`wait_drain` will not accept an empty `condor_q` as completion unless a
`-totals` query demonstrably reached a schedd — an empty queue means the schedd
is down about as often as it means the jobs finished, and this project has
already been bitten by that twice.
