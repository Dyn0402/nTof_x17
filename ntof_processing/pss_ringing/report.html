Do the n_TOF plastic scintillators ring?

Run 224572, v12 reprocessing · raw stream1 chunks segments 8, 20, 40 · 2026-08-09

Yes — and the Pulse Shape Analysis is right to report it. Every large plastic pulse is followed by a train of real secondary pulses in the raw trace, from ~20 ns out to about 1 µs. The PSA turns them into 4.4 extra hits per large pulse, against 0.007 on the SiPM walls in the same beam — a factor of ~653. And it accounts for essentially all of the long tail in the DREAM/PSS match: on the reference pair the plastic excess at 150–1000 ns is 122,133 hits against a core of 47,292, and a flag that knows only “this hit is under 5 % of a bigger hit on the same channel in the previous microsecond” removes 99.5 % of it — see the companion note.

It is not a resonant ring. Two components sit on top of each other: a broad, sporadic after-pulse population peaking at 32–40 ns and decaying through a microsecond, and a razor-sharp 2 ns-wide echo at 81–82 ns that appears on all four plastics at the same delay — the signature of a cable reflection (~8 m of cable, one way).

What to do about it: Rejecting plastic after-pulses in the slim — a per-hit flag, a per-trigger metric, and what each costs.

What was measured, and against what

Take every isolated large hit on one plastic channel at physics times — amp_0 > 3000 with nothing on the same channel for 5 µs before it — and histogram the delay to every PSA hit that follows it on that channel. Because the plastics run at ~720 kHz singles, the accidental level is measured, not modelled: the identical construction is repeated with each leader’s time transplanted into a different bunch of the same channel, which carries the same rate profile and the same dead time but no correlation.

The SiPM walls are the control that matters: same beam, same digitiser, same PSA, same run — and a pulse three times wider, so if this were the PSA mis-fitting a long tail the walls would show it worst.

Four things that say this is real and detector-side

1. It is strictly forward in time

Running the same analysis with the clock reversed — asking what precedes a large pulse instead of what follows it, with the isolation requirement dropped so the two directions are symmetric — gives 4.13 excess hits per leader forward against 0.90 backward over 18–800 ns on PSSB. The echo is sharper still: 0.220 forward against 0.009 backward, a factor of 26. Real physics and accidentals are symmetric in time; this is not.

2. The walls, with a wider pulse, show none of it

+0.010 and +0.004 excess hits per leader on WALA and WALB against ~4.4 on the plastics. The wall pulse is ~72 ns FWHM against the plastics’ ~5–18 ns and its tail is an order of magnitude fatter (next figure), so a fit artifact driven by pulse tails would have to be worse there, not absent.

4. The 81 ns hits sit on a real bump in the raw samples

Split the leaders by whether the PSA gave them a hit at 79–85 ns, pull the raw stream1 trace behind each, and average. The ones that got the hit show a bump peaking at 82 ns worth 1.5 % of the primary; the ones that did not are smooth there. The PSA is not inventing this.

What this does not establish

  • The mechanism of the broad component. Delays of 20–500 ns with no amplitude proportionality fit PMT late pulses (photoelectrons elastically backscattered off the first dynode, which arrive at roughly twice the cathode–dynode transit time) or multiple reflections on a mismatched line. Ion-feedback afterpulsing, which lives at 0.1–5 µs, cannot explain a peak at 32–40 ns. Nothing here distinguishes those, and it would take a bench pulse-injection test to do so.
  • The 8 m cable length. That is arithmetic from 81.5 ns of round trip at 0.66 c, not a measurement. The delay is solid; the cable it implies is a hypothesis to check against the EAR2 cabling.
  • What the remaining core is. The after-pulse flag costs ~10 % of the background-subtracted core at |dt| < 25 ns, all of it small-amplitude. Whether those are genuine low-light coincidences or after-pulses that happen to land inside the accept window — the effect turns on at 18 ns — is not separated here.
  • Generality across the campaign. One run (224572), ten segments of hits, three raw chunks. All four plastics agree to within 30 % on every number, which argues the effect is structural rather than a channel fault, but no other run has been checked.
  • Whether the liquids share a mechanism. LIQA shows a much larger correlated excess (12.5 per leader) with its own time structure. That is a separate question, already partly covered by the v12 liquid pile-up work.

Reproducing

All of it runs from local data (/media/dylan/data/x17/ntof_reproc/v12_liqpileup and /media/dylan/data/x17/ntof_raw_224572) in a few minutes:

python afterpulse_spectrum.py --parts 1 -o afterpulse.json
python afterpulse_spectrum.py --parts 1 --dets PSSB --quiet 0 --max-dt 2000 -o fwd.json
python afterpulse_spectrum.py --parts 1 --dets PSSB --quiet 0 --max-dt 2000 --reverse -o rev.json
python raw_pss_blocks.py  <head_8.bin> --dets PSSA..LIQA --stack stack_head8.npz
python echo_conditional.py <head_8 20 40> --det PSSB -o echo_cond_PSSB.npz
python same_block.py       <head_8 20 40> -o same_block.json
python event_display.py    <head_8.bin> --det PSSB -n 4
python make_figures.py && python make_report.py
