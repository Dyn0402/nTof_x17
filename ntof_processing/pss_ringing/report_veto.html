Rejecting plastic after-pulses in the slim

Companion to “Do the n_TOF plastics ring?” · measured on the reference pair run_79/stat090_0000 × 224572, 400 bunches, slimmed at ±3 µs

One per-hit flag, and one per-trigger metric.

The flag — is this hit in the shadow of a bigger one? amp_0 < 0.05 × max(amp_0 on the same channel in the previous 1000 ns). It removes 99.5 % of the 150–1000 ns excess and 94.8 % of the 25–150 ns excess, for 10.4 % of the core — and the core it costs is entirely the small-amplitude end.

The metric — is the main peak where it should be? Per (DREAM trigger, arm) take the largest-amplitude plastic hit, not the earliest, and cut on its residual. On the arm the trigger was assigned to, 89.5 % land within ±25 ns at a median of -5.6 ns. Choosing the earliest hit instead gives a median of -589 ns, because in a microsecond-wide window the earliest hit is usually an unrelated single.

If you cannot add a branch: amp_0 > 250 alone, computable from the slim exactly as it stands today, already removes 95.7 % of the late tail for the same core cost. It is worse only in the 25–150 ns band (85.8 % against 94.8 %).

The metric, in the form to implement

Per plastic hit, computed on the full n_TOF stream with a full 1000 ns of lookback — not on the slim, because an after-pulse whose parent sits just outside the slim window is exactly the case a slim-only recomputation gets wrong:

shadow  = amp_0 / max(amp_0 of same-channel hits in the previous T ns)   # 0 if none
dt_prev = ns since that hit

after-pulse candidate  <=>  shadow < R          (R = 0.05, T = 1000 ns)
usable plastic hit     <=>  not an after-pulse candidate

Store shadow and dt_prev, do not store the boolean. Two float32 per hit is ~8 B, about 18 MB on a 74 MB segment, and it lets an analysis re-tune R and T without re-slimming 21 TB. A boolean freezes a choice that this study has only tuned on one segment.

The last column is the flag firing on the +100 µs accidental control, i.e. on hits that cannot be after-pulses of anything in the window. It runs far above the core loss because control hits are small random singles while core hits are MIPs — which is the same fact that makes the amplitude floor work. Lookback, not ratio, is what buys tail removal: at R = 0.05, T = 100 ns removes 0.3 % of the late tail and T = 1000 ns removes 99.5 %.

Both together — and why the floor adds nothing on top

At equal core cost the shadow flag beats the amplitude floor (99.5 % of the late tail against 95.7 %, 94.8 % of the middle band against 85.8 %), and combining them is not better than the flag alone — a floor of 150 ADC on top gives 99.1 % / 95.3 % at 10.9 % core cost. The two cuts are removing the same hits, because a hit small enough to be in the shadow of a MIP is a small hit.

The per-trigger metric

“Is the main peak where it should be?” — per (DREAM trigger, arm), pick one plastic hit and cut on its residual:

largest, arm & amp>25040,20189.5 %-5.6

Choose the primary by amplitude, not by time. In a window several microseconds wide the earliest hit is almost always an unrelated single, which is why “earliest” sits at a median of -589 ns. Restricting to the arm the trigger was actually assigned to is what turns this into a usable efficiency.

What this does not settle

  • One segment, one run. Tuned on run_79/stat090_0000 × 224572 over 400 bunches. The plastics’ singles rate varies across the campaign and the shadow flag’s cost scales with it, so R and T should be re-checked on a high-rate and a low-rate segment before a campaign-wide number is quoted.
  • The 10.4 % core loss is not all loss. Some of the small-amplitude core excess is genuinely correlated with the trigger and some of it may itself be after-pulsing that happens to land inside ±25 ns (the effect turns on at 18 ns). This study does not separate those.
  • No wall-side requirement is included. The metric above is plastic-only; combining it with the wall coincidence is the analysis’s choice and is not measured here.
  • The amplitude floor is in ADC, not in MIPs. Per-channel gains differ, so 250 ADC is not the same physical threshold on all four arms. It should be set per channel off the MIP peak before it is used for a physics yield.
