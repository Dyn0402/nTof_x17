What is measured, and why it is decisive

bunch_join defines a burst's time base by its first trigger: that trigger is called the gamma flash, and every other trigger in the burst is timed from it. If the flash itself was not recorded, the first scintillator single takes its place and the whole burst sits ~1 ms off the n_TOF clock. Two independent properties say whether the tagged flash is really the flash, and both come from the DREAM files alone — no n_TOF, no lock, no fit:

  • gap1, the delay from the tagged flash to the first physics trigger. The N93B gate admits singles only from ~1 ms after the flash, so this is a hard edge; the next gap (single to single) is ~15 µs, two orders of magnitude smaller. A burst whose “flash” is really a single shows gap1 in the tens of microseconds.
  • flash hits, the number of hits in the tagged flash event. The flash saturates every chamber: ~4,000 hits against 16–830 for a physics trigger.

Both are compared against the sub-run's own medians, never against a campaign constant: the gate width is a DAQ setting and the flash hit count depends on which chambers were live. A burst is flagged when gap1 falls below 0.5× its sub-run's median or the flash hit count below 0.35×. Two things the sweep found on its own

Two of the three bursts the brute-force pass could not pair are explained. run_102/stat090_0001 burst 0 and run_118/stat090_0019 burst 0 are the FIRST burst of their sub-run, and their tagged flash carries 22 and 13 hits against ~4,080 — there is no gamma flash in the file at all. Their physics triggers are feeble too (median 23 hits against ~150), so the DAQ started recording partway through the gate and caught the weak tail of a burst. Their time base is anchored to a background trigger, which is why no bunch could be found for them; they are not simply small. The third, run_116/stat090_0028 burst 1073, has a perfectly normal flash (gap1 1.11 ms, 2,992 hits) and stays unexplained.

41 of the 1,516 pulses the ledger calls “n_TOF not recording” also delivered no beam to the EAR2 target. Their bursts have no gamma flash (23–794 hits) and feeble physics triggers (14–38 hits each against 138–178 in the healthy bursts of the same sub-run), at ~23 triggers per burst against ~90. They are interleaved with healthy bursts, pulse by pulse, rather than forming a block — so this is not a detector warming up, it is the PS pulse not arriving. The beam record says those pulses were extracted; DREAM says they did not reach this target. n_TOF's own PKUP would have settled it, and n_TOF was not recording — the two absences agree. Nothing moves: the class is already outside the matching denominator.

What this does not settle

  • Bursts of fewer than 5 triggers have no meaningful gap1 and are not judged here; they are noise clusters rather than beam bursts and the ledger already counts them as such.
  • A flash recorded but mis-ordered — arriving in the file after a single of the same burst — would not be caught by gap1 (it would look normal) but would be caught by the hit count, since the tagged event would then be the single. A flash both recorded and correctly first, yet displaced in time by the DAQ, would be caught by neither; nothing in the campaign suggests that mode.
  • This sweep says the time reference is right. It says nothing about the per-trigger timing resolution within a burst, which the clock fit's residuals measure.
