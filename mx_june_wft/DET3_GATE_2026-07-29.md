# det3 gate: the waveform-first chain against the hits chain

> **GATE PASSED (03:31) once the candidate-cluster seeder was in.** Re-running
> det3 with it gives **within 5 mm 93.47 %** (threshold 93.0, hits chain 93.13),
> core σ **0.470 mm**, median |r| **0.763 mm**, reco-at-all **97.29 %** — every
> threshold met, and ahead of the hits chain on within-5 mm and coverage. Angles
> are unchanged at 1.20 / 1.15°. The body below documents the *first* pass with
> the largest-cluster seeder (92.15 %) and the diagnosis that led to the fix;
> the numbers in it are superseded but the reasoning is not.

unbiased and angle-independent. Position is at parity, marginally behind.

## Both chains scored the same way

The published baseline numbers (`rerun_baseline.json`: 93.4 %, core σ 0.48 mm)
come from `09_efficiency_breakdown.py`, which uses its own alignment, its own
active box and its own event list. To compare anything, the old chain has to go
through the *same* accounting, so `02_efficiency.py --source hits` does exactly
that: same M3 rays, same active box recipe, same spark tagging, each chain
reconstructing whatever it can.

That reproduces the old chain at **93.13 % / 0.448 mm** against its published
93.4 % / 0.48 mm — so the accounting is validated, and the wft numbers above are
directly comparable. (Getting there needed two corrections worth recording:
`09` reads the *un-vetoed* `event_results.pkl`, not the veto50 cache, and
restricting the hits chain to the wft event list is unfair to it.)

## Where the remaining position gap comes from — measured, not guessed

**1. Multi-cluster events (the dominant term).** For plane-fits landing > 4 mm
from the reference, the reference sits a median of **37 mm outside the fit
window**; for good fits it is 1.9 mm *inside*. These are events where the
muon's cluster is not the largest one, so "largest cluster wins" seeds the fit
on the wrong charge. Their median `n_dropped` (strips in competing clusters) is
**4**, against **0** for good fits.

The hits chain has the same seeding rule but its line fit *failed* on such junk
clusters, quietly removing them. The forward fit converges on whatever charge it
is given, so the rejection has to be explicit. Cutting `n_dropped ≤ 2` — the
threshold the hits chain already uses for its alignment subset — gives:

| | no cut | n_dropped ≤ 2 |
|---|---|---|
| within 5 mm | 92.15 % | 91.24 % |
| reco_far | 5.13 % | **2.11 %** |
| reco-at-all | 97.28 % | 93.36 % |
| core σ | 0.472 mm | 0.472 mm |

i.e. it halves the tail but costs coverage; within-5 mm does not improve because
rejected events move into `hit_no_reco`. Both variants are written
(`efficiency_breakdown.json` = no cut, `_cut.json` = with cut). **Neither is a
fix** — the fix is a seeder that picks the muon's cluster rather than the
largest one, which is the top follow-up.

**2. Reference-free seeding (small, ~0.02 mm).** The R&D fits were seeded at the
M3 position; production cannot do that without making alignment and efficiency
circular. A controlled same-window test (250 events, same fitter, only the seed
changed) puts the cost at 4 % on X and 17 % on Y of the mesh resolution, and
**zero on the angle** (1.04° both ways). Part of the R&D's quoted mesh
resolution was therefore optimism.

## What this does not change

`has_any` (100.0 vs 99.99 %) and `spark_frac` (8.22 % both) are identical, as
they must be: detection is still decided by the analyzer's trigger on the hits
tree. If those ever move, the seeding is broken, not the physics.

## det2 — and a caveat that kills the naive reading

The second detector through the chain (`o22_long_det2`):

| metric | hits chain, cache as it stands | **waveform-first** |
|---|---|---|
| within 5 mm | 80.86 % | **91.22 %** |
| core σ \|r\| | 0.607 mm | **0.484 mm** |
| median \|r\| | 1.073 mm | **0.806 mm** |
| reco-at-all | 95.60 % | 96.29 % |
| σ_θ X / Y | ~3.03 / 2.44° | **1.31 / 1.56°** |

**Do not read the position rows as +10 points.** det2's hits cache has no
`.meta.json` sidecar, which means it predates the July-25 per-plane
significance floor — that fix was only ever run through the real pipeline for
det3 and det4 (`june-rerun-matched-filter-reco-regression`, open item). The
floor is worth roughly ten points on det2: the offline estimate at the time was
**91.4 %**, against the 91.22 % the waveform-first chain reaches here. So the
honest statement is **parity again**, not a win, and what the comparison
actually shows is that the waveform-first chain gets there *without* needing the
floor fix applied to it.

The angle rows are unaffected by this caveat (the floor changes which strips
enter the cluster, not the 20–30 % ladder compression) and remain a genuine 2×.

**Open action:** rebuild det2/det6/det7's hits caches with `--sigrel=0.10` before
quoting any position comparison for them. That also closes the July-25 item.
det3 and det4 are already floor-corrected, so their comparisons stand as given.

The seeder bought 1.3 points of within-5 mm for +4 % runtime and cost nothing
elsewhere. What remains is core σ: 0.470 against the hits chain's 0.448 mm, a

17 % on Y in a controlled test) — the price of not letting the reconstruction
see the answer.

## Verdict and follow-ups

The chain is **accepted for angles and depth** — that is where the hits basis
was wrong, and the improvement is large, unbiased and angle-independent. It is
**at parity, not ahead, on position**, and the gate threshold is missed by 0.85
points for a reason that is understood and fixable.

1. **Cluster selection is the next real win.** Pick the seed cluster by
   compatibility with a track (charge, extent, time span) instead of by strip
   count; that is worth ~3 points of within-5 mm and would remove the tail
   without the coverage cost of `n_dropped ≤ 2`.
2. Try a sub-pitch p0 refinement (the free-ladder machinery from
   `37_threading_displays.py` already does this) to close the last 0.02 mm.
3. The `n_dropped ≤ 2` variant is the one to use for anything where a clean
   sample matters more than coverage (alignment, resolution maps).
