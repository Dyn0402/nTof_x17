Question: the chambers are mechanically 30 mm deep, but the charge-visible
column read 27.9 mm on det3 (R&D, old kernel). Is that reconstruction
systematics, gas physics (attachment), or chamber geometry?

Method (`bench/gap_study.py`): full NNLS charge-arrival profiles from the
RC-ladder reconstruction (production config), for **geometrically contained
tracks** (the M3 reference guarantees the track crosses the whole gap inside
the active area, so its column must span the full drift depth). Stacked
profiles fitted with two endpoint hypotheses: sharp edge
(`A * erfc((u-T)/sig)`) vs attachment tilt (`* exp(-u/tau_att)`). The ns->mm
conversion uses the **geometric** drift speed v_geom = kw * v (the w/tan_ref
slope against M3) — the timing-only chi2(v) is degenerate with the kernel
(fixed-hyper scans run to ~40 um/ns on BOTH kernels; the R&D's sharp 36.7
required re-profiling kernel nuisances per v; see below).

## Results

| | det3 X | det3 Y | det2 X | det2 Y |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| n contained | 4,716 | 4,706 | 3,345 | 3,242 |
| sharp fit: T_end | 757 +- 3 ns | (poor fit) | 782 +- 3 ns | (poor fit) |
| attach fit: T_end / tau_att | 759 ns / 22 us (= flat) | 746 ns / 0.8 us | 782 ns / inf (= flat) | 766 ns / 0.9 us |
| **column [mm] @ v_geom** | **27.9** | 28.0 | **30.5** | ~31 |

- **det2 reads the full mechanical gap (30.5 mm), det3 reads 27.9 mm** —
  same gas, same reconstruction, same estimator, same selection. The det3
  deficit is **chamber-specific geometry**, full stop.
- **Gas attachment is excluded**: both X profiles are flat to the endpoint
  (tau_att fit runs to 22 us / infinity). The Y planes on BOTH chambers show
  the same ~0.8-0.9 us apparent tilt — a common Y-response systematic (the
  resistive-side response is harder to model), not gas: gas would tilt X
  identically. Quote X.
- The old-kernel bias identified earlier (spurious late charge, Y basis-end
  pile-up 67 % -> 18 %) cancels out of the stacked-profile endpoint: the
  R&D's 27.9 mm det3 number is CONFIRMED, now with the kernel and
  attachment systematics closed and a clean control chamber.

## Fringe-field cross-check (edge distortion vs geometry) — RULED OUT for the bulk

Could the deficit be drift-field fringe distortion at the chamber edges rather
than cathode geometry? No — and the discrimination is physics, not judgement:
a field perturbation imposed at the side boundary of a parallel-plate gap
relaxes by Laplace's equation, decaying into the volume as exp(-pi*s/d) with
s = distance from the edge, d = 30 mm (e-folding ~10 mm; <1 % beyond
s = 45 mm, ~3e-5 at s = 100 mm). Fringe effects MUST hug the edges; a dished
cathode need not. The hires map can't show this cleanly (its r = 45 mm kernel
bleeds edge features ~45 mm inward), so `bench/edge_fringe_test.py` rebins the
same contained-track profiles **kernel-free by distance to the nearest edge**
and fits endpoint T and erfc width per bin (`edge_fringe_test.png`).

- **Interior (s > 100 mm, fringe-free by construction): det3 28.02 +- 0.11 mm
  vs det2 31.26 +- 0.16 mm** — the full 3.2 mm (~15 sigma) deficit survives
  where fringe is mathematically dead. The bulk deficit is geometric.
- A real edge systematic DOES exist: det2 droops 31.3 -> ~29.3 over the outer
  ~50-70 mm rim (range beyond 45 mm explained by binning on the mid-plane ref
  position — cathode-end smear up to ~17 mm for inclined tracks). det3's
  outermost bin instead rises to 29.9 (four edges with different local
  topography superposed). **Caveat: the outer ~50 mm of the hires map — ~100 mm
  including kernel bleed — is edge-contaminated; don't over-read the rim.**
- The tilt is not an edge effect either: det2's edge droop is y-symmetric (as
  fringe from identical edges must be); det3's y-profile is asymmetric
  (high-y ~1.5 mm below low-y) and the high-y tercile column is short at all
  x, including mid-detector bins ~100 mm from any edge.
- Endpoint softness (fringe smears drift paths -> wider erfc) shows only mild
  rim inflation (~3.8 vs ~2.9 mm interior) on both chambers — consistent with
  the modest rim effect, nothing more.

Further levers if ever needed (not required for the conclusion): the
300-1100 V drift scan (gap in mm must be HV-invariant for geometry; field
artefacts move with v(E) curvature) and a per-edge decomposition binned by
cathode-end position. Hardware depth gauge remains the definitive word.

## v systematics note

`bench/vscan_lp.py` fixed-hyper chi2(v) scans minimise at ~40 um/ns under
BOTH kernels — the v <-> kernel valley, not a measurement. The geometric
v_geom = kw * v_drift (36.8 X / 37.5 Y on det3; 39.0 / 41.5 on det2,
corridor-measured kw, +-2-3 %) is anchored by the unbiased M3 angle
validation and is what the mm columns above use. A +-3 % v_geom scale error
moves the columns by +-0.9 mm — not enough to close det3's 2.1 mm deficit,
and irrelevant to the det3-vs-det2 contrast (which is differential).

## Consequences

1. The "gap" used in depth-dependent analyses must be the per-chamber
   **charge-visible column**, not 30 mm — and for det3, ideally the (x, y)
   map, not a single number.
2. Field distortion corollary: if det3's cathode is 2 mm closer at the same
   HV, its drift field is ~7 % higher than nominal — its v, gain-vs-position
   and any E-field-derived quantity inherit the topography. Worth folding
   into the v(E) analysis.
3. Hardware: det3's cathode flatness should be inspected at the next
   opening; the map above predicts where.
