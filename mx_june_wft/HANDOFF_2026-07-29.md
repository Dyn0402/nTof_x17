## The three things worth knowing before you touch it

**1. Comparisons against `rerun_baseline.json` are invalid unless both chains go
through the same accounting.** `09_efficiency_breakdown.py` uses its own
alignment, active box and event list, and reads the *un-vetoed*
`event_results.pkl`. Use `02_efficiency.py --source hits`, which reproduces the
published numbers (det3 93.13 vs 93.4; det2 80.86 vs 80.3) and is therefore the
only fair baseline.

**2. Reconstruction must stay reference-free.** The R&D fits were seeded at the
M3 track. Production cannot do that — alignment and efficiency would be
circular. The cost is 4 % (X) / 17 % (Y) of mesh resolution and nothing on
angle, measured in a controlled same-window test. If you ever see a suspiciously
good position resolution, check whether something reintroduced the reference.

**3. Detection stays hits-defined, deliberately.** Seeds, the significance
floor, the spark veto and the efficiency numerator/denominator all still come
from the hits tree, because whether the detector saw a muon is a property of the
analyzer's trigger, not of the fit. `has_any` and `spark_frac` are identical
between chains; if they ever move, the seeding broke, not the physics.

Both chains through the same accounting (`02_efficiency.py --source hits`):

| det3 | 93.13 % | 92.15 % | 0.448 mm | 0.472 mm | 2.42 / 2.60° | **1.20 / 1.14°** |

| det6 | 44.43 %* | **75.41 %** | 0.659 mm | **0.429 mm** | ~3.73 / 2.90° | **2.28 / 2.52°** |

*Those two hits caches predate the 2026-07-25 significance floor. Against that
fix's own offline estimates (det2 91.4 %, det6 70.9 %) the waveform chain is at
parity on det2 and ~4.5 points ahead on det6 — still a win, but not the raw
+10 / +31 the table suggests. `finish_fleet.sh` rebuilds those caches.

Angles improve ~2x on every detector and are unbiased and angle-independent,
which is what the rebuild was for. Position is at parity or ahead everywhere;
det3 — the detector the hits chain was most tuned on — is the one case where the
waveform chain is marginally behind.

det6 deserves a note: 75.4 % within 5 mm and core σ 0.429 mm on a chamber whose
X plane is dead for micro-TPC purposes and whose calibration is provisional.

## det6/det7 calibration — works, but treat the numbers as provisional

`wft.calibrate` ran end to end on det6, which had no bundle: 180 training
events, 183 objective evaluations, landing at v = 26.74 µm/ns, c1 = 0.216,
kY = 1.745, τ_s = 97 ns, σ_s = 134 ns. So the protocol does transfer to a
detector nobody had calibrated. Two reasons not to trust it yet:

- **The impulse templates rest on 30 (X) and 10 (Y) strip candidates**, against
  hundreds on det3 — these are the low-gain chambers, so few strips clear the
  `|tan| > 0.22` and `amp > 500` template cuts. The Y template's −0.103
  undershoot is a 10-waveform median.
- **v = 26.7 µm/ns at 700 V** sits well below det3's 35.5 µm/ns at the same
  field. That may be real (different chamber, different gas history) or may be
  the thin template biasing the fit. It needs a cross-check before it is quoted.

Also remember det6's X plane is dead for micro-TPC purposes (no drift-time
development — `june-cosmic-qa-fleet-summary`), so its angle numbers are expected
to be poor whatever the calibration does.

   before the HV/drift scans (a bundle per scan point) are practical.
