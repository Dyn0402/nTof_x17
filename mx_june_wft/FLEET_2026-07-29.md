Roughly 2× better on det3, det2 and det7, and clearly better on det6; det4 only
matches its pre-regression numbers. Bias is consistent with zero everywhere
except det6-Y. The implied-v spread — the median `w / tan_ref` across
angle bins, which must be flat if the reconstruction is geometrically honest —
runs 1.3–6.4 µm/ns against the production ladder's ~17. That spread *is* the
compression signature, and it is largely gone.

## Two findings worth following up

**det6's X plane is not dead.** `JUNE_RESULTS_SUMMARY.md` records it as dead for
micro-TPC (v railing to 119 µm/ns, plateau σ68 ≈ 16°, board-C mesh defect). The
waveform fit gets σ_θ 2.28° with +0.03° bias from it over 4,892 events. A
velocity railing to 119 µm/ns is a degenerate fit, not a measurement. The plane
is degraded, not dead — see the handoff for the two checks that would settle it.

**The calibration's v is not identifiable from one detector alone.** det7's fit
ran to c1 = 0.004 (no charge sharing, impossible on resistive strips) with
v = 36.68 µm/ns at a field where the drift scan measures 26.4 — yet its angles
came out unbiased at 1.96 / 1.71°. The sharing kernel and v trade off while
`tan θ = w / v` stays determined (`WAVEFORM_FIRST_THREADING.md` §17.2). So a
fitted per-detector v is a nuisance parameter unless it is checked against an
independent measurement. det3's and det6's do agree with the drift scan (det6's
free fit landed 1.3 % from det3's measured 700 V value) — that agreement is what
makes them trustworthy. det7 is being recalibrated with v pinned.
