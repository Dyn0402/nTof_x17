# Bolting det4 into the H4 rail set — mechanical note

**2026-07-31.** Companion to `DET4_SPS_ASSESSMENT.md` (where the live band comes
from), `SPS_BEAM_GEOMETRY_2026-07-31.md` (where the beam is) and
`14_board_map.py` (where the board geometry comes from). Figure:
`beam_mount_layout.png`, produced by `17_beam_mount_layout.py`.

Board numbers are **measured** (DFS3498A Gerbers + the June cosmic band).
Rail-set numbers are **assumed** from the 2026-07-31 verbal description and are
CLI flags on the script — re-run it once the real ones are taped.

## 1. The four board numbers the mount has to respect

| | value | where from |
|---|---|---|
| PCB outline | **470 × 470 mm** (local −20.32 … 449.68 on both axes) | `DFS3498A_det.gbr` |
| board margin | **20.32 mm** on the two bare edges, **50.32 mm** on the two connector edges | same |
| metallised square | 399.36 mm, centred on the Gerber origin (local 199.68) | `DFS3498A_activearea.gbr` |
| frame mounting holes | **Ø4.2 mm on a 428 mm square, 55 mm pitch**, 32 of them | `DFS3498A_det.gbr`, aperture D15 |
| the live band | local X **177–215 mm** (38 mm wide, full 360 mm long) | `DET4_SPS_ASSESSMENT.md` §3b |

Two consequences:

- **The band is the board centre.** Band centre local X = 196 mm; the active-area
  centre is 199.68 mm and the PCB centre 214.68 mm. Aim the beam at the middle of
  the board and you are on the band to 4 mm. No offset to remember, and it is
  checkable with a tape and a diagonal.
- **Standing the chamber on a bare PCB edge puts the band centre
  216.3 mm above that edge** (196 + 20.32). Add whatever the chamber frame
  protrudes below the PCB. That one number sets the entire mount height:

  > mount height available under the chamber
  > = (beam height above the rail top) − 216.3 mm − (frame below the PCB edge)

  With the placeholder 250 mm beam height that is **34 mm** — less than one
  40 mm profile. **Measure the real beam height above the rail top face before
  cutting anything.** If the number comes out small or negative, do not put the
  cross bar under the chamber: hang the chamber beside it (side brackets reaching
  down past the bar to its outer face), or bolt the bar into the rails' *side*
  slots so its top face sits at or below rail top.

## 2. Which way up to mount it

The two connector edges are adjacent (Gerber +X and +Y), so two of the four PCB
edges are bare and adjacent. Both useful roll positions therefore stand on a bare
edge, with the cables leaving from the top and one side:

| roll | bands | yaw does | µTPC lever arm | beam caught by the 38 mm band |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `board_map_g_det4_rot90ccw` | horizontal | runs the track **along** a band | Y plane (FEU 8) | 25 % flat, 34 % at the best height |
| that view turned 180° | vertical | runs the track **across** the bands (12 mm excursion at tan θ = 0.4, inside 38 mm) | X plane (FEU 6) | **51 %** |

The 25/34/51 % come from `sps_beam_lab_frame.json` and depend on the P2 fan being
mounted apex-up, which `SPS_BEAM_GEOMETRY_2026-07-31.md` §4 says is **not
established**. If the beam really is taller than it is wide (σ_v 37.8 vs σ_h
28.6 mm), bands **vertical** wins twice over: 1.5× the rate, and the micro-TPC
lever arm lands on the X plane, which is the plane that measures the band
coordinate. Settle it with one photo or one tape measurement at H4, and build the
mount so either roll is a ten-minute swap.

## 3. The two joints

**Rail → cross bar.** An ordinary 90° angle bracket does work; the feeling that
they are "for parallel profiles" comes from trying to butt the cross bar into a
rail end-on. Don't. Lay the bar **across** the rails: the rail's top slot faces
up and runs along the beam, the cross bar's side slot faces sideways and runs
across the beam, and a plain L bridges them with one bolt per leg — each T-nut
slides freely in its own slot. Two Ls per rail, one either side of the bar. A
flat drilled plate laid on the rail and bolted up into the bar's bottom slot is
stiffer and is what a proper cross-connector is.

**Chamber → mount.** Ignore the random hole heights. Put a short **vertical post**
either side of the chamber, bolted to the cross bar with the same Ls (vertical to
horizontal, in plane — the case those brackets are actually made for). The post's
slot is continuous, so every one of the chamber's side brackets lands wherever its
hole happens to be, and the whole chamber slides up and down for beam-height trim.
Re-rolling the chamber 90° moves the holes; the slot does not care.

Both trims come out free from the slots that are already there:

- **vertical** trim — slide the chamber's brackets in the **posts'** slots;
- **transverse** trim — slide the **posts** along the **cross bar's** slot.

Placement wants to be within ~10 mm of the spot centre (`SPS_BEAM_GEOMETRY` §5:
beyond ~2 cm you lose half of what the band could have collected), which both of
those give easily.

**Stiffness.** One 600 mm bar carrying a ~500 mm tall chamber is a goalpost — it
will pivot about its own axis. Use two bars, ~150 mm apart along the beam, tied at
the ends by two short bars: a rectangular sled, four Ls at the corners. Same
bracket type again, and it is what makes the rotation below rigid.

## 4. Rotating it in the beam

**Yaw about the vertical axis is the rotation worth having, and the 250 mm gap is
the limit.** A 500 × 50 mm footprint yawed by θ sweeps 500 sin θ + 50 cos θ along
the beam:

| | angle | tan θ |
|---|---|---|
| hard limit, 250 mm gap | **±24°** | 0.45 |
| with 20 mm clearance each side | **±19°** | 0.34 |

That covers three of the four inclination bins §3b measures the band in
(|tan θ| 0.05–0.12, 0.12–0.25, and most of 0.25–0.60). Past ±24° the neighbours
have to move.

**Option A — no custom parts, 20 minutes.** Bolt the sled to the two **outer**
rails with one bolt each, through a round Ø9 hole on one side and a ~25 mm
transverse slot on the other. The rails' own slots run along the beam, so sliding
one bolt forward and the other back by the same amount yaws the sled about its own
centre. Angle from a ruler: θ = atan(Δz / 260 mm) with the outer rails 260 mm
between centres. The slot takes up the 260·(1/cos θ − 1) ≈ 17 mm the two bolts
spread by at 20°.

**Option B — recommended, and it is what the figure draws.** Use the middle rail,
which is already on the centre-line, as a proper pivot: **one bolt into a T-nut in
the middle rail, directly under the beam spot**, plus **one clamp bolt into each
outer rail**. Rotating to ±19° runs the clamp bolts ±45 mm along their rail slots
and pulls them 7 mm inward transversely, which a Ø18 clearance hole (or a short
transverse slot) in the sled absorbs. The pivot stays under the beam, so the spot
does not walk off the band as you scan, and a protractor scratched on the sled
gives reproducible, indexed angles.

Roll (about the beam axis) is the other rotation available and costs nothing in
along-beam space, but it does not change the track angle — it only swaps which
plane gets the micro-TPC lever arm (`DET4_SPS_ASSESSMENT.md` §3c). Do it by
remounting, not by building a cradle.

**Services.** The connector edges end up at the top and one side in both roll
positions, i.e. near the yaw axis, so cables and gas move little — but leave a
service loop, and check it at both extremes before the first spill.

## 5. What still has to be measured at H4

1. **Beam height above the rail top face**, and its lateral offset from the middle
   rail. This sets §1's height budget and is the one number that can invalidate the
   whole mount.
2. **Profile size and the rails' actual centre-to-centre spacing** (300 mm was
   quoted outer-face to outer-face).
3. **Which way up the P2 basket is mounted** — one photo closes the
   horizontal-vs-vertical band question in §2.
4. **The chamber frame's own side-hole positions and how far the frame protrudes
   below the PCB edge.** Not in any file here; it is the only geometry in this note
   that is neither measured nor assumed.
