The two planes agree to 60 ns, and it is not the depth bin

What dt_xy is, what the x/y coincidence gate is worth, and why HANDOFF_T0_PRIOR.md §1 was reading a real number through a wrong mechanism.  ·  2,111,162 stage-3 tracks  ·  2026-09-12

The handoff's measurement stands; its diagnosis does not. The two planes of one chamber really do disagree on t0 at the 66.1–77.4 ns level. But it is not the 60 ns depth-bin degeneracy: a periodicity test that detects the known 5 ns T0_STEP snap at 8.3 σ finds nothing at 60 ns (|σ| ≤ 0.97). And it is not the x/y plane separation either — the handoff's own first check — because the residual does not vanish at normal incidence.

What it is: the forward model's per-plane t0 is smoothly uncertain at about 54.8–60.1 ns, against a fitted x_t0_err that medians a few ns. Same scale as one depth bin — which is why the bin was a tempting explanation — but a broad uncertainty, not a two-minimum ambiguity, and it needs a different fix.

1 · The census, exactly: the measured offset never ran on A, B or C

The handoff predicted "~100 % miss on A and C from the parity argument" and asked for it to be confirmed rather than trusted. It is not approximately 100 %. ftst is a 6-phase counter; A, B and C only ever produce even ftst_diff and their bundle keys are odd, so the intersection is empty by construction — no statistics involved. Every one of those tracks ran on the hardcoded -18.8 ns.

2 · What dt_xy actually is — and it needs no bench

It is not a per-chamber constant to be looked up. The whole x_t0 − y_t0 distribution translates linearly with ftst_diff, by the same amount on all four arms. ftst has 6 phases over a 60 ns sample, so one unit is 10.0 ns of readout phase — and that is what is measured, to within the known low bias of the estimator (the accidental pedestal does not shift with ftst and pulls the correlation peak toward zero).

So dt_xy is a readout-clock effect with no chamber physics in it. The bench measured two ftst_diff classes per arm; on A, B and C neither of them occurs in beam data. The beam data measures it directly, for every class that actually happens.

3 · The shape nobody had looked at

Drawn per ftst class, on unambiguous tracks with no gate, the x/y time difference is a ~200 ns-wide peak sitting on an accidental pedestal. Two planes seeing one track at the same instant would give a spike a few ns wide. This is the whole result, and no choice of offset changes it.

4 · Two mechanisms tested, two ruled out

Not the depth-bin degeneracy

wft/model.py says the χ² surface has near-degenerate minima 60 ns apart and that only ~35 % of free fits land in the physical one. That predicts a comb in the residual at 60 ns. There is none — and the test is not blind, because the same statistic finds the known 5 ns T0_STEP snap at 8.3 σ or better on every chamber.

Not the x/y plane separation

The handoff called this "the first thing to check and it could explain a large part of the table". It is real and it is small. A plane separation crossed by an inclined track must vanish at normal incidence; the lowest-inclination quintile already carries 65.7–70.3 ns of half-width, and the full tan θ range adds about 10 on top.

5 · The gate is the metric — so §1's number is truncated and its test circular

wft.reco.select_tracks sets gated from |(t0x − t0y) − dt| ≤ 120.0 ns AND both planes plausible. Zero of 2,111,162 gated tracks lie outside that window. The handoff measured the x/y residual on the gated sample, so it measured a distribution that had already been cut on — at ±120.0 ns around a centre that was wrong by up to 84 ns.

The consequence for §3 step 4 is direct: a prior that moved t0 would move which tracks pass this cut, so the residual measured on the survivors would improve partly by construction. That target must be declared on the unselected population — where the half-widths are not 66/76/77 but 106.9/133.4/141.8 ns.

6 · What the coincidence test is worth

Against a scrambled pairing — same arm, same ftst class, someone else's y_t0 — the window keeps most true pairs and about a third of accidental ones. That is an enhancement of 1.94–2.43×, bought for roughly 30 % of the real tracks. Useful; not the discriminator select_pair's docstring describes when it says it uses information "that single-plane selection cannot use".

8 · What this does not settle

  • The in-situ prior (handoff §3 step 3) is not built. This supplies the dt_xy law it would need and removes the reason to expect it to collapse the residual the way §1 hoped — a ~60 ns per-plane uncertainty is not something a better centre repairs.
  • The absolute level of dt_insitu is provisional. The shape (linear, -8.8…-7.7 ns per unit) is good to a few ns; the anchor is a median on a broad peak and the whole column can move ~10 ns together. No acceptance or efficiency number is re-derived from it here.
  • Nothing is re-reconstructed. Every number is read from the stage-3 track table. Deciding whether a t0 prior helps needs the waveform path, which is not this.
  • Why the per-plane t0 is uncertain at one bin is not answered. That it is smooth rather than combed rules out the two-minimum picture; it does not say what replaces it.
