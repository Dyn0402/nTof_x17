In-situ efficiency and performance of the four Micromegas

What each chamber surface sees, with and without its own trigger — and what that says about chamber B.

PRELIMINARY

An unbiased hit map is possible, and it needs no track removal. The production trigger is a wall AND plastic coincidence in ONE arm, OR’d over the four, and 96.9% of the read-outs this analysis can tag have exactly one arm lit. So every chamber spends most of its life being read out on somebody else’s trigger — a bystander, whose occupancy carries no trace of its own scintillator acceptance. 6,928,053 such tracks over 36 runs, against 1,665,444 trigger-matched ones.

The construction is testable and the test was declared before it ran. The plastic bars leave a gap that shadows u ≈ +7 mm on every chamber; if that dip is the trigger’s, it must vanish when another arm does the triggering, except where the chamber is genuinely blind underneath it. It does exactly that. Chamber A, which has no dead readout channels, goes from a dip of 0.526 to -0.193 — past zero, because the beam illuminates the centre more and the trigger had been carving a hole in it. Chambers C and D, which have ~10 and ~130 dead channels under that window, keep theirs: 0.476 → 0.504 and 0.862 → 0.822.

The removal method — find the track that fired the trigger, drop it, map what is left — is built too, as leftover, and it is worth being precise about how far it agrees. Where the dip is real the two methods land on top of each other: C 0.517 against 0.504 (0.8σ), D 0.845 against 0.822 (1.3σ). On chamber A, where the trigger shadow is genuinely absent, they do not: -0.042 against -0.193, a 4.9σ difference, and the removal method is the one left closer to a shadow. That is the expected direction and it is the argument for the bystander construction rather than against it: a leftover track shares its event with the particle that fired the trigger, so it is still weighted by the trigger’s acceptance through whatever correlated it with that particle. The bystander sample has no such partner. The removal method is also 20× smaller, since a self-triggered event with a second reconstructed track is rare, so it is the cross-check and not the result.

What was compared

Every gated track in the campaign is put into exactly one of five samples, so the census is a partition and every fraction below has an honest denominator. The split is made from the n_TOF scintillator slim alone — which arm’s wall and plastic fired within the -100…60 ns accept window — and, for the two matched samples, from whether the track’s own extrapolation lands on the channel that fired.

What had to be masked, and why

Two reasons, and they are different statements about the detector. Fiducial: outside ±176 mm the plane fit rails at the edge of the strip map and piles up there, so a position outside it is not a position on the detector. Hot: a cell holding at least 25 tracks and more than 5× the median of the occupied cells in its 9-square neighbourhood. The mask is derived once from the occupancy summed over all mapped samples and applied to each alike, so it cannot manufacture a difference between the two samples being compared.

The asymmetry in that table is the price of the method, and it is worth stating plainly. Junk accumulates in the bystander sample by construction: a cluster manufactured by a noisy strip points at no scintillator, so it can never be matched, so it lands in bystander and never in triggered. On chamber D the hot cells hold 43.5% of the bystander sample against 15.1% of the matched one. The bystander map is unbiased in where it looks; it is not purity-selected, and nothing in it confirms an individual track.

Chamber B

B has no field-shaping ring chain. A, C and D ground their degrader rings through three ~1.3 GΩ resistors and it is that chain which draws their 0.180 µA; B draws zero because it has no divider to draw it, which says nothing about whether its cathode is at voltage — it should be. Without the rings the drift field fringes, so there is no clean time↔depth ladder and no angle. The amplification stage is untouched, so B still measures position and time.

The fingerprint that predicts: a fringing field spreads the same charge over more strips, so the signature is a wide, dilute cluster and not a weak one.

Median over all gated clusters — the basis chamber_b.py established on run_145, carried here to the whole campaign. B is the widest of the four and the most dilute by a clear margin, which is what a fringing drift field predicts and a weak amplification stage does not.

One number moves between run_145 and the campaign, and it is D’s. chamber_b.py measured D at 42 strips on run_145 (1.68× A), which is where STATUS.md’s “D is nearly as wide, so width alone does not isolate the fault” comes from. Pooled over 36 runs D is 35.1 strips (1.46×), level with C rather than with B. So on the campaign sample width does separate B, and the conclusion is unchanged and better supported — but the run_145 ordering should not be quoted as if it were the campaign’s. Chamber D is also the chamber whose cluster sample is most contaminated by hot channels, which is the first thing to check before reading anything into the difference.

What this does not rule out

  • These are occupancy maps, not efficiency maps. There is no independent denominator, so a cold cell is “less illuminated or less efficient” and nothing here says which. What the bystander construction removes is the chamber’s own scintillator acceptance; that is all it claims to remove. A scintillator-tagged efficiency, with an MM-independent denominator, is efficiency.py and lives only where the scintillators cover.
  • The bystander sample still shares a beam. It sits in an event some other arm triggered, so the illumination is the beam’s own and is not flat, and an opposing arm’s trigger selects a direction through the target which weights the surface gently. Neither is the chamber’s own trigger footprint.
  • Chamber D’s unbiased map does not work, and no choice of mask rescues it: its ratio of 99th-percentile to median cell occupancy is 207 at the loosest setting scanned and still 68 at the tightest, where the mask has taken 45 % of its sample. A quarter of D’s x plane is dead. That is a statement about D, not about the method.
  • Nothing here is an angle measurement and nothing here depends on one, which is the point. The angle scale remains preliminary (STATUS.md, 2026-09-10); the pointing match used for the triggered sample does use the reconstructed direction, so that sample — and only that sample — inherits the angle scale’s uncertainty. The bystander sample does not.
  • The masks are campaign-pooled, not per run. A channel that went hot part way through the campaign is masked throughout. Per-run masks were not built because a single run’s mask is Poisson noise at this binning.
