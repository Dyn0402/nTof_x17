The double bump in χ²/dof, and why only chamber A shows itPRELIMINARY

n_TOF 2026 campaign · 45,421 tracks over 36 runs · generated 2026-09-12 by chi2_shape.py

Two things are true, and the first one has to be said first.

1. The figure exaggerated the low bump by about 5× — now fixed. make_pair_qa_figures._hist divided each bin by np.diff(edges) — the bin’s linear width — while the edges are geometric and the axis is log. The plotted height was therefore the per-bin fraction divided by χ²/dof, which lifts the left of the axis and renders a small shoulder as a mode of comparable height. Drawn as a per-bin fraction, only 5.3% of A–A pairs sit below χ²/dof = 5. All five log-scaled panels of that set carried the same lift. Corrected 2026-09-12: _hist now returns the per-bin fraction, which is what its y-axis label always said, and the nine figures are regenerated. The correction also revealed something the lift had flattened — D–D’s χ² sits an order of magnitude above every other arm pair.

2. The A-specific excess underneath it is real. That same 5.3% is 1.5% on C–C and 0.8% on D–D. At the single-track level, where the pair variable’s worst-of-four does not hide it, 37% of chamber A’s tracks fit their waveforms to within the strips’ own noise, against 8% on C and 10% on D.

The mechanism, in one line. χ²/dof here is the mean squared residual per waveform sample in units of that strip’s own measured noise, so 1.0 is the noise floor and it means the same thing on every chamber. It climbs with track length on all of them — that mixture is the bimodality — but A’s short tracks are the only ones that start from the floor (1.5), which puts them 13× below its long ones and opens clear air between the two modes. On C and D the short tracks are already at 2.8 and 3.1, too high to resolve from the long-track continuum. The low bump is not missing on C and D — it is not separated.

What was compared

The question was asked of qa_chi2dof_worst, which is one row per pair and takes the worst of four track fits. That variable compounds four draws of the same single-track distribution, so it can show that A is different but never why. Everything below is at the track level, on the legs of that same published sample — gated, angle-calibrated, DCA < 30 mm, in a trigger that made two tracks — 45,421 of them.

Chamber B carries no angle calibration, so it is in no pairing and on none of these figures. It reaches the cross-check table below by another route, and it is the worst of the four there.

The definition this all turns on

wft.model.chi2_plane fits the forward model to the raw waveform window and dof = (~saturated).sum(). Measured here: dof / n_strips has median 20.000 and is exactly 20 on 99.2% of tracks — so dof counts samples, and χ²/dof is a mean squared residual per sample. wft.model.prep_plane takes the noise per strip from the event itself, so the unit is self-calibrating and the chambers are directly comparable. A chamber whose best tracks sit at 3 has a model that does not describe its data; it is not a units problem.

On a linear axis, at 0.1 resolution

A linear axis binned at a width of 1 put 36 % of chamber A in one bin — that bin was the peak, and the panel showed a spike with no shape. At 0.1 the shape is there, and it says something the coarse view could not: A’s peak sits at χ²/dof = 1.05 — on the noise floor, not merely near it.

The refinement this forces on the headline: all three chambers do have a peak at the floor. The difference is how much of the sample is in it and how tight it is — A’s is 3.6× taller than C’s and about half the width. "C and D never reach the floor" was too strong; "C and D put a fifth as many tracks there" is what the data says.

Where a chamber constant could live: the bundles

Calibration is per detector and per run condition. The four bundles differ structurally, and this is a matter of record rather than a result — nothing here ranks these differences or shows that any one of them causes the χ² offset. That needs a refit with one knob moved at a time.

Read this before reading the table. These are the bench bundles. The campaign ran calib_bundle_prelim, which ntof_tracking.wft_beam.make_bundle derives from them: it carries the impulse template and the kernel hypers through verbatim (sigma_p0 and Dp included), replaces v_drift with one shared Magboltz prior, and drops t0_abs and t0_prior_sigma for every arm alike — deliberately, because a bench t0 is an absolute arrival time against the bench trigger and is a wrong answer for an n_TOF-triggered run (RUN145_R06_2026-08-19.md §1, commit 11ce347). So the t0 prior is off on A, B, C and D in every product here and explains no chamber-to-chamber difference. What its absence costs is a separate question — HANDOFF_T0_PRIOR.md.

  • No chamber masks a single channel, at any stage. dead is empty on A, B and D and absent on C, and no bundle carries a hot map at all — so chamber D’s noisy cells, the 22.4 % of its sample that HANDOFF_D_NOISY_CHANNELS.md is about, are in every fit at full weight. The classifier and the bundle-patching tool both exist and were only ever run on run_145. See HANDOFF_CHANNEL_MASKS.md.
  • C is a bundle generation behind. calib_bundle_lp, with no share_mode, no dead key, a bare c2 instead of c2_over_c1, and — the part that matters, because make_bundle carries it through verbatim and calls it “the largest un-validated assumption in this chain” — sigma_p0 and Dp an order of magnitude below A’s, B’s and D’s.
  • A is the only one calibrated on a dedicated scan. Its run_key names the resistive and drift voltages; D’s conditions are empty strings.

What this does not rule out

  • Which bundle difference causes the offset. Four chambers is four points; the differences are correlated and cannot be separated by looking. The decisive test is a refit of one chamber with one knob moved — D with t0_prior_sigma = 5, C on an r06-generation bundle — which needs the waveform path, not these tables.
  • That A is “right” in any absolute sense. A reaching the noise floor says its model describes its waveforms; it says nothing about whether its angles or depths are accurate. A well-fitted wrong model is still wrong.
  • That χ²/dof is a useful cut. Nothing in this chain cuts on it, and the event-mixed null in the QA set sits under the data over most of the range. This investigation explains the shape; it does not argue for using it.
  • The charge columns. q_total and x_q_sum run to 1030 on a tail of tracks — the NNLS profile escaping into a near-null direction. They are excluded from the amplitude tables here and are their own bug, untouched by this work.
