DREAM pedestal stability over the n_TOF campaign

56 pedestal acquisitions · 8 FEUs · 4 096 channels · 01 July – 10 August 2026 · built by re-deriving every number from the decoded pedestal runs on EOS

The pedestals were stable to a few per cent for weeks at a time, and then moved in three ways worth knowing about.

One change was ours and global: on 23 July around 13:00 the DREAM readout clock divider went from 6 to 4, and the per-channel residual noise rose by ×2.0 on every one of the eight FEUs at once and stayed there for the last 18 days — which is the whole production period. The coherent common mode did not change (×1.07), so this is a real degradation of the incoherent noise floor, not a rescaling.

Two changes were chamber A's and physical. From 21 July its common mode tripled on all sixteen chips of both views, and from 22 July its x-view connector 8 — 64 strips — was electrically disconnected. Both were fixed together at an access on 27 July between 11:23 and 14:11. The dead connector covers every sub-run of run_79, the first long production run, and it is confirmed in the physics data: 41 of those 64 channels recorded literally zero hits.

Everything else is quiet. Baselines held, no chamber drifted, and the three other connector dropouts (all on connector 8, all in early July or on 9 August) recovered within days.

What a pedestal here actually measures

Each acquisition is a no-trigger readout of all eight FEUs — 512 channels × 32 samples × ~1 000 events each. Every channel is decomposed the same way, and the three numbers mean different things:

  • Raw σ — the spread of the raw ADC about the channel's own baseline. On these detectors it is dominated by coherent pickup, not by the amplifier: at the end of the run the raw σ is 8× the residual.
  • Common mode — per chip, per time sample, the median over its 64 channels of (amplitude − channel mean). Its RMS is the coherent swing that chip sees. This is the same definition the end-of-run report uses.
  • Residual σ — what is left of a channel once that common mode is subtracted. This is the number that sets the zero-suppression threshold (5 σ) and therefore what the experiment's sensitivity actually rests on.

Two checks that the decomposition is honest. The raw spread is not a fixed waveform shape repeated every event — the event-averaged shape carries under 0.1 % of the raw variance, so raw σ really is noise. And the residual agrees with the firmware's own per-channel σ (the one it used to program the thresholds) at a median r = 0.980 over all 448 FEU-acquisitions, with 81 % above 0.9 — recomputed independently from the samples, and from a different acquisition than the one the firmware used.

Where they disagree is instructive rather than alarming. The poorest agreement (r = 0.14, Ay on 21 Jul) is on chamber A's y view during its high-common-mode excursion: the louder the coherent swing, the noisier a median-over-64-channels estimate of it becomes compared with the firmware's. Our residual also sits 6 % above theirs in absolute terms for the same reason. Ratios and steps are the trustworthy part of this analysis, not the last digit of any single sigma.

23 July: the readout clock, and a doubled noise floor

Between the pedestal at 10:05 and the one at 16:47 on 23 July, the residual noise rose on all eight FEUs — by ×1.74 to ×2.46. Over the same boundary the common mode moved by at most ×1.25 and the baseline by under 20 ADC. A gain change would have scaled all three together; only the incoherent part moved, so the per-channel noise floor genuinely got worse.

Exactly one configuration parameter changed at that boundary. The DREAM readout clock divider RdClk_Div went 6 → 4 while the sampling clock WrClk_Div stayed at 6 — the ADC digitises 1.5× faster into the same 60 ns sampling period. Everything else the FEUs were programmed with is byte-identical across the step, including Feu_Pwr_Dream, the DREAM registers, the polarity masks, latency and the 32-sample window. The HV was also identical: 200 V on all eight channels, powered, for every pedestal quoted here.

Which runs are on which side. No run straddles the change, but one sits inside the bracket the pedestals leave. run_67 and earlier are on the quiet side (run_67 ends at 09:52, before the 10:05 pedestal); run_69 onward are on the noisy side (run_69 starts at 17:27, after the 16:47 one). run_68 ran 14:54–16:08, between the two, and the pedestals do not place it — it loaded the 10:04 pedestal, which suggests the old configuration, but that is an inference. Exclude it from either side, or check it directly.

The campaign ran three clock configurations, and it is the combination rather than the readout speed alone that matters: the first epoch also used RdClk_Div 4 and was quiet, but with WrClk_Div 2 beside it. That is an observation, not a mechanism — these pedestals cannot say why.

Chamber A: 21–27 July

On 21 July at 12:04 chamber A's common mode went from 31 to 103 ADC on the x view and 23 to 77 on the y — a factor 3.3, on all sixteen chips of both FEUs at once and on no other chamber. A fault on one cable moves one chip; sixteen chips moving together is a shared ground, shield or supply path at the chamber. The next pedestal, on 22 July at 10:13, shows x-view connector 8 gone as well.

Both were fixed at the same intervention. The pedestal at 27 July 11:23 still has them; the one at 14:11 has neither, and A is back to 34 / 25 ADC — its pre-21-July values to within a few per cent.

This lands on run_79. The window 22–27 July covers runs 67 to 82, all 16 sub-runs of run_79 among them — the first long statistics run at the production setpoint. Read directly off the hits of run_79/stat090_0001, connector 8 of A-x delivered 3.7 % of that FEU's hits against 11.3 % in run_100 after the fix, 41 of its 64 channels recorded no hits at all, and the hits that did survive sit in a narrow band at threshold (median amplitude 30, 90th percentile 34) rather than the wide spectrum of a live connector (median 192, 90th percentile 3 467). The pedestal diagnosis and the physics data agree.

Channel health

A disconnected strip is diagnosed on the raw σ, not the residual: losing the strip loses the pickup, and the raw noise collapses while the residual barely moves. A channel counts as disconnected when its raw σ is below 12 % of its FEU's median or below 8 ADC outright; loud means a residual more than 3× the FEU median. Both cuts are relative to the same acquisition, so the 23 July scale change does not masquerade as four thousand new dead channels.

Which pedestal was in force

All 2 695 sub-runs carry a pedestal_run.txt naming the pedestal directory whose memory files the DAQ loaded, so what was in force is a fact rather than an inference. Of the 56 acquisitions, 35 were ever loaded — the rest were superseded within the hour or taken as cross-checks.

The pedestal in force was a median 11.6 hours old, but the tail is long: 30 % of sub-runs ran on a pedestal more than a day old and the worst was 117 hours (4.9 days), in early August.

What this does not establish

  • Not that the 23 July change hurt the physics. A pedestal measures noise, never signal. If the same clock change also raised the signal amplitude, the signal-to-noise ratio could be unchanged or better. Settling that needs hit amplitudes across the boundary, which is a different measurement; the honest statement here is that the noise floor doubled and the threshold with it.
  • Not which physical object moved. A pedestal measures a FEU and its cable, not a chamber. "Chamber A's common mode tripled" means the electronics chain labelled A did; separating chamber, cabling and FEU needs the record of which board sat where.
  • Not a continuous history. These are 56 snapshots over six weeks, some hours apart and some days. Every window in the event log is bounded by the two pedestals either side of it, and something that started and finished between two of them leaves no trace here at all.
  • Not the state of chambers before 1 July. The first pedestal preserved in the campaign area is 1 July 19:52; data taking began on 28 June, and the 30 June commissioning set lives outside this tree.
  • Not a claim about the other three connector dropouts' effect on data. Only the run_79 case was checked against hits. The early-July ones (A-y and B-y) fall in the commissioning period and the 9 August one lasted at most three hours, but neither was confirmed in the recorded data the way run_79 was.

How to rebuild this

Three read-only extractions on lxplus, then everything else locally:

  • ntof_pedestal_qa/lxplus/extract_pedestals.py — decodes every pedestal ROOT under /eos/experiment/ntof/data/x17/july_beam/pedestals/ into per-channel mean, raw σ, residual σ and per-chip common mode, and parses the firmware's own _ped.aux / _thr.aux alongside for comparison.
  • lxplus/extract_usage.py — walks all 2 695 sub-runs for pedestal_run.txt.
  • lxplus/extract_context.py — the configuration and HV each pedestal was taken under, which is what makes a clock change distinguishable from a cable change.
  • figures.py then make_report.py rebuild the pictures and this page from data/ped_stats.npz.
