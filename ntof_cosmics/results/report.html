The beam-off cosmic runs: what we have

41 cosmic runs at the production operating point hold 57.6 h and 5.18 M scintillator-singles triggers, all decoded on EOS, all under the post-23-July noise condition, and all with the run_79 HV and readout — only the trigger differs. 35.2 h of it never saw an n_TOF pulse. Everywhere else the beam sits only at run edges (beam loss and beam return around the auto-substituted cosmic run), so an event-level veto around the pulse times leaves the whole sample usable. Beam inside the cosmic running: run_73. For 15.1 h, n_TOF was recording PS-timed triggers with no protons on target, which gives a random-trigger view of the scintillators during the cosmic running. Most of it is in run_149 and run_103.

Headline

  • Trigger: scintillator singles (per-sector wall×plastic), veto open, no PS gate. Rate 25.0 Hz at the production point, stable run to run. One arm is enough to trigger, so a particle through two arms is a subset still to be counted, not the sample.
  • run_80 still has chamber A x connector 8 dead (channels 448–511 of FEU 3, live again from run_83): mask it, or drop run_80 (1.2 h) from any A-x study.
  • Readout identical to run_79: no zero suppression, latency 27, 20 samples × 60 ns. HV resist A540/B540/C525/D520, drift 700 on all four.
  • Pre-production (run_54–74): 5.0 h at other HV and timing (latency 35, 32 samples), mostly resist ladders. Useful for gain studies, not as a background reference for the production data.
  • run_148 is empty.

Which runs to use

Background reference at the production point: the five longest, which make up 41.0 of the 57.6 h — run_149 (21.8 h), run_133 (6.0 h), run_89 (5.5 h), run_103 (4.8 h), run_134 (2.8 h). PLAN §S4 named run_83 and run_146; they are 0.2 h and 0.35 h and both have beam at an edge, so they are a poor choice.

With an n_TOF random-trigger companion: run_149 (8.64 h), run_103 (4.28 h), run_159 (0.73 h), run_111 (0.69 h), run_138 (0.42 h), run_153 (0.25 h), run_121 (0.08 h), run_125 (0.05 h). These are the only places a cosmic scintillator time (and hence arm-to-arm time of flight) could exist. That depends on two things not yet checked: that cosmic triggers land inside n_TOF acquisition windows, and that the DREAM↔n_TOF clock join can be carried across a run with no flash to lock on.

What this does not establish

  • Beam flags are minute-resolution. They come from the per-minute beam_class log (n_TOF-destined pulses only). A sub-run shorter than about 6 min has almost no "interior", so edge on a short sub-run is weak evidence. run_157's 22 pulses in 7 min (0.031 Hz residual beam, found by the flash-charge study) are exactly that case. An analysis must veto by pulse time, not by this flag.
  • Configured HV, not measured. The hv_monitor.csv per sub-run is on EOS and was not read; a trip would not show here.
  • "n_TOF quiet" is inferred from file size (bytes per bunch, a gap of two decades between beam and no-beam runs), confirmed against beam_state only for 224706. Whether n_TOF's windows contain any of our cosmic triggers is not checked.
  • No tracking has been run on any of this; nothing here says how many triggers carry a track, let alone a through-going one.
