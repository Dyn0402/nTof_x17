# The HV-slope test and what it dragged out — det3, 2026-08-09

Problem 1 of the T14 follow-up: T14 left the simulated peak amplitude at
×0.55–0.63 of data with an angle-independent floor, and the question was
whether that floor is **one constant** (a Garfield absolute-gain
miscalibration, which a single fitted gain factor could legitimately absorb) or
a **wrong response to field** (in which case a fitted factor hides broken
physics). The discriminator is the slope: gas gain is the only thing the mesh
voltage changes.

It is not one constant. Chasing that answer then turned up two more things —
that the isobutane axis cannot be closed by drift velocity, and that both T14
data legs are biased subsamples of the detector, which moves several per-view
fitting targets.

Everything here is waveform-level (`wft.io.FeuReader` on `decoded_root`); no
`combined_hits` time is read anywhere (RECONSTRUCTION_BASIS.md). Nothing in
`~/x17/response_sim/stageB_w2/t14_compare/` was regenerated — its tables were
read only.

---

## ⚠ Two things in this record were wrong first time and are withdrawn

1. **The T14 X/Y amplitude difference is not the θ window.** First reported
   here as a θ-window effect; it is not — peak amplitude is flat to ±3 %
   against the M3 reference angle from 0 to 8°, and both windows sit inside
   that flat region. The cause is the per-view reconstruction-quality cut
   (§4). The main report was corrected and links to the follow-up.
2. **There is no undershoot "cap".** The Y undershoot was reported as
   saturating at ≈ −290 ADC. That came from a coarse top bin that included
   **railed** events — whose clipped peak is not a valid denominator — sitting
   next to the 3000–3500 bin at a similar value and looking like a plateau.
   Rail excluded, fine bins: over a ×3.00 peak range the absolute undershoot
   grows ×3.01 (Y) and ×2.36 (X). No ceiling in either view (§5.3). The
   **target correction of §4 is unaffected** — it is a direct median
   measurement and never depended on the mechanism.

---

## 1. The slopes disagree — a single fitted gain factor is not defensible

Over 460–490 V, the widest window both ladders cover with the data median
unsaturated:

| | Δln per 10 V | gain doubles every |
|---|---|---|
| **det3 data** (median peak-strip waveform max) | **0.449 ± 0.009** (X), 0.438 ± 0.010 (Y) | 15.4 V |
| **Garfield dry Ar/iC₄H₁₀ 95/5** | **0.296 ± 0.008** | 23.5 V |

Ratio **1.52**, ≈ 12 σ. Propagated along the two slopes from the frozen T14
ratio (×0.565 at 490 V), the simulation would sit at ×0.89 of data at 460 V and
×0.36 at 520 V — a deficit that were one constant would be flat. Matching on
*gain* instead of voltage does not help: the sim reaches the data's 490 V gain
at ≈ 509 V, where its local slope is 0.320 per 10 V, still 1.40× short.

**Data leg.** The saturday scan's two interleaved mesh ladders (`hv_scan`
425–525 V, `hv_scan2` 460–520 V). Observable = the one T14 itself compares,
`wfm.max(axis=1)` on the raw 32 × 60 ns samples. Three properties make the
slope meaningful: **no amplitude threshold anywhere** (a threshold truncates
the low tail and fakes a shallower slope); a **voltage-independent population**
(M3 track, χ² < 1, NClus = 4, inside a fiducial fixed once from the ≥ 505 V
map); and **efficiency 1.000 at every voltage down to 425 V**, where the median
peak is still 22 σ above noise.

**Sim leg.** `aval_calib_meshfield_hvscan.json`, 8 dry 95/5 points 460–530 V,
each with its own T6 FEM field map — verified to be the per-voltage rerun, not
the quarantined single-map campaign.

### Threats, and why none of them rescue the sim

| threat | test | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Rail saturation | 5 estimators (p02/p10/p25/p50 peak + neighbour sum), everything above 0.70 × rail dropped | all agree ±5 %; the rail-immune ones sit *below* the median |
| Threshold turn-on | no threshold; efficiency measured | 1.000 everywhere — nothing to truncate |
| Population drift | M3 selection external to det3 | N per point 644–685 across the fitted range |
| Gas drift over the ~6 h scan | the two ladders were taken ~3 h apart and interleave | scan2 within 2–4 % of scan1's bracketing geometric mean |
| Drift field moving with the mesh | drift ladder at fixed 490 V mesh | signal survives at **100 V drift ⇒ the mesh is grounded**, so the drift field never moved; and at the 1000 V operating point the amplitude is on a plateau (−0.13 % per 10 V) |
| Sparks | spectrum shape vs voltage | pure rescaling: p50/p10 stays 1.81–1.98 over 425–490 V |
| Electronics/shaper | (parallel session) 44 saved `CosmicTb_MX17.cfg` copies, all peaking code 2 = 180 ns, `Dream 6/7 = 0xAAAA` (200 fC, 10 mV/fC) | electronics-scale hypothesis dead |
| Series resistance dropping part of V | arithmetic: any drop ∝ I ∝ V shrinks the true ΔV | makes the true data slope **steeper** — wrong direction |

### Read as physics

In the parallel-plate Townsend form α = A·p·exp(−B·p/E) with β = B·p·d/V, the
sim sits at β = 1.43 at 490 V; reproducing both the bench slope and the bench
gain needs β = 2.06, i.e. **B·p·d larger by 44 %**. No single knob does it:
raising B·p·d alone buys the slope but costs gain (×0.77 instead of ×1.06);
raising A alone moves gain and slope in the same ratio and cannot change their
ratio at all. Both at once takes ~1.44× in B·p and ~1.4× in A — a genuinely
different α(E), not a rescaling. Suspects: **gas table and Penning first,
geometry second**, with the caveat that a woven-mesh gap is not parallel-plate
at all, so a T6 map wrong in *shape* would mimic this and is not excluded by
the algebra.

## 2. HV current monitor — null, crossed off

A cosmic puts **1.9 pC** into the resist supply (≈ 300 primary e × gain 4 × 10⁴);
at ≈ 24 Hz through the active area that is **46 pA**, against a standing
**1.126 µA** with 49 nA RMS — 4 × 10⁻⁵ of the current and 10⁻³ of its noise.

Worse than merely flat: det3's May HV scan *does* have per-subrun
`hv_monitor.csv`, and imon there rises **×9.5 over 460 → 530 V**, far above
ohmic — but crediting that to avalanche needs a gas gain of 2 × 10⁸. It is
leakage / dark current and it would masquerade as a gain curve for anyone who
fitted it. The saturday scan wrote no per-subrun monitor; only the 490 V long
run has one, starting after the ladders finished.

## 3. The isobutane axis

**v(E) cannot constrain it.** The June forward-fit velocity is *gap-free*
(depth comes from the M3 track angle, not from filling a nominal gap), so the
drift gap enters only through E = V/gap — and det3's gap is 27.9 ± 1.0 mm
(dished 25.7–29.2), not the nominal 30. Once the gap is profiled — free over
24–32 mm, or tied to det3's own measured charge-column **time** of 757.5 ±
2.2 ns — **iso 3–10 % are indistinguishable** (Δχ² ≤ 7) and the ordering
*flips* between point sets (≥ 500 V mildly prefers 10 %, ≥ 700 V prefers 4–5 %).
Only ≥ 15 % is excluded, and only dry.

The June conclusion "iso 5 % clearly preferred" reproduces **only** under gap =
30.0 mm exactly, no v-scale freedom, ≥ 700 V only — where 5 % wins by Δχ² = 5.3
over 4 %, 7.8 over 7 % and 14.8 over 10 %. Change any one of the three and it
evaporates. Two things sharpen this: the water grid is 7× finer at 5 %
(30 mixtures against 4 elsewhere), so every variant is *biased toward* 5 % and
5 % still does not win; and tightening the per-point error 3 % → 1 % sharpens
the ≥ 500 / ≥ 700 contradiction rather than the answer.

**But isobutane is disfavoured anyway, by the gain ladder.** Richer isobutane
*lowers* gain steeply at fixed voltage, deepening the T14 deficit instead of
curing it. A Townsend fit to the two simulated gases says matching the bench's
gain wants **≈ 4.0 %** isobutane — *less* than 5 % — and that fraction leaves
the slope at 0.306 per 10 V against 0.311 at 5 %. Gain and slope pull in
opposite directions along the axis; no fraction satisfies both. (Extrapolation
between two gases; the S3 iso ladder supersedes it.)

**No June gas record exists — anywhere.** `run_config.json` carries one
free-text `"gas": "Ar/Iso 95/5"` operator label (20 of 22 bench runs) and no
flow, mixture, pressure or bottle field in the schema. The Bronkhorst MFC mixer
that sets and logs isobutane percentage (`nTof_x17_DAQ/gas_mixer_control/`) was
commissioned **2026-07-07**, ten days after the saturday scan; its log dir and
the argon bottle log both start then. No certificate or e-log in `nTof_x17`,
`nTof_x17_DAQ` or `MX17_Documentation`. So June isobutane has exactly the
humidity's status.

> **Hardware recommendation.** From 2026-07-07 onward isobutane percentage is a
> *set and logged* quantity. A bench mesh-voltage ladder now, at two known set
> mixtures, would measure d ln G/dV against isobutane **directly** instead of
> interpolating two simulated gases. A few hours of bench time, and it settles
> the axis.

## 4. Both T14 data legs are biased subsamples

**The detector's two views are identical.** On the 490 V long run, same
subrun, same reader, M3 fiducial, no reconstruction cut: X 2606.5 vs Y 2607.0
ADC, 0.02 % apart, and both saturate equally (0.326 / 0.327 of events ≥ 3500
ADC). T14's legs read 2532 / 2140 with rail fractions 0.260 / **0.110**.

Ruled out as causes: the **θ window** (amplitude is flat to ±3 % against the M3
reference angle 0–8°, and both windows sit inside that region); the
**2500-event cap** (0.996 of the full-sample median); **within-run gain drift**
(±3 %, no trend). What remains is the per-view reconstruction-quality cut,
which **removes railed events** — the forward fit fails on clipped waveforms,
three times more often in Y. The sim leg's own quality acceptance is 0.954 (X)
/ 0.989 (Y) and the sim barely saturates, so the bias is one-sided and does not
cancel: the corrected Y amplitude ratio is ≈ 0.433 rather than the published
0.528 (X barely moves, 0.549 vs 0.565).

**Both legs are fully accounted for by two selections.** Adding T14's θ window
to an M3-selected sample, then depleting the rail to T14's own surviving
fraction, reproduces all four shape observables in both views. The two cuts
drive *different* observables:

| observable | unselected | + θ window | + rail depletion | T14 leg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| X undershoot | −3.2 % | −3.5 % | −3.4 % | −3.4 % |
| **Y undershoot** | **−9.3 %** | **−9.8 %** | −11.6 % | −12.0 % |
| X FWHM [ns] | 366 | 524 | 522 | 544 |
| Y FWHM [ns] | 353 | 402 | 402 | 396 |
| X rise p50 [ns] | 191 | 274 | 286 | 285 |
| Y rise p50 [ns] | 199 | 247 | 267 | 262 |
| X frac rise < 240 ns | 0.714 | 0.406 | 0.372 | 0.344 |
| Y frac rise < 240 ns | 0.665 | 0.477 | 0.415 | 0.412 |

The θ window drives the **widths** (an inclined track spreads charge over more
strips so each strip sees a shorter pulse: FWHM falls 539 → 330 ns from |θ| < 1°
to 7–12°, and T14's windows are ±2.4° in X but ±4.9° in Y — twice as wide,
because the sim's Y reco angle spread is wider). The rail depletion drives the
**Y undershoot**.

### Which "Y anomalies" survive

| claim | verdict |
|---|---|
| Y undershoot −12.0 %, unreachable by any β ≥ 0 | **survives, strengthened** — target moves to −9.3 %, *further* from β = 0's −18.9 % |
| X undershoot −3.4 % | survives unchanged (−3.2 %) |
| Data X/Y undershoot asymmetry | survives, at ~2.9× rather than 3.5× |
| Y FWHM sim/data ×1.39 vs X ×1.19 | **does not survive as stated** — the denominators come from windows differing 2× in width; unselected the views are 366 vs 353 ns |
| X/Y peak-amplitude asymmetry | does not survive — 0.02 % |
| "the data has a fast-rise population" | **understated** — unselected frac < 240 ns is 0.71 / 0.67, not 0.34 / 0.41 |

### The NaN-rise population

9.0 % (X) / 3.0 % (Y) of the frozen legs have `rise_ns = NaN`. They are not
slow events — they are **near-empty** events: median peak 137 ADC (X) and
65 ADC (Y) against 2685 / 2187, none railed. Rise and FWHM are unaffected (a
NaN is skipped by the median — lost statistics, not bias), but peak amplitude
and undershoot are, and 9 % of the X leg at ~137 ADC is part of why it reads
2532 against the detector's 2606. The M3-selected sample has 0.2 %; the sim
legs have zero, so it never cancels in a ratio.

## 5. The deep Y undershoot

### 5.1 CNS is excluded — and no reprocess was needed

The saturday scan was written with `pedestal_subtraction: false` and
`common_noise_subtraction: false`, so `decoded_root` holds **raw** samples and
`FeuReader` applies pedestal + CNS itself, in software, on every read. CNS is a
switch on the read, not an mm_processor run — and because FeuReader computes
its pedestal from the raw stack *before* the CNS step, both legs share one
pedestal exactly. (The mm_processor gotcha that pedestal RMS is always post-CNS
does not apply; that path is unused here.) The local reader carrying the switch
is gated on a bit-identical check against `FeuReader` at CNS = on.

CNS off leaves Y where it was (−292 → −281 ADC in the top bin, ≤ 4 % anywhere)
and moves X not at all. The block common mode is **−4 to −7.5 ADC** in the
tail against the −292 being explained — two orders of magnitude short — and is
actually a small *positive* bump (+35 ADC) during the pulse. CNS is not idle:
it takes median channel noise 8.90 → 7.41 ADC (×1.20) on both FEUs. *(This run
does **not** show the large FEU 6/8 common mode seen elsewhere on the bench —
that memory is epoch-specific.)*

**Consequence:** the concern that the data legs are post-CNS while the
simulation has no CNS emulation is **quantitatively closed** — CNS moves the
data undershoot by ≤ 0.3 pp in Y and ≤ 0.05 in X.

### 5.2 It follows the LAYER, not the FEU — so it is detector-side

On det3 alone the deep view is also the FEU 8 view, so "resistive-side layer"
and "FEU 8 chain" are perfectly confounded. The 6-26 overnight run breaks that,
because it wires the same two layer types onto different FEUs:

| | X layer | undershoot | Y layer | undershoot | Y / X |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| det3 | FEU 7 | −90 | FEU 8 | −291 | 3.3× |
| det6 | FEU 3 | −86 | **FEU 4** | −436 | 5.1× |
| det7 | **FEU 6** | −39 | FEU 8 | −314 | 8.1× |

*(median undershoot in ADC, shared 3000–3500 ADC bin, railed excluded)*

Every Y layer is deeper than every X layer, across three detectors and three
different FEUs. The **deepest** plane is det6's Y on FEU 4 and the
**shallowest** is det7's X on FEU 6 — if this were the FEU 8 chain, det6's
FEU 4 would look like an X plane, and it does not. So the return current that
makes the resistive side undershoot several times deeper belongs in the
**response model**, not an electronics calibration.

Caveats: these legs use no M3 and no fiducial (a shape comparison at fixed
amplitude, so population matters far less, but sparks and junk are not vetoed),
and cross-detector magnitudes carry gas, gain and epoch differences. The
X-versus-Y contrast *inside* each detector is internally controlled.

### 5.3 It is amplitude-dependent, but there is no cap, and it is gain-independent

Railed events excluded, fine bins, det3 490 V M3-selected:

| peak [ADC] | 1065 | 1464 | 2015 | 2615 | 3196 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| X undershoot | −36 (−3.4 %) | −46 (−3.1 %) | −62 (−3.1 %) | −72 (−2.8 %) | −85 (−2.7 %) |
| Y undershoot | −96 (−8.9 %) | −171 (−11.6 %) | −246 (−12.5 %) | −272 (−10.4 %) | −288 (−8.9 %) |

Over the ×3.00 peak range the absolute undershoot grows ×2.36 (X) and ×3.01
(Y) — **no ceiling**. The Y *fraction* is non-monotonic, peaking near 2000 ADC;
X falls monotonically. That amplitude dependence is what makes the rail
depletion move the Y median: removing the high-amplitude events lands on the
shallow side of the curve in both views, but Y's fraction is ~3.5× larger and
Y lost twice as many events.

At **fixed amplitude** the undershoot is essentially gain-independent — Y's
2300–3000 bin reads −300 / −282 / −272 / −268 ADC at 460 / 475 / 490 / 500 V,
≤ 12 % over a 3.4× gain change and in the *opposite* direction to gain.

The **drift ladder does not** break the amplitude/charge degeneracy, contrary
to the plan: the low-drift amplitude loss comes with an unchanged peak-strip
FWHM (335 → 349 ns from drift 300 → 1100 V) and *narrower* clusters (5 → 10
strips over 5 σ), i.e. genuine per-strip charge loss, so amplitude and charge
stay proportional there too.

Block secondary, weak: within det3 at 490 V and peak ≥ 2300 ADC the undershoot
by 64-channel block runs −259 to −328 ADC (Y) and −66 to −100 (X), no block
standing out — but 18–39 events per block and 6 of 8 populated, so
connector-level structure at the ±20 % level is not excluded.

## 6. What to use downstream

- **Do not fit a single gain factor** to absorb the T14 amplitude deficit. It
  would be valid at exactly one voltage and would bury a wrong field response.
- **Per-view data targets: take the θ window, drop the rail depletion.** The θ
  window is a legitimate population match that both legs need; the rail
  depletion is a one-sided defect (the sim saturates 3.8 % in Y against the
  detector's 33 %, so the same cut has nothing to remove on the sim side).
  Like-for-like: undershoot X −3.5 % / Y −9.8 %; FWHM X 524 / Y 402 ns; rise
  p50 X 274 / Y 247 ns; frac rise < 240 ns X 0.406 / Y 0.477.
- **Any β fitted to an undershoot median must carry the amplitude distribution
  it was fitted on.** The fraction varies ~30 % across the amplitude range and
  non-monotonically in Y, so a single median inherits whatever amplitude mix
  its selection happened to leave. (This is the weaker, correct form of the
  caveat — see the withdrawal at the top.)
- **The resistive-side return current is a response-model item.** It is 3–8×
  deeper than the X side on every detector measured and it is not electronics.

## 7. Open — what this does not settle

- **Which sim input is wrong.** The slope says the field response is wrong; it
  does not separate gap, gas density, Penning transfer, α(E) or the FEM field
  map shape.
- **The absolute gain.** This is a shape test throughout.
- **The angle-dependent part of the deficit** (×0.55 → ×0.38 with inclination)
  is a separate problem, untouched here.
- **The gas.** No humidity and no isobutane fraction was ever measured; both
  remain free.
- **The voltage axis.** vmon reads 490.24 V for a 490 V setting, but the drop
  across any filter resistance between supply and resistive layer was not
  measured. It steepens the true data slope, so it cannot rescue the sim, but
  the absolute axis carries an unquantified offset.
- **What in the resistive layer produces the deep return current.** Localised,
  not explained.
- **Scope.** One detector, one day for the slope; one subrun at 490 V for the
  shape work; the amplitude dependence comes from the natural Landau spread,
  not a gain scan.

## Scripts and outputs

Products live under `~/x17/response_sim/hv_slope/` (outside the repo, per the
usual split — generators here, outputs there). Each directory carries a
`report.html` for the DAQ Analysis tab.

| script | what it does | output |
|---|---|---|
| `extract.py` | threshold-free peak-strip amplitude per event per view, M3-selected; mesh ladder, drift ladder, or one named subrun | `peaks*.parquet` |
| `analyse.py` | quantile ladders, bootstrap slopes, sim ladders, drift control | `mesh_ladder.csv`, `drift_ladder.csv`, `slopes.json` |
| `make_report.py` | §1–2 figures + report | `report.html` |
| `iso_ve.py` | measured v(E) against every Ar/iso Magboltz grid, three gap treatments | `iso_ve/iso_ve.json` |
| `make_iso_report.py` | §3–4 figures + report | `iso_ve/report.html` |
| `xy_shape.py` | per-view shape observables on unbiased samples, selection decomposition | `xy_shape/xy_shape.json` |
| `make_xy_report.py` | §4–5 figures + report | `xy_shape/report.html` |
| `cns_undershoot.py` | CNS on/off A/B (`--verify` gates the local reader against `FeuReader`) | `cns/cns_undershoot.json` |
| `cap_scan.py` | undershoot vs amplitude across the mesh and drift ladders | `cns/cap_scan.json` |
| `cap_crosscheck.py` | FEU-versus-layer, six planes on three detectors | `cns/crosscheck.json` |

python3 mx17_sim_wft/hv_slope/extract.py --out ~/x17/response_sim/hv_slope/peaks.parquet
