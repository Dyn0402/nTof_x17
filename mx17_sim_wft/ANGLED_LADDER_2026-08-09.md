# T14 angled ladder — sim vs data as a function of track inclination

**2026-08-09. DIAGNOSIS, not the verdict.** These points carry the corrected
noise spec (`noise_det3_satscan_feu07.json`) and were produced after the frozen
default closed. The frozen one-shot verdict lives in
`~/x17/response_sim/stageB_w2/t14_compare/` and is untouched by any of this.

Report: `~/x17/response_sim/stageB_w2/t14_ang_trend/report.html`
(+ `trend.csv`, `angle_closure.csv`, 4 figures), generated by
`t14_angle_trend.py`.

## Why the ladder exists

The frozen default answered "does the simulation reproduce the data at one
angle" — no in absolute amplitude (×0.53–0.64), roughly yes in shape. It is a
**vertical** point, and a vertical point is structurally blind to anything that
scales with `tan θ`. The ladder asks the question the forward-reco
parameterization actually needs: **does the agreement hold as the track
inclines?**

Five Stage A points, 3k muons each, `MX17_Geant` 1b7d42b, all at rho2M W2 with
the corrected noise: `th00`, `thx10`, `thx20`, `thy10`, `thy20`.

## 1. The gun truth, and the sign convention

Stage A's gun is set in the **Geant** frame as
`SetParticleMomentumDirection(tan θx, tan θy, 1)`. Per-event straight-line fits
to the drift-region `ClusterTree` hits reproduce that to 5 decimals with a
zero-width IQR — a pencil beam in angle, so every point has an exact truth.

Every Stage A file's `Meta` tree carries the detector-local mapping
`origin = (0, 0, 17.64405) mm`, `sign = (+1, +1, −1)`.

**`sign_y = +1` — the y axis is NOT flipped.** The handedness is entirely in
`sign_z = −1`, and because an angle is `dx/dz`, mirroring z flips the sign of
**both** views' angles alike. So the detector-local truth is negative:
`thx10 → −10°`, `thy20 → −20°`.

**wft does not return that.** Measured medians against a local truth of −10/−20:

| point | view | truth (local) | wft reco | IQR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| thx10 | X | −10.0° | **+10.743°** | 0.98° |
| thx20 | X | −20.0° | **+19.999°** | 5.25° |
| thy10 | Y | −10.0° | **+10.551°** | 0.97° |
| thy20 | Y | −20.0° | **+20.677°** | 2.37° |

wft's depth axis runs opposite to `Meta.sign_z` and the two cancel, so wft
reproduces the **Geant-frame** sign. A convention mapping, not a physics error
— but **any signed angle quoted from wft must state which frame it is in.**

Null control: the untilted view reads −0.018° … +0.003° at every point, so
nothing leaks across views.

> ⚠️ **Vertical Y angles are near-degenerate.** th00's Y IQR is 3.10° against
> X's 0.85°, and tilting to 10° collapses it to 0.97°. At 0° the Y slope has no
> lever arm. Do not condition a vertical analysis on reconstructed Y angle.

## 2. The drift-velocity systematic, measured rather than quoted

The sim drifts at the dry Saclay table's **39.1407 µm/ns** (Stage B provenance
`digitizer.gas.v_um_per_ns`, `v_scale = 1.0`); wft reconstructs with the
bundle's **36.60** — the pre-registered 6.94 % systematic. Because θ comes from
strip position against arrival time, a v mismatch scales `tan θ` directly.

**It is a pure multiplicative tan-scale, and this was verified, not assumed.**
Re-reconstructing with `bundle_variant_vtable` (v = 39.1424, matched to
0.004 %, self-labelled `run_key: sat_det3_VTABLE_VARIANT`):

| point | gun | default reco | predicted residual | matched-v reco | **measured** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| thy10 | 10° | 10.551° | −1.22 % | 9.881° | **−1.22 %** |
| thy20 | 20° | 20.677° | −3.04 % | 19.439° | **−3.03 %** |

Agreement to 0.01 pp at both angles. Dividing the v term out is therefore a
legitimate operation. (The variant bundle keeps hyper-parameters fitted at the
default velocity; that was flagged as a possible confound and turned out not to
matter at this level.)

> ⚠️ **`thx20` X reconstructs 19.999° for a 20.000° gun.** This looks like
> perfect validation and is **two large errors cancelling**: +6.94 % velocity
> against −6.50 % reco bias. Without the matched-v control it reads as evidence
> there is nothing to fix.

## 3. The residual reco bias is VIEW-ANTISYMMETRIC

After the v term is removed, a genuine reco bias remains — and it is **not the
same in the two views**:

| view | 10° | 20° | estimator spread at 10° |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | **+0.59 … +0.62 %** | −4.40 … −6.50 % | 0.03 pp |
| Y | **−1.24 … −1.22 %** | −2.28 … −3.04 % | 0.02 pp |

Opposite signs at the **same** inclination, both tight against estimator
choice. That rules out any generic geometric or estimator effect, which would
act identically on both views. The 20° points are the noisy end (IQR 2.4–5.2°,
16 % of thx20 X events outside a ±50 % window) — quote them as ranges.

This joins two other per-view anomalies — the bundle's `kY = 1.375`, and a Y
undershoot unreachable by any β ≥ 0 (−18.9 % at β = 0 vs data −12.0 %, from the
β scan). Three independent observables on the same axis.

### Mechanisms tested and excluded

All four were killed or confirmed **by measurement**, not by argument:

| mechanism | verdict | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Drift-velocity scale | **Confirmed, removed** | matched-v closes to 0.01 pp at both angles |
| Window clipping | **Dead** | `pad_strips` 3→10 widens the window 20→34 strips (vs a ~14-strip footprint) and moves the residual 0.23 pp. The seed window already grows with inclination: Y seeds/event 13.1 → 15.1 → 20.2 for 0/10/20°, roughly in quadrature with the footprint |
| Regression dilution | **Dead** | Spearman(bias, fit residual) = −0.086 / −0.115, flat across residual quartiles. Structurally inapplicable anyway: `fit_plane` fits **waveform samples** over `(p0, w, t0)` — wft never regresses depth on position, so there is no errors-in-variables term |
| Cluster fragmentation | **Dead** | `n_dropped` does grow strongly with angle (Y 11.7 % → 26.6 %, X flat ~10 %), but within-point it runs **backwards** at thy10: binning by `n_dropped`, tan-scale *rises* 4.57 → 6.75 %. More dropping, *less* attenuation |

The fragmentation result is worth keeping as a **measured non-effect** —
"inclined tracks fragment and lose their extremes" is the obvious thing for the
next person to assume, and it is wrong.

## 4. Amplitude: the deficit WORSENS with inclination

Tilted view, fixed ±3° window:

| gun | X peak | Y peak | X q | Y q | X rise ratio | Y rise ratio | X shapeRMS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0° | **0.553** | **0.570** | 0.612 | 0.675 | ×1.14 | ×1.04 | 0.124 |
| 10° | **0.447** | **0.441** | 0.516 | 0.511 | ×1.43 | ×1.37 | 0.156 |
| 20° | **0.378** | **0.375** | 0.461 | 0.456 | ×1.43 | ×1.42 | 0.179 |

**Monotonic, ~32 % relative worsening from vertical to 20°, and the two views
agree closely** — so amplitude degradation is view-**symmetric**, unlike the
angle bias. These are two distinct effects and must not be conflated.

**The frozen ×0.55 verdict is the BEST case, not the typical one.** Most of the
cosmic spectrum is inclined. A gain correction fitted at vertical will not
transfer.

Two artifacts were checked first and **both exclude themselves**:

- **Data saturation.** Flat with angle in X (0.263 → 0.241 → 0.241) and
  *rising* in Y (0.040 → 0.156 → 0.147). More clipping under-estimates the data
  peak and pushes the ratio **up**, so in Y saturation *opposes* the observed
  fall — the true degradation is if anything stronger than measured.
- **Transverse over-spreading.** Peak ≈ charge/width, and the sim runs a
  near-constant 4–5 strips wider at every angle. As data's own width grows,
  `w_data/w_sim` *rises* (6/11 → 10/14 → 15/19), which would push the ratio
  **up**. It falls, and event charge falls independently, so the charge term
  falls faster still.

**Rise mismatch rises then plateaus** (×1.14 → ×1.43 → ×1.43). The data's
sub-200 ns fraction explodes with angle (0.23 → 0.82 → 0.99) while the sim
barely responds (0.020 → 0.048 → 0.066) — the ion term setting the sim's floor.

**Transverse over-spreading is a constant offset** (sim 4–5 strips wider at
every angle). Unlike the amplitude, that one **is** a candidate for an
angle-independent correction.

### The ion mechanism: confirmed at low angle, open beyond 10°

Predicted: an inclined track broadens each strip's current, and an
already-slow response (ion-dominated, ~90 % of induced charge, t50 ≈ 172 ns)
converts that into peak loss. Tested with `--no-ions` at 10° and 20° on the
same angled cluster sets (condor 3919436):

| X peak ratio | 0° | 10° | 20° | slope 0→10 | slope 10→20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| with ions | 0.553 | 0.447 | 0.378 | −0.106 | −0.069 |
| ions removed | 0.632 | 0.601 | 0.535 | **−0.031** | **−0.066** |

**71 % of the 0→10° slope is ion-driven — confirmed. Only 4 % of the 10→20°
step is — unexplained.** Whatever drives amplitude loss beyond 10° is not the
ion term, not saturation, not over-spreading, and not gain alone (gain is
angle-independent by construction). The 20° end is also the statistically
weakest (814 angle-matched data events in X, 435 in Y), so part of the step
could be sampling. Resolving it needs more 20° statistics or an intermediate
15° point.

## 5. ⚠️ Methodology trap: the p5/p95 angle window stops matching

`t14_compare` originally derived its data cut from the sim's reco p5/p95. That
tracks the sim's own spread, which is right while the spread is narrow — and
**breaks down completely at a strongly inclined point**. At thx20 X the reco
tails span **61°**, so the "angle-matched" cut admitted near-vertical cosmics,
whose charge is spread over fewer strips and whose peak amplitude is therefore
*higher*, biasing every ratio.

**Tell-tale: data strip multiplicity read 9** — squarely the *vertical* range
(th00 shows 6) — against the sim's 19 at the same nominal 20°.

Fixed by `--theta-halfwidth` (median-centred fixed width). The whole ladder was
re-run at ±3° into `t14_angW_*`; `t14_angle_trend.py --prefix` selects which set
to read, defaulting to the fixed-window one. Effect at thx20 X:

| | p5/p95 (61° wide) | fixed ±3° |
|---|---|---|
| n_data | 2500 | **814** |
| strips sim/**data** | 19 / **9** | 19 / **15** |
| peak ratio | 0.336 | 0.378 |
| rise ratio | ×1.23 | ×1.43 |

Gun-centring the window was considered and rejected *at the time* because it
would have assumed the very sign convention the ladder existed to measure; once
the convention was measured, a median-centred fixed width became safe.

## 6. Other traps worth carrying forward

- **A `decoded_root` symlink is not a complete data-leg mirror.**
  `t14_compare` needs the **data** leg's `sim_hits/…_seedstats.json`. A bare
  symlink passes T13 and then dies at the T14 stage. This follows from the
  dual-use seeding design: both legs go through `t13_reco`, so both legs must
  carry its outputs.
- **The `noise` branch is a re-characterisation and is inflated on BOTH legs.**
  Measuring like-for-like instead (per-channel sample std on quiet channels,
  identical code both legs): sim **7.05** vs data **6.59**, a ×1.07 mismatch —
  not the ×1.19 the branch implies nor the ×1.5 the pre-correction report
  quoted. Compare injected spec against data spec, never re-characterisations.
- **Stage B/C logs printed `still missing: ZS, noise`** immediately before
  writing files that *do* contain noise. Fixed in `567abf1` (MX17_Geant); logs
  predating it lie.

# ⚠️ CORRECTION, 2026-08-10 (overnight session) — §4's "the sim barely
# responds" is a threshold artifact, and the ladder's verdict is now stated

Two things were wrong or missing above, and both change the conclusion the
ladder was built to reach. Full record and the reproducing scripts:
`MX17_Geant design/report/OVERNIGHT_2026-08-10.md`,
`MX17_Geant scratchpad/overnight_2026-08-10/`.

## 1. WITHDRAWN: "the sim barely responds" to inclination

§4 states, and the trend report repeats, that *"the data's sub-200 ns fraction
explodes with angle (0.23 → 0.82 → 0.99) while the sim barely responds (0.020 →
0.048 → 0.066) — the ion term setting the sim's floor."*

**The counting is right and the inference is wrong. 200 ns sits below the sim's
own rise floor**, so a counter placed there registers almost nothing while the
sim's whole distribution slides past it. At the 240 ns threshold the rest of
the campaign uses, X view:

| \|θ\| | sim f<240ns | data f<240ns | sim f<200ns | data f<200ns |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0° | 0.045 | 0.378 | 0.020 | 0.226 |
| 10° | **0.269** | 0.963 | 0.048 | 0.820 |
| 20° | **0.749** | 0.995 | 0.066 | 0.988 |

The sim goes 0.045 → 0.749. It responds strongly.

## 2. The missing verdict: a RIGID ~70 ns offset, not a missing population

The threshold-free statement. Sim − data rise time [ns] at matched quantiles of
each angle-matched pair (`rise_offset_vs_angle.json`):

| view | \|θ\| | p5 | p10 | p25 | p50 | p75 | p90 | span |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| X | 0° | 90.5 | 94.1 | 81.8 | 40.0 | −33.1 | −31.8 | 127 |
| X | 10° | 76.8 | 82.4 | 78.3 | 75.7 | 71.1 | 66.6 | **16** |
| X | 20° | 79.2 | 79.9 | 72.6 | 69.3 | 69.0 | 72.0 | **11** |
| Y | 0° | 64.2 | 74.5 | 72.6 | 12.5 | −108.6 | −88.2 | 183 |
| Y | 10° | 53.2 | 66.4 | 68.6 | 67.2 | 62.3 | 52.7 | **16** |
| Y | 20° | 37.8 | 55.0 | 66.5 | 68.1 | 69.3 | 69.9 | **32** |

At both inclined points the offset is flat across the whole distribution and
the two legs have the same width (at 20° the sim's p5–p90 span is 63 ns against
the data's 71 ns). The sim's angular response at the fast end matches the
data's to within 17 % (p25 speed-up ratio 1.17 X, 1.16 Y).

**Verdict: track inclination is NOT the missing mechanism.** There is no absent
fast population to generate — there is a constant additive delay of ~65–80 ns
on every pulse, which inclination cannot remove because inclination is already
being converted into rise time at roughly the right rate.

This **strengthens** the S3 ion closeout instead of competing with it. A rigid
uniform displacement is the signature of a fixed extra time constant, and the
f_ion dial is the one measured to move rise by 96 ns while moving nothing else.
The demand is not "add fast pulses", it is "take ~70 ns off all of them" —
i.e. f_eff ≈ 0.16. The last live candidate for the rise contradiction closes
negative.

## 3. The vertical point is different, and it is NOT open thread #4

At 0° alone the offset is not rigid (span 127 ns X, 183 ns Y): the data's
vertical leg is broader than the sim's in *both* tails, faster at p5 and slower
at p90.

Open thread #4 proposed the cause — `_sel_ids` windows the data on one view's θ
only, so the "vertical" leg admits cosmics steeply inclined in the *other*
view, while the sim gun is a pencil beam vertical in both. **Tested, and the
answer is no.** Intersecting the vertical leg with the June wft analysis of the
same run (`…/mx17_3/wft/events.parquet`, which carries per-view θ) and
additionally requiring |θ_other| < 3°:

| view | leg | n | offset span |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | published | 2324 | 127.2 ns |
| X | overlap only | 692 | 129.2 ns |
| X | overlap + \|θ_y\|<3° | 171 | **126.2 ns** |
| Y | published | 1729 | 183.1 ns |
| Y | overlap only | 645 | 185.4 ns |
| Y | overlap + \|θ_x\|<3° | 145 | **187.3 ns** |

The cut removes 75 % of the events and moves the span by 1 ns in X and −4 ns in
Y. **The vertical broadening is real, not a one-view-window artifact**, and
open thread #4 is answered negative. The control matters here: the comparison
is overlap-with-cut against overlap-without-cut, both drawn from the same
events, so the subsample is not doing the work.

What remains is a genuine statement: at normal incidence the data's rise
distribution is much wider than the sim's at both ends, and the sim's
pencil-beam monoenergetic gun does not reproduce the topological diversity of
real vertical cosmics. (The data's χ²/dof at vertical — 83.5 X, 149.4 Y against
the sim's 19.3/20.4 — says the same thing from a different direction.) The
clean measurement of the rise offset is therefore the **inclined** points,
where both legs compress to similar-width distributions.
