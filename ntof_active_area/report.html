MX17 detector active areas, measured on n_TOF beam data

2026-08-11 · run_79 (27 files, 215,481 DREAM events) · n_TOF 224572 · ntof_active_area/

Verdict

The chambers are bigger than the simulation assumes, in both directions. The Geant SimConfig.hh carries mm_size_u_cm = 38.0, mm_size_v_cm = 34.0. The beam data says u = 39.9 cm (the full metallised strip region, no passivation) and v = 36.0 cm (a ~19 mm dead band at each end of the beam axis), for +11% active area. Both numbers agree with the June cosmic-bench telescope measurement already recorded in common/mx17_active_area.py, which was taken with an external reference and is the better of the two; this analysis is the independent confirmation in the n_TOF configuration.

The active band is centred: its midpoint sits at 199.1 mm against a strip-plane centre of 199.3 mm, so only the size changes, not the placement.

The scintillators are a different matter. Their sizes in SimConfig.hh come from tape-and-STEP surveys (2026-07-15/17/18) and are accurate to millimetres. Chamber pointing has a blur of σ = 47 mm at the plastic plane, so it cannot improve on them and this analysis does not try to. What it does do is confirm the placement: the plastic L/R boundary lands at -6.8 ± 5.3 mm where the geometry puts it at 0.

1. What was measured, and why this observable

The active area is where a particle crossing the chamber produces a signal. In the beam the illumination is smooth — the source is the He-3 target 235 mm away plus the whole neutron flight path, so nothing about the illumination changes over a few millimetres. A physical edge does: it is a step. So the measurement is a search for steps.

The observable is a paired track: exactly one particle-like cluster on each plane of the same chamber in the same event, with the two planes' charges balanced (0.6–1.6). That balance requirement is what makes this work — an MX17 avalanche splits about 50/50 between the two strip planes, so demanding it rejects the uncorrelated per-plane noise that otherwise swamps the raw occupancy exactly where the edges are. On three of the four chambers a raw y-plane occupancy is higher beyond 380 mm — outside the chamber — than in the chamber's own interior (B 3.6×, C 2.1×, D 15×). Paired tracks go to zero at 379 on all four.

No hit times are used anywhere here, so this stays on the right side of RECONSTRUCTION_BASIS.md: strip identity and charge are detection and QA quantities, which is exactly what an occupancy edge is.

Coordinates: the x plane is u, the chamber's tangential coordinate; the y plane is v, along the beam. Both are 512 strips at 0.78 mm spanning 0–398.58 mm centre to centre.

v (along the beam): a ~19 mm dead band at each end

Three chambers give a determined edge at both ends, and they agree with each other and with June: A 19.5–376.7; B 18.7–379.1; C 20.3–379.1 mm. Mean active span over the beam and June measurements together: 359.9 ± 1.8 mm. The two methods define the edge differently — June is a 50 % efficiency point against an external track, this is the outermost strip that ever takes part in a track — so agreement at the 1–2 mm level is as close as they can come.

u (tangential): full width, no passivation

Chamber B is live at strip 0 and at strip 511, i.e. across the whole metallised region; A reaches strip 2 at the low end. Nothing anywhere in the data shows a passivation band on this axis, which is what June found too. The u active size is therefore the strip region itself: 398.58 mm centre to centre, 399.36 mm of metal, 39.9 cm.

3. Readout health in run_79 — not chamber geometry

Dead in this run: Ax connector 8 (strips 448–511, 349–399 mm). Chamber A's X-plane connector 8 was fully alive on 18 July (run_55) and is dead in run_79, so this is a cabling or front-end fault during the campaign, not a property of the chamber. Anyone simulating run_79 specifically needs it; anyone simulating the chamber must not.

Chamber D's u plane is largely dark in this run and its edges are reported as undetermined rather than fitted. Chamber C carries a genuine interior dead stripe near u = 190 mm. These are all readout/detector pathologies on top of the geometric active area, and the table above keeps them separate from it.

4. Scintillators

Using the merged n_TOF ↔ DREAM sample for arm A (6,839 tracks with a full waveform-first fit), a track can be extrapolated to each scintillator plane and asked whether that detector tagged the event. Two things limit this and both are fitted rather than assumed: the pointing blur, and the flat pedestal of accidental tags from the other arms (the DREAM trigger is an OR over all four).

What this does establish

  • The plastic pair is centred on the chamber. The L/R boundary is at u = -6.8 ± 5.3 mm; the surveyed geometry — two 200 mm bars abutting on the pinwheel-shifted chamber centre line — puts it at -0.0 mm. This is the sharpest statement available because it is a boundary between two live detectors, not an edge, so no acceptance falls off across it.
  • The wall segment ordering is right, r = +0.972 across the four n_TOF channels, confirming the descending mapping. The slope of 0.53 against geometry is accidental-tag dilution, not a wrong segment pitch — it matches the pedestal the acceptance fits find independently.
  • The pointing blur is σ = 47 ± 6 mm at the plastic plane. That is a useful number in its own right for anyone planning to use chamber pointing.

6. What this does not rule out

  • The v edge is measured on strip liveness, not on efficiency. A region that is live but at reduced efficiency would not show up here as an edge. June's telescope measurement is the one that carries efficiency information, and it puts the 50 % points within 1–2 mm of these, so the two together bound this well — but neither says the efficiency inside is uniform.
  • Chamber D is not measured. Its u plane is mostly dark in run_79 and its v edges are undetermined. The recommendation above rests on A, B and C from the beam plus all four from June.
  • Only two sub-runs, one run. 27 files of run_79. Nothing here tests whether the passivation band is the same at other times, though there is no mechanism by which it would move.
  • Whether the drift volume is larger than the readout. This measures where charge is collected. Ionisation outside that region still happens and still draws current; if the sim needs the gas volume rather than the sensitive area, this is a lower bound.
  • The scintillator sizes are not confirmed, only their placement. A 10 cm error in the wall length would sit comfortably inside these error bars.
