Why two papers

  • The claims are now distinct. Paper II's original headline was "resistive strips make hit times fail". The P0 study (2026-11-05) retired that: a metallic readout in the same gas keeps most of the bias (X compression 1.17–1.22 against 1.20–1.28 for the full model), and resistive spreading adds only on Y.
  • IIa claims that a waveform forward fit gives about 1° on five chambers, and shows how to calibrate it.
  • IIb claims that any single time per strip biases the angle as C/N² + b, measures and decomposes C (7.0 on X, 11.7 on Y), and shows that the published 15–30 % capacitive coupling is neither needed nor seen.
  • The readers differ. IIa is for people who use or copy the reconstruction. IIb is for anyone running a strip µTPC with a hit readout.
  • Length. IIa is planned at about 10 pages for NIM A and the IIb draft is 24 pages. Merged, the general result ends up at the back of a detector-specific methods paper.
  • Maturity. Paper I cites the companion for every result, and that dependence is entirely on IIa. IIb still has open items (listed below). Merged, those would hold up Paper I's companion.

The main argument for one paper is the duplicate-headline risk that papers/JOURNAL_STRATEGY.md already rules out. Two papers handle it by giving each number one owner.
