\title{Reconstructing micro-TPC tracks on resistive strips with a waveform forward model}

\begin{abstract}
In the micro-TPC mode of a Micromegas the angle of a track segment is usually taken from a line through per-strip hit
times, which is biased because every strip collects charge from several drift depths. We describe a reconstruction
that fits a forward model of the sampled strip waveforms instead: a straight track, a free charge profile along the
drift, and three lateral terms (transverse diffusion, the prompt footprint, and the spreading of the charge along the
resistive strips), with no fitted coupling between strips. Every constant is measured on the chamber's own data, per
chamber and per run condition. On five resistive-strip chambers with a 30\,mm drift gap, tested with cosmic muons against
an external tracker, the angle resolution is 0.9 to 1.1\textdegree{} on the two uniform chambers, where the hit line
gives 2.0 to 2.6\textdegree{} with its scale calibrated on the reference; at a known angle of 25.4\textdegree{} in the
SPS H4 beam the fit reads $0.98\pm0.03$ of the stage tangent. The same model fitted to hit lists (one time and one
amplitude per strip) reaches 1.2 to 1.3\textdegree{} on the bench and 1.5 to 2.0\textdegree{} in the beam. The
resolution is three times the limit set by diffusion; the difference is characterised but not explained. Why single
hit times fail, and what a hit-based readout must record, are the subject of a companion paper.
\end{abstract}
