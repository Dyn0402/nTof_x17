\section{How far from the physics floor}\label{sec:floor}
\sectionstate{partial}{\texttt{rerun/model\_audit/ws\_floor} (F3, 2026-10-27); figure \texttt{figs/make\_toy\_floor.py} (numbers re-read from the result files 2026-11-18); budget and bootstrap \texttt{rerun/paper1\_floor/README.md}. Replaces the withdrawn ``$\sim$1\textdegree{} physics floor''.}
What limits the angle once the bias is gone? An electron-by-electron simulation (cluster sizes and
$\delta$-ray ranges, Magboltz diffusion, Polya gain, the measured footprint, pulse response, sampling, noise and ADC
ceiling), fitted by the production estimator with the simulation's own mean model, gives the resolution a perfect
model would reach (\cref{fig:toy_floor}). For chamber~A it is 0.34 to 0.36\textdegree{} ($s_{68}$) up to 20\textdegree, and 0.30 to
0.35\textdegree{} over the five chambers. Transverse diffusion sets it: without diffusion the simulated resolution is below
0.1\textdegree{} up to 35\textdegree, and the electronics noise alone gives at most 0.03\textdegree. The closed form
$\sigma_\theta\approx D_T\sqrt{6(1+f)/(NG)}$, with $N$ drift electrons, gap $G$ and $f=1/(1+\theta_\mathrm{Polya})$,
gives 0.29\textdegree{} for chamber~A.

The measured resolution of chamber~A, 0.99\textdegree{} (X) and 0.90\textdegree{} (Y), is about three times this
floor and flat in angle up to the 20\textdegree{} the reference tracker reaches. The difference was taken apart on
the calibration runs, with the mean dependence of the residual on angle and position removed. In that convention
chamber~A measures 0.81\textdegree{} (X) and 0.73\textdegree{} (Y), the simulation with every known effect 0.42 and
0.48\textdegree, and the difference in quadrature is 0.69 and 0.54\textdegree. It is not the reference (no dependence
on the reference's $\chi^2$), not a cause common to the two views (their residuals correlate by less than 0.09), not
position structure finer than the map (at most 3\,\% of its variance) and not a non-linearity in angle (at most
2\,\%). Saturated planes, the brightest events and events with a second particle account for 12 to 37\,\% of it on
chambers A, B, C and E, and for most of it on chamber~D. What remains on clean events is 0.56 to 0.65\textdegree{} in the X view of chambers A, B and D. It is
present at normal incidence, the fit partly sees it (keeping the best quarter in $\chi^2$ removes more of the variance
in the data than in the simulation), and the $\chi^2$ per degree of freedom of the data is 4 to 15 times the
simulation's and grows with the charge. When the data's own fit residuals are added to simulated tracks they move
the angle 1.5 to 2 times as much as the simulation's residuals do, and account for $59\pm10$\,\% of the remaining
variance on chamber~A. The remainder therefore behaves as an event-to-event fluctuation of the charge pattern that
is larger in the data than in the simulation. Which part of the signal carries it is not identified; it is bounded
and characterised, not explained.

\paragraph{The depth-bin bias.} The waveform fit describes the charge column in bins of one sample, 60\,ns. Even with a
perfect model this degrades steep tracks and reads them shallow: in the simulation the bias is $-0.08$, $-0.23$ and
$-0.51$\textdegree{} at 25, 30 and 40\textdegree, and the resolution grows from 0.34\textdegree{} at 20\textdegree{} to
1.35\textdegree{} at 40\textdegree. With 20\,ns bins the bias stays within 0.1\textdegree{} up to 45\textdegree{} and
the resolution within 0.27 to 0.41\textdegree. The cause is that when $v\,\Delta t\tan\theta$ approaches the strip pitch,
a charge uniform within a bin cannot represent clustered ionisation; the effect vanishes in the simulation without
clustering. On the bench it is small ($\sim-0.05$\textdegree{} at 20\textdegree), but it is the main term for tracks
at 30 to 45\textdegree. A finer charge grid costs about three times more columns and has not been tested on data.
