\section{Signal formation on resistive strips}\label{sec:kernel}
\sectionstate{partial}{first prose draft 2026-11-18. Numbers: \texttt{rerun/paper1\_spsfigs/README.md} (kernel\_forms, angled\_kernel; condor 4605384), \texttt{docs/PARAMETERS.md} \S2. The copy-kernel numbers of the old outline ($c_2/c_1$ 0.42--0.47, 49\,ns) are superseded and not used.}
An electron cloud that has drifted for a time $u$ arrives at the mesh with a lateral width set by transverse
diffusion, is multiplied, and induces its signal through the resistive layer onto the two layers of readout strips
below (\cref{fig:sharing_cartoon}). Three things put the charge of one depth on more than one strip. The diffusion
during the drift grows as $\sqrt{u}$. The avalanche and the induction through the resistive layer give a prompt
footprint that is the same for every depth. And the charge deposited on a resistive strip does not stay where it
landed: it spreads along the strip as on an RC line, with a variance that grows linearly with the time since it
landed. The resistive strips run along one direction, so this last term acts in one view only: the Y strips, which
measure the coordinate along the resistive strips, see the charge spread onto their neighbours over the following
samples, while the X strips, which measure the coordinate across them, do not.

The size of these terms is measured without a model in the beam, on tracks at normal incidence recorded without zero
suppression. For such a track all depths land on the same strip, so the signals of the neighbours of the leading
strip, relative to it, are the response of the readout to charge on one strip (\cref{fig:kernel_forms}). At
243\,V/cm a first neighbour carries on average 0.377 (X) and 0.390 (Y) of the leading strip's amplitude, and
a second neighbour 0.19 (X) and 0.26 (Y) of the first; the values at 156 and 96\,V/cm agree within 0.01, on about
2\,100 tracks per view. The second neighbour is below the first in both views, as it must be if charge reaches it
only through the first. The X view is not symmetric: the first neighbour on the low side is larger by 0.07 to
0.085 of the leading strip, where the Y view is symmetric within 0.01~\cite{paperI}. The neighbour signals of the Y
view outlast the 3.84\,\textmu s readout window, so the long-time part of the spreading is bounded, not measured.

The same quantities do not depend on the track angle in the view that stays at normal incidence. With chamber E
turned by 25.4\textdegree{}, so that the Y view is inclined and the X view is not, the X view reads first-neighbour fractions of 0.43 and
0.35 on its two sides, against 0.43 and 0.36 with the chamber flat in the same run (\cref{fig:angled_kernel}).
