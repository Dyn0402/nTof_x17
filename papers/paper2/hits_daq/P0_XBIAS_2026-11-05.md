# P0: where the X hits bias comes from (2026-11-05)

**Question (Dylan):** the production model has no slow spreading across the resistive strips (`rc_D_x = 0`), so X
should behave like a metallic readout — yet hits on X compress the ladder. Is the X bias real, and what makes it?

**Answer: it is real, and the production model reproduces it with no X resistive term. It is same-time mixing —
drift diffusion compounded by the prompt footprint — read through a single-time-per-strip estimator. A metallic
readout in the same gas would show almost all of it. The resistive spreading adds on top, on Y only.** The "delayed
copies of the neighbours (29 % at 47 ns)" explanation in `RECONSTRUCTION_BASIS.md` is the retired copy kernel's
language and is wrong for X; on Y it is a part, not the whole.

## Method

`p0_xbias.py` on the residual-bootstrap extractions (`rerun/paper1_floor/boot_extract.py`; pass 1, clean planes): per
plane the data window W, the pass model M on it (the event's fitted p0, w, t0 and depth profile q), the noise. One hit
emulator (peak ≥ 5σ, 30 % leading-edge time as `WaveformAnalyzer.cpp`; peak time as a second estimator) runs on W, on M,
and on M regenerated through `wft.model.model_waveforms` with one ingredient switched off. Per plane an
amplitude-weighted line t(x) through the hits gives u_h = dx/dt; the truth is the fit's own w (mm/ns).
**Compression = slope of binned-median u_h against w** over the inclined planes (|w| ≥ 0.05 v); 1 = unbiased.
Regeneration closure: max relative difference to the stored M ≤ 1e-7. Results: `results/p0_<row>.json`.

Independent of the fit, det3 against the M3 reference: hits u vs tan_ref 43.9 µm/ns on X, 50.1 on Y, against 36.8 / 36.6
for the waveform fit's w — compression 1.19 / 1.37, the same as the table below (and as `hits_arm.py`'s 46/51).

## Reading

- **The model reproduces the data's hit bias** to 0–4 % on det2/3/7 (it over-predicts by 3–5 %, X and Y alike), so
  the hits ladder of the data is explained by the same kernel the waveform fit uses. det4 (bench `g_det4`) is the
  exception: the model over-predicts by 10 % X / 21 % Y, mostly at the largest angles; a det4 limitation to note.
- **X:** turning the footprint down to the induction floor changes almost nothing (1.21 → 1.17); turning diffusion off
  halves the excess (→ 1.09); both together leave 1.04 (the 60 ns sampling and the template). The two act together:
  diffusion widens the deep charge onto neighbours whose own charge arrived earlier, and a leading-edge (or peak) time
  is pulled toward the earliest charge on the strip, increasingly with depth — so the cathode end is pulled in more
  than the mesh end. Dp is Magboltz-consistent on det3/det4 (`docs/PARAMETERS.md`), so this is gas physics.
- **X is effectively a metallic readout** in this design (resistive strips run parallel to the X strips): metallic X
  1.17–1.22 against the full model's 1.20–1.28.
- **Y:** the RC spreading roughly doubles the excess over metallic (det3 1.22 → 1.43, det2 1.24 → 1.49, det7
  1.21 → 1.54); it is the larger term on det7 (largest rc_D_y, 6.8e-4) and the smaller on det4 (2.2e-4).
- **Consequence for the paper's story:** "hits fail because of resistive sharing" is too narrow. Hits fail because a
  single time per strip mixes charge from different depths; diffusion does that in any µTPC, and resistive spreading
  adds to it in the view across the resistive strips. That makes the resistivity scan (Arm S) more informative: its
  zero-resistivity end is not unbiased, it is the metallic 1.17–1.24.

## Caveats

- The truth here is the waveform fit's w (checked against M3 on det3 above). The model's split between Dp and σ0 is
  not unique (they trade in the calibration), but the "both off" and "metallic" arms do not depend on that split much.
- The X ±1 one-sided delayed copy (det3, ~110 ns, `rc_asym_x`) is off in these bundles; it is not needed to reproduce
  the X data, so it is not the source of the X bias.
- Clean bootstrap planes only (inclined range |tan| ≲ 0.3 on the bench); the full benchmark uses the interior_good sample.
