# Capacitive coupling cannot alone explain MX17's compression

Capacitive coupling is real, and the literature shows that it creates "ghost" edge strips, but no published study isolates it as the cause of the micro-TPC angle bias. On MX17 chamber A, a same-time, same-shape nearest-neighbour coupling is allowed only at a modest level (κ ≈ 0.03–0.10 along a valley shared with the same-time footprint), and κ ≥ 0.15 is disfavoured. Producing the observed X compression of 1.19 by coupling alone would take κ ≈ 0.2–0.3 with diffusion switched off. In the forward model the total compression does not change as same-time sharing moves between footprint and coupling (X 1.26, Y 1.58–1.60), and removing diffusion costs far more than removing κ (0.155 against 0.047 of X's 0.264 excess at one valley point). The paper should therefore neither adopt "capacitive coupling causes the bias" nor claim coupling is absent. The defensible statement is that same-time neighbour sharing is present at the several-percent level, that its label (footprint or coupling) is not resolved by the data and does not change the compression, and that depth mixing from diffusion, plus the resistive-strip RC spreading on Y, supplies most of the effect. Three limits apply: the MX17 test covers one chamber and only the same-shape form, its χ² is dominated by model error (χ²/dof ≈ 66), and the pass was calibrated at κ = 0. The derivative-shaped term that the mechanism analysis expects for direct strip-to-strip coupling has not been fitted.

## "Capacitive coupling" names four different physical effects

The literature uses one phrase for mechanisms with different time structure, and a citation for one does not support another. **Weighting-field induction** on a non-collecting strip is bipolar with zero net charge unless electrons land there. Flierl's Garfield and Ramo calculation for a GEM gives the first neighbour 80 % of the central pulse height while electrons are far away, falling to a final 15 % (0.8 % on the second neighbour) ([Flierl 2018](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/22198/1/Flierl_Bernhard.pdf)). That 15 % sits awkwardly with strict Ramo, which gives zero net charge if all electrons finish on the central strip, so it should not be read as pure induction ([mechanism note](../research_notes/Capacitive%20coupling%20in%20strip%20microTPCs/mechanism_and_scaling.md)).

**Direct capacitance between amplifier-fed strips** is a short transient. Hohlmann's PSpice model describes the crosstalk pulse as a "CR-differentiator", of opposite sign to the signal for a detector in normal operation, while a voltage pulser gives same-sign pulses ([Hohlmann 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11596)). So pulser numbers do not transfer automatically to avalanche signals. **Charge on a floating, high-impedance electrode** imaging onto several readout strips is the only class that gives a same-time, same-polarity, net-charge scaled copy independent of the amplifier. This is the class of Bortfeldt's floating-strip LTspice model (non-hit strip at 0.25 of its neighbour) ([Bortfeldt 2014](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/16972/1/Bortfeldt_Jonathan.pdf)) and of Lösel's statement that coupling "is mainly due to the coupling between the resistive strips" ([Lösel 2017](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/21251/3/Loesel_Philipp.pdf)). Reading these two models as one class is the mechanism note's inference; neither thesis says so. **Resistive spreading** is different again: neighbours are delayed and longer, as in the measured V-shape ([Lin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.6871)), the T2K pads ([T2K ERAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06541)), and time-dependent weighting-field simulations of a 2D resistive-strip Micromegas ([Janssens et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01883)). For a thin layer insulated from the strips the signals are strictly bipolar after full drain ([Riegler 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07949)). On MX17 that drain takes about 17 ms ([mechanism note](../research_notes/Capacitive%20coupling%20in%20strip%20microTPCs/mechanism_and_scaling.md)).

The route to a bias is common to the same-time classes. A coupled signal appears on a strip before that strip's own charge arrives, pulling times toward the middle of the cluster and rotating the fitted line steeper ([Lösel 2017](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/21251/3/Loesel_Philipp.pdf); [Scholer 2021](https://inspirehep.net/files/362b665c04e2575c975ef950e4a5c63b)). A same-time Gaussian footprint does exactly the same, and this is the central difficulty: "same time, fixed charge ratio", the ghost-hit criterion, cannot separate coupling from a footprint ([methods note](../research_notes/Capacitive%20coupling%20in%20strip%20microTPCs/separating_methods.md)).

## The published attribution is plausible, but no study isolates it

**Coupling itself is documented; its causal share of the bias is not.** Two roots date from about 2014: Bortfeldt's LTspice model and the ATLAS/NTUA Maxwell/Simplorer model with edge-strip charge correlations. The ATLAS sentence that the effect "has been understood to be due to the capacitive induction" carries a figure caption saying "mainly due to" and text calling it "the mechanism assumed to be responsible" ([Iodice 2015](https://inspirehep.net/files/69437e5e54ad974430c3aac860b62c8d)). BESIII repeats the wording nearly verbatim, citing Ntekas ([Alexeev 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06253)).

The strongest evidence against sole causation comes from the field's own simulations. **Ntekas's gas-only Monte Carlo, with no electrical circuit, still shows a bias of 0.2° at 30° rising to 4° at 10°**, which he calls "purely geometrical" combined with diffusion ([Ntekas 2016](https://inspirehep.net/files/5d0a746b3db9fe13871fa68223192c65)). Scholer's digitisation with charge sharing disabled still peaks at about 7° for muons above 25°, though his simulation overshoots his own test beam, which peaks at the true 28° ([Scholer 2021](https://inspirehep.net/files/362b665c04e2575c975ef950e4a5c63b)). These two no-coupling figures disagree by more than an order of magnitude and neither is explained. Bortfeldt's model, conversely, includes coupling and the edge-position effect but no diffusion, with no decomposition between them, so calling his +9° "capacitive only" is wrong ([Bortfeldt 2014](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/16972/1/Bortfeldt_Jonathan.pdf)). The repository's `LITERATURE_MIXING.md` currently makes that error.

**No source runs a clean diffusion-on against diffusion-off comparison at fixed coupling, and none varies a single parameter to test the cause**; pitch, strip length, and drift field are all confounded ([attribution note](../research_notes/Capacitive%20coupling%20in%20strip%20microTPCs/attribution_evidence.md)). Corrections are weak evidence. Lösel's untuned simulated subtraction is the best pro-coupling test, restoring the angle at 30–40° where the uncorrected bias was already small and failing at 10° ([Lösel 2017](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/21251/3/Loesel_Philipp.pdf)). Ntekas himself notes that the reduction "is naively expected since the corrections tend to rotate the reconstructed µTPC segment" ([Ntekas 2016](https://inspirehep.net/files/5d0a746b3db9fe13871fa68223192c65)). A factor tuned to the angle, such as Flierl's 30 %, absorbs every effect that compresses the ladder, so it is not a capacitance measurement ([Flierl 2018](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/22198/1/Flierl_Bernhard.pdf)). BESIII's corrections explicitly lump "diffusion and capacitive effects" together ([Farinelli 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06548)).

The steep fall of the bias with angle does not discriminate either. Bortfeldt's predictions imply a nearly fixed end-strip time shift (Δtan·tanθ ≈ 0.03 from 10° to 30°), the signature of perturbing the ends of a line fit, which coupling and diffusion both produce ([attribution note](../research_notes/Capacitive%20coupling%20in%20strip%20microTPCs/attribution_evidence.md)). Simultaneity of ghost strips is asserted repeatedly, but no histogram of edge-minus-neighbour time exists in any Micromegas source read. The one direct injection found, in a GEM chamber, reports about 5 % "after about 15 ns" ([Farinelli 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06548)). Alternatives appear in print: first-arrival suppression by diffusion ([Flierl 2018](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/22198/1/Flierl_Bernhard.pdf)), noise on edge strips ([Bortfeldt 2014](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/16972/1/Bortfeldt_Jonathan.pdf)), and diffusion in a pixelised low-pressure TPC simulation, bias below 4.5° ([Maire et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.6837)).

The headline "23–32 %" is simulation only, without uncertainties, and "9 cm" is not Lösel's length. Farinelli's "ATLAS observed an average of 15 %" traces to one correlation slope on one chamber at 10°, while the ATLAS summary quotes a simulated 10 % ([Vanadia](https://inspirehep.net/files/cf2a35f9dc497f132c073ce83c5ef49c)). Scholer's 67 % assumes the edge strip is pure coupling, so it is an upper envelope. **No value exists for a 0.78 mm pitch, a roughly 40 cm strip at that pitch, or a DREAM or AFTER strip detector.** Lösel's slope extrapolates to about 25 % at 40 cm for 0.4 mm pitch, but Ntekas's width scan moves the value by a factor of two at fixed pitch, so that transfer is unreliable ([measured-values note](../research_notes/Capacitive%20coupling%20in%20strip%20microTPCs/measured_values.md)).

Published biases are larger at small angle: 20° reads about 23° at ATLAS, a tan ratio of 1.17 ([Iodice 2015](https://inspirehep.net/files/69437e5e54ad974430c3aac860b62c8d)), and Flierl's GEM reads a most-probable 19° at 15°, 1.28 ([Flierl 2018](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/22198/1/Flierl_Bernhard.pdf)). These sit in the same range as MX17's X 1.19 and Y 1.37 ([P0 study](../P0_XBIAS_2026-11-05.md)), but those chambers have gaps of about 5–6 mm and MX17 has 28 mm. Size agreement therefore implies nothing about a shared mechanism: diffusion over 28 mm differs in kind from a few-mm chamber, and the end-perturbation scaling depends on the strip count.

A bottom-up estimate for MX17, which is calculation and not measurement, gives direct comb-to-comb coupling of 0.3–4.5 % of the main peak (best guess 1–2 %), a derivative-like pulse leading the main peak by 40–115 ns, with DREAM's input impedance bracketed because it is unpublished. Coupling via the resistive strip over its own column to the next comb is about 1 % per side. Resistive-strip-to-resistive-strip coupling is at most 0.3 %, which disagrees with Lösel by about ×100, and that discrepancy is unexplained. The largest candidate is geometric: the 0.80 mm resistive strips beat against 0.78 mm readout columns with a 31.2 mm period, giving a same-time near-side share of 0 to about 100 %, mean about 22 %, in a projected-area estimate that remains a hypothesis ([mechanism note](../research_notes/Capacitive%20coupling%20in%20strip%20microTPCs/mechanism_and_scaling.md)). A separate arithmetic bound applies: if σ0 = 0.30 mm were entirely a kernel (1−2k, k, k), then k ≈ 0.074 at this pitch, and k = 0.15 alone would imply a 0.43 mm width, more than the fitted footprint ([methods note](../research_notes/Capacitive%20coupling%20in%20strip%20microTPCs/separating_methods.md)).

## MX17 detector evidence: a several-percent same-time term, not a literature-size coupling

This section reports the group's own tests on chamber A (det3), bench cosmics, Run 1 and Run 2, in which the pass model was extended by M_i → M_i + κ(M_{i−1} + M_{i+1}) ([detector tests](../research_notes/Capacitive%20coupling%20in%20strip%20microTPCs/detector_tests_mx17.md); code `coupling_scan.py`). None of it comes from the literature.

**The waveforms allow small κ and disfavour large κ.** The χ² runs along a valley from (σ0, κ) = (0.30, 0) to (0.07, 0.10); the pass point is not uniquely preferred. With the track refitted per grid point (3,076 planes), median χ² relative to (0.30, 0) is 0.964 (X) and 0.963 (Y) at (0.15, 0.08), and 0.956 and 0.954 at (0.07, 0.10), rising to 1.043 and 1.032 at (0.07, 0.15). In the fixed-track grid κ = 0.3 costs 50–120 %. These differences are real as trends but are not statistical significances, since χ²/dof is about 66 and model error dominates. A 3–5 % preference for a narrow footprint plus κ ≈ 0.06–0.10 is therefore suggestive and no more. The waveform-fit angle against M3 is insensitive to where the point sits along the valley: s68 changes by +0.030 ± 0.014–0.018° on X and +0.008 ± 0.010° on Y from bases of 0.966° and 0.985°.

**The compression is invariant along the valley.** In pure-model ladders:

| View | σ0, κ | Full | No κ | No diffusion | No diffusion, no RC | Floor (no κ, diffusion, RC) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| X | 0.30, 0 | 1.259 | 1.256 | 1.106 | 1.106 | 1.106 |
| X | 0.15, 0.08 | 1.264 | 1.217 | 1.109 | 1.110 | 1.051 |
| X | 0.07, 0.10 | 1.269 | 1.204 | 1.103 | 1.103 | 1.035 |
| Y | 0.30, 0 | 1.595 | 1.585 | 1.345 | 1.145 | 1.142 |
| Y | 0.15, 0.08 | 1.594 | 1.556 | 1.352 | 1.149 | 1.065 |

Moving along the valley only re-attributes: κ takes what σ0 gives up. At (0.15, 0.08), removing κ alone costs 0.047 of X's 0.264 excess (18 %) and removing diffusion costs 0.155 (59 %). On Y, RC adds a further 0.20 and diffusion 0.24. These are differences in a chosen order, not a unique partition. The model also overshoots Y (1.59 against 1.47 in real data in the same selection), for reasons not traced to coupling. A floor of 1.035–1.106 on X remains with no diffusion, RC or coupling, growing with σ0; its low end is comparable in kind to Ntekas's "purely geometrical" term. Coupling alone with no diffusion and σ0 = 0.07 reaches the observed X 1.19 only near κ ≈ 0.2–0.3 (1.152 at 0.15, 1.224 at 0.30), which the waveforms disfavour.

**The model-free reach test finds structure that supports a same-time term but rules out a pure nearest-neighbour kernel.** Near-normal tracks in the early window show a far-side first neighbour of 0.060–0.063 (X) and 0.102–0.109 (Y), which the κ = 0 model already matches on average (0.054, 0.107). Two features do not match. First, the far-neighbour ratio versus position in strip flattens to a floor (X ~0.06, Y ~0.105) where a Gaussian footprint keeps falling (X data 0.056–0.066 for phase 0.2–0.5, model 0.079–0.039), and injected position error does not make that floor. A phase-independent floor is what a fixed coupling predicts ([methods note](../research_notes/Capacitive%20coupling%20in%20strip%20microTPCs/separating_methods.md)), and the X floor matches κ ≈ 0.06. This is the best positive evidence for coupling on MX17, though a non-Gaussian footprint is not excluded, and near the strip centre the data lie below every model tried. Second, the −2 strip carries **1.3–1.7 %** against κ² ≈ 0.4 % at most, while −3 and −4 stay at or below about 0.2 % (the control for baseline and zero-suppression excess), so nearest-neighbour coupling cannot make it. Lorentzian tails reach 0.9–1.3 % on X but only 0.2 % on Y. A kernel with a direct second-neighbour term is not excluded by this test: the implied c2/c1 ≈ 0.2 resembles the 0.3 used by Scholer's digitiser, although the bottom-up estimate puts direct second-neighbour coupling at or below 0.2 %. The origin of the −2 charge is unidentified.

**The pitch-beat prediction is untested where it matters.** The far first neighbour over 31.2 mm shows no clear modulation (0.056–0.071, errors 0.003–0.008), but only the far side was examined, and the projected-area prediction is for the near side. The hypothesis stands open.

## The paper can argue for shared mixing rather than coupling, with stated limits

Against the published explanation, the strong argument is structural: **on MX17 the compression does not depend on how same-time sharing is labelled, and the waveform-allowed coupling is too small to carry it alone.** That argument survives without any claim that coupling is absent. In favour of conforming, a fair reading admits that σ0 = 0.30 mm exceeds any identified avalanche or static-induction width (about 33 µm and 0.07 mm), so part of the "footprint" is likely neighbour coupling in the broad sense, and the data show a phase-independent floor at the 6 % level on X. Calling the same-time term capacitive would not be wrong for that fraction; calling it the cause of the angle bias would be.

A wording the evidence supports: *"Neighbour-strip signals at the several-percent level are present and are degenerate with the same-time footprint in the waveform fit. Moving same-time sharing between the two leaves the drift-ladder compression unchanged. In the forward model most of the compression comes from depth mixing by drift diffusion, and on the view across the resistive strips also from RC spreading. Published work attributes the bias to capacitive coupling, but has not separated it from diffusion and edge geometry; a coupling of the published 15–30 % size is disfavoured on chamber A in the same-shape form."*

What remains untested limits that wording. A derivative (bipolar) neighbour term was scanned with the track fixed: the median χ² is minimal at zero on both views, rising by 0.5–1.9 % at ±0.05 per 60 ns sample and 24–47 % at ±0.3, so the form the mechanism analysis expects for direct comb coupling is not requested above a few percent per sample, though 60 ns sampling limits how sharp a transient this can see, and a term with its own delay was not tried. Only chamber A was examined, reach and beat tests are near-normal only, and the pass was calibrated at κ = 0, so re-calibration with κ free has not been done. Whether MX17's ratio follows the 1/N² end-perturbation form or stays multiplicative in tan θ is not established in the material reviewed, and at median tan ≈ 0.11 (N ≈ 4) versus tan 0.3 (N ≈ 11) that distinction bears on whether MX17 sees the same effect as 5–6 mm chambers ([attribution note](../research_notes/Capacitive%20coupling%20in%20strip%20microTPCs/attribution_evidence.md)).

All of this can proceed on existing data and simulation. Freeing k0 and k1 in the forward model together with the track, with a boundary-calibrated profile and an injection closure test, comes first. Next are the neighbour ratio against depth at the mesh end (constant for coupling, growing with drift for diffusion), the near-side strips in the 31.2 mm beat, stacked ±1 waveforms at fixed phase and depth in the LAr manner ([MicroBooNE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.02583)), the same tests on the other chambers, and a field-and-circuit simulation of the real checkerboard stack to explain the ×100 disagreement with Lösel. If these do not settle the point, it goes in the paper as a stated limitation.

## Conclusion

The two-way choice in the question turns out to be a false one. The literature does not establish coupling as the cause, so conforming to it would import a claim its authors never isolated, and MX17 cannot refute it outright, because the data cannot split a same-time term into footprint and coupling. What MX17 adds is more useful than either: the waveform forward model shows that, for the same-shape form, this split leaves the compression unchanged, a test none of the cited studies performed.

The citation record also needs care in the paper itself. The safe forms are "ATLAS test-beam edge-strip ratios indicate about 15 %, rising to about 25 % for narrower strips", "circuit simulation gives 10–32 % depending on geometry", and "Bortfeldt's model includes coupling and edge-position effects together". The −2 strip charge and the position-in-strip behaviour of the first neighbour (a floor at large phase, a deficit near the strip centre) are the two MX17 observations that no model tried reproduces in full; the paper should present them as open rather than fold them into either story.
