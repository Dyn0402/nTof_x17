## Geometry and operating parameters per detector

### Takeaway
Everyone with a published µTPC angle bias works at 3-8 mm gaps and 25 ns sampling (APV25) or peak-time VMM. The NSW σ_T at full drift is 0.26 mm **(derived)**, against about 0.6 mm for MX17. In ratio to the pitch the gap is less dramatic than assumed: σ_T/pitch is about 0.6 for NSW and 0.75-0.86 for MX17. The largest geometric difference is the gap, which gives MX17 11-22 strips per column where NSW has about 6.

### Cited Findings
- **ATLAS NSW resistive-strip MM.** Ar:CO2 93:7, 600 V/cm drift, v = 47 µm/ns, 5 mm gap, maximum drift about 105-106 ns, "the transverse (longitudinal) diffusion of electrons with a drift distance of 1 cm is 365 µm (198 µm)" (PyBoltz, Sec. 6.2.1, p. 57) — [Scholer 2021](https://inspirehep.net/files/362b665c04e2575c975ef950e4a5c63b). This fills the D_T the repo review could not find.
- NSW readout: the copper strips "receive the signal via capacitive coupling to the resistive strips" (p. 56). VMM records peak height, and "At the peak, a TAC is started" (Sec. 7, line 2775 of the extraction), i.e. time at peak in that chain — [Scholer 2021](https://inspirehep.net/files/362b665c04e2575c975ef950e4a5c63b). Test beam at 28°: 6-7 strips expected per cluster, peaking time 100/200 ns, threshold 6x RMS (p. 89-90) — same source.
- NSW strip pitch 425/450 µm and strip width 300 µm, as in [Scholer 2020](https://inspirehep.net/files/e7de94e1bacfaef8203072b56f9c75d7) via the repo's DAQ survey; I did not re-read these two numbers in that paper.
- ATLAS test chambers (T chambers): 400 µm pitch; other prototype strips 0.25 mm; 150 GeV µ/π at SPS H4 — [Vanadia, ATL-MUON-PROC-2015-008](https://inspirehep.net/files/cf2a35f9dc497f132c073ce83c5ef49c).
- **LMU floating strip (metallic strips, capacitive readout).** 6 mm, 0.5 mm pitch, APV25 — [Bortfeldt et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.00802) and the repo review. Klitzner's variant used the fast gas Ne:CF4 80:20 with 23 MeV protons — [Klitzner MSc 2016](https://www.etp.physik.uni-muenchen.de/publications/theses/download/master_fklitzner.pdf).
- **BESIII CGEM development chambers.** Triple GEM, 3 or 5 mm conversion gap, 0.65 mm pitch, Ar:iC4H10 90:10 at 1.5 kV/cm, APV25 (Sec. 4) — [Alexeev et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.06253). D_T about 350 µm/√cm is quoted from [Farinelli thesis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.06548), Sec. 5.3. TIGER (60 ns T branch) was validated only with charge centroid according to the repo DAQ survey. A 2020 talk reports a first µTPC reconstruction in a CGEM read out by TIGER — [Farinelli slides](https://indico.inp.nsk.su/event/20/contributions/911/attachments/520/605/20200228_Farinelli_BESIII_CGEM_Cosmic.pdf) (slides; no angle-bias statement found).
- **CLAS12 BMT.** 3 mm drift gap, 128 µm amplification gap, DREAM electronics (the same ASIC family as MX17); "Time of a hit: time of the signal" peak with respect to trigger, cluster time = smallest hit time — [Bossù & Vandenbroucke, MPGD 2022 slides](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1219224/contributions/5130363/attachments/2564786/4421936/2022_MPGD_CLAS12_Bossu_Vandenbroucke.pdf). BMT pitches 330-860 µm per layer — [CLAS12 alignment paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.05054), Table 2. Used for centroid clustering: time serves as a cluster-selection variable. The paper gives tracks to about 70 mrad and no strip-by-strip drift fit.
- **KLOE-2 CGEM.** 650 µm pitch XV strips, digital GASTONE front end — [KLOE-2 status](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.3770); about 200 µm r-φ with digital readout — [KLOE-2 proceedings](https://pos.sissa.it/150/071/pdf) (search snippet). No per-strip time, hence no µTPC.
- **sTGC.** 2.8 mm gap, 3.2 mm strip pitch; wires with strip cathodes — [ATLAS Run 3 detector paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16623) (snippet). Position comes from strip charge. I found no µTPC-type use.

## Estimator, reported bias against a reference, explanation and correction

### Takeaway
The bias is reported repeatedly, nearly always as a mean or peak angle against the set chamber angle, always towards steeper tracks and shrinking with angle. Published causes are capacitive or charge-sharing coupling and edge-strip position mis-assignment. The best quantified treatment is ATLAS's Scholer 2021, whose simulation shows bias growing with charge sharing and a residual +7° with sharing off.

### Cited Findings
- **Bortfeldt 2014 (floating strip, APV25, Fermi rise-time time).** "The most probable reconstructed angle is always larger than the true track inclination and approaches the correct value for increasing track angles" with causes "Capacitive coupling of neighboring strips, a systematic shift of the measured charge positions on hit strips at the edges of the strip cluster and mis-identified noise" (Sec. 7.4, p. 182). "For 10◦ inclination a deviation of +5◦ is observed, that reduces quickly for increasing track inclination" (p. 118). The bias also propagates to velocity: "the reconstructed µTPC line slopes are too small resulting in too large values for the reconstructed drift velocity" (Sec. 6.1.7) — [Bortfeldt PhD](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/16972/1/Bortfeldt_Jonathan.pdf). This is the same v-inflation symptom as MX17's hit-ladder v of 47-50 against 36.6 µm/ns.
- Bortfeldt's LTspice model gives +9° at 10° (repo review; the phrase "a deviation of +9" was string-matched), and also reproduces the angle dependence (Fig. 7.7) — same source.
- [Bortfeldt et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.00802), Sec. 3.2: "signals couple capacitively onto neighboring strips. Thus measured signal timing is shifted towards earlier values for the strips, hit by electrons, created close to the cathode. Second, for strips at the edges of the strip cluster, the ionization electrons position is not calculated correctly."
- **Klitzner MSc 2016.** In Ne:CF4, at low drift field below 300 V/cm "for all inclination angles the reconstructed value is bigger than expected", attributed to capacitive coupling (p. 57). "Even for non hit strips in the cluster, a pulse height of 25 % of the neighboring strips could be detected" (p. 57). Contrary case: "This is only observed for the x-layer of the second detector ... All other layers reconstruct a slightly smaller angle than expected" (p. 115) — [Klitzner MSc](https://www.etp.physik.uni-muenchen.de/publications/theses/download/master_fklitzner.pdf).
- **Flierl 2018 (GEM, 8 mm gap, 0.4 mm pitch).** Uncorrected MPV 19° at a 15° tilt (Fig. 5.10); "a significant deviation even at a set angle of 30◦ was observed, where the mean reconstructed angle was 32◦"; time-bin-wise correction by 20 % gives (16.99 ± 0.04)° at 15° (Sec. 5.3, p. 80) — [Flierl PhD](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/22198/1/Flierl_Bernhard.pdf).
- **Sessa 2014 (ATLAS MM, non-resistive, 250/400 µm, 5 mm).** "A small angular bias is observed due to capacitative induction ..." (string-matched); edge strip skipped if charge is more than 6x below its neighbour — [Sessa](https://inspirehep.net/files/09876cb48a49208d97e7312de1a60d94).
- **Vanadia 2015/17 (ATLAS MM).** "due to capacitive coupling between the strips, the first and last strips in a cluster may have a signal which is only coming from the coupling with their neighbours. It was determined in simulation that charge-sharing between neighbouring strips is at the 10% level. This effect may lead to a bias in µTPC reconstruction." Position: the mid-strip assumption was "found to be correct only for strips in the middle of a cluster, while for strips at the edge of a cluster the assumption was observed to introduce a bias". Fig. 8 plots mean µTPC angle against incident angle, default versus filtered and corrected, at SPS H4 — [Vanadia](https://inspirehep.net/files/cf2a35f9dc497f132c073ce83c5ef49c).
- **ATLAS public results.** Figs. 12-13 (T-type chamber, 510 V, 150 GeV/c, tilts 10-40°): after refinement "a significant reduction in the observed mean reconstructed angle bias is observed" — [NSWPublicResults](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/AtlasPublic/NSWPublicResults). The numbers are in the figures only, and I did not read the images.
- **Rinnagel 2019 (NSW-type, VMM).** Tail to larger angles from capacitive coupling; "the first/last strip of a cluster are discarded" when PDO is below half of the neighbour's (string-matched) — [Rinnagel MSc](https://www.etp.physik.uni-muenchen.de/publications/theses/download/master_mrinnagel.pdf).
- **Scholer 2021 (NSW SM2, VMM3, 2017-18 test beam).** Test beam at 28°: "The peak of the distribution corresponds to the actual inclination of the chamber of 28°. A resolution of 7.1° was obtained around the peak, but a long tail towards higher angles" (Fig. 8.8, p. 90). Measured sharing: first-to-second strip charge ratio about 1.5 "indicates a magnitude of CS of around 67 %" at the longest strips, versus 16 % and about 30 % elsewhere (Sec. 8.3.3). "it also biases the time measurement of the other strips towards the time measured on the central strips, since those tend to have more charge" (Sec. 8.3.4, p. 95). Simulation for tracks above 25°: "For the case of CS disabled, the distribution is symmetric around a peak value of 7° while in the case of a correct angle reconstruction a peak position at 0° would be expected. This offset most likely stems from the positions of the first and last ionizations above the corresponding strips." "With CS enabled, the peaks of the distributions move towards higher angles while the distributions alse become asymmetric" (Sec. 11.3.3, p. 141) — [Scholer 2021](https://inspirehep.net/files/362b665c04e2575c975ef950e4a5c63b). The simulation includes diffusion (Sec. 10).
- Scholer on time distributions: "Adding CS pulls the time measurements more towards the mean time" (Sec. 10.3.6, p. 120), which is ladder compression stated in time units — same source. Without sharing, end peaks are geometric: "For the outermost strips the ionizations cannot happen centrally above the strip anymore since the initial particle leaves the gas volume" (Sec. 10.3.6) — same source. Also: for small angles "The time measurement of that strip will be biased towards the time of the charge deposit with the highest charge, which is not necessarily the earliest one" (Fig. 10.12 caption, p. 123) — same source.
- **BESIII.** "An angular bias is observed ... due to the capacitive induction of the signal on neighboring strips ... In addition the transverse diffusion plays another important role since the signal of a single primary electron can be spread on more than one strip" with first/last strip weighting or suppression, "still in a preliminary stage" (Sec. 4, p. 8) — [Alexeev et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.06253). Farinelli App. B has the 3-step data-driven shift — [Farinelli](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.06548). The strip-time estimator family is covered in `DAQ_SURVEY.md`.
- **µ-RWELL.** µTPC is combined with centroid; no angle bias found in [Bencivenni et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.03223).
- **Correction catalogue.** Drop or down-weight edge strips ([Sessa](https://inspirehep.net/files/09876cb48a49208d97e7312de1a60d94), [Rinnagel](https://www.etp.physik.uni-muenchen.de/publications/theses/download/master_mrinnagel.pdf), [Alexeev](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.06253), [Vanadia](https://inspirehep.net/files/cf2a35f9dc497f132c073ce83c5ef49c)); edge-position shift ([Farinelli](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.06548), [Vanadia](https://inspirehep.net/files/cf2a35f9dc497f132c073ce83c5ef49c)); time-bin-wise neighbour subtraction ([Flierl](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/22198/1/Flierl_Bernhard.pdf)); polynomial position correction in earliest time, a second-order fit ([Scholer](https://inspirehep.net/files/362b665c04e2575c975ef950e4a5c63b), Fig. 8.6); centroid combination; and a filter rejecting angles below 5° or above 85° ([Scholer](https://inspirehep.net/files/362b665c04e2575c975ef950e4a5c63b), Fig. 8.5).

### Inferences
- **(derived)** Tan ratios from quoted numbers: Bortfeldt 10° → 15° measured 1.52; Flierl 15° → 19° (MPV) 1.28; 30° → 32° (mean) 1.08; Flierl corrected 15° → 16.99° 1.14. Taking Scholer's +7° at face value at 28° gives tan(35°)/tan(28°) = 1.32 (at 35°, 1.29), but the sample covers all angles above 25° and the true-angle distribution is not given. MX17 lies inside this range.
- Scholer's CS-off +7° is a simulated, single-time bias that survives with no strip coupling, with diffusion on, on a metallic-equivalent readout. Scholer attributes it to first/last ionization geometry and leaves it open ("subject to further investigation"). It is the nearest published equivalent of MX17's "metallic keeps most of it" and "strip-width geometric end bend" results, but it does not separate diffusion from geometry.
- Scholer's data and simulation are in tension. The test-beam peak equals 28° with no visible shift, while the CS-off simulation peaks 7° high, and the thesis says the simulated CS behaviour "matches" the test beam. The filters (NL off, angle cuts) and peak-versus-mean choice could matter. No published resolution of this exists in what I read.

### Gaps
- Bias numbers from Vanadia Fig. 8 and ATLAS Figs. 12-13 exist only as images.
- Alexopoulos et al., NIM A 937 (2019) ([record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168900219305194)), Iodice's JINST/proceedings, Lösel's PhD, Klitzner's PhD and the 2010 Alexopoulos resistive paper were not retrieved as text. I found no open copy.
- [G-RWELL for ePIC 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168900226006960) could not be read (HTTP 403); a snippet reports 120-180 µm at 30° with µTPC, with no information on angle bias.

## Why smaller or larger than MX17: metallic vs resistive, diffusion, gas, gap, and the lever-arm test

### Takeaway
The simple lever-arm picture is only partly supported. MX17 has the smallest σ_T/(L·tanθ) of the group, yet a tan ratio similar to or larger than the published values at comparable lever arm, so mixing width over column length alone does not explain it. I found no controlled metallic-against-resistive comparison of the bias and no bias-versus-D_T study.

### Cited Findings
- Published angle dependence (shrinks with angle) is consistent across [Bortfeldt](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/16972/1/Bortfeldt_Jonathan.pdf), [Flierl](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/22198/1/Flierl_Bernhard.pdf) and the ATLAS caption in [NSWPublicResults](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/AtlasPublic/NSWPublicResults) ("The bias to larger angles decreases with the track angle", as returned in a search extract; the phrase is not in my fetched caption list, so treat as secondary).
- Klitzner explicitly links few strips to bias: "The decrease in cluster length for smaller inclination angles leads to a false reconstruction of the earliest and latest timing, as they are more biased by neighboring strips: For 35◦ around nine strips are typically hit, whereas for 25.5◦ only around six" (Sec. 5, p. 53); the bias is largest at low E_drift in a fast gas (p. 57) — [Klitzner MSc](https://www.etp.physik.uni-muenchen.de/publications/theses/download/master_fklitzner.pdf).
- Diffusion-driven simultaneity is stated once outside the BESIII papers, for 10B-converter ion tracks in Flierl Ch. 3: minimal cluster size "due to lateral diffusion is in the order of 1 mm ... For clusters with a smaller inclination angle the timing difference of the incoming electrons is suppressed by the first arriving electrons, as they already occupy the width of the full cluster and the signal on all strips appears simultaneous" (p. 56) — [Flierl PhD](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/22198/1/Flierl_Bernhard.pdf). This is the mechanism of `RECONSTRUCTION_BASIS.md` in words, for a small-angle limit, and it qualifies the repo review's statement that only GEM papers mention diffusion "in one sentence" (Flierl's is a different passage from Alexeev's).
- Resolution against drift field: "the best angle resolution here was obtained at a drift voltage where this diffusion was also minimal" (longitudinal diffusion, resolution not bias, Sec. 5.3, p. 80) — [Flierl PhD](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/22198/1/Flierl_Bernhard.pdf). Bortfeldt shows reconstructed inclination against drift field at four angles (Fig. 6.14) — [Bortfeldt PhD](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/16972/1/Bortfeldt_Jonathan.pdf). I did not extract values from that figure.
- Farinelli: "the correction depends on the gas mixture" (App. B, per repo review) — [Farinelli](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.06548).
- Resistive design raises coupling: "the very large strip length" and resistive-over-copper geometry give high sharing — [Scholer 2021](https://inspirehep.net/files/362b665c04e2575c975ef950e4a5c63b), Sec. 8.3.3, 10.2.3. Vanadia's 10 % (simulation), 16 %, about 30 % and 67 % are all quoted sharing fractions for ATLAS MM, so "sharing" is not one number — [Vanadia](https://inspirehep.net/files/cf2a35f9dc497f132c073ce83c5ef49c), [Scholer](https://inspirehep.net/files/362b665c04e2575c975ef950e4a5c63b).

- Against hypothesis (b): with the assumed 365 µm/√cm, Flierl at 30° has r about 0.07 and a tan ratio of 1.08, whereas MX17 at r 0.04-0.08 has 1.19 or more. A pure r-scaling would predict MX17 at or below 1.08. Caveats: published values are after edge filtering and are mean or MPV angles, and those detectors are small-gap with different time response.
- A second candidate that favours (b): the strip-column-end term, pitch/(L·tanθ), is NSW 28°: 0.17; MX17 tan 0.3: 0.093, tan 0.6: 0.046 **(derived)**. MX17's own arithmetic (strip width alone 1.04-1.07) tracks that term. The repo's end-bin observation (+84 / −65 ns at gap ends on about 780 ns of ladder, about 19 % of ladder **(derived)**) suggests that for MX17 the effect is an end-region shift, not a whole-ladder scale. In NSW the entire 106 ns ladder is a small multiple of the pulse width, so the ladder has no "mid-column" and end effects cover most strips.
- Why MX17 could be larger than the r-scaling: 60 ns sampling and shaper tails; more primary clusters per strip; many strips per column, each with an interior charge competitor; long drift, so diffusion at the cathode end is about 2.3-2.6x NSW's **(derived)**. Why published effects could look smaller: edge strips are filtered before the fit, and the peak of the angle distribution is quoted, while MX17's 1.19/1.37 is a slope ratio per track against an external reference. These are my inferences, not stated in any source.
- Resistive versus metallic in the literature is confounded: NSW resistive-strip resistive-layer coupling, floating-strip metallic but capacitively coupled, and GEM metallic all report steepening, so the sign survives with or without a resistive layer. This is consistent with, but not a controlled test of, the MX17 result that metallic retains most of the effect.

### Gaps
- No source I read compares metallic and resistive strips with otherwise identical geometry for the bias.
- No source measures bias as a function of D_T, gas or gap with an external angle reference. Bortfeldt's drift-field scan and Klitzner's low-E_drift observation are the nearest. No RD51 resistive-strip µTPC angle-bias study was found.

## Waveform forward models, charge deconvolution, and work since 2024

### Takeaway
I found no µTPC paper that fits a forward model to strip waveforms for track reconstruction, as a negative search result. The closest strip-MPGD work is Flierl's time-bin-wise neighbour subtraction; outside strip MPGDs, T2K ERAM and MicroBooNE use response models for other purposes.

### Cited Findings
- Flierl: time-bin-wise subtraction of 15 % (simulated) or 20 % (empirical) of a neighbour's signal, improving the bias at 15° (Sec. 2.4.2, 5.3) — [Flierl PhD](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/22198/1/Flierl_Bernhard.pdf).
- Bortfeldt's LTspice model simulates capacitive-chain signals forward, then derives an angle-dependent rescale (not per-event) — [Bortfeldt PhD](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/16972/1/Bortfeldt_Jonathan.pdf).
- ATLAS digitization sets a sharing ratio (0.3 next strip, 0.09 next-to-next) and studies reconstruction — [NSWPublicResults Figs. 6-7](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/AtlasPublic/NSWPublicResults), [Scholer 2021](https://inspirehep.net/files/362b665c04e2575c975ef950e4a5c63b). This is a simulation model, and the reconstruction remains a line fit.
- Scholer's Cluster Time Projection method uses charge per strip plus an external angle estimate to project the cluster: the alternative to a single-strip fit used by ATLAS — [Scholer 2021](https://inspirehep.net/files/362b665c04e2575c975ef950e4a5c63b), Sec. 8.4.
- T2K ERAM analytical waveform model for RC mapping — [Attié et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.12634) (repo review). MicroBooNE 2D deconvolution in wire and time for a LArTPC — [Adams et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.02583). Both are out of this scope and listed only as nearest relatives.
- Since 2024: resistive-pad VMM3a (2025) reports that saturated pad signals are delayed by peak finding (per `DAQ_SURVEY.md` [R23]); fine-granularity resistive Micromegas, Fermi-Dirac rise, 5.7 ns (per `DAQ_SURVEY.md` [R7]); NSW Run 3 production uses the centroid with time-based clusterisation "being investigated" — [Francescato 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16735) (per `DAQ_SURVEY.md` [R26]); [MIMAC 2026 directional limits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23527) (title only, from INSPIRE). An INSPIRE title query for micro-TPC since 2023 returned 1 strip-unrelated 2025 item and this MIMAC paper; it would miss papers without "µTPC" in the title.

### Inferences
- Per-event waveform forward fit that solves the drift-depth profile remains, on this search, unpublished for strip µTPCs. The claim must be worded as "not found" in a limited search.

### Gaps
- No access to full texts behind paywalls after 2024; IEEE NSS 2024-25 abstracts not searched exhaustively. Neural-network µTPC strip work: nothing found in two searches.

## Hypothesis tests (b), (c), (d)

### Takeaway
(c) is well supported, with a caveat on what is "calibrated away". (d) is supported: per-track external slope comparisons at MX17's precision were not found. (b) is only partly supported and the table above is contrary evidence.

### Cited Findings
- (c) Same effect under other names: "capacitive coupling", "charge sharing (CS)", "edge strip mis-reconstruction", "induction". The time-pulling statement is explicit: "Adding CS pulls the time measurements more towards the mean time" — [Scholer 2021](https://inspirehep.net/files/362b665c04e2575c975ef950e4a5c63b), p. 120.
- (c) Calibrated away: earliest-time polynomial correction (Scholer Fig. 8.6), an angle-dependent rescale (Bortfeldt Eq. 4.28, repo review), edge filters (above).
