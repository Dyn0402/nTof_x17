## 1. ATLAS Micromegas µTPC angle bias: the Bini 2014 and Alexopoulos 2019 primaries

### Takeaway
Bini 2014 (JINST 9 C02032, open access CC-BY) describes the µTPC method and the centroid/µTPC combination but contains **no
angle-bias number and no mechanism statement**; its only "bias" sentences concern the Lorentz angle in a magnetic field and the
back-to-back doublet that cancels it. Alexopoulos et al. 2019 (NIM A 937, 125-140) is **closed access everywhere I could check**
(INSPIRE: no file; Unpaywall and Semantic Scholar: "closed"; IRIS Roma1 and Roma3 records: HTTP 403; no arXiv version), so
nothing from it can be quoted. The primary ATLAS statements with numbers therefore remain those already in the repo (Iodice
2015, Vanadia 2015/17, Ntekas 2016, Scholer 2021) plus the public-results figure captions.

### Cited findings
- **Bini 2014, the operating premise.** "Muons in the NSW are expected to come at angles with respect to the direction
  perpendicular to the chambers, in the range 8÷35◦. This implies that the µTPC operation mode (see below) has to be used."
  (pdf p. 4, printed p. 2) — [Bini 2014](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-0221/9/02/C02032/pdf).
- **Bini 2014, the method.** "The precision coordinate x can be obtained in two ways: either making the charge centroid of the
  fired strips (that is the best algorithm for almost orthogonal tracks) or using the so called "µTPC mode" [6] for tracks at
  larger values of θ. The µTPC concept is described in figure 4. It requires: the measurement of the ionization electrons drift
  time on each strip; the determination through a linear fit of the "tracklet" parameters, as shown in figure 4; finally the
  evaluation of the x coordinate of the track at half gap x_half." (pdf p. 6, printed p. 4). Reference [6] is "T. Alexopoulos
  et al., Development of large size Micromegas detector for the upgrade of the ATLAS muon system, Nucl. Instrum. Meth. A 617
  (2010) 161." (pdf p. 11) — same source. Test chambers: 10 × 10 cm², 400 or 250 µm pitch, resistive strips, beam angles 0-40°,
  120 GeV pions at SPS H6 (pdf p. 6).
- **Bini 2014, Fig. 4 caption:** "The error-bars on the points depend on the charge collected by each strip." (pdf p. 7,
  printed p. 5) — i.e. a charge-weighted line fit, no time correction mentioned.
- **Bini 2014, the combination:** "for each event the two algorithms are combined giving an x value having a resolution that is
  well below 100 µm in the full angular range. Such a combination profits from the observed anti-correlation in the position
  reconstruction between the two methods." (pdf p. 7).
- **Bini 2014, the only biases named are magnetic:** "A magnetic field component orthogonal to the electric field directly
  affects the drift motion of the ionization electrons, and systematically biases the measurement of the precision coordinate
  x." (pdf p. 7); "Particular care is needed to avoid biases in the measurement. MM chambers mounted in doublets with the
  back-to-back read-out electrodes allow a self-correction, since the effect of the Lorentz angle in the two gaps is equal and
  opposite" (pdf p. 9); Fig. 9 caption: "the mean of the coordinates measured in the two chambers provides a bias-free estimator
  of the precision coordinate." (pdf p. 10). A text search of the whole paper for "capacit", "bias", "inclin" returns only these.
- **Alexopoulos 2019, record only.** INSPIRE record 1737078: NIM A 937 (2019) 125-140, DOI 10.1016/j.nima.2019.04.050, authors
  Alexopoulos, Bianco, Biglietti, Bini, Byszewski, Iakovidis, Iengo, Iodice, Karentzos, Leontsinis, Ntekas, Petrucci,
  Sekhniaidze, Sidiropoulou, Vanadia, Wotschack; no document attached. Abstract excerpt via the INSPIRE API: "The ATLAS
  collaboration at the Large Hadron Collider at CERN has endorsed the resistive-strip micromegas technology". The IRIS record
  states (snippet, unverified): "A position reconstruction method has been developed for inclined tracks, called the
  "micro-TPC method". A description of the method along with performance studies is presented." and that results cover track
  impact angles 0-40° with Ar + 7 % CO2 and APV25 — [INSPIRE record](https://inspirehep.net/api/literature/1737078),
  [IRIS Roma3 record](https://iris.uniroma3.it/handle/11590/351105).

## 2. Drift tubes and drift chambers: the single-wire time depends on the track inclination and is calibrated away

### Takeaway
Three primary sources state, in words, that the one time read per wire is a function of the track inclination and is removed by
an angle-dependent calibration: CMS DT (the mean-timer Tmax "depends on the track inclination and on the pattern of cells";
the effective drift velocity depends on "the inclination of the track"), BESIII MDC (X-T relations "depend on the incident
direction of the track, i.e. entrance angle α", calibrated in 10° steps of α, with differences growing "up to as much as several
hundred microns" at long drift times) and Belle II CDC (XT relations determined per layer, left/right and "(α × θ)
categorization"). For the ATLAS MDTs (round tubes) the angle enters differently in the text read: as the spread of incident
angles that the r(t) autocalibration needs to converge, with tracks at 30° giving residuals that are "per definition zero".
The textbook statement (Blum-Riegler-Rolandi) could not be retrieved as text.

### Cited findings
- **CMS DT, effective drift velocity.** "The other relevant quantity for the DT calibration process is the effective drift
  velocity. It depends on many parameters, including the gas purity and the electrostatic configuration of the cell, the
  presence of a magnetic field within the chamber volume, and the inclination of the track." (pdf p. 5, printed p. 4) —
  [CMS, Calibration of the CMS drift tube chambers and measurement of the drift velocity with cosmic rays,
  arXiv:0911.4895 (submitted to JINST 2009)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.4895).
- **CMS DT, the mean-timer.** "The calibration algorithm is based on the mean-time technique described in detail in Ref. [10].
  In this method, the maximum drift time in a cell, Tmax, is calculated considering nearby cells in three adjacent layers and
  using a linear approximation to determine the average drift velocity. ... In general, Tmax depends on the track inclination
  and on the pattern of cells crossed by the track. Taking into account these dependencies, a spread of about 28 ns has been
  observed in the calculation of Tmax from CRAFT data." (Sec. 7, pdf p. 13, printed p. 12); v_eff = L_semi-cell / <Tmax> with
  L_semi-cell = 2.1 cm, giving "approximately a constant value of 54.3 µm/ns" (same page). Ref. [10] is "G. Abbiendi et al.,
  "Offline Calibration Procedure of the CMS Drift Tube Detectors", JINST 4 P05002 (2009)" (pdf p. 22) — same source. The 2009
  paper itself states (snippet, unverified, from a search extract): "Different meantimer relations for different track angles and
  patterns of hit cells are used."
- **CMS DT, the alternative:** the track-fit method "determines track-by-track the time of passage of the muon and the drift
  velocity as additional free parameters of the fit, together with the track position and inclination angle." (pdf p. 6) — same
  source. And in the companion performance paper: segments "that have a large incident angle and pass near the boundary between
  neighbouring drift cells may have more than one associated hit in a given layer" (pdf p. 13) — [CMS, Performance of the CMS
  drift tube chambers with cosmic rays, arXiv:0911.4855](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.4855).
- **BESIII MDC.** "In trapezoidal-shaped drift cells, the X-T relations depend on the incident direction of the track, i.e.
  entrance angle α, which is defined as the difference of the azimuth angle between the track (the tangent direction of the hit)
  and a vector between the origin and the wire in the r-φ plane, i.e. φtrack - φwire." (Sec. 4, pdf p. 2); "It is evident that
  the differences are very small near the sense wires, but grow with the increasing drift times up to as much as several hundred
  microns." (pdf p. 2); "Separate X-T relations are determined for 10 degree steps in α. Figure 5 (b) shows the mean value of
  the residual distribution as a function of drift time after calibration, in which the bias become much smaller." (pdf p. 3);
  summary: "we use separate X-T relations for left and right and also for different entrance angles which are related to the
  track's momentum." (pdf p. 5). Cells are "approximately 12×12 mm² for the inner eight layers and 16.2×16.2 mm² for the
  others" (pdf p. 1) — [Kang et al., Study of the calibration of X-T relation for the BESIII drift chamber,
  arXiv:1405.3397](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.3397).
- **Belle II CDC.** "The XT function is parametrized as a fifth order Chebychev polynomials and a linear function around the cell
  boundary. By taking the dependence concerning the layer, left/right passage, incident angle (α), and polar angle (θ) of the
  particle into account, we determine XT's for each layer, left/right passage separately with proper (α × θ) categorization."
  (pdf p. 6) — [Kandra et al. (Belle II), Calibration and alignment of the Belle II tracker, CTD/WIT 2019 proceedings,
  arXiv:1910.06289](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06289).
- **ATLAS MDT.** "This approach of the autocalibration needs, as the conventional method as well, a spread of the incident track
  angles to converge. Hence, only one r(t) relation per MDT chamber will be determined. Each chamber has a specific range of track
  angles depending on its position in the spectrometer." (pdf p. 2); "As the residuals of tracks with an incident angle of 30◦
  are per definition zero, these tracks provide no information about the systematical error of the r(t) relation." (pdf p. 4);
  chambers whose "interval of track angles ... is spread around 30◦" perform worst because "Residual based calibration methods
  are least sensitive in this region of track angles due to the chamber geometry." (pdf pp. 3-4) — [Deile, Hessey, Kortner,
  Kroha, v. Loeben, Staude, An Efficient Method to Determine the Space-to-Drift-Time Relationship of the ATLAS Monitored Drift
  Tube Chambers, IEEE NSS 2007 manuscript (MPP preprint)](https://wwwatlas.mpp.mpg.de/ftp/outgoing/papers/ieee07/2007_ieee_autocalibration.pdf).
  The underlying note is "M. Deile, Autocalibration: The Influence of the Track Incidence Angles and a New Method",
  ATL-MUON-2004-021 (cited there as ref. [3]); its CDS abstract says (snippet, unverified) that "speed and precision of
  autocalibration convergence depend crucially on the range of track incidence angles".

### Derived numbers
- BESIII: "several hundred microns" of X-T difference between adjacent 10° entrance-angle bins at long drift times, against a
  half-cell of 8.1 mm (16.2 mm / 2), is a few per cent of the drift distance **(derived)**; the effect is of the same order as
  the 123 µm single-hit resolution the paper quotes, which is why it is calibrated rather than ignored.
- CMS: the 28 ns spread in Tmax from inclination and cell pattern, on a maximum drift of about 390 ns (2.1 cm / 54.3 µm/ns),
  is about 7 % **(derived)**.

### Gaps
- Abbiendi et al. 2009 (JINST 4 P05002) and Amapane et al., CMS Note 2009/008 ("Local Muon Reconstruction in the Drift Tube
  Detectors") were not retrieved as text; the mean-timer angle dependence is quoted from the 2010 paper, which cites them.
- Deile's ATL-MUON-2004-021 is on CDS behind the bot check; only its abstract reached me through a catalogue copy.
- Blum, Riegler, Rolandi, *Particle Detection with Drift Chambers*: no excerpt found; cannot give a page.
- The MDT statement is about calibration convergence, not about an angle-dependent r(t) of a single tube; a source saying that
  round tubes make r(t) angle-independent was not found in what I read.

## 3. Charge-weighted (centroid-in-time) per-channel times: who uses them, and the only strip-µTPC estimator comparison

### Takeaway
No strip µTPC paper read uses a charge-weighted time per strip; the estimators found are a rising-edge Fermi fit (Herrmann:
inflection, or extrapolation to baseline or to maximum), the VMM peak time (Scholer, in the repo), and the first-electron time in
Fang's framing (Sec. 5). Herrmann 2019 is the one strip-µTPC source that compares time estimators against the angle: the two
extrapolated times give "systematically too small track angles", while the inflection time "follows at large angles the
diagonal". For pad TPCs the charge-weighted time is confirmed at the code level for sPHENIX (TpcClusterizer: cluster time =
ADC-weighted mean of time-bin centres). TPOT (resistive Micromegas, 1 mm straight and 2 mm zigzag strips, SAMPA) clusters by
ADC-weighted strip centroid and does not reconstruct a per-strip drift time at all; when a strip has several hits "only the first
one (in time) is kept". CLAS12 BMT: the MVT paper is inaccessible; the slide-level description already in the repo (peak time per
hit, cluster time = smallest hit time, centroid position) stands.

### Cited findings
- **Herrmann 2019, estimators.** "The time of the primary ionizations can be reconstructed using the signal-time of each strip.
  ... This is achieved as mentioned in chapter 4.3.1 with a fit of an inverse-Fermi-function to the rising-edge of the signals.
  The fit gives an estimation for the 50%-time of the signal and the slope of the rising-edge. The 50%-time is called inflection
  and is investigated as one possible evaluation-time." (Sec. 7.2.1, pdf p. 89; printed = pdf − 11 throughout) —
  [Herrmann 2019](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/25055/1/Herrmann_Maximilian_G.pdf).
- **Herrmann 2019, estimator against angle.** "The reconstructions using the two extrapolated-times show a systematic shift of
  the MPV with respect to the diagonal (in black). This can be explained by the broadening of the event-wise time-distribution
  using these evaluations (seen in figure 7.10b) resulting in higher reconstructed track-slopes. Therefore systematically too
  small track angles are reconstructed (expected from formula 7.2)." and "The MPV follows at large angles the diagonal (in
  black) and shows for small angles deviations due to the problematic reconstruction." (pdf p. 96, on Fig. 7.15; angle formula
  θ = arctan(s / (m_µTPC · v_drift)), pdf p. 95). Note the sign: a larger fitted time-per-strip slope means a smaller angle in
  this convention, so the extrapolated-time estimators err in the opposite direction from the ladder compression.
- **Herrmann 2019, mean-time alternative for the reference time only.** "The alternative methods for the determination of t_mid
  use the mean timing of the strip time-distributions. ... Also the event-wise calculated mean strip time can be used." (Sec.
  7.2.3, pdf p. 98) — a cluster-level mean, not a per-strip charge-weighted time.
- **sPHENIX TPC (code).** In `calc_cluster_parameter`: `double t = my_data.layergeom->get_zcenter(it); t_sum += t * adc;`
  `adc_sum += adc;` and later `double clust = t_sum / adc_sum;` with `clus->setLocalY(clust);` and `zdriftlength = clust *
  my_data.tGeometry->get_drift_velocity()` — [TpcClusterizer.cc, sPHENIX coresoftware master, fetched
  2026-11-09](https://github.com/sPHENIX-Collaboration/coresoftware/blob/master/offline/packages/tpc/TpcClusterizer.cc).
  This is a charge-weighted time centroid over the cluster's time bins, the STAR/ALICE pattern.
- **sPHENIX TPOT (code).** `/* when there are multiple hits on the same strip, only the first one (in time) is kept */`; the
  cluster position is `local_coordinates += strip_local_coordinate*weight` with `weight = double(hit->getAdc()) - pedestal`,
  then `local_coordinates *= (1./weight_sum)` — [MicromegasClusterizer.cc, sPHENIX coresoftware
  master](https://github.com/sPHENIX-Collaboration/coresoftware/blob/master/offline/packages/micromegas/MicromegasClusterizer.cc).
  No per-strip time enters the position.
- **TPOT paper.** "For TPOT, the SAMPA operation parameters are: (i) 160 ns shaping time," (Sec. 2.5.2); "The timing of the
  signal with respect to the trigger is verified by counting the number of hits above threshold as a function of time after
  trigger. ... The majority of signal hits are well contained between samples 20 and 40, corresponding to a time window of 1 µs."
  (pdf p. 27, Fig. 30, 50 ns samples); "The mean cluster size is 3.5 (2.6) for ϕ (z) views." (Sec. 4.4) — [Aune et al., The
  sPHENIX Micromegas Outer Tracker, NIM A 1066 (2024) 169615, arXiv:2403.13789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13789). A text search
  for "centroid", "weighted", "barycent" finds no definition of the cluster estimator in the paper; it is in the code above.
- **BESIII CGEM-IT with TIGER.** "The µ-TPC (micro Time-Projection Chamber) uses the drift gap like a time-projection chamber.
  The positions of the primary ionizations in the drift gap are reconstructed by knowing the drift velocity and the arrival time
  of the signal at the anode [7]." (pdf pp. 1-2) — [Bortone et al., Development and operation of the CGEM Inner Tracker for the
  BESIII experiment, arXiv:2212.10896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10896). TIGER gives one time per channel (TDC); no charge-
  weighted time is described.

## 4. µ-RWELL and GEM µTPC after 2020; Kuger 2017; Herrmann 2019 and the "about 30 %" coupling correction

### Takeaway
Herrmann 2019 (LMU; SM2 NSW module, APV25, cosmic-ray facility) is the source of the "coupling-correction factor about 30 %": the
factor is taken from Lösel's simulation and Flierl's analysis, not fitted; without it the first-to-last strip time difference
never reaches the value expected from the drift velocity, and even 40 % does not reach it; with it the reconstructed angle
distribution narrows and its MPV approaches the 21° reference, at the price of lost strips and tracks. Herrmann also gives the
mechanism in strip-count terms ("the time difference is biased for lower inclination due to the low number of affected strips").
Kuger 2017 (Würzburg) describes the µTPC and the inevitability of capacitive charge sharing between neighbouring strips but has
no angle-bias statement. The BESIII CGEM-IT, installed October 2024, has so far published only charge-centroid residuals versus
angle; the µTPC "will also be implemented". For µ-RWELL, Gnanvo et al. 2025 reject µTPC for ePIC (worst within 0-15°, needs fast
electronics and slow gas) in favour of thin gaps, and D'Angelo's 2024 ePIC slides list "Is it compatible with charge sharing?"
among the cons. An arXiv API query for micro-TPC/µTPC with "angle" or "bias" returns three papers, the newest from 2017; I found
no 2023-2026 arXiv paper reporting a µTPC angle offset or correction.

### Cited findings
- **Herrmann 2019, the factor and its origin.** "Another effect of the long strips is the capacitive-coupling of signals on
  neighboring strips. A correction is necessary and was implemented by [Lösel, 2013] and [Flierl, 2018]. The factor for the
  coupling-correction was determined to be about 30%. The implementation of this correction influences heavily the signal-shape."
  (pdf p. 90); "A correction factor of 30% is expected from simulation [Lösel, 2017] and showed reasonable results in previous
  analyses [Flierl, 2018]." (pdf p. 94) — [Herrmann 2019](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/25055/1/Herrmann_Maximilian_G.pdf).
- **Herrmann 2019, the uncorrected ladder is too short.** "Without correction no mean-time difference is in the range expected
  from the drift-velocity around 4.3·25 ns. For all signal-evaluation-points the expected effect of smaller time differences at
  low track-slopes is observed. Around a track-slope of about ±0.35, which corresponds to angles of about 20°, a constant
  difference is reconstructed. For larger slopes the projection of the track onto the readout-pattern should lead to more than
  four hit strips. Therefore the time difference is biased for lower inclination due to the low number of affected strips."
  (pdf p. 93, Fig. 7.10); "The left plot shows the influence on the inflection-time for various factors of the coupling
  correction. For higher values the mean time difference increases, but even for a factor of 40% the expected value is not
  reached. ... For increasing correction-factors less strips are reconstructed in the clusters." (pdf p. 94, Fig. 7.11); Fig.
  7.11 caption: "The decrease in the mean number of strips is expected to come from removing signals at the borders of the
  cluster which are only capacitively coupled." (pdf p. 94).
- **Herrmann 2019, effect on the angle.** "As can be seen for larger correction factors the width of the reconstructed
  angular-distribution is reduced. Also the mean and the most probable value are approaching the expected value of 21°. An
  undesired observation is the loss of reconstructed tracks." (pdf p. 96; tracks selected "with an inclination between 20° and
  22°", pdf p. 95); summary: "The correction for capacitive-coupling drastically improves this reconstruction. Angular-
  resolutions better than 10° are obtained averaged over the whole active area." (pdf p. 112), against "Much better resolutions
  in the order of 3° to 6° are expected from small chambers [Ntekas, 2016] and reconstructed by [Flierl, 2018] for module zero in
  the testbeam at H8." (pdf p. 96). The correction also removes a slope dependence of the µTPC residual (Fig. 7.21 caption: "For
  all evaluation-points the capacitive-coupling-correction reduces the dependence.", pdf p. 101) and improves the position
  resolution: "Therefore it can be concluded that the coupling-correction indeed improves the resolution." (pdf p. 98).
- **Kuger 2017, µTPC described, no bias.** "An alternative approach uses a TPC-like reconstruction algorithm, within the
  Micromegas community referred to as µ-TPC due to the thin drift volume. ... Accordingly, this method fails for perpendicular
  tracks with very small spatial separation and becomes more precise for steeper inclined tracks. It is, therefore, complementary
  to the centroid method." (Sec. 6.1.1, pdf pp. 132-133); "Ideally each resistive strip would couple capacitively only to its
  readout counterpart. Considering capacities between neighboring strips, a charge sharing is inevitable, but can be minimized by
  an accurate alignment of the strips on top of each other." (pdf p. 139, on the TQF misalignment prototype) — [Kuger, Signal
  Formation Processes in Micromegas Detectors and Quality Control for large size Detector Construction for the ATLAS New Small
  Wheel, PhD Würzburg 2017, arXiv:1708.01624](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01624). Text searches for "bias" and "inclin" find no
  µTPC angle-bias statement.
- **BESIII CGEM-IT 2025 status.** "Figure 2 (right) shows the residual distribution for the charge centroid as a function of the
  incident angle of the track. For tracks with incident angles below 5◦, the residual is about 200 µm. ... Moreover, the µTPC
  algorithm will also be implemented to extend these results to other incident angles." (pdf p. 4) — [Mezzadri, Spataro, The
  CGEM-IT of the BESIII experiment, PoS(EPS-HEP2025)551](https://pos.sissa.it/485/551/pdf). The TIGER chain is described in
  [Bortone 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10896) and [Melendi, PoS(ICHEP2024)1086](https://pos.sissa.it/476/1086/pdf); neither
  reports a µTPC angle result.
- **µ-RWELL, 2025 assessment.** "Past studies of MPGD trackers operating in µTPC mode[1, 2, 3] have shown that spatial resolution
  performance is worst for tracks perpendicular and almost perpendicular (within 0-15◦) to detector plane. ... Additionally, µTPC
  mode works effectively with fast electronics, which would increase the cost, and slower gases, which would degrade the detector
  timing performance." (pdf p. 2) — [Gnanvo et al., Development of Thin-Gap GEM-µRWELL Hybrid Detectors,
  arXiv:2506.17735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17735).
- **µ-RWELL, 2024 slides.** Cons listed for the µ-TPC algorithm: "Precise charge timing information", "A start timing
  information", "A fit for each strip signal", "A fit for each track", "Never systematically applied on 2D µ-Rwell detectors",
  "Never applied to 2D GEM- µ-Rwell detectors", "Very complicated for multiple tracks", "Is it compatible with charge sharing?"
  (slide 12) — [D'Angelo, ePIC collaboration meeting, 2024-07-26](https://indico.bnl.gov/event/20727/contributions/94283/attachments/56139/96072/uTPC_2024_07_26.pdf).
- **arXiv API.** Query `(all:"micro-TPC" OR all:"microTPC" OR all:"uTPC") AND (all:angle OR all:bias)` returned 3 entries:
  1707.04456 (Alexeev et al. 2017, GEM µTPC in magnetic field), 1310.5923 (neutron microTPC) and astro-ph/0403518 (gamma-ray
  µTPC). Nothing from 2018 onward under these terms.

### Derived numbers
- Herrmann's cosmic-ray angle resolution (<10° over 2 m²) against the 3-6° of small chambers means a 10°-class resolution would
  hide an angle offset of the size MX17 measures (2.5-3° at the sample median) in any single-chamber MPV comparison; this is an
  inference, not a statement in the thesis.

### Gaps
- Herrmann's MPV shifts (Fig. 7.15) and the Fig. 7.10/7.11 time differences exist as figures; the text gives "about 20°", "21°",
  "4.3·25 ns" and "40%" only.
- The 30 % factor is a simulation value (Lösel 2017) adopted, never measured on SM2 in the thesis.
- No published µTPC angle-bias number for TIGER-read CGEM-IT or for any µ-RWELL; the BESIII 2024 cosmic campaign and the 2025
  commissioning (PoS 485/551 and the 2026 indico abstract) report centroid results only.
- Kuger's thesis is not a µTPC source; it is relevant only for the sharing statement and the TQF misalignment study.

## 5. MPGD2024 poster by Fang (USTC): a diffusion-based time correction for strip µTPCs

### Takeaway
Zhujun Fang, Jiajun Tang, Hang Zhou and Siqi He (USTC) presented "Study on the bias analysis and correction in MPGD μTPC mode"
as a poster at MPGD2024 (Hefei, 13-18 October 2024). Their claim is exactly the first-electron mechanism: each strip's time is
the time at which the first of several primary electrons crosses threshold, so with more than one electron per strip "the
measured time is ahead of the real drift time", the reconstructed track is "lower" than the true one and the segment midpoint is
biased by roughly one spatial resolution (~100 µm), normally hidden in alignment. The correction uses the fitted incidence angle
and the strip charge to shift each strip time from a transverse-plus-longitudinal diffusion analysis; in simulation it partly
removes the midpoint bias and improves the resolution by about 15 % (abstract), or reduces the spatial bias by 40-75 % with only a
slight resolution gain (poster). No journal or arXiv version exists as of this search. This is the closest published analogue to
MX17's "diffusion, not coupling" attribution, phrased as a position bias rather than an angle bias.

### Cited findings
- **Abstract (aconf.org, read in the fetched HTML).** "The induced signal of any readout channel may come from several primary
  ionized electrons that arrive at the readout plane at different times, but the measured signal time is usually the time of the
  first electron signal exceeds the energy threshold. When the number of electrons is larger than 1, the measured time is ahead
  of the real drift time. Thus, most of the measured time of readout channels are ahead of expected, the reconstructed center
  point of the track segment is also biased. However, this effect is not significant, and the deviation of the bias is related to
  the detector structure, the electronic system parameters, the gas parameters, and the track incidence angle, which is roughly
  around 1 times of the spatial resolution, such as 100 μm. In the experiment, this bias is often covered by the alignment
  deviation. In order to correct this bias, we analyzed the measurement principle of μTPC method, and proposed the correction
  method based on electron transverse and longitudinal diffusion analysis. Using the initially fitted track incidence angle and
  the measured charge of readout channel as input, the signal time of each readout channel can be corrected, and more accurate
  reconstruction performance is obtained. The simulation results show that this method can partially correct the position bias of
  the track segment mid-point and improve the spatial resolution by about 15% at the same time. The experimental data based on
  the cylindrical μRGroove detector is also being analyzed, and the preliminary result also presents an improve the μTPC method
  performance." — [MPGD2024 abstract 69](https://www.aconf.org/conf_192113/abstract/69.html); keywords "μTPC, bias, time
  correction, MPGD"; track "Simulations and software".
- **Poster (single page, read).** Section 2: "Strip signal consists of more than 1 primary electrons"; "Signal of 1st electron
  determines strip time, having a in-advanced time bias"; "The reconstructed track is "lower" than real track, introducing
  spatial bias". Section 3, parameters that influence the bias: "Track incident angle", "Drift length", "Gas property (electron
  drift velocity, diffusion coefficient)", "Magnetic field", "Strip width and pitch", "Timing resolution of electronic system".
  Fig. 4: "Relationship between time bias and drift length, with different incident angle" (curves for θ = 20° and 45°). Fig. 5:
  µTPC against charge-centroid resolution at 10° for "Ideal electronics 1 fC threshold" and "Normal electronics baseline RMS=0.3
  fC peaking RMS=0.2 fC time RMS=3 ns 1.9 fC threshold". Fig. 8: "Time advance matrix. Left: 10 deg angle. Right: 45 deg angle."
  (axes: "Primary electron number in the strip" 0-10 against "Electron drift length (mm)" 0-5; "Signal time in advance (ns)").
  Section 4 results: "(Planar detector) Drift time correction based on electron diffusion analysis: Decrease spatial bias by
  40%-75%; Slightly improve spatial resolution"; "(Cylindrical detector, with magnetic field): Strip position correction based on
  empirical formula: ID = ID0 ± (1/2 − 1/(N+1)) · f_w(θ)", "N is the primary electron number", "f_w(θ) is the weighting field-
  related parameter", "Decrease spatial bias significantly, with 1.4 GeV/c muon". Section 5: "Theoretically, the μTPC mode has
  bias in spatial reconstruction, which is easily covered by the assembly error in planar detectors. In cylindrical detector, it
  is not negligible." and "New algorithms are developed to correct the bias, and should be optimized to cover all track incident
  angle range and cases." — [poster PDF on CERN indico](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1453371/contributions/6176710/attachments/2947474/5180061/zhujun%20fang.pdf).
- **Context (snippets, unverified).** The cylindrical µRGroove prototype is 131 mm in diameter and 100 mm long, with >95 %
  efficiency and <100 µm resolution for vertical tracks (companion MPGD2024 talk); a December 2024 DRD1 talk reports about
  330 µm at 1 T with an incomplete Lorentz-angle correction.

### Derived numbers
- The poster's 40-75 % reduction of the spatial bias and the abstract's "about 15 %" resolution gain are both simulation numbers;
  the midpoint bias itself is given only as "roughly around 1 times of the spatial resolution, such as 100 μm". Nothing to
  convert to a tan ratio: the bias is stated for the segment midpoint, not the angle. Fig. 7 ("Xerror and Zerror distribution with
  various incident angle") would hold the angle information; its values are in the figure only.

### Gaps
- No paper. The indico contribution page itself is behind the CERN bot check; the attached poster PDF and the aconf abstract were
  readable.
- The poster's time-advance values (Fig. 8) and bias-versus-drift-length curves (Fig. 4) are not given numerically.
- Whether the correction changes the reconstructed angle (as opposed to the midpoint) is not stated.

## 6. LArTPC: a hit-TIME bias versus track angle

### Takeaway
Still not found. Five targeted searches (terms: "hit time" bias, "peak time" shift, θxz/"angle to the wire plane", "earlier
arrival", "dx correction", "drift coordinate correction", for MicroBooNE, ICARUS, SBND, ProtoDUNE, DUNE, LArSoft) returned only
width/charge dependence, hit-finder definitions and t0/drift-offset material. The LArTPC literature defines the hit time as the
Gaussian peak (collection) or the mean of the bipolar peaks (induction, in one laser study) and reports angle dependence of
signal shape, width and charge, never of the fitted time. This confirms the negative result in `lartpc.md` with a wider net.

### Cited findings
- **Definition in use (snippet, unverified).** LArIAT: hit peaks "are fit with a Gaussian function and the best fit parameters are
  stored, such as the peak time, height, width, and area under the Gaussian fit. ... the peak time is proportional to the
  coordinate in the drift direction where the ionization occurred." — [LArIAT detector paper,
  arXiv:1911.10379](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.10379).
- **Induction-plane time (snippet, unverified).** In MicroBooNE's laser analysis "the time of the highest peak amplitude is
  defined as the hit time in the collection plane, while the average of the two bipolar peak times is taken as the hit time in
  the induction planes" — [MicroBooNE UV-laser field paper, arXiv:1910.01430](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01430).
- **Angle dependence stated for shape, not time (snippets, unverified).** "This procedure reduces the signal as well as the
  noise, in a manner that depends on the angle of the track or shower with respect to the drift field." — [ProtoDUNE-SP TDR,
  arXiv:1706.07081](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.07081); ICARUS CPAD 2025: signal shape "depends strongly on the track angle to
  the wire plane" — [OSTI 3018560](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/3018560); MicroBooNE's wire-modification systematics vary
  hit charge Q and width σ as functions of position and angle, with no time variable — [arXiv:2111.03556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03556).
- **MicroBooNE dQ/dx (snippet, unverified).** The calibration splits crossing muons into |θXZ| bins and finds "very little
  angular dependence in the calibrated sample" — [arXiv:1907.11736](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11736). This is a charge
  statement; it says nothing about hit times.

### Derived numbers
None.

### Gaps
- DUNE/SBN DocDB, LArSoft validation pages and the GausHitFinder code were not examined; a hit-time study could exist there
  unpublished.
- No LArTPC paper read compares peak, charge-weighted and leading-edge hit times on inclined tracks (unchanged from `lartpc.md`).

## 7. Silicon strips with timing: neighbour-strip time shifts in AC-LGADs

### Takeaway
No paper found that measures a threshold-crossing time shift in a neighbouring AC-LGAD strip caused by inter-strip crosstalk.
The nearest published statement is Shekar et al. (2025/26): hit-position-dependent time delays from the finite propagation
velocity in the resistive layer, corrected offline with a position map; the same paper combines the leading and sub-leading
strip times with amplitude-squared weights. Bishop et al. (2024) recorded time of arrival along and across strips but defer the
result to a future publication. One citation at most was asked for; use Shekar et al.

### Cited findings
- "We also note that there are hit-position dependent time delays originating from the finite signal propagation velocity in
  the resistive layer and wirebonds only at the end of the centimeter-long strips. These delays are corrected offline by creating
  a reference map of correction values across the sensor's active area." (Sec. 2.3.2, pdf p. 5); multi-channel timestamp
  t = (a1² t1 + a2² t2) / (a1² + a2²) "where a1/2 and t1/2 are the amplitude and ToA of the leading/sub-leading channels" (Eq. 3,
  pdf p. 5) — [Shekar, Nanda, Ye, Heller, Apresyan, Studying AC-LGAD strip sensors from laser and testbeam measurements,
  arXiv:2511.14095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14095).
- "In addition, the rise time and fall time and the time of arrival for scans along and across the strips were recorded and will
  be the topic of a future publication." (Sec. 2, pdf p. 4) — [Bishop et al., Long-Distance Signal Propagation in AC-LGAD,
  arXiv:2403.01052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01052).
- (snippet, unverified) UCSC slides (Mazza) quote a time-of-arrival delay of about 2 ps/µm perpendicular to the strip; Ott et al.
  (NIM A 2023, OSTI 1913808, not retrieved) report neighbour-strip charge from "induction of signal charge and subsequent
  re-sharing over the n+ layer".

### Derived numbers
None.

### Gaps
- The Ott et al. 2023 paper (BNL-223886) did not download from OSTI; its arXiv ID was not found.
- The effect asked about (a crosstalk-induced shift of the neighbour's threshold crossing) is not isolated in any AC-LGAD paper
  found; AC-LGAD work treats neighbour signals as wanted shared signal and corrects delays by position.
