# Others saw steepening; long tracks hid it

"No one else has this problem" is wrong for strip micro-TPCs and right, in a limited sense, only for large TPCs. **Most one-time-per-strip micro-TPCs that publish the comparison report reconstructed tracks too steep**, with the size shrinking as the angle grows (one NSW test-beam peak and most layers of one Ne:CF4 chamber are exceptions); they name it capacitive coupling, charge sharing or edge-strip mis-assignment and treat it with filters and calibrations. Large pad TPCs and LArTPCs have transverse diffusion comparable to their readout pitch, as MX17 does, but a full-drift track spans 7 to 110 times more mixing widths at equal angle, and pad TPCs also use a charge-weighted time. MX17's bias is therefore a known effect in an unusually well-measured place, not a new phenomenon. What survives the survey is narrower: a per-track slope ratio against an independent telescope, a measured split of the bias into diffusion, footprint, strip-end geometry and RC, and a per-event forward fit of a depth-resolved charge profile. The apparent conflict between "lever arm is strongest" (pad and LAr notes) and "lever arm fails for MX17" (strip note) mostly dissolves: MX17's headline 1.19/1.37 comes from tracks at |tan θ| of about 0.05-0.3, not the 0.3-0.6 assumed in that comparison, and binned by angle MX17 sits on the same curve as the published strip chambers. Paper II must credit the strip "too steep" literature and the LArTPC 2D deconvolution and diffusion work, and must not say "first" for the effect or the model equation. Every absence below is a negative result of a limited search, not proof of absence.

## The effect is a family trait, and only one family lacks the conditions for it

The mechanism has a precise form. A strip's waveform holds charge from several drift depths, because diffusion, the prompt footprint and (on MX17's Y view only) RC spreading put neighbouring depths on it, and a single time then pulls toward the earliest substantial charge. On the bench gas, **Ar/iC4H10 95/5 with about 1 % water** (`docs/PARAMETERS.md`; not Ar/CF4/isobutane as the research briefs stated), det3 hit ladders read 1.19 (X) and 1.37 (Y) times the slope of the M3 telescope, and the pass model reproduces this at 1.21 and 1.43 with no RC term on X (`papers/paper2/hits_daq/P0_XBIAS_2026-11-05.md`). A "metallic" arm keeps 1.14-1.22 of it. In ladder terms 1.19 is a 16 % compression and 1.37 is 27 %.

Every Λ₀.₃ is **derived** from cited inputs; no source states it. The lever-arm argument itself is also ours: no source makes it.

## One definition makes the notes agree, and shrinks the gap to large TPCs

The notes quote MX17's ratio as 0.04-0.08, 0.07 and 9.5 %. All three are σ_T/(L tan θ), the same formula with different inputs, and the differences have traceable causes. The 0.04-0.08 range spans tan θ = 0.6 to 0.3 at D_T = 350-400 µm/√cm. The 0.07 is the pad note's toy, whose D² = 0.012 mm corresponds to 346 µm/√cm at tan θ = 0.3. The 9.5 % takes σ_T ≈ 0.8 mm from the research brief and `RECONSTRUCTION_BASIS.md`, which would need D_T ≈ 480 µm/√cm and exceeds what the fit gives.

The definition used here is **Λ(θ) = D_T√L / (L tan θ)**, with L the drift extent of the fitted column at full drift, tan θ measured from the drift axis, and diffusion only, since footprints are not known for the other detectors. Reference angle tan θ = 0.3. For det3 the fitted Dp = 0.023 mm/√ns (`docs/PARAMETERS.md`, stated to agree with Magboltz on det3 and det4) and v = 36.8 µm/ns give D_T = 379 µm/√cm, σ_T(cathode) = 0.63 mm and **Λ₀.₃ = 0.076 (derived)**. Adding the 0.30 mm footprint in quadrature gives 0.084, but that version has no counterpart elsewhere.

On this definition the MX17 advantage over large TPCs is smaller than the 30-70× in the LAr note, which used 9.5 %. MX17's Λ₀.₃ is about 16× ALICE, 14× STAR, 7× T2K, 25-38× MicroBooNE, 34-54× ProtoDUNE-SP, 23-34× ICARUS, 13-20× ArgoNeuT and 38-110× ILD (all derived; the LAr D_T range of 5-12 cm²/s comes from [ICARUS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11925), and the T2K factor drops to about 3 for its 15 cm test-beam drift). Comparability has limits. Strip µTPC values use a D_T measured in only some cases: NSW has [365 µm/√cm](https://inspirehep.net/files/362b665c04e2575c975ef950e4a5c63b) and BESIII about [350](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.06548), while the LMU floating-strip and Flierl values assume 365. MIMAC, NEWAGE, DRIFT, BEAST, ACTAR and MAIKo cannot be placed at all because no D_T was found. Λ is also the wrong quantity for GridPix, which has no aggregation, and for IXPE, which has no drift-time ladder in the passages read.

σ_T/pitch does not separate the families. ALICE reaches 0.58-0.87 across its pads and [MicroBooNE 0.51-0.78](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06551) at full drift (derived), against MX17's 0.75-0.86. The older repo explanation that pads are "much wider than the diffusion" holds for T2K only, and a reviewer note in `papers/paper2/hits_daq/LITERATURE_MIXING.md` already corrected it.

## The lever-arm tension is mostly a sample mismatch

The strip note concluded that MX17 has the smallest Λ of the group and yet a larger bias, so lever arm cannot be the whole story. That comparison paired the 1.19/1.37 with tan θ = 0.3-0.6, a range inherited from `LITERATURE_MIXING.md`. It is not the sample. P0 states its planes are inclined by |tan θ| ≲ 0.3, and of 6,191 det3 Run 1 X planes only 157 lie above 0.3, with the median near 0.11 (`results/ana1106_tiers_sat_det3.json`). The headline ratio belongs at Λ of roughly 0.1-0.3, not 0.04-0.08.

Binning by angle shows this directly. The table is computed from the repo's per-|tan θ| medians (`ana1106_tiers_sat_det3.json` for data against M3, `ana1106_resim_score.json` for re-simulation with exact truth). The ratios are **derived** by converting each bin's median angle error at the bin centre, so they are approximate.

The published points then fall on this curve. [Flierl's](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/22198/1/Flierl_Bernhard.pdf) GEM chamber gives 1.28 at Λ of about 0.15 and 5.4 strips (15°, most probable value) and 1.08 at 0.07 and 11.5 strips (30°, mean), against MX17 X data of 1.30 at 0.15 and about 1.09 near 0.07. [Bortfeldt's](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/16972/1/Bortfeldt_Jonathan.pdf) floating strip chamber at 10° gives 1.52 at Λ about 0.27 and 2.1 strips, below MX17's 1.6-1.8 there. All Λ and tan ratios here are derived, the LMU values rest on an assumed D_T, and those analyses quote a peak or mean angle where we quote a median ratio. The agreement is suggestive, not a measurement, but it removes the claim that MX17 is anomalous.

Lever arm and end effect are not rivals, because **within one detector they are collinear**. Λ and pitch/(L tan θ) both scale as 1/tan θ, so no angle scan can separate them, and the same is true of a Flierl-versus-MX17 comparison at equal strip count. The switch-off arms do separate them. At |tan θ| = 0.15 on X, removing diffusion alone cuts the excess over 1 from 0.24 to 0.12, and leaving only geometry gives 0.07. Diffusion is therefore about half the excess, and a strip-column end term, present without any diffusion, is about a quarter. The remainder is the footprint and its combination with diffusion, as P0 found.

Depth resolves it further. Mid-column (0.2-0.8 of the depth, |tan θ| ≥ 0.15) the data ladder compresses only about 4 % and the model and metallic arms 7-8 %, while the end bins carry +84 ns at the mesh end and −65 ns at the cathode end. A point-like, no-diffusion arm already bends by +62 and −44 ns, so only about +20 and −25 ns of the end shift is mixing (`papers/paper2/hits_daq/HITS_DAQ_PLAN_2026-11-05.md`). This answers why the families differ: **in a µTPC the column ends are in every fit**, while on a metre-long track they are a fraction of a percent of the hits. The pad toy reproduces the pattern, giving a mid-track ratio of 1.000 for a charge-weighted mean and 1.036 for an early quantile at MX17, but only 1.06-1.09 overall against 1.14-1.22 from the full simulation, so it is a scaling tool only.

Three items remain open. Λ cannot be the sufficient condition, since MicroBooNE's 3 cm segment at full drift has Λ of about 0.17-0.26 (derived) and GridPix has 0.072 with no published bias: the second is explained by construction, the first is simply untested. [Scholer's](https://inspirehep.net/files/362b665c04e2575c975ef950e4a5c63b) NSW test-beam peak sits at the true 28° at Λ of about 0.10, while his sharing-off simulation peaks +7° high, an unresolved tension inside one thesis, and [Klitzner](https://www.etp.physik.uni-muenchen.de/publications/theses/download/master_fklitzner.pdf) finds most layers slightly below the expected angle in Ne:CF4. The pitch scan `hits_daq/gang_scan.py` has since been run (`results/gang_scan.json`): summing strips increases the compression at every angle, and most of the growth is already present with no diffusion, i.e. the strip-end term (see the plan's 2026-11-08 entry).

## The four hypotheses each explain a different family

**(a) Reconstruction axis: partly right, and misdescribed.** Large pad TPCs do not use time slices. They build one charge-weighted (pad, time) cluster per row, as [STAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/nucl-ex/0301015) and [ALICE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.1950) describe, so the estimator is a centroid in time, not a slice centroid in position. LAr production uses both per-wire Gaussian hits ([ProtoDUNE-SP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.06722)) and per-slice imaging ([Wire-Cell](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04850)), and no slope bias is reported for the per-wire chain, so the per-channel axis alone does not produce a visible bias. An analytic argument in the pad note shows a charge-weighted time has a depth-independent offset, leaving the interior slope unchanged, while an early-arrival time compresses it. [MIMAC's](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.11812) per-slice barycentre still shows deviating end slices, so a time-slice axis does not fix the ends. T2K's time of maximum shows the estimator does not by itself explain the absence there, because T2K does not fit a time ladder. **The charge-weighted time has not been tested on MX17 data.** The repo's "estimator-independent" result covers leading edge, CFD, matched filter, peak, threshold, TOT, binary and Fermi-Dirac (`emulate.py`), and none of those is a charge-weighted mean. The toy's 1.06 for all strips is only a prediction.

**(b) Lever arm: supported as the main separator between families, and necessary but not sufficient.** It orders the families by one to two orders of magnitude, and within MX17 the excess falls by more than a factor of ten from Λ = 0.30 to 0.061. It does not describe a GridPix, which lacks aggregation, or a NEWAGE-type detector, whose resolution hides it. No paper states the argument, so it is Paper II's to make as an inference.

**(c) Same effect, another name: true for strip µTPCs, true for LAr only in width and charge, weak for pad TPCs.** [Bortfeldt](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.00802) writes that "measured signal timing is shifted towards earlier values" for strips hit by electrons created near the cathode. [Scholer](https://inspirehep.net/files/362b665c04e2575c975ef950e4a5c63b) writes that "Adding CS pulls the time measurements more towards the mean time", which is ladder compression stated in time units. [Alexeev](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.06253) adds transverse diffusion as a second cause, and [Flierl](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/22198/1/Flierl_Bernhard.pdf) describes signals appearing simultaneous because the first electrons already occupy the full cluster width. Bortfeldt also records the velocity symptom, drift velocities reconstructed too large, which matches MX17's hit-ladder v of 44-51 µm/ns against 36. BEAST's [canoe shape](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.06657) is the same family at the pixel level, unquantified. In pad TPCs the named terms ([angular pad](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.0982), [tan²α](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.1950), E×B) are variances, and the resistive-anode bias of [Dixit's group](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0510085) is in plane. Nothing there is a drift-coordinate compression.

**(d) Few check against an external angle at this precision: supported, with one correction.** Drift scales are checked well: [STAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/nucl-ex/0301015) and [ALICE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.1950) use lasers and silicon at about 0.1 %. But a track-based scale calibration absorbs a multiplicative compression into an effective velocity, and [GasPix's](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.00480) telescope-based z(t) calibration likewise absorbs any compression. Strip µTPCs compare with a set chamber angle, typically quoted as [(10 ± 1)°](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/16972/1/Bortfeldt_Jonathan.pdf), which is itself about 10 % in tan θ at that angle (derived). MAIKo's [up-down halves](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.02287) are blind to a common scale error, and [D3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.7013) states it cannot collimate in θ without absolute z. MIMAC has true external kinematics, but at [10-16° resolution](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.12842) a 3-4° bias would be hard to see. The comparison of per-track slope against a telescope, at the level MX17 makes it, was not found in any public source read; internal ATLAS and BESIII notes are inaccessible.

## Paper II can claim the measurement and the method, not the effect

LArTPC work constrains the novelty claim most. [Adams et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08709) write the raw signal as charge convolved with field and electronics response in wire and time, and solve it in two dimensions; [ProtoDUNE-SP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.06722) uses the same form. "Nobody models this as a convolution in channel and time" would be false. The differences are real but must be stated as differences: their kernel omits diffusion, which stays in the deconvolved image, their solver is Fourier with Wiener filters, and the one per-event regularised fit, [L1SP](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.02583), is 1D and region-specific. [MicroBooNE 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06551) states the mechanism for width: adjacent clouds overlap under D_T "causing additional" time smearing that grows with θxz. Its null test at θxz = ±6° does not transfer to large angle, because the expected effect there is small from kinematics alone (derived). [ICARUS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11925) tunes waveforms in angle bins, and [Adams](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08709) notes charge from neighbouring wires "stretched in time" for large θxz. [T2K ERAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06541) separates undelayed sharing from delayed RC spreading, models multi-channel waveforms for RC and gain maps ([X-ray fit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04481)), and has flagged a global waveform fit as under study.

The strip µTPC record must be credited as the direct precedent for the bias: Bortfeldt (+5° at 10° measured, +9° in an LTspice model), Flierl (the 15-20 % time-bin neighbour subtraction, the closest published correction), Sessa, Vanadia, Rinnagel, Scholer, Alexeev and Farinelli. Scholer's sharing-off +7° in a diffusion-on simulation is the closest published analogue of the "metallic keeps most of it" result, and he leaves its cause open, so it should be cited as such rather than claimed as confirmation.

The safe wording is: *"Single-time-per-strip µTPC reconstruction is known to read tracks too steep, an effect attributed to capacitive coupling and edge strips. We measure it per track against an independent telescope, show that it is dominated by same-time mixing from diffusion and the prompt footprint with a further strip-end term, that it would persist on a metallic readout, and that a per-event forward fit of the depth-resolved waveform removes it. We did not find this decomposition or such a fit in the strip µTPC literature in a limited search."* The last clause is a negative search result and should be labelled so. Coverage was partial: none of the about 21 LAr papers was read cover to cover, theses, DUNE/SBN notes and ND-LAr were not covered, ALEPH and PEP-4 were read only at abstract or slide level, and Lösel, Alexopoulos 2019, Bradt and Mauss were not retrieved.

Some current wording should change. The claim in `papers/paper2/OUTLINE_PLAN_2026-11-05.md` that the mixing is "on resistive-strip micro-TPCs" and made of "delayed copies" is the retired explanation that P0 overturned; the X view has no RC term. "Whatever the estimator" needs the qualifier "of those tested", since a charge-weighted time is untested. "~4° too steep" holds only near normal incidence (3.5-4.2° below |tan θ| of 0.1); the sample median is 2.5° X and 3.1° Y. "20-30 %" describes 16 % X and 27 % Y. Avoid "only MX17 sees this", any direct numerical comparison with Scholer's test beam, and any statement about NSW resistive strips against metallic strips, since no controlled comparison exists in print. The SPS stage-angle point (Y hits over waveform fit 1.07-1.13 at 25.4°) is relative to the waveform fit in CO₂ rows, so it cannot join the bench curve without caveat.

## Conclusion

The useful reframing is that this is not a question of who has the bias but of who could see it. The bias requires one aggregated time per channel, a column short enough that mixing width and strip-end terms are a visible fraction of it, and a reference that does not absorb the result. Large TPCs fail the second condition by one to two orders of magnitude, and most directional detectors fail the third; strip µTPCs meet all three and did report it, under other names. MX17's contribution is the external reference and the decomposition, and those are what the paper should lead with.

One analysis would most sharpen this, using existing data only: add a charge-weighted time family to `hits_daq/tiers.py` and the re-simulation, and read the pitch scan (`results/gang_scan.json`, done 2026-11-08). The first converts the charge-weighted case from a prediction to a result; the second breaks the collinearity between lever arm and strip-end term that no comparison across the literature can break.
