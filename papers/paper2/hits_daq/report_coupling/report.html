Capacitive coupling and the micro-TPC bias on MX17

Chamber A (det3), bench cosmics, Runs 1 and 2, existing waveforms. Code: papers/paper2/hits_daq/coupling/. Literature: reports/Capacitive coupling in strip microTPCs.md.

Verdict. A pure capacitive replacement for σ₀ does not describe chamber A, but same-time sharing is a large part of the compression, and part of it behaves like coupling.
  • Removing σ₀ works less well than it first looks. With σ₀ at 33 µm the fit asks for k₁ = 0.13 (X) and 0.11 (Y). But data generated with no coupling, only the 0.3 mm Gaussian, gives k₁ = 0.10 and 0.09 in the same fit, so most of that number is a Gaussian in another language.
  • Position-in-strip dependence rejects pure coupling. It predicts a far neighbour fixed near 0.13; the data are 0.07–0.09 on X, and on the near side the pure-coupling fit is too low by 30 % at the strip centre.
  • A flat floor is still there: the X far neighbour stays at 0.07–0.09 where a Gaussian falls to 0.04. That caps a same-time nearest-neighbour term at about 7 % on X and 9 % on Y, with a smooth width of about 0.25 mm still needed on X.
  • Same-time sharing matters for the bias. With σ₀ removed, X compression goes from 1.043 (geometry) to 1.115 with diffusion only, 1.115 with coupling only, and 1.188 with both (data 1.162): equal shares on X. It is not negligible, and our text must not say so.
  • The −2 strip is mostly electronics. It reads 1.3 % (X) within one chip and 0.4 ± 0.1 % when the strip sits across a chip or connector boundary.
Physics we cannot supply: nothing we can calculate gives a 7 % same-time term. Direct strip-to-strip capacitance estimates at 1–2 % and is derivative-shaped, and the 31.2 mm resistive-strip beat that could give more is not seen. So the data fix the size of the same-time sharing better than they fix its origin.

1. What "capacitive coupling" physically is

The phrase covers separate mechanisms. They differ in time structure, in net charge and in what sets their size, and a citation for one does not support another.

2. What a coupling does to a track

Take a same-time copy, Si(t) → Si(t) + κ[Si−1(t) + Si+1(t)]. On an inclined track the strip i's neighbours are one earlier and one later by Δt = pitch/w.

  • Interior strips: both neighbours contribute. A leading-edge time moves earlier by the same amount on every interior strip, so the interior ladder stays straight: its slope is not changed to first order.
  • Track ends: the end strip has a neighbour only on one side, and the strip beyond the end receives only a copy of it, at the neighbour's time (the "ghost hit"). Ends and ghosts rotate the fitted line. This is why the effect is an end effect, why it shrinks with the number of strips (roughly 1/N² in slope, the form Bortfeldt's curve follows) and why cutting edge strips helps.
  • Diffusion behaves differently: charge from a range of depths reaches a strip, and the spread grows with depth, so it can also act on the interior. Both act one-sidedly at the ends. So the angle dependence alone cannot tell them apart, as the literature review found; depth and position-in-strip can.

In the σ₀-removed arms, coupling and diffusion each add about 0.07 on X. With σ₀ kept, the same term falls to 0.058 of the 0.148 excess. So the size of "coupling's share" depends on how same-time sharing is parametrised. What is firm is that same-time sharing, by any name, is a substantial part of the X bias. The time shift sits at the track ends and beyond them, as the argument in section 2 expects. In the middle 60 % of the track, data move about half as much as any arm with diffusion, close to the arm with coupling only. At and beyond the ends, the data need diffusion. This is one chamber and the difference inside the track is about 5 ns in 780 ns.

6. Wording for the paper

"Single-time-per-strip reconstruction reads tracks too steep. The literature attributes this to capacitive coupling and edge strips; those studies have not separated coupling from diffusion and geometry. In our chamber, the bias arises from same-time charge sharing between neighbouring strips combined with depth mixing by drift diffusion, each comparable on the view across the resistive strips' direction [X], with resistive spreading added on the other. The same-time sharing cannot be a pure nearest-neighbour coupling of the size needed to replace the prompt width: its dependence on the position in the strip requires a smooth component and caps a same-time floor at about 7–9 %. Its physical origin is not identified."

7. What this does not rule out

  • Only chamber A. The strength of coupling depends on the board stack and the electronics, so other chambers may differ.
  • Near-normal samples for sections 3 and the track held at the pass fit for the joint fits; the pass itself was calibrated with no coupling.
  • The bottom-up circuit estimate uses an assumed board stack and DREAM impedances; a factor of 3–5 either way is not excluded.
  • Non-Gaussian footprints with long tails are not excluded by the floor; Lorentzian tails match the −2 strip on X but not Y.
  • The fall of the −1 ratio with amplitude and the data lying below all models near the strip centre are unexplained.
  • Noise correlations could not be tested with the saved windows.
