\subsection{What ``detected'' means}\label{sec:detected}
Denominator: one clean reference ray ($\chi^2<1$, four clusters) whose footprint lies inside the measured active area
(the whole chamber: the edge cut changes the efficiency by ${\le}0.9$\,pt, so the headline numbers use the full area
and \cref{sec:edge} studies the edges). Detected: both views fitted, ${\le}2$ competing strips per view, and the
reconstructed track within $R$ of the reference ray at the mesh. When the chamber reconstructs several tracks, the event is
detected if one of them is the reference track (multi-track credit; this is the default number). Nothing is removed from the denominator: tracks far from the reference with the right angle (0.16\,\%) are only half
explained by muons scattering between the reference doublets, so they count as losses with everything else we cannot pin on
the reference.
\textbf{The radius.} We use $R=5$\,mm, the knee of $\varepsilon(R)$: nine core $\sigma$ of the residual at the mesh,
where the accidental rate (event mixing) is 0.07\,\%, 70$\times$ below the inefficiency; $R\in[4,8]$\,mm moves the
efficiency by $-0.5/{+0.7}$\,pt (systematic). A muon is found if the candidate nearest to the reference ray in each view,
whichever the reconstruction ranks first, is within $R$. The definition, the choice of $R$ and the full residual
distributions are in \cref{app:effdef}.
\textbf{Area.} Chamber~A, Runs 1 and 2: full active area / interior (${\ge}30$\,mm from the $x$ sides, ${\ge}8$\,mm from
the $y$ sides) / ${\ge}30$\,mm from every side: production pick 91.5 / 92.3 / 92.3\,\%; single-track (defined below) 94.8 / 95.7 /
95.8\,\%. The angles need the interior (\cref{sec:edge}); the efficiency does not.

\textbf{What the HV buys on chamber~A: time, not efficiency.} Across its 6-27 scan (\cref{fig:hvmetrics}) the
gain grows by a factor 13 from 425 to 490\,V: the median fitted track charge doubles every 18.8\,V (fitted below
465\,V, where no track saturates), and the fraction of tracks with a strip at the ADC rail rises from 0 to 30\,\%.
Over the same range the efficiency (90--94\,\%), the angle resolution ($s_{68}$ 0.91--1.03\textdegree{} X,
0.80--0.96\textdegree{} Y), the angle scale (fitted against the reference $\tan$: 0.99--1.02 in both views, up to 510\,V; \cref{fig:hvmetrics_extra}) and
the position residual core at the mesh (0.43--0.56\,mm, reference pointing included) are flat. The time resolution is not:
the uncorrelated part $(t_X-t_Y)/\sqrt2$ falls from 109\,ns at 425\,V to 23\,ns at 490\,V, the event time from 105 to
42\,ns. In a TPC time is depth: at $v\approx35$\,\textmu m/ns that is 3.9\,mm against 0.8\,mm. The improvement is statistically firm ($109.4\pm4.5$ against $23.2\pm0.7$\,ns, 19 standard errors) but it is a property of the measurement: binned in the early amplitude, the strip sum at the rising-edge cut that the time is read from, every HV point lies on one curve ($\sigma\propto q^{-0.7}$; \cref{fig:hvmetrics_extra}), and in a fixed amplitude window the time barely changes with HV (1000--1600\,ADC: $44\pm5$\,ns at 435\,V, $30\pm4$ at 490\,V; $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}=1.5$ against a constant). The gain moves the typical track along the noise-over-slope curve, nothing else.
\textbf{Why the angle does not need the gain and the time does.} The two come from different parts of the
signal. The time is read from the start of the charge column (\cref{sec:timing}), the first electrons, made next to
the mesh: a small part of the signal, whose timing degrades as it nears the noise. The angle is fitted to the whole
column, every strip and every sample. Two measurements show it. Track by track, pooling the scan points below
510\,V and keeping unsaturated tracks, the angle residual to the reference does not depend on the track's charge
($s_{68}$ 0.82--0.94\textdegree{} in X and 0.72--0.87\textdegree{} in Y over the ten charge deciles, a factor 17
in charge, with no rise at low charge), while the time resolution of tracks binned the same way falls with the early
amplitude as noise over slope (\crefp{fig:timing}{b}). The fit's own angle error does not move either (median
$\sigma_{\tan}$ 0.014--0.021 at every HV up to 510\,V), and the angle scale does not move with the gain, so a lower gain adds no
tilt. Two limits: the $s_{68}$ includes the reference angle error and the scattering, which could hide a small change in the
chamber's own term; and it holds down to 425\,V, $1/13$ of the operating gain, below which the scan does not go.

\subsection{Chamber~A: single-track efficiency}\label{sec:single}
\textbf{The selection.} A ``single-track'' sample selected by chamber~A itself is biased: a failure that looks like two
tracks would leave the sample by its own failure. An external tag does not help on the bench: reference sees a second straight
track in 2 of the 276 events where chamber~A sees two particles, because those particles are local (soft electrons and
secondaries at large angles). We use the two views instead: a real second particle is ionisation in the gas, seen in both
views with the same charge (the X/Y charge ratio of one particle is known to 6\,\%). An event leaves the single-track
sample only if it carries such a second particle, in one of three forms: (i) a separate, charge-matched segment in each
view, not attached to the muon; (ii) two segments in one view and, in the other, a single segment that is the two merged
in projection: wider than a single track (median 15--25 strips against 12), carrying the charge of both, or placed
between them within the 12\,mm seed gap; (iii) a clean single-track muon in both views plus a separate deposit, which
together trip the discharge veto (${>}50$ strips). Everything else stays in the sample and counts against the chamber.
\textbf{Result.} Over the full active area (Runs 1 and 2: 29\,398 reference tracks, of which 931 come with a second particle in the chamber
(\cref{sec:pairing}); the 28\,467 \emph{single-track} reference tracks left, which are all the rays but the second-particle events):
$\varepsilon_\mathrm{single}=94.8\pm0.1$\,(stat)$^{+0.7}_{-0.5}$\,($R$)\,\%; in the interior 95.7\,\%. Interior
losses (4.3\,\%): $\delta$-rays 2.7 (below), discharges 0.5, noise bands 0.1, fits off with no flag 0.4, second deposits
not particle-like 0.2, fits right in angle but far from the reference 0.2, edge of the other coordinate 0.2. The chamber hardware itself (discharges, noise bands) accounts for 0.6\,\%; the rest is the
reconstruction of a muon accompanied by its own $\delta$-ray. The extra 0.9\,pt lost over the full area is the edge
response (\cref{sec:edge}). Every loss class, with one real event display each and what a reference-free change to the
reconstruction recovers of it, is in \cref{app:losses}.
\textbf{$\delta$-rays: one population, three outcomes by size.} Median strips over threshold: a clean muon 12; a
compact saturated blob 19 (the fit is pulled off, 0.9\,\%); a $\delta$ along the track 34 (the fit is pulled beyond
5\,mm, 0.9\,\%); a large branching deposit 67 (0.7--0.9\,\%), which trips the discharge veto (${>}50$ strips) so that no
fit is attempted. None of them shows extra activity in the reference planes above or below chamber~A (0.49/0.47 extra clusters per event for the largest
deposits, 0.56/0.72 for detected muons, 1.55/1.46 for events with an independent second particle): the deposit is
made in chamber~A's own gas, cathode or window, by the muon. Their rate is consistent in order of magnitude with knock-on
electrons ${\gtrsim}0.1$\,MeV (${\sim}1$--1.5\,\% of muons). They are counted as losses: a TPC that measures a
straight track has to cope with the $\delta$-rays it makes. Real discharges are different: 113 strips over threshold
and 220--230 at SNR\,$>$\,6, with chip-wide ringing.

\textbf{Overlap in one projection.} The reconstruction is built for it: a joint two-track fit offers a merged
candidate a second track, with a lowered threshold when the other view resolves more candidates. In its first version
it rarely fired on the bench: both its trigger and its acceptance required candidates to pass a plausibility test tuned
on beam tracks (full drift column, raw $|\tan|<0.6$) that short, steep secondaries fail, and a short secondary drifts from
only part of the gap, so its column time misses the coincidence. A charge-corroborated trigger and acceptance (the
merged view's charge must match the sum of the other view's two segments, and each child its partner; only the muon
child need be plausible; the 4-pitch separation floor kept) recovers the muon in 53.8\,\% of the 195 interior merged
events (from 41.0\,\%), with correct X/Y pairing in 36.4\,\% (from 20.5\,\%), and splits no clean single muon on any of the
five chambers (\texttt{rerun/effbudget/single/MERGED\_PROJECTION\_2026-11-02.md}). The rest fail the geometric guards:
the two children share almost no depth (one track cut in half) or lie within the floor.

\textbf{The discharge veto.} Configuration (iii), the large $\delta$-rays and the edge-zone losses all come from
dropping every event with more than 50 strips over threshold. A rescue that re-seeds such events from their clean
clusters (track-sized clusters only, rail samples censored, a one-particle gate on the result) recovers muons, but they
are right far less often than ordinary tracks: 57--75\,\% on B/A (ordinary 95\,\%), 11--26\,\% on E/C/D, where a
discharge rings every chip within a sample of the muon's own signal. It therefore stays off, and vetoed muons count as
losses (\texttt{rerun/effbudget/single/DISCHARGE\_VETO\_2026-11-02.md}). The clean case, a muon plus a separate deposit,
would be worth rescuing alone (29 right, 4 wrong on B/A) if it could be told from a shower without the reference.
\textbf{Pairing the views.} Two particles give two segments per view and two possible X/Y pairings; the wrong
one makes two ghost tracks. The views share no coordinate, but they read the same avalanche charge at the same drift
times, so the reconstruction pairs them by the charge balance and the charge-arrival time (Paper~II). On 567 real
two-particle cosmic events where the reference marks one particle in both views, the pairing is right in
$97.7\pm0.6$\,\% (charge alone: 95.6\,\%; 100\,\% when the two charges differ by more than a factor 1.65, 95\,\% when they
are similar). This is the feasibility demonstration for pairs at n\_TOF. The production reconstruction makes the same choice (the
matching with the lowest summed pairing cost, then the coincidence gate per pair) and pairs the two-particle events
correctly on every chamber: A 95.9\,\%, B 96.8\,\%, C 92.2\,\%, D 90.1\,\%, E 94.0\,\%
(\texttt{rerun/effbudget/single/PAIRING\_FIX\_2026-11-02.md}).

\subsection{Avenues for improvement}\label{sec:improve}
\intent{Dylan 2026-11-02: name what better reconstruction could still recover, with the size of each, under one
umbrella; the losses the chamber hardware causes are separated from those the reconstruction causes.}
Of the 5.2\,\% of chamber~A single tracks lost over the full active area (\cref{fig:lossbudget}), only the discharges
(0.47\,\%) and the noise bands (0.10\,\%) are the chamber's own: about 0.6\,pt is a hardware floor. The rest is the
reconstruction of real ionisation, and each part has a named route to recovery:
\textbf{$\delta$-rays, up to 2.4\,pt.} The three $\delta$ classes (along the track 0.81, saturated blob 0.87, large
branching deposit tripping the veto 0.76\,\%) are a muon with its own knock-on electron. A straight-line fit cannot
separate them, and the one model tested here (track plus a compact deposit) recovers only ${\sim}3$\,\% of the saturated
views (\cref{app:losses}): the deposit is a short curling track, not a point. A topological or image-recognition
algorithm that identifies the $\delta$ and fits the muon on the remaining charge could recover up to 2.4\,pt; how much
of that is reachable is not measured.
\textbf{The edges, up to 1.5\,pt over the full area.} Near the unpassivated $x$ sides the drift field fringes
(\cref{sec:edge}): tracks there spread over more strips (1.01\,\% exceed the discharge veto) or are fitted off (0.32\,\%), and
0.19\,\% leave through the other coordinate's edge. An edge-aware drift model and a veto that counts strips per cluster
rather than per plane could recover these; the interior is unaffected.
\textbf{A smarter discharge veto, ${\sim}0.1$--0.2\,pt.} Re-seeding vetoed events from their clean clusters recovers a
muon accompanied by a separate deposit correctly (29 right, 4 wrong on B/A), but in discharges and large
$\delta$-rays it mostly finds the wrong track (overall 57--75\,\% right on B/A, 11--26\,\% on E/C/D, against
95\,\% for ordinary tracks), so it is not used. Telling a separate deposit from a shower without the reference would
make the clean part usable (\texttt{rerun/effbudget/single/DISCHARGE\_VETO\_2026-11-02.md}).
\textbf{Two particles merged in one projection, 0.8\,\% of tracks.} The charge-corroborated split recovers the muon
in 54\,\% of them (from 41\,\%) without splitting any clean single muon; most of the rest fail geometric guards that
protect single tracks (relaxing the column-sharing guard would add ${\sim}6$\,\% of them at the price of
one-track-cut-in-half splits). A joint fit of both views at once, using the charge and time correspondence directly,
is the route beyond that.
Together these bound what the named improvements could add at ${\approx}4$\,pt (A single-track efficiency over the
full area from 94.8 towards ${\approx}98.8\,\%$); the remaining 0.7\,pt of reconstruction losses (fits off with nothing
flagged, non-particle second deposits, right-angle tracks far from the reference) have no identified cause. The hardware alone
would allow 99.4\,\%.
