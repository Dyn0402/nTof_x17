In 2016 the ATOMKI group reported an excess of $e^+e^-$ pairs at large opening angles in the decay of an excited
state of $^8$Be~\cite{Krasznahorkay2016}, and later in $^4$He~\cite{Krasznahorkay2021} and
$^{12}$C~\cite{Krasznahorkay2022}. All three are consistent with the emission of a light neutral boson of mass
${\approx}17$\,MeV that decays to $e^+e^-$, now called X17~\cite{Feng2016}. Independent tests have not settled the question:
MEG~II, repeating the $^8$Be measurement, sees no significant signal yet remains compatible with ATOMKI at the
1.5$\sigma$ level~\cite{MEGII2024}, while PADME, searching for X17 produced resonantly in $e^+e^-$ annihilation, sees an
excess of about 2$\sigma$ at $\sqrt{s}\approx16.9$\,MeV~\cite{PADME2025}.

The n\_TOF experiment~\cite{nTOFX17proposal,Gustavino2025} reaches the $^4$He case with neutrons instead of protons: capture on $^3$He
forms $^4$He 20.58\,MeV above its ground state, which decays by $\gamma$ emission, by internal pair creation (IPC)
or, possibly, by emitting an X17~\cite{Viviani2022}. This paper describes the detectors built to track the leptons
from that reaction.

\paragraph{What the measurement needs.}
The experiment must detect the rare $e^+e^-$ pairs and reconstruct each lepton's direction from the target. The two
leptons share about 19.6\,MeV of kinetic energy; from an X17 of 16.8\,MeV each carries between 3.8 and 15.7\,MeV, and
the pair opens by more than 109\textdegree. The opening angle is the key input to the invariant mass, which is what
separates a resonance from the smooth IPC continuum. The figure of merit of the tracking is therefore the accuracy of
each reconstructed track angle; the position resolution matters less, and the time resolution is needed mainly to
show that the two leptons were produced together and to reject combinatorial background. Tracking is the first layer
of the experiment. Behind it, scintillators trigger and measure the energy, which adds the energy split of the pair to
the mass and rejects events whose total energy is not that of the capture. The tracker must therefore put as little
material as possible in front of its own measurement, and then measure the track well.

\paragraph{Why a compact TPC.}
A time projection chamber does both: the leptons cross a thin window into a gas volume, and the drift times of the
ionisation along each track give a three-dimensional segment, hence a direction, from a single chamber (\cref{fig:principle}). We built
Micromegas~\cite{Giomataris1996} TPCs for the measurement at n\_TOF. They must also sit close to the target, since the
air in between scatters the leptons: over 20\,cm an 8\,MeV electron is deflected by 1.7\textdegree{} rms in each
projection (3.2\textdegree{} at 4\,MeV), already comparable to the chamber's own angular resolution, and the deflection
grows as the square root of the path. The chambers are therefore compact, with a 30\,mm drift gap, so that four of them form a box
around the target with their entrance windows 20.4\,cm from it. Each has an active area of about
$40\times36$\,cm$^2$, for a large acceptance to the back-to-back pairs. The trigger and calorimetry stack then fits
close behind each chamber: a plastic scintillator wall 11\,cm behind the window, followed by plastic bars and liquid
scintillators, all within about 50\,cm of the target.

\paragraph{Why resistive strips.}
The instantaneous intensity at n\_TOF was expected to be high, starting with the $\gamma$ flash at each proton
pulse, so the chambers were built as resistive Micromegas~\cite{Alexopoulos2011}, which quench discharges, for
survivability and stability. This appears to have worked (\cref{sec:operation}). The resistive strips, however, spread
the charge of each avalanche over neighbouring strips and delay it, which poses new problems for the TPC
reconstruction. They are discussed here where they affect the performance, and in a companion paper devoted to
them~\cite{paperII}.

\paragraph{This paper.}
It covers the chambers themselves, on the Saclay cosmic bench and in the CERN SPS H4 beam; the n\_TOF campaign and
its physics are reported separately. \Cref{sec:design} describes the design and construction of the chambers, and
\cref{sec:setups} the two test setups and the five chambers, A--E, four of which later ran at n\_TOF. \Cref{sec:operation}
covers the high voltage, the gain and the discharges, \cref{sec:efficiency} the charge balance between the two
readout views and the efficiency, including events with two particles in one chamber, and \cref{sec:gas} the gas:
drift velocity and electron attachment. \Cref{sec:tracking} gives the angle and position resolution, with two
geometric effects: a bow of the readout boards and the field fringe at the chamber edges. \Cref{sec:timing} covers
the timing, and \cref{sec:det4} a production defect of one chamber, chamber~E: stripes of its area that do not
amplify. The reconstruction used throughout is that of the companion paper.
