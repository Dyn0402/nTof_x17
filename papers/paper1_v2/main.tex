\begin{abstract}
\askDylan{Draft abstract (2026-11-10), written from the results as they stand in this version; every number is in the
body. Rewrite freely.}
We describe compact resistive-strip Micromegas time projection chambers built to track the $e^+e^-$ pairs of an X17
search at the CERN n\_TOF facility, and their performance on a cosmic-ray bench and in the SPS H4 beam. Each chamber
has an active area of $40\times36$\,cm$^2$ and a 30\,mm drift gap, and measures a three-dimensional track segment
from the waveforms of two strip views that share one plane of pads. Because a chamber sees a short segment far from
the target, the quantity that matters is the track angle. With a reconstruction that fits the full
strip-by-time waveform image, a single chamber measures it to 1.0\textdegree{} in one view and 0.9\textdegree{} in
the other, independent of the track angle within $\pm20$\textdegree, of the gain over an order of magnitude and of the
drift field; the reference telescope contributes at most 0.14\textdegree. The drift velocity and the drift gap are
calibrated from the data themselves, in two independent ways that agree to 2\,\%, which makes each chamber a monitor
of the water and oxygen in its gas. The best chamber detects 94.8\,\% of single tracks over its full active area, of
which 0.6 percentage points are lost to the chamber hardware and the rest to the reconstruction of real ionisation,
mostly $\delta$-rays. The two views carry equal charge, which pairs the segments of two particles in one chamber
correctly in 97.7\,\% of events. We also report an intrinsic position resolution of about 120\,\textmu m, an event
time resolution of 40\,ns, a bow of 1.3--1.9\,mm of every readout board and its correction, the field fringe at the
chamber sides, and the production defects that separate two good chambers from three.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Use of AI tools.}
\askDylan{Placement follows IOP/JINST (an AI statement in the Acknowledgements; Elsevier/NIM~A, for Paper~II, wants
a ``Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process'' before the
references). Both publishers also ask for AI used \emph{in the research} to be described in the methods. No AI model is
part of the reconstruction or of any measurement here, so we propose no methods text unless the editor asks
(Dylan 2026-11-02: keep an open mind). Code release: Zenodo (CERN's free repository) would give the analysis code and
its history a citable DOI; to decide. Wording to agree among all authors.}
This work made extensive use of Claude Code (Anthropic), an AI agent that writes and runs code, with the Claude
Opus, Fable, Sonnet and Haiku models (versions 4.6--5.5), from November 2025 to the submission of this paper. We state
its role in full because it was large. Under the direction of the authors it wrote most of the analysis software:
the waveform reconstruction of the companion paper, the calibrations, and the efficiency, resolution, gas and timing
studies. It ran those analyses on the CERN batch system, proposed and carried out cross-checks, found errors in
earlier results, made every figure from scripts, and drafted much of the text of this paper, which the authors then
revised. Of the 1\,353 changes to the analysis repository, 1\,131 are marked as written with it.
The authors decided what to measure, the strategy and the conventions, and made every analysis choice. We verified
the results through independent cross-checks of each headline number, a complete from-scratch re-processing of the
data, event displays inspected by eye for every class of lost track, and Monte Carlo closure tests of the
reconstruction. No data, measurement or figure was generated or altered by an image or language model: every figure is
produced by a script from the recorded data. The code and its full history are available from the authors
[repository / DOI]. The authors take full responsibility for the content of this paper.
