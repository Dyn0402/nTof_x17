Paper I audit: organisation, key figures, what to add

10 November 2026 · the Overleaf version of that day (same as repo main, 18f3aad8) · 47 pages, 52 figures. Figure and section numbers are the current ones.
  1. The content is there. The weight is in the wrong places. The introduction names the track angle as the figure of merit. The paper then gives it about 50 words (§7.1, on page 21 of a 30-page body), against 2,500 words on efficiency accounting, 1,000 more in appendix A, and 1,900 on the bow. The order follows how the analysis was done, not what a reader needs first.
  2. Your eight figures are the right core, with two qualifications. A second reader, given only the PDF and not your list, also put Fig. 21 first and picked 9, 14, 16 and 7. It did not pick 15, 17 or 18, and it ranked three results you left out above them: everything flat over a factor 13 in gain (Fig. 10), the bow (Figs. 23, 24) and the edge (Fig. 25). My own view sits between the two: 15 and 16 are the best argument in the paper, 17 supports them, 18 is the one of your eight least about this detector, and the bow earns one body figure.
  3. Fig. 21 undersells the main result. Half of it is a diagonal line. Chamber B, a beam point at 25°, the fact that the reference adds at most 0.14°, and the comparison with the hit-time method all exist in the repo and none is in the figure or its 50 words.
  4. Proposed order: follow the measurement, tan θ = w/vd. Calibration from the gas (15–18), then the angle (21), then efficiency (7, 9), then two particles (14), then the chamber as built. Every results section opens on one of your key figures. About 21 body pages and 18 figures, from 30 and 27; appendices from 17 pages to about 10.
  5. 29 numbers or statements disagree between places. Most are bookkeeping. A few change what a reader concludes: the gap (30 mm designed, 28.2 measured, never discussed), two versions of the timing curve, two gain-doubling voltages, and three descriptions of what the resistive layer does.
  6. The largest additions need no new data: the rebuilt Fig. 21, a table of the five chambers, the material budget (which may show the chamber is already below its own scattering floor), and a two-track separation curve from overlaid real muons.

Why it reads the way it does

One topic, many places

The same scan or data set is cut by observable and spread over the paper, so no single place carries the point.

  • Chamber A's HV scan is in Figs. 7a, 10, 26a and 45, and argued in §4, §5.3, §8 and appendices C and F.
  • Timing is in §5.3, §8, appendix C and appendix F.
  • The geometry as built (gap, bow, fringe, passivation) is in §2.1, §5.2, §6, §7.5 and §7.6.
  • Chamber E and the beam test are in §3.2, §6, §7.4, §9 and appendix D.
  • Event displays: Figs. 5, 12, 13, 28 and 29–42. Six of the fourteen single displays repeat the panels of Fig. 28.

Effort shows, importance doesn't

Words of running text per section (captions and draft notes left out): efficiency 2,480; bow 1,860; introduction 725; setups 690; design 445; angle, position and beam cross-check together 365; edge 280; gas 205; operation 205; timing 60.

  • §5.3 is one 1.5-page paragraph that goes from maps to discharges to gain to timing to angle against charge.
  • §5.5 and §5.6 tell the history of the reconstruction (“in its first version it rarely fired”). That is Paper II, or nowhere.
  • §5 to §7 are still notes with bold run-in heads: up to forty numbers in a paragraph, and no sentence saying which one matters.
