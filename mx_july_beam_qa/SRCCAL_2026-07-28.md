# Two-source plastic calibration — Y-88 + Cs-137, runs 224588-224596 (2026-07-28)

Design and operating instructions for the second scintillator absolute-energy
calibration. Successor to `HANDOFF_Y88_SCAN.md` (the 07-17 single-source Y-88
scan, scripts 21/22/23); this campaign is scripts **33/34/35** plus the shared
modules `srccal_runs.py` and `edgefit.py`.

## 1. The campaign

Nine ~6-minute beam-off runs, 603-621 timer triggers each (20 ms acquisition per
trigger, so ~12 s of live time per run). Two sources at once, **centred on one
plastic bar each, on opposite arms** (ring order A-D-C-B, so A<->C and B<->D):

| 224594 | DR | BR | |
| 224595 | DL | BL | |
| 224596 | AR | — | **control**: Y only, repeats 224588's Y position |

Every one of the 8 bars is measured with **both** sources. Bar AR is measured
twice with Y-88 (224588 and the control).

## 2. What the design buys over the 07-17 scan

1. **A second CLEAN energy per plastic** — 477 keVee (Cs-137, 661.66 keV)
   alongside 699 keVee (Y-88, 898.04 keV). The energy scale becomes a fitted
   line `mV = a*E + b` with the intercept **measured**; 23 had to assume
   `b = 0`. A non-zero `b` is what a threshold- or baseline-induced scale error
   looks like, and it comes out -5 to +2 mV, i.e. 3-10 % of the 699 amplitude.
   (The Y-88 1612 keVee edge is measured too, but it is NOT a third calibration
   point — see §5.)
2. **The source is centred on one bar**, so the plastic scale needs no
   "source between the bars, both illuminated symmetrically" assumption, and
   the neighbouring bar becomes a light-sharing measurement instead of a
   contaminant.
3. **Both energies in the same DAQ state** (simultaneous exposure), so a gain
   drift between source exposures cannot fake nonlinearity.
4. **Four built-in controls** — AR repeatability, Cs leakage into the far arm
   (from the Y-only control run), the `detn` <-> L/R map, and transport to the
   07-17 campaign.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Read pass (`33_srccal_spectra.py`, runs on lxplus)

Per run, per channel of **all four arms** (8 PSS + 32 WAL + 4 LIQ): fine linear
amplitude histograms in mV (0-400 mV, 0.25 mV bins), a pileup-vetoed copy, a log
overview, a coarse tof profile, and hit/saturation/pileup counts, plus the
trigger count. ADC->mV per channel from `DAQsettings` via `adc_mv.py`.

Two things had to change from `21_y88_spectra.py`:

* **range**: 21 stopped at 160 mV, correct for the 07-17 gains (699 keVee edge
  at 20-30 mV). Here the gains run from about the same (arm A) up to ~2x that
  (BL, CL): the 699 keVee edge spans 29-65 mV and the **1612 keVee edge reaches
  125-136 mV**, so the old ceiling sat right on top of the outer edge and cut
  away the continuum above it that the fit needs for its background.
* **every arm is histogrammed**, because in this campaign 4-5 arms are dark in
  any given run and those dark runs are the background template (below).

### 3.2 Edges (`34_srccal_edges.py` + `edgefit.py`, local)

For each illuminated channel: subtract the background, fit the edge(s),
bootstrap the error.

* **Background** = the *same channel* summed over the runs in which its arm
  carried no source at all (`srccal_runs.dark_runs_for`: 4-5 runs per arm),
  rate-normalised by trigger count. Same day, same DAQ state, same channel —
  strictly better than 22's "use another arm's channel as a shape reference".
  Fit weights use `sig + scale^2 * bkg`, and the bootstrap resamples both.
* **The Cs-137 edge anchors everything** (this is the central design decision).
  A Cs-137 spectrum has exactly ONE edge from a single gamma; a Y-88 spectrum
  has two, plus the cascade-sum continuum, plus — on the brightest bars — a
  pileup shoulder near threshold that carries more `|dN/dA|` than the real edge
  does. So 34 runs three passes: **(1)** fit the Cs-137 edge on every bar
  autonomously, ranking candidate steepest-descent points by *contrast* (how
  far the spectrum falls across them), which on a single-edge spectrum is
  unambiguous; **(2)** fit each bar's Y-88 spectrum with the 699 keVee window
  placed at `1.4636 x` its own Cs-137 edge; **(3)** repair — for a bar whose
  Cs-137 edge was lost into the threshold turn-on, run the anchor backwards
  from the Y-88 699 keVee edge. Centres are always free inside their windows,
  so every measured ratio is still a measurement. The outer 1612 keVee edge is
  in turn searched around `2.307 x` the fitted 699 one.

  Pass 3 is not hypothetical: AL and BL are the two brightest bars
  (4300-5400 hits/trigger, 23-33 % pileup) and both lose an autonomous fit.
  With the anchoring, all 8 bars yield both source energies.

  Three simpler seed rules were tried first and each fails on this data;
  `edgefit.py` records all three, because each looks reasonable on paper.
  `-d(log N)/dA` (scale-free, so gain-independent) puts the seed where the
  spectrum runs out of statistics — it labelled the 1612 keVee edge as 699 and
  found its "partner" at 220 mV in the empty tail. The *lowest* significant
  candidate is unstable at the few-% prominence level: it moved the AR
  699 keVee edge between 20 and 31 mV across two runs (224588 / 224596) whose
  spectra are the same shape to 2 %. The *most prominent* candidate is right on
  a Cs-137 spectrum but loses BL entirely to its pileup shoulder.

* **A fit that found no edge says so.** The step centre is rejected unless the
  step stands 3 sigma above its own error and sits clear of its window bounds.
  This matters: several channels legitimately have nothing to fit (below), and
  without the bound check the outer Y-88 edge rails at the window edge and
  returns a number that really means "no step here".
* **Models.** Plastics: a resolution-smeared step on a **sloped** continuum
  (22 used a flat one). The slope matters here because (i) the 1836 keV Compton
  continuum runs straight through the 699 keVee edge and (ii) **Y-88's 898 and
  1836 keV gammas are a cascade**, so a source sitting on the bar produces
  true-coincidence summing, which adds counts above every single-gamma edge. A
  flat background pushes that slope into the step and biases the edge outward —
  a plausible origin for 22's measured 1612/699 ratio of ~2.4 against the
  expected 2.307. Walls and liquids: Gaussian bump + linear background, as in 22.
* **Two estimators** are always reported: `edge_mv` (fitted step/bump centre,
  the 22 convention, so the campaigns compare directly) and
  `edge_mv_halfheight` (model-independent). Their difference is the convention
  systematic.
* **Pileup**: the primary fit uses the pileup-vetoed spectrum; the all-hits
  spectrum is fitted too and the difference is stored per edge as
  `pileup_shift_mv`. At ~3000-7000 hits/trigger on a lit bar this is a real
  systematic and is measured, not assumed away.

Because of the cascade summing, **the Cs-137 477 keVee edge is the clean
single-gamma anchor** and the Y-88 1612 keVee edge is the least trustworthy of
the three; 34 marks it `secondary`.

### 3.2c The liquids: model, geometry, and the one dead cell

* **They are fitted with the STEP model, like the plastics.** 22 fitted a
  localised bump on these channels; that bump is the Compton step riding on the
  threshold turn-on, and its peak sits ~25 % BELOW the half-height point
  (LIQA: bump 26.45 mV vs step 33.73 mV on the same spectrum). The step centre
  is the defensible convention and it matches the plastics. The bump fit is
  kept alongside (`edges_bump_convention`) purely so the 07-17 transport is
  like-for-like.
* **It is not cosmetic — it is what recovers LIQD.** LIQD's edge is a shoulder
  that never turns over into a local maximum, so the bump finder returns
  nothing at all, while the step fit gives 32.16 +- 0.30 mV.
* **A and D answer to the R bar only, and it is not understood.** With Y-88 on
  AL the LIQA rate is 0.94x its dark-run rate — nothing — while on AR it is
  5.5x. Same for D (DL 1.2x, DR 5.1x). LIQB answers to both bars, LIQC to
  neither. Three things rule out the easy explanations:
  - it is **not a placement fluke**: the pattern repeats with the **Cs-137**
    source on the same bars (LIQA 1.9x from AR, 0.7x from AL);
  - the **source was equally bright either way** — the plastic itself reads
    5406 hits/trigger on AL vs 4952 on AR;
  - it is **not distance**: the two bars sit about equally far from the cell
    centre (Dylan, 2026-07-31), which was the first explanation tried here and
    it is wrong.

  It costs **rate, not scale**: on LIQB, the one cell that answers to both bars,
  the two give the same edge to 1.2 % (30.54 +- 0.36 vs 30.18 +- 0.35 mV), so
  the three calibrations stand regardless. **Source runs cannot settle this** —
  the follow-up is the beam data, which lights every cell continuously from the
  physics direction and where wall x plastic x liquid coincidences can separate
  a quiet cell from a shadowed line of sight.
* **LIQC has no usable edge, and that is a detector fact, not a fit failure.**
  It is ~5x quieter than the others even in the dark runs (27 vs 68-128
  hits/trigger) and the source lifts it only 1.5-1.6x: **472 excess counts**,
  against 7000 (D), 26000 (A), 56000 (B). Not a gain shift either — its
  spectrum reaches 66 mV, comparable to A (69), D (71), B (57). **Worth a
  hardware look, and it is the other question for the beam data.**
* **Only the 699 keVee edge is fitted on.** The liquids do see Cs-137 (LIQA
  +66 % with it on the near bar) but at their gain a 477 keVee edge would sit
  at ~23 mV against a valley at ~16 mV — inside the turn-on — and the fit
  returns 32.56 mV, *above* that channel's own 699 keVee edge. Reported, not
  fitted on (`CALIBRATION_ENERGIES` in 35).

### 3.2b What the detectors actually give (measured, not assumed)

* **Plastics: both source energies on all 8 bars.** This is the deliverable.
* **Liquids: 3 of 4 cells, Y-88 only** (LIQA/B/D; LIQC has no usable edge).
  See §3.2c.
* **SiPM walls: nothing usable.** The wall sits in FRONT of the plastic and the
  source is clamped on the bar, so the walls collect 30-230 hits/trigger against
  the plastic's 3000-8000, with no fittable edge on any of the 32 channels. The
  07-17 scan did get clean wall bumps because the source sat *between* the bars.
  **If wall edges are wanted, that needs its own source position** — this
  campaign cannot give them, and 34 reports "no edge" rather than fitting noise.
* **Pileup is a real shift, not a worry.** On the source bar the 699 keVee edge
  moves by ~-0.9 mV (~3 %) between the all-hits and pileup-vetoed spectra, and
  the 1612 keVee edge by ~+8 mV (~13 %). Every edge carries its own
  `pileup_shift_mv`.

### 3.3 Calibration and controls (`35_srccal_calib.py`, local)

Per channel: weighted line fit through the measured edges (`mv_per_kevee`,
`offset_mv`), the through-origin slope for direct comparison with
`calib/y88_energy_calib.json`, residuals/nonlinearity, and the two free ratio
checks (1612/699 = 2.307, 699/477 = 1.4636 — both independent of gain, so they
test the *energy assignment*, not the calibration). Plastic scales use only the
runs in which the bar itself carried the source; wall/liquid scales use all lit
runs, and their spread across source positions is the position systematic.

Controls: AR repeatability (224588 vs 224596), Cs leakage (arm C rate in the
Y-only control vs its dark-run mean), the `detn` <-> L/R map (the source lights
exactly one bar, so the hot channel names the mapping — the README's
"detn 1 = left, 2 = right" was assumed, never confirmed), and the per-channel
gain ratio against 07-17.

## 4. Running it

Read pass on lxplus (nine condor jobs, ~3 minutes wall in total):

```bash
rsync -av -e 'ssh -o ControlPath=none' \
      33_srccal_spectra.py srccal_runs.py adc_mv.py lxplus/srccal.sub \
      lxplus/srccal_wrapper.sh lxplus:x17qa/
ssh -K lxplus 'cd ~/x17qa && chmod +x srccal_wrapper.sh && myschedd bump \
               && condor_submit srccal.sub'
ssh -K lxplus 'cd ~/x17qa && for t in srccal_out_*.tgz; do tar xzf "$t"; done'
rsync -av -e 'ssh -o ControlPath=none' lxplus:x17qa/cache/33_srccal_\*.npz ./cache/
rsync -av -e 'ssh -o ControlPath=none' lxplus:x17qa/calib/adc_to_mv_run2245\*.json ./calib/
```

Then locally (minutes, all of it on ~1 MB caches):

```bash
python 34_srccal_edges.py        # -> calib/srccal_edges_<run>.json, figures/33_srccal/edges_<run>.png
python 35_srccal_calib.py        # -> calib/srccal_energy_calib.json, figures/33_srccal/energy_calib.png
                                 #    + SRCCAL_RESULTS_2026-07-28.md
```

`ssh -K` matters: without an AFS token condor auth fails (every "permission
denied" in this workflow traces back to it). Condor also rejects a trailing
comment after a `request_*` value.

* **All 8 bars, two clean anchors**, relative errors 0.2-2.3 %. Measured
  699/477 within **-1.8 % to +8.5 %** of the 1.4636 the source energies demand.
* **The 1612 keVee edge is NOT a third calibration point.** Anchored on the two
  clean points it lands **-22 % to +1 %**, bar-dependent, against its own
  0.3-0.9 % statistical error — a systematic, and not a reproducible response
  curve either: the free 1612/699 ratio scattered 1.81-2.61 on 07-17 and
  1.91-2.28 here, both around the expected 2.307. Y-88's two gammas are a
  cascade, so a source in contact with the bar gives true-coincidence summing,
  and multiple scattering fills in above every single-gamma edge; both distort
  exactly the region the outer edge lives in. Extracting it properly needs a
  spectrum *model* (two Compton continua + summing), not a step fit.
  **Consequence: nothing above 0.70 MeVee is anchored.**
* **Liquids: 3 of the 4 cells calibrate** — LIQA 48.3, LIQB 43.4, LIQD 46.0
  mV/MeVee (699 keVee, through origin, step convention). The two with a 07-17
  counterpart **reproduce it to 2 %** (ratios 1.02 and 0.98) across 11 days,
  two source geometries and two independent fitters — the end-to-end validation
  of the whole chain (their HV and signal path did not change; the plastics' did).
  Three things had to be sorted out to get there, all in §3.2c.
* **detn <-> L/R map CONFIRMED, 17/17** — the README's "detn 1 = left,
  2 = right (per Dylan)" is now measured.
* **AR repeatability -0.5 %** (699 keVee, 224588 vs the 224596 control), and
  with the Cs source removed the AR spectrum keeps its shape to 4 % — no
  cross-contamination between the two sources.
* **Nothing saturates** (max clipped fraction 6e-5), but **pileup is heavy**:
  30-36 % of hits on a lit bar carry `pileup1`, and keeping them moves the edge
  by -7..+4 % at 699 keVee and +4..+17 % at 1612.
* **No cosmic fallback**: checked the dark-run plastic spectra out to 400 mV —
  no MIP bump. ~50-60 s of live time per arm is simply too little.

### What is still not covered

The trigger point (~1.7 MeV) and a MIP (~3.4 MeV) sit **2.4x and 4.9x above the
top anchor**, and the 1612 point is too unstable to bound the curvature between.
Two ways to close that, in order of cost:

1. **Use the beam data you already have** — a wall x plastic coincidence is a
   through-going MIP in the same channels at the same HV. Tie that peak to this
   0.48-0.70 MeVee scale and the response is anchored at both ends. No beam
   time, no source handling.
2. **One more short source set** (beam off, one shift): a higher-energy anchor
   (Th-228, 2.615 MeV -> 2382 keVee; or Am/Pu-Be, 4.44 MeV -> 4197 keVee, which
   *brackets* the MIP — Co-60 does not help, its edges are 963/1118 keVee);
   four runs with the source **between** the bars to recover the 32 wall
   channels; two or three HV points on one bar to check the power-law index.
   **Not longer runs** — statistics are not the limit anywhere here.

## 7. Equalization cross-check — and a warning about comparing campaigns

The 07-19 equalization aimed every bar's 699 keVee edge at a common **31.2 mV**
and those volts are still set, so it is a hard prediction. We measure 29-65 mV.

Before concluding the fleet drifted: the 07-17 source runs were reprocessed by
the same 07-30 pass, so putting **them** through **this** chain measures the
analysis-side shift on its own. It is **1.01-1.68x, and bar-dependent** — the
new PSA `amp` definition plus the sloped-continuum fit. Divide it out and
**6 of 8 bars sit within 15 % of the target**; only CL (+49 %) and BL (+24 %)
are genuinely off. `36_srccal_legacy_check.py`,
`calib/srccal_legacy_check.json`, `figures/33_srccal/legacy_check.png`.

**The operational lesson is the bar-dependence.** Any future HV equalization has
to be derived from data processed with the *same* PSA as the physics analysis,
or the volts get set against numbers that no longer mean the same thing.
