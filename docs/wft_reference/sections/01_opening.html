The waveform-first reconstruction

A complete account of how a track's position, angle and depth are recovered from raw FEU waveforms — starting from the ADC counts the DAQ writes to disk and ending at the numbers that go into a physics result. Every figure is generated from real sat_det3 data with the calibration that production runs.

Those four numbers are what this document exists to explain. The chain that produces them replaced an older one that read tracks from per-strip hit times, and it replaced it because the old chain was measurably, structurally wrong about geometry — not slightly noisier, but biased in a way no threshold change could fix.

How to read this

The document is linear and cumulative. It starts at the raw ADC and never uses a concept before building it. If you want the algorithm in one paragraph, read the next section and stop. If you want to be able to modify the code, read all of it; Parts III and IV are the ones that matter most, and Part IX maps every claim onto a source file.

Four kinds of statement are marked, because in this subject the difference matters:

Rule A non-negotiable constraint of the analysis. Breaking one silently produces wrong physics rather than an error message.
Measured A number that came out of data, with the dataset it came from. Reproduced live for this document wherever the data were still on disk.
Trap Something that has actually gone wrong in this analysis at least once, and how it announced itself.
Open A known limitation or an unfinished piece. These are not rhetorical — each one is a real item someone could pick up.

The dataset behind every figure

Unless a figure says otherwise, it comes from sat_det3: detector mx17_3, the Saturday long run of 2026-06-27, resistive 490 V / drift 1000 V, Ar/isobutane 95/5 with a few tenths of a percent of water. 512 channels per plane, 32 samples of 60 ns, FEU 7 = X and FEU 8 = Y, non-zero-suppressed waveforms. This is the run the whole waveform-first study was built on, and its calibration bundle, its 400-event calibration cache and its 7,093-event reconstruction are all still on disk beside the data, so the figures here are not reproductions of old plots — they are the analysis, re-run.

An external M3 reference telescope supplies an independent track for most events. It is used for calibration, for alignment and for every validation number in Part VII, and it is never an input to a reconstruction fit. Its own pointing resolution at the detector, ≈0.4 mm, is the floor under every position comparison in this document.

The algorithm in one paragraph

For each event, the detector's own hits say which strips carry charge; a window of those strips plus three either side is cut out of the pedestal- and common-mode-corrected waveforms. Inside that window the reconstruction assumes a straight track, so the charge liberated at drift depth u arrives at transverse position p₀ + w·u and at time t₀ + u. The drift column is chopped into eighteen 60 ns slices; each slice's charge is spread over the strips by a geometric integral that includes diffusion, then copied onto the neighbouring strips by the detector's measured resistive sharing kernel, then folded with the measured amplifier impulse response. That generates a complete (strip × sample) picture from three geometric numbers and eighteen non-negative charges. The eighteen charges are solved exactly by non-negative least squares at every trial geometry, so only (p₀, w, t₀) are searched — first by a coarse scan, then by Nelder-Mead. The answer is the track's position at the mesh p₀, its transverse speed w, and hence its angle tan θ = w / v_drift.

The single idea that makes it work: the neighbouring strips' delayed, dispersed copies of the charge are part of the model. In the old chain they were contamination in every per-strip time measurement, and there was nowhere to put them.

Rule Never reconstruct position, angle or drift depth from combined_hits times. A per-strip hit time is an aggregate of that strip's own charge and delayed copies of its neighbours'. It is not a drift-time measurement and no threshold, CFD or matched filter makes it one. Hits remain correct for two jobs: finding candidates (which events and strips to look at) and QA (rates, amplitudes, occupancy, detection efficiency). Everything geometric comes from the waveforms. Part II is the measurement behind this rule.
