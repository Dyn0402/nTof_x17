Part VII

Does it work?

Every number here is measured on the full 7,093-event sat_det3 reconstruction against the M3 reference, which never entered a fit.

38 · What "works" has to mean

A reconstruction can agree with a reference for the wrong reasons, so four independent things are checked, in increasing order of how hard they are to fake:

  1. Resolution and bias — does it get the right answer, and how often?
  2. Error honesty — does the quoted uncertainty mean what it says?
  3. Internal physicality — is the implied drift velocity the same at every track angle? This one needs no reference at all and cannot be tuned away.
  4. Closure — when the truth is known exactly, because the data were simulated from the model itself, is the residual scatter reproduced?

39.1 One finding worth keeping

det6's X plane was recorded as dead for micro-TPC work: its fitted velocity railed to 119 µm/ns and its angular plateau σ68 was about 16°. The waveform fit gets σθ = 2.28° with +0.03° bias from it over 4,892 events. A velocity railing to 119 µm/ns is a degenerate fit, not a measurement. The plane is degraded, not dead.

That tail is not noise. It is the wrong-cluster failure of §27: when the seed lands on the wrong charge, the fit converges confidently onto something 40 mm away. The n_dropped ≤ 2 cut removes most of it, and the remaining excursions beyond 5 mm are 3.9 % of reference tracks.

41 · Are the error bars honest?

42 · The resolution floor is physics, not fitting

This is the most important validation result in the study, because it says when to stop optimising.

Waveforms were generated from the calibrated model with known tracks — real event geometries, real fitted charge profiles, real noise, real clipping — and then fitted back. The only thing varied was a per-depth-bin transverse jitter of the charge centroid, standing in for the fact that a 60 ns slice of track contains a finite number of avalanche-weighted electrons.

per-bin centroid jitterrecovered angle σ X / Yrecovered p₀ σ
0 — electronics noise only0.02° / 0.02°7 / 6 µm
0.15 mm0.55° / 0.54°219 / 172 µm
0.30 mm1.05° / 1.04°354 / 295 µm
0.50 mm1.77° / 1.78°473 / 484 µm

Electronic noise contributes essentially nothing — 0.02°. And a 0.30 mm RMS jitter, which is the scale expected from transverse diffusion (~0.3–0.4 mm at mid-drift) sampled by a few effective electrons per 2 mm of track, plus δ-rays, reproduces the observed 1.05° exactly.

Measured The ~1° per-event scatter is the physical information limit of this detector geometry, and the fit is extracting essentially all of it. Gains beyond this come from ensemble averaging, not from a better per-event fitter. This is why FLOOR_TAN = 0.018 is added in quadrature to every reported error, and why the Nelder-Mead tolerances are loose.

43 · Which parts of the model actually matter

Every single-component ablation — no gain map, one shared template instead of two, no saturation censoring, c₂ = 0, σ_s = 0, D_p = 0, kY = 1 — stays within ±0.05° of the full model on per-event angle σ.

That sounds like the components are pointless. They are not — they matter for ensemble physicality, which is the thing that cannot be tuned:

Measured The essential ingredients are the sharing kernel (c1, tau_s) and the per-plane template. Everything else is a refinement. A production implementation of this model can be simple — which is worth knowing if it is ever ported to firmware or to another experiment.

45 · Threading: does the track go through the charge?

The sharpest test, and the one designed to avoid circularity. Drawing the fit's own charge profile along the fitted line and observing that the reference lies on top would prove nothing. So the cluster in the threading displays comes from two line-free estimators — a 2-D charge deconvolution over (strip × depth) with a Tikhonov penalty, and a "free ladder" of one independent transverse position and charge per 60 ns bin. Only t₀ is borrowed from the fit, and it moves both clusters together.

Both agree at the mesh, at the ≈0.4 mm M3 pointing floor. The hits cluster then walks away with depth — by 1.5× in the common frame and 2.5× in its own — while the waveform cluster stays flat. That divergence is the effect the whole rebuild was about, and it is largely removed.

Trap Per event, the waveform cluster is not uniformly better: of 24 plane displays examined, 13 improved, one was clearly worse and the rest were a wash. One display where the charge sits on two strips has the deconvolution centroid collapsing onto a single strip. The claim lives in the population statistics, and a single convincing event display is not evidence.
