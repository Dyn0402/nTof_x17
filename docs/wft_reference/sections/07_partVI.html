Part VI

Calibration

Measuring a detector's impulse response, its sharing kernel and its drift velocity — and the one failure mode that has cost this analysis more time than everything else combined.

31 · What has to be measured, and why per detector

The model has seven kernel/geometry hyperparameters, a drift velocity, two impulse templates and an inter-FEU timing offset. All of them are properties of a specific chamber under specific conditions.

32 · The reference-pinned principle

Calibration is the one part of the chain that is allowed to use the M3 reference. The logic: reconstruction asks "where was the track?"; calibration asks "given that we know where the track was, what is the detector's response?" Pinning the geometry to the reference and fitting only the response is the same kind of operation as alignment, and it is legitimate for the same reason.

Rule Calibration may see the reference. Reconstruction never does. If it did, alignment and efficiency measured against that same reference would be circular. wft/reco.py takes the reference only as an optional event filter — which events to bother reconstructing — never as a fit input.

The obvious worry is that pinning to the reference forces the answer. It was tested directly: recalibrating all hyperparameters with the lines pinned to the old production ladders instead converges 49 % worse in total χ² (7.44×10⁷ against 4.98×10⁷) and picks contorted values — τs = 176 ns, σs = 164 ns, Dp ≈ 0, v = 31. No sharing model can make the waveforms look like the steep ladders. And free fits run with those production-taught hypers still agree better with the reference (σ 3.4°/3.9°) than with production's own slopes (σ 4.5°/4.7°).

33 · Stage 1 — the calibration cache

For a few hundred reference-matched muons, cut a waveform window along the whole reference corridor: every strip the track could have touched between z = −3 mm and z = 33 mm, plus 5 mm of pad. This is a wider window than reconstruction uses, and deliberately so — the calibration needs to see the sharing skirts that the hit finder never fires.

The cache also stores, per event, the reference mesh anchor and the reference tangents rotated into the detector's own strip frame. Those are what "pinned" means in the stages below. Building it requires the hits-chain alignment to exist first — a genuine dependency, and the reason 03_alignment_and_tpc.py has to be run before wft.calibrate on a new run.

34 · Stage 2 — the impulse template

The template is measured, not assumed. Take the brightest unsaturated strips of clearly inclined tracks (|tan θ| > 0.22, peak > 500 ADC), align each on its own 50 % crossing, normalise to its peak, and take the median.

The inclination requirement is the subtle part: on a steep track a bright strip's own charge arrives within about 1.5 samples, so its rising edge is not contaminated by a neighbour arriving at a different time. On a vertical track every strip's charge spans the whole gap and there is no clean pulse to measure.

Trap Low-gain chambers do not produce enough bright inclined strips to build a good template, and a thin template poisons everything downstream. This is the suspected root cause of det6's and det7's calibration trouble; the fix is to loosen the template cuts before touching the kernel, and it has not been done.

35 · Stage 3 — the hyperparameter fit, and the degeneracy

With the geometry pinned and the template measured, an eight-parameter Nelder-Mead minimises the total χ² over ~180 training events: the seven kernel and geometry hypers plus v. Each objective evaluation refits t₀ per event on a warm-started grid and sums the χ². It costs about 15 seconds per evaluation on twelve cores, and runs for 100–130 iterations.

And here is the problem that dominates this whole subject.

Read that again against §29. The calibration χ² has almost no opinion about the drift velocity, because a faster v and a weaker kernel produce nearly the same waveforms. Left free, the fit drifts up the valley to v ≈ 40 and c₁ ≈ 0.2. And since tan θ = w/v, that 10 % of χ² nobody can see becomes a 10 % error on every angle.

In every case the signature is the same: X-plane sharing driven toward zero, kY blown up to compensate, sigma_p0 inflated to absorb the missing charge spread. Any two of those three appearing together should stop a calibration from being promoted.

35.1 How the degeneracy is broken

Not by better optimisation — the valley is real, and a better optimiser finds the wrong bottom of it faster. It is broken by pinning parameters to external measurements, which is what --fix-hyper and --fix-v exist for. There is a hard floor at c1 ≥ 0.05 in the objective as a last-ditch guard.

the validated recipe 
kernel (c1, c2, tau_s)  <- MEASURED, pinned
        beam at normal incidence, or the bench estimator on near-normal cosmics
sigma_p0                <- pinned physical (~0.10 mm) under a delay kernel;
                           may be freed under an lp kernel (see below)
v                       <- pinned from the drift scan, the implied-v plateau,
                           or a Magboltz curve for the known gas and field
kY, sigma_s, Dp         <- fitted (plane asymmetry, dispersion, diffusion)

The kernel measurement needs no forward model and no v: take near-normal tracks, stack the ±1 and ±2 traces against the central strip, and read off the delay and the charge budget directly. It runs on decoded waveforms plus a handful of reference tracks, which every chamber has.

The instructive part: under the delay kernel a free sigma_p0 was a pathology, degrading reconstruction to 4.1°/3.4°. Under the lp kernel the same freedom improves every angle metric. When the model shape is right, a parameter stops being a place to hide error.

36 · Stage 4 — the inter-FEU offset

With the hypers known, measure_dt_xy() refits t₀ on both planes of ~200 events with the geometry pinned, and takes the median of t0x − t0y grouped by fine-timestamp difference. det3 gives −7.6 ns and −16.0 ns for the two ftst classes (Figure 30). The only consumer is the joint fit and the pair selector.

Rule conditions is a required field and check_conditions() compares it against the run being reconstructed. This exists because a bundle used outside its conditions produces no error, no warning and no obviously wrong plot — only wrong physics. Treat a conditions mismatch the way you would treat a failed assertion.
Trap The fleet's calibration bundles for det2, det6 and det7 were lost with the campaign machine. det3's and det4's have been rebuilt from EOS data on the current machine and are what this document uses. Any det2/6/7 number quoted anywhere in the analysis predates that loss and cannot currently be regenerated without redoing the calibration — which, per §35.1, should now be done with the measured-kernel recipe rather than a free fit.
