Part III

The forward model

Every term in the equation, where it comes from, what it is worth, and what it cannot represent. This is wft/model.py.

10 · The shape of the model

The model describes one plane of one event. Its job is to turn a small number of parameters into a complete predicted (strip × sample) array that can be compared with the data.

modeli(t) = Σk qk · [ Fik h(t − t₀ − uk) + c₁ (Fi−1,k + Fi+1,k) h₁(t − t₀ − uk) + c₂ (Fi−2,k + Fi+2,k) h₂(t − t₀ − uk) ]

i = strip index  ·  k = depth bin  ·  uk = (k + ½)·60 ns  ·  F depends on (p₀, w)

Read it from the inside out. The drift column is divided into K = 18 bins of 60 ns. Bin k holds a charge qk ≥ 0 that was liberated at drift time uk after the mesh charge. Because the track is a straight line, that charge sits at transverse position p₀ + w·uk. The factor Fik says what fraction of it lands on strip i. The three terms then place that charge on the strip itself, on its ±1 neighbours scaled by c₁, and on its ±2 neighbours scaled by c₂, each folded with the appropriate impulse response.

11 · The track line and the depth basis

Two coordinates run through everything. u is time since the mesh charge arrived — the drift time. z = v·u is drift depth. The model works in u and never needs v; the conversion to an angle is the only place v enters, which is a deliberate and important property (§29).

position of the charge liberated at drift time u:   p(u) = p₀ + w·u
angle:   tan θ = w / v_drift

The 18 bins of 60 ns span u = 0 to 1080 ns, which at 36.6 µm/ns is 39.5 mm — comfortably past the ~29 mm gap. That headroom is deliberate: it lets the fit place charge beyond the physical cathode rather than piling it up against a wall, which would bias t₀. At low drift field the column takes longer than the window and set_depth_bins() extends the basis to K = 26.

12 · Where a depth bin's charge lands: Fik

strip_fractions() answers a purely geometric question: given that bin k's charge is spread transversely as a Gaussian centred on the track line, what fraction falls within strip i's 0.78 mm pitch?

Three widths add in quadrature:

sigma_p0
the initial cloud — how wide the ionisation and early avalanche already were before drifting anywhere.
Dp · √u
transverse diffusion, growing as the square root of drift time. This is what makes deep charge land on more strips than shallow charge.
|p(uk+1) − p(uk)| / √12
the bin is not a point: over its own 60 ns the track moves sideways. On a steep track this term dominates. It enters as the RMS of a uniform segment.
σp(u)² = σp0² + Dp²·u + (half-span)²/3
Fik = ½ [ erf( (pi + pitch/2 − pc,k) / √2 σp ) − erf( (pi − pitch/2 − pc,k) / √2 σp ) ]
Trap sigma_p0 is the parameter that absorbs model error. If the sharing kernel's shape is wrong, the fit can hide the missing charge spread by inflating the initial cloud. det4's delay-mode calibrations landed at 0.42–0.47 mm — half a millimetre of primary cloud, against det3's 0.098 mm in the same parameterisation. That was the signal that the kernel form itself was wrong, which is §14.

13 · The impulse response h(t)

The electronics do not report charge, they report a shaped pulse. The template h(t) is that shape: the response of one plane's amplifier chain to a delta of charge, measured empirically from the data rather than assumed. Part VI §34 shows how it is built; here is what it looks like.

14 · The sharing kernel — the model's most interesting term

The neighbour term needs a response h₁(t): the shape a strip sees when its neighbour is hit. Two parameterisations exist in the code, and the history of moving from one to the other is instructive.

14.1 share_mode = 'delay' — the original

The first model said: the neighbour sees a copy of the same pulse, delayed by tau_s and smeared by sigma_s. Simple, and it fitted the det3 cosmic data well enough to produce every result in the original study.

h₁(t) = hs(t − τs)    h₂(t) = hs(t − 2τs)    hs = h smeared by σs

14.2 share_mode = 'lp' — what the beam measured

The H4 beam test put det4 at normal incidence, where every 60 ns slice of drift charge lands at the same transverse position. That removes the degeneracy a cosmic fit cannot break, and it showed the delayed-copy picture is wrong in a specific way: the neighbour's pulse peaks essentially with the central strip, but carries a long tail the central strip does not have. That is not a delay. It is RC dispersion.

h₁ = hs * Kτ    h₂ = hs * Kτ * Kτ    Kτ = one-pole low pass, constant τs, area-preserving

The discrete one-pole is applied on the template's own grid and preserves area, so c1 and c2 keep their meaning as charge fractions in both modes. The template grid stops at +1.4 µs, but an RC tail with τ of a few hundred nanoseconds is still alive there, so _lp_copies() zero-pads to +6 µs before convolving — omitting that pad quietly loses charge from the model.

The beam also showed the kernel is invariant across drift field (92–233 V/cm) and gas. The kernel is a property of the resistive layer, not of the operating point — which is the premise that lets one bench calibration serve a detector's whole run range.

Trap The RC constants are representation-dependent. The beam's full-window fits give τ ≈ 300–800 ns; the bench's 1.92 µs window gives τ ≈ 108–141 ns for the same physics, because the bench never sees the far tail. Transplanting the beam's τ and its associated amplitudes into a bench bundle was tried (arm B of the 2026-08-05 recalibration) and made things distinctly worse — kY collapsed to 0.59 and χ² rose 16 %. Transfer the structure, re-fit the constants.

14.3 kY — the plane asymmetry

The X and Y strips sit at different depths under the resistive layer, so they do not couple to it equally. kY multiplies both c₁ and c₂ on the Y plane only. det3 needs 1.22; det4 needs about 2.1.

Open kY scales the copy's amplitude but not its arrival. det3's direct kernel measurement finds the Y-view copy is both stronger and ~2× later (+87 ns against X's +37 ns). A single-τ kernel with an amplitude-only kY cannot represent both planes. A per-plane tau_s is the identified next structural improvement and has not been done.

15 · Assembling the design matrix

Everything above is linear in the charges qk. That is the structural fact the whole fit is built on. Collect the model's response to a unit charge in each depth bin and you get a matrix M with one column per bin and one row per (strip, sample):

model = M(p₀, w, t₀) · q    M is (n_strip · n_sample) × K  —  here 736 × 18

15.1 Why t₀ is quantised to 5 ns

Building M means interpolating the template onto (n_sample × K) time offsets, three times over (own, ±1, ±2). That is the expensive part, and it depends on t₀ but not on p₀ or w. So _time_tensors() caches the three response tensors on a 5 ns t₀ grid, and chi2_plane() snaps t₀ to that grid during the coarse searches. The final Nelder-Mead runs with snap_t0=False and pays the full cost, because by then it is only a few hundred evaluations.

16 · Saturation, and the one-sided penalty

Above about 3550 ADC the digitiser clips. A clipped sample carries real information — "the true value was at least this" — but not the information a χ² term assumes. Treating it as a measurement pulls the fit down; discarding it entirely lets the model put arbitrary charge there.

The model does neither. Clipped samples are censored: excluded from the χ² sum, but penalised if the model falls below the clipped value.

wft/model.py · chi2_plane 
ok  = ~sat.reshape(-1)                      # clipped samples leave the fit
q, rn = nnls(A[ok], y[ok])
chi = rn * rn
if censor and sat.any():
    model = (M @ q).reshape(W.shape)
    pen = np.maximum(0.0, W[sat] - model[sat]) / noise[sat]
    chi += (pen ** 2).sum()                 # only if the model dips below the clip

17 · Per-channel gain

Channels do not all have the same gain. prep_plane() divides each strip's waveform and its noise by a per-channel factor before anything else happens. In practice the map is nearly always unity:

Measured The measured channel-gain spread on det3 is 1.4–1.5 % over 452 channels, and the ablation study found that removing the gain map entirely changes per-event angles by less than the statistical noise. Bundles therefore ship with unit gains unless a measured map exists. This is a real result, not laziness: it says strip-to-strip gain is not a limiting systematic on these detectors.

18 · What the model cannot represent

Being explicit about this is more useful than any list of features.

  • Two tracks. One line, one charge profile. A δ-ray or a second particle in the window is fitted as a compromise between them. This is the dominant failure mode in the tail (Figure 34).
  • Curvature. The track is straight over the 29 mm gap. For cosmic muons and beam particles this is exact to well below the resolution; for a low-energy electron spiralling in a field it would not be.
  • Attachment or gain varying with depth. There is no term for it — and there does not need to be, because qk is free per bin and absorbs any depth dependence of the collected charge. This is a genuine strength: the model measures the depth profile rather than assuming one.
  • A cathode. Nothing stops the fit from putting charge past the physical gap. The column's endpoint is an output (q_uend), and Part VIII §50 uses it to map the cathode.
  • Per-plane t₀ coupling. In the standard single-plane fit, X and Y are fitted independently. §22 covers the joint fit that ties them.
  • Non-linear electronics. Below saturation the chain is assumed linear. The censoring in §16 is the only concession.
