MPGD conference visuals — setup renderings

Publication-grade 3-D views of the two test setups: the SPS H4 beam telescope in P2, and the Saclay cosmic bench.

What this is. Two rebuildable 3-D scenes, driven entirely from the run configs and the measured records — not from a drawing package. Every detector position, the P2 fan's exact shape and all 1280 of its pads, the measured beam spot, the muon angular acceptance and the trigger aperture come from files in this repository or on lxplus. Anything not measured is listed under “What is drawn but not measured” below and is stated in each figure's caption.

The physics case

The one diagram in this package that is not a render. Neutron capture on 3He leaves the compound nucleus with 20.58 MeV of excitation, and the question the experiment asks is how it gets rid of it: a photon, a conventional internal pair conversion pair at small opening angle, or — if the ATOMKI anomaly is real — a 16.8 MeV boson whose e+e- pair cannot open by less than 109°.

Neither curve in panel 3 is traced from anyone's plot. Both are sampled from MX17_Simulation/MX17_Simulator.py — X17PhysicsSpectrum and IPCPhysicsSpectrum, the same generators the acceptance and significance studies use, and which that module documents as matching the Geant4 X17PrimaryGenerator event for event. 400,000 events per channel, Gaussian-smeared by 3° so the X17 Jacobian divergence has a width on paper. The figure therefore cannot drift away from the simulation: change the generator and the figure follows.

The X17 channel is additionally solved analytically in scenes_x17.py (opening_angle_pdf), purely as a check on the sampler — make_x17.py --validate prints both minima, and they agree to better than 0.01°.

Worth knowing before you quote this panel. IPC is not confined to small opening angle the way a quick sketch suggests: because its pair invariant mass is drawn from dN/dM ∝ 1/M, it is a superposition of two-body decays over the whole mass range, with a median opening angle near 30° and roughly 30 % of its yield above 60° — i.e. real background sitting underneath the X17 peak. The two curves are each normalised to unit peak, so nothing in the panel implies a branching ratio; their relative rate is the measurement.

The long version

Same physics, five beats over two rows, for when there is a whole slide to spend on it: the EAR2 beam coming up into the 3He capsule, one capture, the level drop and its three channels, what the boost does, and only then the distribution.

Beat 1 is deliberately generic — a beam and some 3He, no vessel — because early in a talk the target hardware has not been introduced yet. --capsule swaps in the version that draws the real thing: the He3Gas / He3Cap_Al / He3Cap_CFRP polycones from MX17_Full_Geant/src/DetectorConstruction.cc, sectioned from the STEP solid MASTINU X17 HPRV 00 01, in true aspect and mounted nose-first as the simulation mounts it. EAR2 is vertical either way, so the neutrons arrive from below.

The fourth beat is the reason this layout exists, and it turns on one fact: the pair is always back-to-back in the parent's rest frame, so everything you see in the lab is the boost. Whether the parent outruns its own decay products decides the shape:

  • Heavy parent, slow (X17 at 16.8 MeV, β = 0.58). The backward lepton still goes backward, so the pair reaches 180° and is bounded below — a hard edge at 109° with the yield piled against it.
  • Light parent, ultra-relativistic (a 2 MeV IPC pair, β = 0.995). Both leptons are swept forward into a cone, so the pair is bounded above — here at 11° — and can close all the way to zero.

The crossover is at m = √(2meE) ≈ 4.6 MeV. Beat 4 shows this as three worked orientations per channel — the decay direction in the rest frame, and the lab opening angle it produces — so the reader can see that no orientation lets X17 close below 109°, and none lets a light IPC pair open past 11°. The boost arrows are drawn to length β, which is why the X17 arrow is visibly stubby next to the IPC one. Since IPC draws its pair mass from dN/dM ∝ 1/M it gets a different band for every mass, and those bands between them fill the whole axis — which is exactly the smooth slope panel 5 shows underneath the X17 peak.

Panel 5 is a stack, not an overlay (2026-08-17). The compact x17_signature panel above compares two shapes, each normalised to unit peak; the story panel instead draws what the measurement will look like — the IPC background, with a small X17 yield sitting on top of it and the filled area between the two curves being the excess. That costs the figure its ratio-free honesty, so the ratio is a declared parameter: scenes_x17.SIG_FRAC (4 % of the IPC yield over the plotted window) is printed on the panel in words, and is illustrative, not predicted. It puts the bump about 80 % above the local background at its peak. The window starts at 40° for the same reason ATOMKI plot from 40°: the IPC forward peak is eight times the yield at 109°, and including it flattens everything the panel is about — the forward sweep is beat 4's argument, made there as kinematics.

Split across two slides

The two rows are a natural break: the top one sets up the physics and ends on “detect the e+e- pair”, the bottom one derives the measurement from the boost. --layout split writes them as two figures, each with its own title, subtitle and caption.

They are not crops. Each part is the same drawing seen through a different canvas band, so a change to any beat lands in the combined figure and in its slide together — there is no second layout to keep in step.

The deck uses this split as of 2026-08-17 — two slides where there was one, on the --no-title variants, since the slide's own title bar carries what each row's headline said. The rows are wide and short (4.6:1 and 3.9:1), so on a 16:9 slide the figure is width-bound and fills about 55–60 % of the height. Rearranging the beats inside a slide does not recover that — two stacked rows halve the height per row and give back what the extra width buys (measured: gains of ×1.0 or worse for every 2-row arrangement). The only lever that would is a narrower set of beats, e.g. fewer than five orientation columns in beat 4.

One beat per file

The same five beats are also written one to a file (--layout beats, or --layout beat3 for one), for building a slide up a beat at a time or for lifting a single picture into another deck. Same principle as the split above and the same guarantee: each is the story drawing cropped to its own beat, so nothing is redrawn and nothing can drift — adding them left x17_story_capsule_light.png byte-identical. Each keeps its row's full height by default so beats used in sequence stay in register; --tight trims to the ink instead.

…and the beat that hands over to the detector

Added 2026-08-17. By the end of beat 5 the audience has been told that the observable is an angle, and nothing has yet said what measures one. This is the bridge, and it is deliberately the smallest possible claim: a micro-TPC turns one gas gap into a direction, so two of them give the opening angle. Since 2026-08-18 it is drawn inside the story canvas, in the box beat 4 was using (--layout bot3), so the deck's frame 6.3 is one full-width picture: the spectrum neither moves nor resizes, and what changes beside it is the argument. It sat under the spectrum until then, and two stacked full-width pictures can never be more than ~59 % as wide as one. --layout detect_solo still writes it on a canvas of its own.

Drawn, and not to scale — except for the one thing that is. The opening angle is the real 110° and can be measured off the page; the standoff (204 mm from a 23 mm capsule), the 30 mm gap and the 400 mm chamber are not, because at scale the gap would be a hairline. What is tilted is the chamber, by 21°: a track arriving square to the readout plane leaves all its charge at one depth, and then there is no drift-time ladder to reconstruct at all.

One chamber, exploded

The layer stack of a single MX17: 512 readout strips per view at the strip map's own 0.7785 mm pitch, crossed X and Y, the resistive strips over them, the micromesh 150 µm above (garfield_sim/mm_config.py), and the 30 mm drift volume with a muon in it. Each primary cluster drifts straight down to the mesh, so its arrival time measures its depth — which is the micro-TPC measurement the whole reconstruction rests on.

Micro-TPC operation

The measurement itself. A muon crosses the 30 mm gap at an angle and leaves primary ionisation clusters at ~30/cm; each drifts straight down at the MEASURED v = 36.6 µm/ns, so its arrival time at the mesh is the depth it was created at — 820 ns across the full gap. (The Magboltz value for the same mixture, ~34 µm/ns, would give 882 ns; the two numbers agree within the measurement, but mixing them is how the deck's old caption came to read "36.6 µm/ns · 882 ns", which is neither.) The slope of arrival time against strip position is then the track angle, from one plane. vdrift and σT are the Garfield++/Magboltz values for the mixture the bench actually runs (Ar/iso 95/5 + ~1 % H2O at 333 V/cm, from garfield_sim/results/water_grid.json); the gap, the 0.78 mm pitch and the 512 strips are the detector's own.

Two variants: waveforms (the raw per-strip signals, no fit) and ladder (first arrival per strip, with a straight-line fit). The waveform pulse shape is the plane's own MEASURED single-electron response from the wft calibration bundle, and vdrift = 36.6 µm/ns is the value measured for det3 on that run — the Magboltz table's ~34 µm/ns for the same mixture agrees with it.

The ladder is fitted on the first arrival per strip, a deliberately simple estimator that carries a small bias — which is why the real reconstruction fits the waveforms forward (wft/) instead. See RECONSTRUCTION_BASIS.md.

Charge sharing, and what a predicted waveform is made of

Four figures from make_share.py, behind the two reconstruction slides. They share one colour rule — blue for a strip's own charge, vermillion for ±1, purple for ±2 — in the cartoon, in the kernels, in the diagram and in the real-data decomposition, so a colour means one thing across the whole section.

Everything with a number on it comes from the frozen production bundle calib_bundle_lp2_t0p (det3, Saturday long run, resistive 490 V / drift 1000 V) and from event 1663 of its ref-pinned calibration cache — the same event the "One muon through the forward fit" slide uses. The decomposition is exact rather than estimated: the model is a sum of an own-charge term and two neighbour terms, so rebuilding the design matrix with c1 and c2 zeroed and differencing gives the terms themselves.

The amplitudes are a floor, not a measurement. On this bundle c1 = 0.051 sits on the C1_MIN = 0.05 bound that wft.calibrate imposes: a cosmic-angle fit cannot separate sharing from a wider initial cloud plus a different vdrift, and without the bound it walks c1 to zero and hides the sharing in sigma_p0. The H4 beam test at normal incidence breaks that degeneracy and measures ~0.28–0.30. The shapes and the delays on these figures are the model's own and need no caveat; the X/Y ratio (kY = 2.9) is fitted and is the interesting number; the absolute level is floor-limited. c2 > c1 in this bundle, which is a fit correlation and not a ladder — what the model needs right is their sum and their delay.

The n_TOF setup, built up

The talk's setup section is one figure in eight states: the ³He capsule, a neutron reaching it, the e⁺e⁻ pair leaving it, and then the four detector layers going on one at a time at a fixed camera. Unlike the two bench scenes, the geometry here is not held in geometry.py — it is imported at run time from MX17_Full_Geant/scripts/plot_geometry.py, which is written against the simulation's own SimConfig.hh, so the figure and the Geant4 model cannot drift apart.

The subject changes scale by a factor of fifty between the capsule (23 mm) and the setup (1.2 m) and then changes what it has to show, so the sequence runs in four acts at four cameras: frames 1–3 on the vessel and the event in it, frame 4 as the chambers close in around it, frames 5–7 on the apparatus, and frames 8–9 on the same apparatus from above. The last cut is not decoration: the layers are stacked radially, so from any three-quarter view the trigger wall stands in front of the plastics and the liquid, and a leg arriving in them cannot be seen. Within each act the frames share a camera and a size exactly, so the layers grow onto a still picture; frame 4 repeats frame 5's layer at a larger scale and is the first to cut.

The event is selected, not hand-picked. On top of the legibility ranking, extract_ntof_event.py requires both legs to leave the vessel through the barrel wall — at the barrel radius, inside the straight section, and within ~33° of transverse — rather than through a domed end, where a leg crosses several times the wall thickness at a glancing angle. It then weights heavily towards the event in which both legs cross all four layers, so each can be followed chamber → trigger wall → plastic → liquid and the build-up has something to add at every step: exactly one of the 400 simulated events does that, and it is the one drawn. (A leg can pass between the two plastic bars or out of the side of the stack, so this is rarer than it sounds.) It also prefers events whose legs land in the two arms the figure draws solid, so no deposit ends up glowing inside a wireframe; --prefer-arms, scenes_ntof.NEAR_ARMS and the camera azimuth all have to be kept in step.

Only the charged pair is drawn. The event's bremsstrahlung gammas are real, and they are in the JSON, but a neutral track leaves the frame in a straight line from wherever it was radiated, so on the picture they read as stray rays with no visible cause; scenes_ntof.DRAWN_PARTICLES is what suppresses them.

The legs grow with the apparatus: each is cut where it first reaches a layer that is not on the frame yet (scenes_ntof.truncate_at_next_layer), so a track only runs on to the trigger wall on the frame that puts the trigger wall there. Drawn full length from the start, the tracks claim three frames early that all of it is measured, and the layer being added stops being the thing that changes. The beam column stops at the vessel's nose as soon as any detector is placed: past the target it runs through the middle of the apparatus and reads as a pole holding it up, and drawn even alongside the capsule it sleeves a 23 mm object in translucent grey, so the target goes hazy instead of crisp. The direction dart lies on the axis inside the column, on the close-up act only.

Each build frame names the layer it just added, and drops the name again on the next frame — so the label follows the build outward instead of piling four names onto the finished apparatus. It carries one leader per solid arm: a layer is four objects and the frame draws two of them solid, so a single line would quietly imply the label is about that one — and it is one leader per drawn object, so the plastics, which are two separate bars per arm, carry four and their label drops the "2 ×" the lines already say. The anchors are computed from the same geometry the meshes are built from (scenes_ntof.layer_anchor), so a leader cannot end up pointing at where a layer used to be, and the text is pinned to a corner of the frame rather than offset from its anchor, because a pinwheel's corners are the only reliably empty places — top-left on the build acts, and at the bottom on the close-up, where the chambers fill the frame and the only empty space left is the see-through one. Sizes on the figures are in centimetres, since they are read from across a room; the slide bullets keep the millimetres. --no-labels turns all in-image type off.

The overhead act draws the capsule whole and solid. Everywhere else the vessel is sectioned on a plane through the beam axis with the near half removed — the only way to show 0.6 mm of wall and a lit gas core at once — but that plane contains the overhead view direction, so from up there it does not open the vessel, it deletes the half of it nearest the bottom of the frame (VIEWS['over'] sets cut=False). It is also the one part BARE does not whisper: everything else that drops to BARE_ALPHA is a box the eye can still infer from its neighbours, but the capsule is 23 mm on a 1.2 m frame and faded it is a smudge at the exact point the picture converges on. Solid, it is a small dark disc — the CFRP overwrap end-on, with the gas bore a speck at its centre — where the two legs meet.

The sequence closes on a plan (make_ntof_plan.py, ntof_plan below), which is the one figure here that is not a render. Every frame above it has perspective: the four arms sit at four different distances from the lens, so every length on them is foreshortened by its own amount and the distances can only be written in a caption. The plan is orthographic and 1:1 in both axes — the beam is the view axis, so the drawing plane is the X-Z plane the apparatus is symmetric in. Three things become measurable rather than asserted: the 204.5 mm standoff every arm's window sits on, drawn as the circle they are all tangent to; the layer radii out to the vessels (330 / 410 / 487 mm on arm B, the one arm no leg crosses and so the one the chain is drawn on — the outer two move by a few mm between arms); and the size of the target, which at 1:1 is a 23 mm dot in the middle of a 1.1 m apparatus. It is built from the same geometry module and the same event JSON as the renders, so it cannot drift away from them, and it makes two facts visible that a three-quarter camera cannot: the ~16 mm pinwheel offsets, and that two of the four liquid vessels are laid on their side with their PMTs pointing sideways into the plane. What it gives up is the beam axis: the legs also rise ~135 mm along it, so the opening angle as drawn (122°) is not the space angle (110°), and the figure says both.

The e⁺e⁻ pair is one real Geant4 event; the neutron that made it is drawn, not simulated alongside it, and it has to be that way. The neutron is transported from the measured EAR2 flux and does what the physics list says it does, which is ³He(n,p)t; the radiative branch that forms the ⁴He* this search lives on is ~10⁻⁾ of it, so no neutron run will ever contain one. The pair is therefore thrown by the generator from a vertex sampled in the gas, and the neutron history is translated so its interaction point coincides with that vertex. tools/extract_ntof_event.py records the pairing and the shift in data/ntof_event.json; the talk says it on the slide. What the scene draws for the neutron is a straight line up the beam axis to the pair vertex — the transported history is still selected and stored, but it belonged to a different event, so drawing it meant translating it onto this vertex and then showing its own in-gas scattering, which is a fact about that neutron rather than about this figure. The neutron run is still needed either way: the beam envelope is measured from its sampled primaries. The (n,p)t proton and triton the transported neutron actually made are kept in the JSON and deliberately not drawn — they belong to the other branch.

Drawn but not measured, in this scene only: the liquid scintillator's fill dome is extruded at constant height along the vessel's long axis (the real 6.5 L bulge falls away towards the edges), and the beam envelope is drawn at the radius the simulation's own sampled primaries occupy — 90 % inside 8.8 mm — which is the beam profile, not the collimator; and the arriving neutron is a straight line up that axis rather than a trajectory. The two arms nearest the camera — B in front of it and A on the right of the frame — are drawn as outlines only, so that the target and the two arms this pair actually crossed (D and C) stay visible, and on the overhead frames the structure of the other two drops to BARE_ALPHA so only the active volumes carry colour.

Animations

Turntables for the talk, and build-up sequences whose numbered stills can be dropped on successive slides so the setup assembles itself as you speak. Every frame comes from the same scene code as the stills above.

What is drawn but not measured

  • sps transverse alignment — Every SPS run_config carries det_center_coords x = y = 0 -- nominal, not surveyed. The uRWELLs and MX17 are drawn centred on the beam axis (250 mm above the table), which is how they were mounted, but no transverse survey exists to confirm it.
  • sps mx17 z — MX17 "Detector E" at z = 1155 mm is flagged PLACEHOLDER in the run config itself.
  • p2 mounting — Fan bisector vertical, apex up, centred, 130 mm from the lowest active point to the table top -- supplied by the P2 group, corroborated by the trigger aperture coming out horizontal, but not surveyed.
  • bench scintillator z — The 60 x 60 cm trigger paddles are not in any run config (they are hardware outside the DAQ geometry). They are drawn just outside the stack at z = -110 / +1420 mm, matching the engineer-package schematic.
  • bench structure — The bench uprights and rails are drawn for context and are not surveyed dimensions.
  • drawn thicknesses — Chamber/frame thicknesses are chosen for legibility; only the 30 mm MX17 drift gap is a real dimension.
