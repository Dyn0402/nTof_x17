\subsection{What ``detected'' means}\label{sec:detected}
\tbw{Denominator: one clean M3 ray ($\chi^2<1$, four clusters) crossing the interior (${\ge}30$\,mm from the $x$ sides,
${\ge}8$\,mm from the passivated $y$ sides). Detected: both views fitted, ${\le}2$ competing strips per view, and the
reconstructed track within $R$ of the M3 ray at the mesh. When the chamber reconstructs several tracks, the event is
detected if one of them is the M3 track (multi-track credit; this is the default number). Removed from the denominator:
tracks the reference itself got wrong (bent between the M3 doublets, 0.20\,\%) and tracks leaving through the edge
of the other coordinate (0.15\,\%).}
\tbw{\textbf{The radius.} $\varepsilon(R)$ has two regimes (\cref{fig:effradius}): the resolution core (core
$\sigma=0.55$\,mm per view at the mesh, dominated by the M3 pointing and scattering), steep up to ${\sim}4$\,mm; then a
slow rise of 0.1--0.2\,pt per mm out to 40\,mm, made of mis-measured tracks ($\delta$-pulled fits, saturated
blobs, bent reference tracks) and accidentals. We use $R=5$\,mm, the knee: 9 core $\sigma$, where the Gaussian core
is exhausted and the accidental rate (event mixing) is 0.07\,\%, below the statistical error and 70$\times$ below
the inefficiency. $R\in[4,8]$\,mm moves the efficiency by $-0.5/{+0.6}$\,pt (systematic).}

\subsection{det3: single-track efficiency}\label{sec:single}
\tbw{A ``single-track'' sample selected by det3 itself is biased: a failure that looks like two tracks would leave
the sample. On the bench an external tag does not help: M3 sees a second straight track in 2 of the 276 events where
det3 sees two particles, because those particles are local (soft electrons and secondaries from the material above,
at large angles). Instead we use the two views: a real second particle is ionisation in the gas, seen in both
views with the same charge. Only events with such a particle (both views, charge-paired, not attached to the muon)
leave the sample; everything else stays and counts against the chamber, including second deposits seen in one
view only and showers vetoed by the spark rule.}
\tbw{Result (two golden rows, 25\,875 tracks): $\varepsilon_\mathrm{single}=95.2\pm0.1$\,(stat)$^{+0.6}_{-0.5}$\,($R$)\,\%.
If every one-view second deposit and every vetoed shower were an outside particle, it would be 97.0\,\%; that is
the upper end of the selection systematic. The losses, in \% of single tracks: $\delta$-rays 1.7 (\cref{app:losses}),
vetoed showers and unrelated deposits 1.1, one-view second deposits 0.5, discharges 0.5, noise bands 0.2, fits off
with no flag 0.2.}

\subsection{det3: two particles in the chamber}\label{sec:pairing}
\tbw{\textbf{Efficiency.} With an independent second particle in the chamber (2.0\,\% of triggers), the muon is still
among the reconstructed tracks in 94.5--96.1\,\%: as efficient as with one particle.}
\tbw{\textbf{Pairing the views.} Two particles give two segments per view and two possible X/Y pairings; the wrong
one makes two ghost tracks. The views share no coordinate, but they read the same avalanche charge at the same drift
times, so the reconstruction pairs them by the charge balance and the charge-arrival time (Paper~II). On 552 real
two-particle cosmic events where M3 marks one particle in both views, the pairing is right in
$98.0\pm0.6$\,\% (charge alone: 95.7\,\%; 100\,\% when the two charges differ by more than a factor 1.65, 96\,\% when they
are similar). This is the feasibility demonstration for pairs at n\_TOF.}
