\section{Why hit times fail}\label{sec:hitsfail}
\sectionstate{partial}{first prose draft 2026-11-18; figure \texttt{figs/make\_event\_waveforms.py} (event chosen by rule, json next to it). Motivation only (D04): the numbers and the law are IIb.}
\Cref{fig:event_waveforms} shows one cosmic muon in chamber~A at about 9\textdegree{} from the drift direction. The
charge of the track appears in each view as a slanted band in the plane of strip and time. Each strip's pulse is
several samples long and, more important, several strips carry charge at any one time: the charge of one depth is
shared among neighbouring strips by the spreads of \cref{sec:kernel}, so one strip receives charge from a range of
depths. Its leading-edge time is set by the earliest charge that reaches it in sufficient amount. At the two ends
of the band the strips receive only charge that has spread from depths inside the band, and their times are pulled
towards those of their inner neighbours. The ladder of hit times is therefore shorter in time than the drift across
the column, and a line through it gives a steeper track: here 10.4\textdegree{} in both views, where the reference
track is at 9.0\textdegree{} (X) and 8.3\textdegree{} (Y) and the waveform fit of \cref{sec:model} gives
8.7\textdegree{} and 8.1\textdegree. The event was not chosen for effect: of the 2\,082 tracks of the two runs
between 8\textdegree{} and 16\textdegree{} it is the one closest to the median in both the hit-line bias (ratio of
the slopes 1.16 in X and 1.27 in Y) and the residual of the waveform fit.

The effect grows as the track approaches the drift direction, because the column then covers fewer strips and its
ends weigh more, and it is the same for a rising-edge, constant-fraction, peak or matched-filter time, because it is
in the charge that reaches the strip and not in how the time is read off. Its measurement on four chambers and at a known angle in the beam, the law
it follows and its decomposition into the spreads are in the companion paper~\cite{paperIIb}.
