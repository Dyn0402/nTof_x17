\subsection{Design choices}\label{sec:design_choices}
\askStephan{Dylan (2026-11-02): the section is yours to write; these are the design points we think a reader needs, with
what the data say about each. Keep, reword or drop as you see fit.}
\tbw{\textbf{(1) Pads for an equal X/Y charge share.} The readout is a single plane of square pads, each routed to an X
or a Y strip, instead of two stacked strip layers, so that both views see the same charge. It worked: the X fraction of
the charge is $f=0.485$ (A) and $0.513$ (B), with an event-to-event spread of 0.014--0.032 (D 0.116, its X
readout fault). How much the balance matters for tracking is not measured; its only cost we know of is one more copper
layer in the material budget.}
\tbw{\textbf{(2) A resistive Micromegas in TPC mode.} The resistive layer spreads the charge and delays it: each strip's
signal carries delayed copies of its neighbours' (${\sim}29\,\%$ at $\tau\approx47$\,ns to $\pm1$ strip). That compresses
the drift-time ladder when strip times are used, and is the reason the reconstruction fits the waveforms with a
charge-spreading model (Paper~II). For a TPC alone we might not choose it again. In a high-rate or harsh
environment, however, it quenches discharges and keeps the chamber alive and live (no measurable dead time per discharge
on the bench, \cref{sec:operation}), so on balance it may still be the right choice.}
\tbw{\textbf{(3) Resistive strips rather than a uniform resistive layer.} A continuous resistive layer was tried
first: it came out with too low a resistance, and current flowed through ``hot spots''. Resistive strips with the
strip ends passivated allowed a higher resistance and far less current. \emph{Stephan: the measured resistances and
currents of the uniform-layer boards, if they exist.} The bench measures what the strip chambers draw: a gain-driven dark current of ${\sim}1$\,\textmu A at the working
point (\cref{sec:operation}); the bench has no uniform-layer board of this batch to compare with.}
\tbw{\textbf{(4) Strip direction and passivation.} The strips run along $y$ and end at the two $y$ edges, where their
terminations are passivated (covered) over 18.0 and 18.7\,mm. \emph{Stephan: why the passivation is needed (the
termination / HV connection at the strip ends, and discharges there?).} This removes ${\sim}10\,\%$ of the area
(efficient area $399.4\times359.9$\,mm$^2$). The orientation was chosen mainly for where that loss falls: at the top and
bottom of the chamber, not on the sides that face the neighbouring chambers at n\_TOF. We also expected better
resolution across the strips (the charge spreads along them). The data show no significant difference, and on chamber~A
the opposite: angle $s_{68}$ X/Y $0.98/0.90$\textdegree{} (A), $1.03/1.08$\textdegree{} (B); bench position residuals X/Y within
12\,\textmu m of each other. The two $x$ sides are not passivated, and there the drift field fringes over 20--30\,mm,
which biases the X angle and sets our 30\,mm fiducial (\cref{sec:edge}); the passivated sides are sharp.}
