\intent{A TPC assumes a flat readout plane and a drift field normal to it. Neither holds to a millimeter, in two
measured ways: every readout board is bowed, and the field fringes at the two sides without passivation. Both set how
the tracking numbers are obtained: the first through a correction, the second through the fiducial.}

\subsection{The readout boards are bowed}\label{sec:bow}
A TPC converts the time structure of its signals into a track angle under two assumptions. The first is that the
readout plane is flat, and the second is that the drift field is perpendicular to it. In all five chambers, both
assumptions fail at the millimeter level, in the way expected for a bowed readout board. We show this with three
observables.

\emph{Positions.} Consider a mesh that is displaced by $h$ along the drift direction. A track of slope $\tan\theta$
crosses this mesh at a point shifted by $h\tan\theta$. The slope of the position residual as a function of the slope
of the reference track is therefore the local height of the mesh, $h(x,y)$. This height is measured independently by
the X and the Y strips. \Crefp{fig:bow}{a, b} shows it for chamber~A. The mesh has the shape of a dish, lower at the
center than at the rim, in both directions and in both views. A quadratic surface fitted to all tracks places the
center $1.31\pm0.22$\,mm below a point 150\,mm from the center for chamber~A, and between 1.7 and 1.9\,mm below for
the other four chambers (\crefp{fig:bow}{c}). The center is the part of the mesh farthest from the cathode. This is the
direction in which the weight of the board, the overpressure of the gas and the tension of the mesh all push.

\emph{Drift direction.} If the drift lines leave the mesh perpendicular to its surface, charge produced at a height
$d$ above the mesh arrives displaced by $d\,\partial h/\partial x$. The fitted transverse rate then acquires an offset
$w_0=\vd\,r_f\,\partial h/\partial x$, where $r_f$ is between $1/2$ for a flat cathode and 1 for a cathode bowed in the
same way as the board. For a dish, $w_0$ must therefore vary linearly across the chamber, with a slope fixed by the
curvature measured from the positions. We observe this linear variation in all ten chamber views, with $r_f$ between
0.52 and 1.00 (\crefp{fig:bow}{d}). The sign of the variation is predicted by the geometry and is not fitted.

\emph{Cluster width.} The cluster of strips is narrowest for a track that runs along the local drift lines. This
observable uses only the amplitudes, with no times and no fitted angle. The angle at which the cluster is narrowest
changes across the chamber with the predicted sign in all ten views, and by more than two standard deviations in eight
of them.

The bow is a static property of each chamber. Nineteen runs, taken on other nights, in the other slot of the bench
and at other voltages, all give the depth of their chamber within two standard deviations. A bow of the reference
planes could imitate the position observable, but not the other two. Its contribution to the depth is bounded at
$0.4\pm0.2$\,mm (\cref{app:bow}).

In the reconstruction, the bow is treated as a correction that is part of each chamber's calibration. The correction
is one surface per chamber, which is applied to the drift direction and to the height of the track point. We test it
on tracks that were held out of the fit of the surface. It improves the angular resolution by a median of 16\,\% over
the ten views. For chamber~A, the angular resolution improves from 1.10 to 0.99\textdegree{} in X and from 1.07 to
0.90\textdegree{} in Y. The position residual improves by 5 to 18\,\%.

\subsection{The field fringes at the unpassivated sides}\label{sec:edge}
The resistive strips end at the two $y$ sides, where their ends are passivated. The two $x$ sides, at strip
coordinates 0 and 398.6\,mm, are not passivated (\cref{sec:design_choices}). Within 20 to 30\,mm of the $x$ sides the
chamber behaves differently from the interior. We observe three effects, all of which extend over the same distance
from the side (\cref{fig:edge}).

First, the X angle is biased. Within 5 to 20\,mm of an $x$ side, the fitted slope differs from the reference by up to
0.1--0.36 in $\tan\theta$, and beyond 30\,mm it differs by less than 0.01. The bias has opposite signs on the two sides
(chambers A, C and E). This is the behavior expected if the drift lines converge inwards, so that charge released near
the cathode lands further inside than it started. Second, the drift column is longer, by up to 1.1\,\textmu s at 5 to
10\,mm from a side in chamber~A, and it has more charge on more strips. This is expected if the edge strips collect
electrons from outside the strip area, over longer paths. Third, the efficiency reaches its plateau only 10 to 20\,mm
from an $x$ side, compared to about 5\,mm from a passivated $y$ side. The second of these observations uses no track
model, and the other two use the reference tracks. A fringe field that extends this far is what we expect for a gap of
28\,mm: the field of an open edge decays with a length of about $g/\pi\approx9$\,mm, so three such lengths reach
30\,mm.

This effect is distinct from the bow. The bow places the mesh nearer the cathode at the rim, and therefore predicts a
shorter drift column there, by 45 to 64\,ns. The fringe field makes the drift column longer. The shifts measured at the
edges of the five chambers, between $-115$ and $+78$\,ns, are the sum of the two effects.

The fringe field determines the fiducial region. We quote angles and positions at least 30\,mm from the $x$ sides. At
the passivated $y$ sides the efficiency turns on sharply, but the angle is still affected: in a band 10 to 30\,mm from
the $y$ sides the efficiency is at its full value, while the Y angle is 1.3 to 1.9 times worse than in the interior.
The efficiency does not require a fiducial region at all. Over the full active area it is 0.9 points lower than in the
interior (\cref{sec:single}).
