Five chambers were tested on the bench. We call them A--E. Chambers A--D later ran together at n\_TOF and keep
these names in the companion paper; chamber E, whose readout board has non-amplifying strips (\cref{sec:det4}), went
to the SPS instead. Chamber A is the subject of this paper; B--E show what varies from one board to the next.
Each chamber's bench numbers come from its \emph{golden run}, its long run at fixed HV (\cref{tab:datasets}); these
ran at 490--495\,V, 10--65\,V above the HV the scans later showed to be best (\cref{sec:operation}). Chamber A has two, taken back to back on the night of 27--28 June at the same HV and in the
same slot: \textbf{Run~1}, the long run that closed its HV and drift scans, and \textbf{Run~2}, the overnight run
that followed. We treat them as one data set (10\,h; 29\,398 reference tracks over the full active area, 28\,467 of them without a second particle in the chamber, \cref{sec:single}): taken apart they
agree on everything this paper measures (interior efficiency (all tracks, multi-track credit, reference and geometry classes out; \cref{tab:single}) $95.48\pm0.26$ / $95.26\pm0.15$\,\%, angle $s_{68}$
0.975 / 0.991\textdegree{} in X and 0.896 / 0.900\textdegree{} in Y, position core at the mesh 0.50 / 0.51\,mm in X,
uncorrelated time 22.4 / 22.2\,ns). The one difference is the drift velocity, 35.8 against 37.2\,\textmu m/ns, as
the gas dried through the night (\cref{sec:gas}); the calibration was made on Run~1 and applied to Run~2 with only
$v$ refitted. Where a figure needs a single event it is from Run~1.

\subsection{Reconstruction}\label{sec:reco}
\sectionstate{partial}{added 2026-11-06 at Dylan's request (brief, points to Paper~II); \texttt{wft/model.py}, \texttt{wft/reco.py}, \texttt{RECONSTRUCTION\_BASIS.md}}
Each readout strip records a waveform: the DREAM electronics sample it every 60\,ns over the drift window, so one
view of an event is a picture of amplitude against strip and time (\crefp{fig:reco}{a}). The per-strip hit times that
a conventional micro-TPC analysis fits are biased: each is an aggregate of charge from several drift depths, mixed
onto the strip by drift diffusion and the prompt charge footprint and, in the view read along the resistive strips,
by the RC spreading along them. This compresses the drift-time ladder by 20--30\,\% and reads tracks too
steep~\cite{paperII}, whatever estimator defines the hit time. We therefore use the hits only to
find candidate tracks, and take the geometry from a fit of the whole strip $\times$ time picture. The model is a
straight track, with its position at the mesh, its transverse drift speed and its start time $t_0$ as free
parameters; the charge arriving in each 60\,ns slice of drift is shared onto the strips, spread onto their
neighbours by the resistive layer (an RC kernel), and folded with the measured pulse shape of the electronics
(\crefp{fig:reco}{b}). The charge profile along the track is solved inside the fit, and the angle follows from the
transverse speed and the drift velocity, $\tan\theta=w/v$. The two views are fitted separately and paired by their
charge balance and charge-arrival time. Dead channels and saturated samples are excluded from the fit rather than
read as zero charge. The reconstruction uses no information from the reference tracks. The model, its
calibration (per chamber and per run condition) and its limits are the subject of the companion
paper~\cite{paperII}; every result in this paper uses it.
