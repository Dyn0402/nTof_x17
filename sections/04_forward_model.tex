\subsection{The model}
One view of one track is described by three geometric parameters and a charge profile (\cref{fig:forward_model}).
The track crosses the mesh plane at position $p_0$ at time $t_0$; charge from deeper in the gap arrives later and,
for an inclined track, further along the strips, at $p_0 + w\,u$ after a drift time $u$. The transverse rate $w$ is
what the fit measures; the angle follows from it and the drift velocity. The drift time is divided into $K$ bins of
one sample, $\Delta t = 60$\,ns, and the charge $q_k$ arriving in each bin is free: the fit assumes nothing about the
ionisation along the track, its fluctuations or its loss to attachment.

The charge of bin $k$ lands as a Gaussian of variance
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_k^2 = \sigma_0^2 + D_p^2\,u_k + \frac{(w\,\Delta t)^2}{12},
  \label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
the prompt footprint, the transverse diffusion over the drift time $u_k$ ($D_p^2 = D_T^2\,v$), and the extent of the
track within the bin. In the Y view it then spreads along the resistive strips, its variance growing by
$2\,D_{RC}\,s$ at a time $s$ after landing; in the X view it does not. A strip sees every increment of the charge
above it through the pulse response of the electronics, measured per chamber, and the result is sampled every
60\,ns. There are no fitted couplings between strips: the fractions of \cref{sec:kernel} are what
\cref{eq:sigma} and the RC term produce, not parameters.

The model is linear in the $q_k$. For given $(p_0, w, t_0)$ they are solved by non-negative least squares, and
the three geometric parameters are found by minimising the $\chi^2$ over every sample of every strip in a window
around the candidate, with the noise of each strip measured from the data. Samples at the ADC ceiling and channels
known to be dead are censored: they are treated as unknown, not as zero charge. The two views are fitted
separately. The angle is
\begin{equation}
  \tan\theta = \frac{w - w_0}{k_w\,v},
  \label{eq:tan}
\end{equation}
where $v$ is the drift velocity, $w_0$ the transverse rate of a track along the chamber normal (the drift field
is not exactly along it) and $k_w$ a scale factor of order one, both measured per view (\cref{sec:calibration}).

\subsection{Calibration from the chamber's own data}\label{sec:calibration}
Every constant of the model is measured per chamber and per run condition, in a fixed order, starting from nothing
(\cref{tab:calib}). Hit times enter once, to find the candidate tracks and a first alignment with the reference
tracker; no constant is taken from them.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Pulse response and trigger latency.} The response is measured from the chamber's own pulses in the X
  view \tbw{one sentence on how (\texttt{wft.calibrate.measure\_templates})}; it is used for both views, because the Y
  view's own average shape already contains the resistive spreading. The time $t_0$ is fixed by the trigger up to the phase of a 10\,ns clock, so the
  latency is one number per view.
  \item \emph{Resistive spreading.} $D_{RC}$ is fitted on unsaturated planes with $t_0$ pinned and the track position
  profiled, the other constants fixed. Chamber~E's value is the one measured in the beam.
  \item \emph{Footprint and diffusion.} $\sigma_0 = 0.30$\,mm for all chambers and both views, from the beam
  measurement at normal incidence; left free on the bench it trades against the other constants and is not a
  measurement. $D_p$ comes from the growth of the width with drift time on unsaturated tracks at normal
  incidence and agrees with Magboltz for chambers A and E.
  \item \emph{Drift velocity.} $v$ is the gap divided by the duration of the charge column of the fitted tracks, with
  no gas simulation as a prior.
  \item \emph{Angle mapping and bow.} $w_0$ and $k_w$ are measured against the reference angles, the alignment is
  redone with waveform positions, and the map of the mesh height over the board (its bow~\cite{paperI}) is measured
  and applied. The chain is iterated once more and checked for convergence.
\end{enumerate}
Templates, latency, $D_{RC}$, $w_0$, $k_w$ and the bow converge at the first check on every chamber; the alignment along the
drift direction needed one more iteration on three. After the last step $k_w$ is 1.00 to 1.08: the velocity from
the column duration and the velocity that makes the angles agree with the reference differ by a few per cent, in one
direction, and $k_w$ carries the difference. \askDylan{This few-per-cent scale is the one place the reference angles
enter the angle. On the SPS rows with a column velocity the known angle reads $0.98\pm0.03$ with $k_w$ from the bench
(\cref{sec:knownangle}). Is that the statement you want here, i.e. $k_w$ transfers, or should the text say the
angle scale is tied to the reference?}

\subsection{What goes wrong, and what does not transfer}\label{sec:traps}
Three things were got wrong before the chain above was fixed, and each is silent. A reference position taken from
an alignment made with hit times biases the fitted $v$ by 3 to 15\,\%, which is why the positions are profiled and the
velocity is taken from the column duration, never from a gas simulation. A set of
free coupling amplitudes between strips, which we used before the RC term, has a direction the cosmic data do not
constrain; fitted freely it returned a second neighbour larger than the first on every chamber, which is not
physical. And a calibration is a property of a chamber \emph{and} of its conditions: the readout constants
($\sigma_0$, the pulse response, $D_{RC}$) carry from the beam to the bench, the gas constants ($v$, $D_p$, the gain)
do not.

The last point can be quantified on the beam data themselves. The rows of chamber~E at the known angle in
Ar/CO$_2$/iC$_4$H$_{10}$ were fitted with the bench diffusion constant, $D_p = 0.020$, in a gas that was three to four
times slower than the bench gas; Magboltz gives $D_p = 0.006$ for it. \tbw{Refit at $D_p$ = 0.003--0.020 on 200
planes per view of two rows (\texttt{hits\_daq/sps\_dp\_scan.py}): which value the planes prefer and how far the fitted
slope moves. Running 2026-11-18.}

The main known mismatch of the model is that it is symmetric in the X view and the boards are
not (\cref{sec:kernel}). It shows as structure in the X $\chi^2$ and as a tension between the footprint preferred by
single fits and by averaged profiles. In a simulation with the asymmetry put in, its cost is $-0.05$\textdegree{}
on the mean angle, $\mp0.19$\textdegree{} for tracks at normal incidence depending on the side, and
$+0.007$\textdegree{} on the resolution. It is not modelled: no mechanism predicts its size, so a term for it would be
a free parameter per board and per run condition.
