\intent{This appendix answers one question: how is the efficiency defined, and where does each lost track go? It
holds the definition and the choice of the matching radius, the variants of the single-track selection, and every loss
class with a real event and the result of trying to recover it.}

\subsection{Definition and matching radius}
\textbf{Denominator.} The denominator contains one reference ray per event. We require a clean reference track
($\chi^2<1$, four clusters) in a complete event, and the footprint of the track at the chamber must lie inside the
measured active area. The headline numbers use the whole active area, while \cref{fig:effradius,fig:residuals} are
drawn in the interior. No other track is removed from the denominator. Tracks whose reference may be bent, edge
effects, discharges, $\delta$-rays and second particles that we cannot separate are all counted as losses. The only
exception is a second particle that is shown to be independent of the muon (it is seen in both views with matching
charge). Such an event leaves the single-track sample, but it is not dropped: it is counted with the two-particle
events (\cref{sec:twoeff}).

\textbf{Numerator: any candidate.} When an event has more than one deposit, the reconstruction returns several track
candidates per view. We count a muon as found when, in each view, the candidate nearest to the reference ray is within
$R$ of it at the mesh, whatever rank the reconstruction gave to that candidate. This means that an event is still
efficient if the reconstruction keeps the wrong one of two tracks, as long as the other track matches the muon. Every
efficiency quoted for chamber~A uses this rule (full area: 94.2\,\% of all reference tracks, 94.8\,\% of single tracks;
interior: 95.1\,\% and 95.7\,\%).

We also define a stricter \emph{kept-track count}, in which only the track that the reconstruction keeps may match,
and each view may have at most two competing strips. The kept-track count gives 91.5\,\% over the full area and
92.3\,\% in the interior. It measures the track selection as well as the chamber, and we use it only to compare
chambers (\cref{sec:fleet}).

The any-candidate rule has one limit. The nearest candidates of the two views are chosen independently, so the rule
does not check that they belong to the same particle. The pairing of X and Y is studied separately in
\cref{sec:pairing}.

\textbf{The radius.} \Cref{fig:effradius} shows the efficiency as a function of $R$. There are two regimes. Up to
about 4\,mm the efficiency rises steeply, which is the core of the resolution. Beyond that it rises slowly, by
0.1--0.2 points per mm, out to 40\,mm. We use $R=5$\,mm, at the knee between the two regimes. At this radius the
probability of an accidental match is 0.07\,\%, which is below the statistical error and 70 times smaller than the
inefficiency. Varying $R$ between 4 and 8\,mm changes the efficiency by $-0.5/{+0.7}$ points. The accidental curve is
the probability that an unrelated track lands inside $R$. To estimate it, we compare the chamber's fit of one event
with the reference ray of another event (event mixing, 20 shifts), and scale by the fraction of events with a fit.
This is an upper bound on the rate of fake matches, since in a real event the muon is also present.

\textbf{Why 5\,mm and not a few $\sigma$ of the resolution.} The residual at the mesh is not a measurement of the
chamber's resolution. A Gaussian fit to the core of the residual distribution (\crefp{fig:residuals}{a},
$|r|<1.5$\,mm) gives $\sigma=0.55\pm0.02$ (X) and $0.53\pm0.03$\,mm (Y). The chamber contributes only about 0.12\,mm
of this (\cref{sec:position}). The rest comes from the pointing of the reference ray (0.24 and 0.21\,mm) and from the
multiple scattering of soft cosmic muons. In addition, the tails of the distribution are not Gaussian: only 94 and
92\,\% (X, Y) of the fitted tracks lie within 3$\sigma$ of the reference, while 98\,\% lie within 5\,mm. A cut at
3$\sigma$ (about 1.6\,mm) would therefore count as losses 11 points of tracks that the chamber did find, on the right
particle. This would mix the tails of the reference into the efficiency. The efficiency is meant to answer whether
the chamber saw the muon. How well the chamber measured the muon is a separate question, which is answered by the
resolution (\cref{sec:tracking}). Between 1.6 and 5\,mm the efficiency rises by 11 points while the accidental rate
stays below 0.07\,\%, so the tracks in this range are the muon, only measured less well.

\textbf{What lies beyond 5\,mm} (\crefp{fig:residuals}{b}). The gray curve is the distance from a fit to the reference
ray of another event, scaled as if every fit were unrelated to its muon. An unrelated fit lands mostly more than
40\,mm away. The data have 7 tracks there, where this hypothesis would give 16\,300. At most about 0.05\,\% of fits
are therefore unrelated, and unrelated fits contribute less than one of the 244 tracks at 10--20\,mm. This means that
the shoulder from 5 to 20\,mm is correlated with the muon. It is made of fits pulled by the $\delta$-rays of the
muon, of fits to a secondary instead of the muon, and of bent reference tracks (the classes below). We place the cut
before this shoulder and count everything beyond it as a loss. The residual distributions shown are those of the track
that the reconstruction keeps (both views fitted, no radius cut).

The 7.71\,\% of interior reference tracks that are not detected fall into five groups.

\textbf{The chamber itself: noise bands and discharges (0.72\,\%).} A noise band is a baseline offset that lasts the
whole readout window on a contiguous patch of strips, and it is seen in the X and Y views together. The track itself
is clean in these events, but the view fails the cut on competing clusters. The reconstruction now excludes the band
from the fit, which reduced this class in Run~1 from 1.43\,\% to 0.22\,\%. Under the any-candidate rule of the body,
half of the remaining band tracks are found, which is why the body quotes 0.10\,\%. In a discharge (0.50\,\%), whole
chips light up and ring. A real discharge has 113 strips over threshold, which is beyond the limit of 50 strips above
which the fit is not attempted. These tracks are not recoverable.

\textbf{A second particle or deposit in the chamber (3.68\,\%).} This is the largest group. When the chamber sees two
particle-like clusters in a view, there are three possible outcomes. In the first, the reference particle is fitted
but the view fails the cut on competing clusters (1.15\,\%). The any-candidate rule recovers these tracks. In the
second, the reconstruction fits the other, brighter particle (1.51\,\%). Recovering these tracks would need a rule to
choose the triggering track, so it cannot be done without the reference. In the third, the two particles lie within
the 12\,mm in which candidates are merged in one projection, and the fit falls between them (0.46\,\%). The joint
two-track fit of \cref{sec:twoeff} recovers a reference-matched track in 25 of the 146 lost tracks of this class. In
addition, a clean muon with an unrelated deposit elsewhere in the plane can exceed the 50-strip limit (0.35\,\%), and a
second deposit that is not particle-like (0.21\,\%) is not recovered.

\textbf{$\delta$-rays made by the muon (2.61\,\%).} There are three classes here, which are one population of
increasing size (\cref{sec:delta}): a $\delta$-ray along the track (0.87\,\%), a compact saturated $\delta$-ray
(0.93\,\%), and a shower at the crossing (0.81\,\%) that exceeds the 50-strip limit. None of these is recovered.

\textbf{The reconstruction (0.30\,\%).} This group contains fits that are off although no second cluster or
saturation is flagged (0.28\,\%), and fits that failed in one view (0.02\,\%). The second of these used to be larger:
tracks at normal incidence whose two-strip cluster in X was never seeded made up 0.26\,\% in Run~1. Seeding such
clusters recovers them.

\textbf{The reference and the geometry (0.37\,\%).} This group contains a position tail, in which the fit agrees with
the charge but not with the reference (0.17\,\%; about two thirds are tracks bent between the reference planes), and
tracks at the edge of the other coordinate (0.20\,\%). These are not losses of the chamber. The remaining 0.03\,\% is
not classified further.
