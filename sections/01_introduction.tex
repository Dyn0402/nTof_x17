\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
\sectionstate{open}{the X17 programme at n\_TOF (INTC proposal); \texttt{mpgd26} physics-case figures}
\intent{Why a TPC: the X17 search at n\_TOF needs the $e^+e^-$ opening angle, so each chamber must give a
3D track segment from one drift gap. Why resistive strips (spark protection at the n\_TOF flash). What this paper
covers (design, operation, gas, timing, efficiency, resolution on cosmics and in the SPS beam) and what the
companion paper~\cite{paperII} covers (the reconstruction).}

\tbw{Two or three paragraphs. Cite the ATOMKI anomaly~\cite{Krasznahorkay2016}, the $^3$He(n,$e^+e^-$) calculation~\cite{Viviani2022},
Micromegas~\cite{Giomataris1996} and resistive Micromegas~\cite{Alexopoulos2011}.}

  \caption{\tbw{TPC operation. (a) The chamber in section (the stage below the mesh enlarged): the ionisation left along the track drifts straight down to the mesh and arrives later the higher it started, $t=t_0+z/v$; the avalanche lands on the resistive strips, which couple it to the readout strips below. (b) The X view of a real chamber~A cosmic muon (Run~1), amplitude per strip and 60\,ns sample, on the same vertical scale as (a): the track appears as a slanted column from the mesh ($t_0$) to the cathode ($t_0+g/v$, grey lines). Dashed black: the reference track; dashed vermillion: the fitted track. (c) The forward model at the fit, same colour scale: the whole strip $\times$ time picture is fitted, not per-strip times. Each pulse lasts a few samples after its electrons arrive (the shaping), so the charge extends above the cathode line.}}
