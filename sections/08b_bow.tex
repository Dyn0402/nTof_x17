\subsection{The readout plane is not flat: a bow of the readout boards}\label{sec:bow}
\sectionstate{partial}{model-audit follow-up FB, \texttt{rerun/model\_audit/followups/fb\_bow\_maps/} (2026-10-27); slide note \texttt{notes/mx17-chamber-bow}; numbers re-run on the final tables 2026-11-03, \texttt{fb\_bow\_maps/final\_tables.py}}
\intent{Establish, from bench data alone, that the mesh surface of every chamber is dished by 1--2\,mm and that the drift
direction follows its slope; give its size per chamber, its sign from external facts, how stable it is, the evidence
that excludes the alternatives, and what correcting for it gains. Draft (2026-10-27).}

A TPC converts the time structure of the induced signals into a track angle under two assumptions about the
chamber: that the readout plane is flat and that the drift field is perpendicular to it. On all five chambers measured on
the cosmic bench, both assumptions fail at the millimetre level, in the way a bowed readout board predicts.

\paragraph{Three independent observables.}
(i)~\emph{Positions.} A track of slope $\tan\theta = \mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}z$ crosses a mesh displaced by $h$ along $z$
at a point shifted by $h\tan\theta$. In every position cell the slope of the position residual (fitted mesh position
minus the reference prediction) against the reference slope is therefore the local mesh height $h(x,y)$. It is measured independently
by the X and the Y strips, through the two independent reference projections. All numbers in this subsection come from one
estimator, run on the final reconstruction (pass-1 golden rows, chamber~A from the two-track re-pass): an event-level Huber fit of
the position residual to $\tan\theta\,h(u,w)$ with $h$ a quadratic surface of the track position at mid-gap, and of the fitted
transverse speed to a quadratic surface plus a ladder term, for each view (the two-dimensional fit of
\texttt{fb\_bow\_maps/xcheck.py}; the dish depth pools its four curvature terms, \texttt{summary.py}). Both views see a dish in both directions
(Fig.~\ref{fig:bowprof}): the centre lies $1.3$--$1.9$\,mm below a point 150\,mm out
(B $1.87\pm0.18$, A $1.31\pm0.20$, E $1.69\pm0.20$, C $1.76\pm0.15$, D $1.68\pm0.22$\,mm), i.e.\ a mean curvature of
$1.2$--$1.7\times10^{-4}$\,mm$^{-1}$. The errors are statistical (bootstrap, inflated by the scatter of the four terms). The four
terms of one chamber, two per view, scatter by $0.3$--$0.4$\,mm at 150\,mm, which is the realistic uncertainty of a single depth.
The X strips read $1.2$--$1.8$\,mm and the Y strips $1.5$--$1.9$\,mm; neither view is deeper on every chamber
(Y deeper on A, B, C; X deeper on D, E). Along $y$ the dish is $0.15$--$0.7$\,mm deeper than along $x$ on all five chambers
(at 150\,mm, $1.7$--$2.1$ against $1.0$--$1.8$\,mm), a difference of the size of the scatter that we do not claim as an
anisotropy. The figure shows the binned one-dimensional profiles of the same data; along the own axis of each view these are
distorted by edge truncation of wide clusters and give a larger and less stable X/Y split than the pooled fit.
(ii)~\emph{Drift tilt.} If drift lines leave the mesh normal to its surface, charge produced at height $d$ arrives
displaced by $d\,\partial h/\partial x$, so the fitted transverse speed acquires an offset
$w_0 = v\,r_f\,\partial h/\partial x$ with $r_f$ between $1/2$ (flat cathode) and 1 (cathode bowed with the board).
For a dish, $w_0$ must rise linearly across the chamber. It does, in all ten chamber views: the gradient is
$2.1$--$4.3$\,\textmu m/ns per m on the X strips and $3.3$--$7.7$\,\textmu m/ns per m on the Y strips (a ramp of $\pm0.3$ to
$\pm1.2$\,\textmu m/ns over $\pm150$\,mm), and the ratio of the measured gradient to the one predicted from the mesh curvature of the
same view is $r_f = 0.52$--$1.00$ on all ten views, inside the physical range. (Fig.~\ref{fig:bowclos} uses the
one-dimensional profile estimator instead: $r_f=0.6$--$1.1$ on nine views and $1.9$ on chamber~C\,X, whose binned profile has half the
curvature of the two-dimensional fit.) The Y strips read a steeper ramp than the X strips by a factor $1.2$--$2.1$ on every
chamber, a feature of the fit's Y view rather than of the board, so the $r_f$ of the X views ($0.52$--$0.91$) are the safer ones. The
sign of this relation is predicted by the geometry, not fitted.
(iii)~\emph{Narrowest cluster.} The cluster of strips above threshold is narrowest for the track running along the
local drift lines, at $\tan\theta = -w_0/v$. This uses amplitudes only, with no hit times and no fitted angle; the
narrowest-cluster slope drifts across the chamber with the predicted sign on 10/10 views and beyond $2\sigma$ on 8/10
($-0.10$ to $-0.23$ per m on those eight, against $-0.08$ to $-0.18$ predicted from the tilt; chamber~C\,Y and D\,Y are consistent with
zero and with the prediction).

\paragraph{Sign.}
The reference $z$ axis points up (the ray files carry the top and bottom reference plane heights, 1302 and 24\,mm; the upper DUT slot
is at 702\,mm, the lower at 232\,mm), and the chambers lie with the readout board down and the cathode up (the fitted
TPC slope has the sign of the reference slope on every chamber). The mesh centre is therefore \emph{lower} than its rim,
further from the cathode: the direction in which the board's weight, gas overpressure and residual mesh tension all push.

\paragraph{Alternatives.}
A plane-scale error of the telescope that could produce the $w_0$ ramp would leave a $\sim$6\,\% position scale at the
chamber; the measured residual scale is below 0.13\,\%. A position-dependent $t_0$ bias would vanish when every track is
moved to a fixed trigger latency; $94$--$104$\,\% of the curvature survives. Fringe fields at the frame decay within
$\sim$10\,mm of the edges, while the ramp is linear over 300\,mm. A bow of the reference planes would shift tracks by
$\tan\theta\, g(x,y)$ and can mimic $h$, but cannot produce $w_0$ or move the narrowest cluster; a common telescope
contribution to the depth cannot be excluded and limits the depth to \requote{$0.5$--$1.3$} times the quoted value.
The column duration, which should lengthen where the gap deepens, does not resolve the effect: its X and Y readings of
the same column differ by more than the 1--2\,mm expected.

\paragraph{Stability.}
Nineteen bench runs were reconstructed with each chamber's own calibration and their own alignment: other subruns of
the same night, other nights (B after a remount, A and E on other days), both bench slots (A at $z=702$ and
$232$\,mm) and other drift and mesh voltages. All nineteen give their chamber's dish depth within $2\sigma$ of the chamber's
error-weighted mean (largest deviation $1.8\sigma$; 18 of 19 when each run is compared with the mean of the others).
The lowest is chamber~A on 6-25 in the lower slot ($0.97\pm0.15$\,mm against a chamber~A mean of $1.25\pm0.08$\,mm), while
chamber~A in the same lower slot on 6-23 reads $1.46\pm0.15$\,mm, so the slot does not set the depth. The twelve runs with a statistical error below
$0.25$\,mm scatter by 9\,\% rms around their chamber's mean. The drift-tilt shape measured on one run, applied to the others with only the rigid tilt
re-measured, recovers a median 85\,\% of the angular-resolution gain of each run's own map (24 views; the 6-17 chamber~B and
6-23 chamber~A runs, whose angle scale is unusable, are left out). The bow is therefore a
static property of each chamber at the 10--20\,\% level; which load sets it cannot be separated with the data available.

\paragraph{Plausibility.}
The 1.7\,mm readout PCB spans the 0.40\,m aperture of its support plate with only 5\,mm of Rohacell behind it. A
Kirchhoff-plate estimate gives a dip at 150\,mm of 0.1--0.25\,mm from its own weight, 0.3--0.7\,mm per mbar of gas
overpressure and up to $\sim$1\,mm from a mesh laminated under 10\,N/cm: the measured depth needs no exotic load.

\paragraph{Correction.}
The bow enters the reconstruction only through the conversion of the fitted transverse speed into an angle and through
the height of the track point. A per-chamber surface (static shape) plus the per-run rigid tilt, validated by two-fold
cross-validation on held-out tracks of the final reconstruction, improves the angular resolution by a median of 16\,\% (3--29\,\% per view;
14--25\,\% on the Y views); the
shape predicted from positions alone, with two numbers fitted to the angles, recovers 15\,\%. On chamber~C the steep-track
bias ($0.3<|\tan\theta|<0.45$) falls from $-0.9^\circ/-1.1^\circ$ (X/Y) to $-0.65^\circ/-0.8^\circ$. Subtracting
$h\tan\theta$ improves the position residual by 5--18\,\%. Because the load depends on mounting and the chambers
stand vertical at n\_TOF, the bench surface is not transferred to the beam data without an in-situ measurement.
