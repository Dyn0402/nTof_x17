\section{Performance on five chambers}\label{sec:fleet}
\sectionstate{partial}{first prose draft 2026-11-18. Pass 1: \texttt{rerun/paper1\_res/PASS1\_RESOLUTION\_2026-10-31.md} (headline rows), \texttt{rerun/paper1\_floor/README.md} (\texttt{hits\_arm.py}, the fleet figure). Open: R10 (cost table).}
The five chambers were calibrated independently by the chain of \cref{sec:calibration} and reconstructed with the same
code. \Cref{tab:fleet} gives the angle resolution of each on its long run, for tracks in the interior of the active
area. Chambers A and B, which are uniform, reach 0.9 to 1.1\textdegree{} in both views; chambers C and D, which
discharge, 1.2 to 1.5\textdegree; chamber~E, whose amplification is not uniform, 2.1 to 2.4\textdegree{}~\cite{paperI}.
These widths include the error of the reference angle. Up to the 20\textdegree{} the reference reaches, the
resolution is flat in angle within 0.15\textdegree{} on chambers A, B and D. It is best at the low end of each
chamber's gain scan and degrades by 30 to 50\,\% towards the top, as more of the charge saturates, and it is constant
within 0.87 to 1.00\textdegree{} on chamber~A for drift fields from 166 to 366\,V/cm.

\Cref{fig:fleet} compares the waveform fit with the hit chain that was in production before it, on the same events
of the five chambers. With its angle scale calibrated on the reference per run, the hit line has a resolution of 2.0
to 2.6\textdegree, the waveform fit 0.73 to 1.56\textdegree{} (in this comparison the mean dependence of the residual
on angle and position is removed for both, which is why these values are below those of \cref{tab:fleet}). The
position at the mesh has a core width of 550 to 805\,\textmu m from the hits and 430 to 620\,\textmu m from the
waveforms, both dominated by the scattering of the muon in the reference tracker~\cite{paperI}, and the efficiency
within 5\,mm of the reference is the same within 1 to 5 points.

\subsection{A known angle in the beam}\label{sec:knownangle}
\sectionstate{partial}{R7 done: \texttt{rerun/paper1\_spsfigs/README.md} (\texttt{tan\_known.py}); hit-arm point in \cref{tab:tiers}.}
On the bench the angle scale is measured against the reference tracker. The beam gives a test without one: chamber~E
was mounted on a stage turned by 25.4\textdegree{} (the yaw measured from the alignment of the run; the stage was set
to 25.64\textdegree), so that every beam track crosses the inclined view at $\tan\theta = 0.475$
(\cref{fig:known_angle_geometry}). The fit gives $w$, and the angle needs the drift velocity; it is taken from the
duration of the charge column, which is inside the 64-sample window for drift fields of about 180\,V/cm and above in
Ar/CF$_4$/iC$_4$H$_{10}$. On the 13 settings that satisfy this, the reconstructed $\tan\theta$ is 0.467 to 0.496,
that is $0.98\pm0.03$ of the stage value (\cref{fig:known_angle}). The settings at a second stage angle,
15.5\textdegree, were recorded with 32 samples; the column does not fit, there is no velocity, and the angle is not
measured there. Only the magnitude of the angle is tested: no existing measurement fixes the sign of a tangent in the
beam setup.

\subsection{Limits and outlook}\label{sec:outlook}
The model fits one straight track per window. Two tracks in the same chamber are reconstructed when their windows separate~\cite{paperI}; a joint fit of two tracks to one window exists and is not in production.
The depth bins of one sample bias tracks steeper than 25\textdegree{} (\cref{sec:floor}). The X view's asymmetry is not
modelled (\cref{sec:traps}). The slow part of the resistive spreading outlasts the readout window, so its constants
are bounds. And the comparison with a hit-based front end in \cref{sec:tiers} is an emulation on the sampled
waveforms, not a measurement with such a front end.
