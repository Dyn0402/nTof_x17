## 4 · What to expect, and what to actually check

**The headline should barely move, but it must be MEASURED, not assumed.** The
published result is a back-projection *focus* and an in-situ drift velocity
obtained **by** focusing (`K_GRID` scan, `v_insitu = v_bundle / k_opt`). The
angle scale is therefore fitted, and a kernel change that shifts the fitted `w`
by a few per cent is largely absorbed into `k_opt` rather than into the image.
On the bench the same correction moved σ_θ(Y) by +0.06° and left position and
efficiency untouched.

So the checks that matter, in order:

1. **Does the spot still land inside the r ≤ 10 mm bore?** That is the claim.
2. **How much did `k_opt` move?** The published numbers are `v_insitu = 36.1
   µm/ns at k = 1.18` (note) / 36.3 (earlier memory). If `k_opt` moves by more
   than a couple of per cent, the "in-situ v" number on the deck and in the note
   changes and both must be updated.
3. **Per-arm agreement.** Four arms, one target: the arms should agree better
   after the correction, not worse. If B or D gets *worse*, suspect their refit
   basins (§5) before suspecting the correction.
4. **The 51 % external confirmation rate** (predicted SiPM segment AND plastic
   bar in time) — an independent handle that does not go through the fit's angle
   scale at all. It should not degrade.

## 5 · Traps

- **Beam bundles deliberately carry `w0 = None`, `kw = None`.** The bench's
  per-plane angle map is *not* transferred (checked: arm A's prelim has both
  null), so the whole angle scale rides on `v_drift`, which is a **prior**
  (Magboltz 90/10 @233 V/cm, `V_DRIFT_PRIOR = {A: 42.6, B: 34.0, C: 34.0,
  D: 36.0}`), and is then corrected in situ by `k`. **Do not "fix" this by
  copying the bench w0/kw across** — they were measured against M3 on a
  different gas and would double-count with `k`.
- **The gas is different.** run_145 is **Ar/Iso 90/10**; the bench bundles are
  95/5. `sigma_p0` and `Dp` are explicitly `not_revalidated` in the prelim
  provenance, and they are the gas-dependent ones. The kernel (a property of the
  resistive film, not the gas) is the part that legitimately transfers.
- **det2 and det7's r06 refits moved a long way** — kY 2.44 → 5.41 and
  2.51 → 5.60 (the latter against a fit bound of 6), σ_s 71 → 172 and 109 → 52 —
  while det3 stayed at 2.88. Arms B and D inherit those. If an arm behaves
  oddly, this is the first thing to look at, and re-fitting that detector with a
  wider `kY` bound is a legitimate response.
- **`run_79` has exactly the same problem** (`calib_bundle_prelim`, c2/c1 =
  1.14, seeded from det3) and the same fix. `RUN79_PRELIM_2026-07-30.md` is its
  record. Do them together if the fetch is happening anyway.
- **20 samples at 60 ns** on this run (`n_samples: 20`) against the bench's
  longer window — the sharing tail is more truncated here than on the bench,
  which is the direction that makes the *transferred* τ_s least reliable. The
  kernel's amplitudes transfer; its time constant is window-dependent and does
  not (see the memory note's "Delay: measurable, NOT transferable").
