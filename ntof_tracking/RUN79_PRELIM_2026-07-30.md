# Preliminary waveform-first tracks on run_79, joined to n_TOF — 2026-07-30

**Status: PRELIMINARY. Nothing here is a measurement.** This is the
"does the whole chain hang together?" pass: waveform reconstruction on beam
data (`wft/` via `ntof_tracking/wft_beam.py`), joined to our own reprocessed
n_TOF stream (`v12_liqpileup`, run 224572), with two pointing checks that need
no reference telescope. The in-situ calibration of `TRACK_PLAN_08` §6 has
**not** been done — the bundle is the bench one with the DAQ constants swapped
and a drift velocity that is argued for, not measured.

**The answer is yes, the system works.** Tracks reconstructed from run_79
waveforms point back at the He-3 target, and — through the merge — they point
at the SiPM wall segment that actually fired. The drift-velocity disagreement
that clouded the first pass is resolved in §5: **v ≈ 36.6 µm/ns** on the X
plane, and the leftover angle deficit turns out to be a property of the beam
(not every trigger is radial from the target), not of the reconstruction —
once that shared dilution is divided out the angle scale is unity to ~10 %.
Written while other sessions work on the reconstruction itself; everything new
is in `ntof_tracking/` so `wft/` is untouched.

## 2. New code, and why each piece was needed

`wft/` could not be pointed at a beam run as it stood. Three gaps, all filled in
`ntof_tracking/wft_beam.py`:

* **config** — `wft.io`/`wft.reco` consume a `qa_config` object; `BeamConfig`
  duck-types it off the beam `run_config.json`. `common.Mx17StripMap` and
  `wft.io.file_tag` already worked on beam data unchanged.
* **seeding** — the bench spark veto (50 hits/event, whole event) throws away
  most of run_79, whose median event has 19–44 hits *per plane*. The beam seeder
  vetoes **per plane** at 150 hits, offers 5 candidates instead of 3, requires
  ≥5 strips, and drops clusters that are wide but isochronous (≥10 strips
  spanning <3 samples — the pathology `WINDOW_ABLATION_2026-07-30.md` §1 found
  on det D's X plane). Only channels cross into the fit; the time span decides
  *which* clusters to fit, which is detection, not geometry.
* **streaming** — `wft.reco.reconstruct_run` concatenates a whole sub-run's hits
  (~32 M rows here). The beam driver works one file tag at a time and
  **checkpoints the parquet and its `.meta.json` after every tag**.

Two operational fixes that belong in the record because they cost time:

* **BLAS oversubscription.** Each fit worker was spawning 8 OpenBLAS threads;
  6 workers × 8 = 48 runnable threads on 8 cores, load average 39, and the run
  went *slower*. `wft_beam.py` now pins BLAS to one thread per process before
  numpy is imported. **The same shape exists in `mx_june_wft/bench/run_bench.py`
  and `wft.calibrate` with `--jobs 8`, and is worth fixing there too** — nothing
  in the repo pins threads except `condor/run_calib.sh`, which uses
  `OMP_NUM_THREADS` as a worker count instead.
* **A silent-NaN trap.** The first checkpoint wrote the parquet but not the
  sidecar; `--rescale-v` then multiplied every angle by NaN and the checks
  reported "too few events" and `n = 0` — indistinguishable from a statistics
  problem. Both sides now fail loudly instead.

## 3. Result 1 — the tracks point at the target

A track from the He-3 target at the origin, crossing the arm-A strip plane
234.6 mm away at in-plane position u, must have tan θ = −u / 234.6 (the minus
sign because drift depth increases *toward* the target). So the reconstructed
position and the reconstructed angle — two outputs of the same fit that nothing
in the fit ties together — have to be correlated, with a known slope.

Binned medians (the fit's tan has a heavy tail; nothing bounds it):

| sample | n | slope [/mm] | / expected |
|---|---|---|---|
| all reconstructed | 8 299 | −0.00203 | 0.48 |
| **arm-A wall + plastic tag** | 3 602 | **−0.00340** | **0.80** |
| triggered by another arm (null) | 4 356 | −0.00001 | **0.00** |

The split is the point. A shallow slope on all events is ambiguous — low angle
scale, or many tracks that simply do not come from the target. Requiring the
n_TOF scintillator coincidence *in the same arm* selects particles that went
through arm A, and the slope nearly doubles to 0.80× expected. Events whose
trigger came from another arm are a genuine null: 0.00×, with a broad symmetric
angular spread (IQR ±0.5 in tan, i.e. ±27°) and median tan within 0.002 of zero
in **every** position bin. Not empty, not railed — real angles, no correlation.

Two things this rules out: it is not the fit inventing a correlation (the null
would show it too), and it is not the matcher assigning n_TOF hits at random
(the tagged and null samples would not separate).

The acceptance runs the *wrong* way for this result, so 0.80 is conservative:
requiring a wall hit 96 mm downstream excludes strongly negative tan at positive
u, which biases the median slope shallower, not steeper.

**Y plane is 0.17×** and stays flat. Expected to be weaker — Y is the beam axis,
where the source is not a point (the He-3 capsule spans ~80 mm in Y and the beam
illuminates the whole flight path), and it is the resistive-strip axis that
carries the known Y systematics. Worth revisiting, not yet worth worrying about.

Figure: `figures/01_target_pointing_A.png`. The dashed verticals on all three
panels are the plastic scintillators — the limiting element of the trigger, the
wall being wider once both are mapped back here — projected onto the mesh plane
along target rays: u ∈ [−105, +119] mm and v ∈ [−83, +83] mm, with the 3 mm gap
between the two bars dotted. Exact only for a radial track; a track at some
other angle has its own lever arm to the plastic, so the true acceptance in
(u, tan) is a diagonal band. That is also why the binned medians extend past the
lines: those events were triggered by something else.

## 4. Result 2 — the tracks point at the wall segment that fired

Every run_79 DREAM trigger is a wall AND plastic coincidence in one arm. Through
the merge we know *which* wall segment fired. The wall is 16 read bars of 25 mm,
summed in 4 groups of 4 (100 mm each), 96 mm beyond the strip plane. Extrapolate
the track there and compare.

5 345 wall-matched events:

| segment | n | predicted u, median | IQR | bar group (descending) | inside |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 823 | +67.9 mm | [−48, +119] | +75 … +175 | 45 % |
| 1 | 1 185 | +37.9 mm | [−14, +72] | −25 … +75 | 55 % |
| 2 | 1 173 | −72.6 mm | [−106, −22] | −125 … −25 | 58 % |
| 3 | 1 064 | −135.0 mm | [−173, −67] | −225 … −125 | 49 % |

**Ordering correlation −0.98, spread 203 mm**, four cleanly separated peaks
(`figures/02_wall_pointing_A.png`). This is external position truth at 100 mm
granularity, on a lever arm the chamber knows nothing about, and it validates
the tracking and the matcher simultaneously.

Both panels of that figure also carry the **two plastic bars** (grey), because
the trigger is a wall AND plastic coincidence — outside their footprint nothing
fires at all. The plastics are 200 mm wide in u and sit 82–102 mm *behind* the
wall, so what is drawn is their span mapped onto the wall plane along
target-pointing rays (×0.78), i.e. where a track has to cross the wall plane for
the plastic behind it to have been hit: u ∈ [−171, −14] and [−12, +145] mm. That
is narrower than the instrumented wall (−225 … +175 mm), and it is the outer
segments (0 and 3, 45 % and 49 % inside their bar group) that lose the most —
consistent with the trigger, not the tracking, doing the trimming.

The plastics order the same way, for free. Extrapolating instead to the plastic
plane (188.1 mm past the strips) and splitting on which bar fired:

| plastic | n | predicted u, median | IQR | bar span | inside |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `detn` 1 | 2 388 | +46.1 mm | [−57, +114] | −15 … +185 | 58 % |
| `detn` 2 | 2 468 | −105.4 mm | [−179, −11] | −218 … −18 | 59 % |

58–59 % inside a 200 mm bar, against the wall's 52 % inside a 100 mm group. The
positive-u bar is `detn` 1, which is the README's "detn 1 = left bar seen from
the back" under the same descending convention the wall picked — so the two
mappings agree. Both remain joint statements with the in-plane sign (§7.4).

### A by-product: the wall read-out order

`mx_july_beam_qa/README.md` lists the wall channel→bar mapping as an open item.
The data settles it: **descending** — n_TOF `detn` pair 0 reads the bar group at
*most positive* u. 52 % of tracks land inside the correct 100 mm group under
that order against 12 % under the ascending one (25 % would be random). Stated
as measured here, this is a joint statement about the wall mapping *and* the
sign of the in-plane strip axis, which PLAN_01/04 also leaves open; the two
cannot be separated by this test alone.

## 5. The drift-velocity inconsistency, and how it was resolved

The first pass assumed v = 42.6 µm/ns (clean-Magboltz 90/10 at 233 V/cm) and
found three mutually inconsistent handles. `ntof_tracking/column_bias_check.py`
resolves them by calibrating each ruler on the **bench**, where v = 36.6 µm/ns
and the gap = 27.9 mm, and where the 32-sample window truncates nothing:

| ruler | bench reads | vs bench v | run_79 A raw | corrected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fitted column `q_uend` | 38.8 / 42.3 | 1.06× / 1.15× | 38.8 / 38.8 | **36.6 / 33.6** |
| hits-level column span | 44.4 / 43.6 | 1.21× / 1.19× | 43.3 / 41.9 | **35.8 / 35.2** |

(X / Y.) The bench also shows the fit's column *end* landing where the hits say
it does (+13 ns X, −63 ns Y — within a sample), so the earlier suspicion that
the NNLS parks charge hundreds of ns too late is wrong.

Four numbers from two independent rulers converge on **v ≈ 34–37 µm/ns**, i.e.
~15 % below clean 90/10. That is the same signature as the run_58 result already
on record (v ~12 % low, trace water ~0.2 %, air/O₂ excluded by attachment), so
this is consistent with what is known about the gas line rather than new.

Angles were therefore rescaled to **v = 36.0 µm/ns**. `v_drift` is *not* a
parameter of the forward model — the fit measures the transverse speed w and the
bundle only converts it, tan = (w·10³ − w0)/(kw·v) — so this is a pure
rescaling, no refit (`--rescale-v`). That also means a proper in-situ v
measurement can be applied to the existing table for free.

Caveats, because this chain is not as clean as the table looks:

* The bench v = 36.6 and gap 27.9 mm are **not independent** — the gap study
  derived the gap *from* that v. What anchors the scale externally is det2's
  control coming back at the full mechanical 30.5 mm. This ruler calibration
  inherits that anchoring; it is not an independent measurement.
* It assumes the det3 cathode travelled with the chamber to n_TOF (PLAN_08 §11,
  still open). A different gap moves v proportionally.
* The beam sample rails into the last basis bin (18 × 60 = 1080 ns) **26 % (X) /
  29 % (Y)** of the time against the bench's 2 % / 18 %. Railed events are
  excluded from the median, and that selection differs between the two sides —
  this is the weakest link in the transfer.
* `2 × median arrival` (27–28 µm/ns) is left out of the table above: it is
  biased low by the right-truncation of a 20-sample window, which is exactly the
  regime it cannot be used in.

### 5.1 Closing the transfer's own hole: does a 20-sample window bias the ruler?

The table above calibrates the ruler on a **32-sample** bench and applies it to a
**20-sample** beam run — and truncation is precisely the mechanism that could
distort a fitted charge profile. So the bench was re-run through run_79's exact
framing (`run_bench.py --crop 6:20`, the offset measured in
`WINDOW_ABLATION_2026-07-30.md` §1), same 2 500 events both ways:

| | railed | `q_uend` median | implied v | vs truth 36.6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| X, full 32 | 1.8 % | 720 ns | 38.8 | 1.06× |
| X, crop [6,26) | 2.1 % | 720 ns | 38.8 | 1.06× |
| Y, full 32 | 17.4 % | 660 ns | 42.3 | 1.15× |
| Y, crop [6,26) | 5.4 % | 660 ns | 42.3 | 1.15× |

**The crop does not move the ruler at all** — median shift 0 ns per event (about
half the events move by >30 ns, symmetrically). The transfer is therefore valid,
and two further things follow:

* **A more direct statement is now available.** Under the *same* framing, the
  same detector and the same estimator, run_79's non-railed `q_uend` median is
  **720 ns on X — identical to the bench's 720 ns at v = 36.6 µm/ns**. If the gap
  travelled with the chamber, then v(run_79, X) = v(bench) = **36.6 µm/ns**,
  with no ruler-correction arithmetic in between. Y reads 720 vs the bench's 660,
  i.e. 9 % longer → 33.6. Quote X, as `GAP_STUDY` says for column physics.
* **The 26–29 % railing at beam is NOT a window effect.** The bench under
  identical framing rails 2 % (X) / 5 % (Y). Whatever inflates it at beam is
  beam-specific — pileup, the much wider fit windows (median 31 strips against
  the bench's ~10), or non-track deposits inside them. That is a real open item
  and it is now correctly attributed.

### 5.2 The residual angle deficit is the beam, not the reconstruction

That leaves the tagged sample's 0.80× pointing slope. It survives every
cleanliness cut, which rules out fit quality:

| sample | n | ratio |
|---|---|---|
| arm-A wall + plastic tag | 3 602 | 0.80× |
| + single candidate cluster | 2 480 | 0.83× |
| + χ²/dof below median | 2 530 | 0.84× |
| + \|tan\| > 0.1 | 3 232 | 0.85× |
| + non-railed column | 2 785 | 0.82× |

The test that settles it uses **no angles and no v at all**. Take the fired wall
group as position truth and ask where those tracks crossed the strip plane: a
target-pointing track through group centre u_w must cross at
u = u_w × 234.6/331.0 = 0.709 u_w.

| seg | n | group centre | u expected | u measured (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 710 | +125 mm | +105.0 | +80.9 |
| 1 | 1 103 | +25 | +34.1 | +40.1 |
| 2 | 1 081 | −75 | −36.8 | −45.1 |
| 3 | 962 | −175 | −107.7 | −95.7 |

Correlation +0.991, **slope 0.867**, intercept −3.7 mm.

A 13 % *position* scale error is impossible — `p0` comes straight from the strip
map, and the fitted positions fill the plane (p1–p99 = −23 … +389 mm of a 398.6
mm active area). So the 0.867 is a property of **the beam**: not every triggered
particle is radial from the target, and a non-radial component pulls the median
inward in the position test exactly as it pulls the median toward zero in the
angle test.

Both tests carry the same dilution, so their ratio isolates the angle scale:

> **angle scale = 0.80–0.85 / 0.867 = 0.92–0.98**, i.e. consistent with unity to
> within ~10 %.

That is the resolution. There is no unexplained angle compression: v ≈ 36.6 µm/ns
on X, and once the shared non-radial dilution is divided out, the reconstructed
angle scale is right. The bench crop also shows why the earlier "truncation
compression" explanation was too small to matter anyway — cropping costs
−0.18° (X) / −0.41° (Y) at |tan| > 0.14, ~2 % at 17°, not 20 %.

Caveat: 0.867 is one number from four points, and it assumes the descending
wall mapping of §4 and the group centres of `geometry.py`. It bounds the angle
scale; it does not measure it. The in-situ v measurement of §8.1 remains the
thing that would.

## 6b. A third consistency check that came free: the time-in-pulse shape

`figures/03_track_rate_A.png` — reconstructed-track rate against time since the
gamma flash, with and without the arm-A scintillator tag.

* **Nothing below ~1 ms.** The earliest track sits at 0.993 ms and only 75 of
  12 458 events are under 1 ms; the distribution turns on sharply there. That is
  the DREAM-side gate, not a reconstruction effect, and it matches the >1 ms
  thermal gate the trigger-emulation study (`mx_july_beam_qa/30_…`) worked with.
* **The rate peaks at ~4–7 ms** (2 006 events) and falls away to 75 ms. That
  shape is the thermal-neutron arrival distribution whose 5.3 ms peak the
  Geant4 `timedist_2cm` comparison already reproduced. Getting it out of
  *reconstructed tracks*, through an independent chain, is a fourth
  independent thing that had to line up and did.
* The tagged and untagged curves keep the same shape, with the tagged one a
  roughly constant factor below — as expected if the tag is an acceptance, not
  a time-dependent selection.

This is shape agreement only; converting the axis to E_n over 19.5 m and
overlaying the energy bands is PLAN_08 §8 and is not done here.

## 7. What this does NOT establish

1. **No in-situ calibration.** The template, sharing kernel and the diffusion
   pair (`sigma_p0`, `Dp`) are the bench's, transferred untested. PLAN_08 §6
   steps 2, 4 and 5 are all still owed. Diffusion is gas-dependent and is the
   largest un-validated assumption in the chain.
